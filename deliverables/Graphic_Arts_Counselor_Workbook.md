### Req 4: Production Printing Press Execution
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Produce the design you created for requirement 3 using one of the following printing processes: (a) Offset lithography. Make a layout, and produce a plate using a process approved by your counselor. Run the plate and print at least 50 copies. (b) Screen printing. Make a hand-cut or photographic stencil and attach it to a screen that you have prepared. Mask the screen and print at least 20 copies. Resource: Screen Printing Tutorial (video) (c) Electronic/digital printing. Create a layout in electronic form, download it to the press or printer, and run 50 copies. If no electronic interface to the press or printer is available, you may print and scan a paper copy of the layout. (d) Relief printing. Create a layout in electronic form, download it to the press or printer, and run 50 copies. If no electronic interface to the press or printer is available, you may print and scan a paper copy of the layout.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Graphic Arts Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 45)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear safety goggles and tie back long hair near rotating rollers and moving presses.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Graphic Arts Req 4 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Novices expect mechanical presses to print perfect copies immediately without waste make-ready runs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Every printing run requires calibration sheets to balance ink keys and paper feed alignment.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Chemically sensitized plate coatings harden under ultraviolet exposure before aqueous desensitizing development.
- Image Registration: Precise mechanical guide stops guarantee consistent margin alignment across successive sheet feeding.
- Proper solvent balancing prevents ink tack buildup on printing rollers during operation.
- Press Calibration: Impression cylinders exert calibrated mechanical pressure to transfer images cleanly without smudging.

### Req 5: Postpress Operations and Binding
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Postpress Operations. Do the following: (a) Discuss the finishing operations of padding, drilling, cutting, and trimming with your counselor. (b) Collect, describe, or identify examples of the following types of binding: perfect, spiral, plastic comb, saddle stitch, and case.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Graphic Arts Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 73, p. 75)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never place hands beneath a guillotine cutter blade or disable two-hand interlock controls.
- **Common Myth:** People assume that cutting paper stacks with scissors produces straight professional publication margins.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Industrial guillotine cutters rely on mechanical clamps and precision back gauges to achieve square cuts.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Guillotine Cutting: Clamping bars flatten paper lifts firmly before angled knives slice cleanly.
- Hole Drilling: Hollow spinning bits clear paper cores smoothly without overheating during thick operations.
- Adhesive Padding: Liquid plastic compound binds jogged sheet edges securely to cardboard backing strips.
- Signature Assembly: Folded press sheets nest together chronologically before spine staples drive through folds.
