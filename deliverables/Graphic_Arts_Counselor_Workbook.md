### Req 2: Halftone Artwork and Digital Imaging
**Official Requirement 2:** Explain the differences between continuous tone, line, and halftone artwork. Describe how digital images can be created and/or stored in a computer.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Graphic Arts Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 44, p. 58)* • **Organizer (2-Column Comparison Table):** Req 2 Technical Comparison Matrix (Continuous Tone Line and Halftone)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Graphic Arts Requirement 2: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for halftone artwork and digital imaging, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** A printing press creates light and dark gray shades in a black-and-white newspaper photo by mixing lighter and darker dilutions of black ink.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Presses print solid ink in tiny halftone dots of varying sizes, where large dots create dark shadows and small dots create light highlights (p. 44).

### Req 5: Postpress Finishing and Book Binding
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Postpress Operations. Do the following: (a) Discuss the finishing operations of padding, drilling, cutting, and trimming with your counselor. (b) Collect, describe, or identify examples of the following types of binding: perfect, spiral, plastic comb, saddle stitch, and case.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Graphic Arts Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 73, p. 75)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Graphic Arts Requirement 5: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for postpress finishing and book binding, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Perfect binding is the strongest and most expensive book binding method, using sewn cloth covers that last for centuries.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Perfect binding glues page edges into a heavy paper cover at lower cost than case binding, though it will not withstand rough bending (p. 73).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Glued Paperback Covers: A harder paper cover is wrapped around the glued pages, covering the front, spine, and back (p. 73).
- Perfect Binding Durability: Perfect-bound books are less costly than case-bound books, but they will not withstand bending or other hard use (p. 73).
- Saddle-Stitched Pamphlet Folds: Saddle stitching, or stapling through the fold of a multiple page publication, is another method of binding smaller pamphlets or booklets (p. 73).
- Small Booklet Assembly: Saddle stitching is used for smaller publications (p. 73).

- **Common Myth:** When printing on a digital duplicator, the operator immediately runs the full order of thousands of copies before checking how the page looks.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A digital duplicator first creates an internal master and outputs a test sample print so the operator can verify quality before running the full quantity (p. 29).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- When the layout in electronic form is finished, you send it to the output device for printing (p. 29).

- If the sample is acceptable, you select the quantity on the duplicator'scontrol panel, and then the duplicator prints the correct number (p. 29).
- Automated Run Quantities: In some systems, you choose the quantity when the file is downloaded (p. 29).
