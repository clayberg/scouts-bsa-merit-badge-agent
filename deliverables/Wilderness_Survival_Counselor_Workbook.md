## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 10 (Explain):** Careers. Do ONE of the following: (a) Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. With permission of your parent or guardian, your research methods may include an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. **Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Hazards and First Aid. Do the following: (a) Show or explain first aid for, and prevention of, injuries and conditions that could occur in backcountry settings, including: (1) Dehydration (2) Heatstroke (3) Hypothermia (4) Shock (5) Blisters (6) Eye injuries (7) Ankle and knee sprains (8) Bug bites of chiggers, ticks, mosquitoes, and biting gnats (9) Bee stings (10) Bites of spiders (11) Sting of a scorpion (12) Bite of a wild mammal (13) Bite of a venomous snake (b) Explain how the Scout Basic Essentials address hazards of survival situations and are basic to a survival kit. (c) Explain how a trip plan could help prevent a wilderness survival situation.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wilderness Survival Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 53, p. 58)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never give fluids orally to an unconscious person or someone with altered mental status.
- **Common Myth:** First aid skills are only needed after a severe medical crisis develops.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Early recognition of environmental exposure symptoms prevents progression to life-threatening medical emergencies in remote settings.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Thermoregulation Monitoring: Core temperature drops below ninety-five degrees Fahrenheit require immediate active warming and sheltered insulation.
- Dehydration Mitigation: Regular fluid intake maintaining light urine color prevents heat exhaustion during prolonged exertion.
- Shock Management: Elevating legs eight inches and insulating underneath the victim stabilizes systemic arterial pressure.
- Friction Blister Care: Applying moleskin rings over hot spots protects damaged epidermal tissue from infection.

### Req 5: Wilderness Shelter and Clothing
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Shelter. Do the following: (a) Describe the steps you would take to survive in the following exposure conditions: (1) Cold and snowy (2) Wet (3) Hot and dry (4) Windy (5) At or on the water (b) Show that you know the proper clothing to wear while in the outdoors during extremely hot and cold weather and during wet conditions. (c) Explain how to protect yourself from bears and raccoons. (d) Describe how to build or find survival shelters in a forest or in snow. (e) Improvise a natural shelter. For the purpose of this demonstration, use techniques that have little negative impact on the environment. Spend a night in your shelter.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wilderness Survival Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 43, p. 44)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never build shelters beneath dead overhead tree branches or inside potential flash flood channels.
- **Common Myth:** A larger shelter with high ceilings keeps occupants warmer than a small compact space.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Adequate dead air space and ground insulation provide essential thermal protection against conduction.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Cold Mitigation: Packing dense pine boughs creates an insulating barrier preventing ground conductive heat loss.
- Wet Protection: Steeply sloping debris roofs shed driving rain while protecting dry bedding beneath frameworks.
- Heat Reflection: Positioning reflective space blankets above shelters directs radiant solar heat away from occupants.
- Water Safety: Wearing approved flotation vests prevents drowning when traversing swollen rivers or lakeshores.

### Req 7: Emergency Search Signaling Systems
**Official Requirement 7 (Parent Overview):** Signaling. Do the following: (a) Explain and show how lost or stranded Scouts could send signals to attract the attention of ground, airborne, or water search teams. (b) Demonstrate how to use a signal mirror. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Wilderness Survival Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 38)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never look directly at the sun while aligning sighting holes on reflective glass signal mirrors.
- **Common Myth:** Shouting loudly without a whistle is the most effective audible signaling technique in wilderness areas.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Three smoky fires or three loud whistle blasts represent the standard universal emergency distress code.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Mirror Flashing: Sweeping reflected sunlight beams across horizons alerts airborne search crews from great distances.
- Acoustic Signaling: Blowing three distinct whistle blasts creates a recognizable acoustic distress signal across valleys.
- Adding green boughs creates thick white smoke plumes visible to aerial observers.
- Ground Markers: Laying contrasting timber lines forms universal visual symbols recognized by rescue pilots.
