## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 10 (Explain):** Careers. Do ONE of the following: (a) Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. With permission of your parent or guardian, your research methods may include an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. ## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 1:** Hazards and First Aid. Do the following: (a) Show or explain first aid for, and prevention of, injuries and conditions that could occur in backcountry settings, including: (1) Dehydration (2) Heatstroke (3) Hypothermia (4) Shock (5) Blisters (6) Eye injuries (7) Ankle and knee sprains (8) Bug bites of chiggers, ticks, mosquitoes, and biting gnats (9) Bee stings (10) Bites of spiders (11) Sting of a scorpion (12) Bite of a wild mammal (13) Bite of a venomous snake (b) Explain how the Scout Basic Essentials address hazards of survival situations and are basic to a survival kit. (c) Explain how a trip plan could help prevent a wilderness survival situation.
- [ ] **Station Req 4:** First Aid Kits. Put together a personal first aid kit and a personal survival kit. Show how items in the kits are used.
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Shelter. Do the following: (a) Describe the steps you would take to survive in the following exposure conditions: (1) Cold and snowy (2) Wet (3) Hot and dry (4) Windy (5) At or on the water (b) Show that you know the proper clothing to wear while in the outdoors during extremely hot and cold weather and during wet conditions. (c) Explain how to protect yourself from bears and raccoons. (d) Describe how to build or find survival shelters in a forest or in snow. (e) Improvise a natural shelter. For the purpose of this demonstration, use techniques that have little negative impact on the environment. Spend a night in your shelter.
- [ ] **Station Req 7:** Signaling. Do the following: (a) Explain and show how lost or stranded Scouts could send signals to attract the attention of ground, airborne, or water search teams. (b) Demonstrate how to use a signal mirror. (c) Describe from memory five ground-to-air signals and tell what they mean.
- [ ] **Station Req 8:** Water. Demonstrate three ways to treat water found in the outdoors to prepare it for drinking.

  - Life Threatening Heatstroke Emergency Care: Heatstroke occurs when the body thermoregulatory system fails, causing dangerously elevated core temperature, confusion, and hot red skin that may be dry or moist. - **Pamphlet Fact:** Heatstroke can present with either profusely wet or completely dry skin as body temperature control collapses.

#### Req 1a(3): Accidental Hypothermia Recognition and Rewarming
**[Parent Directive]:** *Show or explain first aid for, and prevention of, injuries and conditions that could occur in backcountry settings, including*
**Official Requirement 1a(3):** Hypothermia (4) Shock (5) Blisters (6) Eye injuries (7) Ankle and knee sprains.

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do search and rescue teams treat hypothermia as a medical emergency even in mild temperatures above freezing?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Accidental Hypothermia Recognition And Rewarming: Hypothermia strikes when cold exposure lowers internal core body temperature below 95 degrees Fahrenheit, leading to stumbling, fumbling, and mumbling. Responders must replace wet garments with dry insulating layers, shield the patient from wind, and provide external warmth and warm sweet liquids if the person remains conscious (p. 14).
- **Common Myth:** Rubbing cold limbs vigorously or placing a hypothermic patient into a hot bath quickly restores core warmth.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Vigorous rubbing or sudden hot bathing sends cold acidic blood from the extremities straight to the heart and causes fatal cardiac arrest.

#### Req 1a(4): Systemic Circulatory Shock Emergency Care
**[Parent Directive]:** *Show or explain first aid for, and prevention of, injuries and conditions that could occur in backcountry settings, including*

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What subtle physical signs tell you that an injured camper is developing shock before blood pressure drops dramatically?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Systemic Circulatory Shock Emergency Care: Circulatory shock develops when severe trauma, blood loss, or illness deprives vital organs of adequate oxygenated blood flow. Care requires lying the victim flat, raising legs roughly twelve inches if no head or spine injury exists, keeping the patient warm with blankets, and reassuring them continuously (p. 15).
- **Common Myth:** Casualties entering traumatic shock should immediately receive large gulps of water and warm food to keep up their energy.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Shock patients must never receive food or drink by mouth because reduced digestive blood flow causes choking or vomiting.

#### Req 1a(5): Friction Blister Prevention and Treatment

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What immediate action should you take the very moment you feel a hot spot developing on your heel?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Friction Blister Prevention And Treatment: Friction and moisture between footwear, socks, and skin cause painful fluid-filled blisters on long treks. #### Req 1a(9): Hymenoptera Sting and Anaphylaxis Care
**[Parent Directive]:** *Show or explain first aid for, and prevention of, injuries and conditions that could occur in backcountry settings, including*
**Official Requirement 1a(9):** Bee stings (10) Bites of spiders (11) Sting of a scorpion (12) Bite of a wild mammal (13) Bite of a venomous snake.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wilderness Survival Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 53, p. 58)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix):** Req 1a
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what ways do you distinguish between a normal localized bee sting reaction and the onset of systemic anaphylaxis?

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wilderness Survival Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 49, p. 51)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 9 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does metabolic digestion of heavy proteins and toxic plants increase bodily water requirements?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Toxic Hazards: Ingesting unfamiliar wild mushrooms and plants risks violent digestive poisoning and debilitating dehydration.
  - Digesting complex wild foods requires metabolic energy and consumes precious bodily fluid reserves.
  - Survival Duration: Healthy humans survive weeks without food when protected from weather and adequately hydrated.
  - Poisoning Symptoms: Ingestion of wild toxins causes vomiting and severe diarrhea that hastens backcountry collapse.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Assess Needs: Recognize that hydration and thermal shelter take total priority over collecting edible food.
  - Step 2 - Conserve Calories: Limit strenuous physical movements to prevent burning vital stored glycogen and body fat.
  - Step 3 - Avoid Ingestion: Refuse to consume unidentified wild plants, mushrooms, or foul smelling insects in camp.
