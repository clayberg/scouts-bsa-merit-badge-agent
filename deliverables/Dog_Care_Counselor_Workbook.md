### Req 2: Canine Skeletal Anatomy Examination
**Official Requirement 2:** Point out on a dog or a sketch at least 10 body parts. Give the correct name of each one.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Dog Care Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 27, p. 46)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Where is the canine stifle joint located relative to the human knee?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Withers Elevation Point: The dorsal ridge formed by third through fifth thoracic vertebrae determines shoulder height.
  - The true knee joint connects femur and tibia to provide hindlimb propulsion.
  - Pastern Shock Absorption: Angled metacarpal bones between carpus and foot pads absorb downward landing impact forces.
  - Hock Joint Mechanics: The canine tarsus raises the heel above ground level for efficient spring motion.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Cranial Identification: Identify cranial landmarks including the muzzle, stop, flews, and prominent occiput bone clearly.
  - Step 2 - Forequarter Palpation: Locate the withers, point of shoulder, elbow joint, and angled front pasterns sequentially.
  - Step 3 - Hindquarter Examination: Palpate the stifle joint, hock, rear pastern, and tail set along the spine.
  - Step 4 - Conformation Review: Cross check measured body proportions against standard anatomical charts to confirm physical correctness.
- **Common Myth:** Canine anatomical landmarks share the exact structural terminology and joint placements as human limbs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Official canine anatomical terms like withers and pastern define functional skeletal proportions and health.

- **Common Myth:** Free feeding unlimited dry kibble allows dogs to regulate their exact caloric nutritional needs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Recording daily ration quantities and weekly body condition scores prevents canine obesity and malnutrition.
