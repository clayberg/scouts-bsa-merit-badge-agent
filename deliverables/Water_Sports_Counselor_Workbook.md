## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 2:** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the characteristics of life jackets most appropriate for water sports, and tell why one must always be worn while waterskiing or wakeboarding. Then demonstrate how to select and fit a life jacket for water sports activities. (b) Review and discuss the Water Sports Safety Code with your counselor. Promise that you will live up to it and follow it in all water work for this merit badge. Review the safety precautions that must be used by the boat operator in pulling waterskiers and wakeboarders.
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Showing reasonable control while using two skis, one ski, or a wakeboard, do the following: (a) Show how to enter the water from a boat and make a deepwater start without help. (b) Starting from outside the wakes, show you can cross both wakes four times and return to the center of the wake each time, without falling. (c) Show you can fall properly to avoid an obstacle. Also show that you can drop handle and coast to a stop without losing your balance.
- [ ] **Station Req 6:** While on shore, show that you know how to properly adjust the bindings of your ski(s) or wakeboard to fit yourself. Then, in deep water, show you can adjust bindings to fit. Recover and put on your ski(s) or wakeboard that has come off during a fall.

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must a skier demonstrate independent swimming proficiency even while wearing a life jacket?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Distance Swimming Benchmark: Swim seventy-five yards using a strong, well-executed forward crawl or trudgen stroke.
  - Resting Stroke Transition: Complete the final twenty-five yards using an easy, restful elementary backstroke.
  - Leap feetfirst into water over your head before beginning the continuous stroke trial.
  - Rest by floating motionless on your back for one minute following the swim.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Feetfirst Entry: Jump feetfirst into water over your head and level off into swimming position.
  - Step 2 - Forward Propulsion: Swim seventy-five yards using a continuous, propulsive crawl or breaststroke without stopping.
  - Step 3 - Stroke Transition: Roll over smoothly to swim twenty-five yards using an elementary backstroke technique.
  - Step 4 - Motionless Float: Float motionless on your back for one full minute to demonstrate resting stamina.
- **Common Myth:** Wearing a life jacket allows a non-swimmer to bypass the required swimmer test.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Every participant must pass the standard swimmer test before beginning water sports instruction.

### Req 4: Skier Hand Signal Communication
**Official Requirement 4:** Show the following skier signals to the safety observer in the boat: skier safe, faster, slower, turns, back to dock, cut motor, skier in water.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 20, p. 21)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Signal skier safe immediately after falling so the observer knows you are conscious and uninjured.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must the safety observer watch the skier rather than the boat operator doing so?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Speed Increase Gesture: Extend one hand forward with thumb pointed distinctly upward toward the tow boat.
  - Speed Reduction Signal: Point the thumb downward toward the water to instruct the driver to decelerate.
  - Motor Cutoff Command: Draw a flat hand firmly across the throat to demand immediate engine shutdown.
  - Skier Safe Posture: Clasp both hands together overhead to signal no injury occurred after a fall.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Signal Preparation: Establish eye contact with the boat safety observer before executing any hand signal.
  - Step 2 - Speed Modulation: Form clear thumbs-up or thumbs-down gestures to fine-tune the boat towing speed.
  - Step 3 - Directional Indication: Point with your open hand toward the left or right to command turns.
  - Step 4 - Recovery Signaling: Raise one ski or clasp hands overhead immediately upon falling into the water.
- **Common Myth:** Yelling loudly from behind the boat is an effective way to request speed changes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Outboard engine roar and wind noise completely drown out shouts from a towed skier.

### Req 6: Binding Adjustment and Recovery
**Official Requirement 6:** While on shore, show that you know how to properly adjust the bindings of your ski(s) or wakeboard to fit yourself. Then, in deep water, show you can adjust bindings to fit. Recover and put on your ski(s) or wakeboard that has come off during a fall.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 49)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never insert dry feet into rubber bindings because friction can tear delicate boot rubber or sprain ankles.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must water ski bindings release your feet during a high-speed fall?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Wet the rubber binding with lake water before sliding feet into the boots.
  - Position your heel firmly against the rear cup before tightening overlay tension straps.
  - Deepwater Retrieval Drill: Swim toward floating skis using breaststroke while scanning continuously for approaching boat hulls.
  - In-Water Fitting Method: Submerge the ski tail beneath you to slide feet into bindings while floating.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Shoreline Sizing: Loosen binding laces, wet rubber boots, and slide feet in for snug fit.
  - Step 2 - Equipment Retrieval: Locate detached floating skis in deep water and gather them alongside your torso.
  - Step 3 - In-Water Mounting: Push the ski tail underwater while guiding your lubricated foot into front binding.
  - Step 4 - Rear Foot Insertion: Slip the rear foot into toe piece and cinch cords securely before signaling.
- **Common Myth:** Bindings should be tightened as rigidly as downhill snow ski boots to maximize control.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water ski bindings must release the feet during violent falls to prevent severe lower-leg fractures.
