### Req 1: Water Sports Hazard Mitigation
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in water sports activities and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for the following injuries or illnesses that could occur while participating in water sports: blisters, cold-water shock and hypothermia, dehydration, heat-related illnesses, sunburn, sprains, strains, minor cuts and bruises, spinal injury, and concussions and head trauma. (c) Review the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Tell how it applies to water sports.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Always shut down the boat engine before bringing a fallen skier alongside or onto the swim platform.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** A boat operator can easily watch the skier behind the boat while steering forward.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The law and safety standards require a separate observer to watch the skier continuously.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Engine Shutdown Protocol: Turn off the boat motor completely before any skier approaches the boarding ladder.
- Submerged Debris Survey: Scout shallow water zones and sandbars prior to towing skiers at high speeds.
- Tow Line Clearance: Keep floating towlines away from rotating boat propellers to prevent sudden line entanglements.
- Weather Awareness Standards: Cease all towing operations immediately whenever lightning strikes or dense fog develops offshore.

### Req 2: Flotation Selection and Safety Code
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the characteristics of life jackets most appropriate for water sports, and tell why one must always be worn while waterskiing or wakeboarding. Then demonstrate how to select and fit a life jacket for water sports activities. (b) Review and discuss the Water Sports Safety Code with your counselor. Promise that you will live up to it and follow it in all water work for this merit badge. Review the safety precautions that must be used by the boat operator in pulling waterskiers and wakeboarders.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 41)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never use an ill-fitting or non-impact-rated life jacket during water sports activities.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Inflatable life vests work well for water skiing because they are lightweight and unobtrusive.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** High-speed water impacts can prevent automatic inflation or tear lightweight inflatable chambers instantly.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Impact Rating Mandate: Select life jackets constructed with heavy-duty foam designed to withstand high-velocity water impacts.
- Fasten every buckle and cinch straps snugly to prevent slippage over the head.
- Code Compliance Rule: Never ski after sunset or before sunrise regardless of clear ambient weather conditions.
- Carbon Monoxide Defense: Avoid teak surfing or dragging behind boat exhaust outlets to prevent poisonings.

### Req 5: Water Skiing Maneuvers and Control
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Showing reasonable control while using two skis, one ski, or a wakeboard, do the following: (a) Show how to enter the water from a boat and make a deepwater start without help. (b) Starting from outside the wakes, show you can cross both wakes four times and return to the center of the wake each time, without falling. (c) Show you can fall properly to avoid an obstacle. Also show that you can drop handle and coast to a stop without losing your balance.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 69)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never wrap any part of the towline around hands, wrists, or limbs while skiing.
- **Common Myth:** Standing up quickly by muscle force alone lifts the skier out of deep water.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Boat propulsion creates hydroplane lift, meaning the skier should remain crouched until planing occurs.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Deepwater Starting Posture: Keep knees bent deeply against chest while holding ski tips above the water.
- Arms Straight Technique: Allow boat pull to draw body upward without prematurely pulling arms into chest.
- Attack wake crests at a sharp diagonal angle while keeping knees flexed.
- Drop the tow handle cleanly and coast smoothly until momentum dissipates in water.

### Req 6: Binding Adjustment and Recovery
**Official Requirement 6:** While on shore, show that you know how to properly adjust the bindings of your ski(s) or wakeboard to fit yourself. Then, in deep water, show you can adjust bindings to fit. Recover and put on your ski(s) or wakeboard that has come off during a fall.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never insert dry feet into rubber bindings because friction can tear delicate boot rubber or sprain ankles.
- **Common Myth:** Bindings should be tightened as rigidly as downhill snow ski boots to maximize control.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water ski bindings must release the feet during violent falls to prevent severe lower-leg fractures.
