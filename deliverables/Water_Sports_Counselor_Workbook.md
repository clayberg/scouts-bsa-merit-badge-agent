### Req 1: Water Sports Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in water sports activities and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for the following injuries or illnesses that could occur while participating in water sports: blisters, cold-water shock and hypothermia, dehydration, heat-related illnesses, sunburn, sprains, strains, minor cuts and bruises, spinal injury, and concussions and head trauma. (c) Review the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Tell how it applies to water sports.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Water Sports Requirement 1: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for water sports hazards and first aid, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Because water-skiers and wakeboarders get splashed continuously, they cannot suffer from dehydration, sunburn, or life-threatening heatstroke.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water reflection and physical exertion increase heat and sun exposure, and a body temperature of 105 degrees or higher is life-threatening heatstroke requiring immediate emergency cooling and medical care (p. 28).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Danger of High Body Temperature: A body temperature of 105 degrees or greater is a life-threatening medical condition and requires immediate medical treatment by health-care (p. 28).
- Emergency Medical Call for Heatstroke: Heatstroke is a life-threatening condition; call for medical assistance immediately (p. 28).
- Immediate Cooling Before EMS Arrives: While waiting for medical personnel to arrive, work to lower the victim's temperature (p. 28).
- Relocating to Shade or Cooling: Move the person to an air-conditioned or shady area (p. 28).

### Req 2: Life Jackets and Safety Code
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the characteristics of life jackets most appropriate for water sports, and tell why one must always be worn while waterskiing or wakeboarding. Then demonstrate how to select and fit a life jacket for water sports activities. (b) Review and discuss the Water Sports Safety Code with your counselor. Promise that you will live up to it and follow it in all water work for this merit badge. Review the safety precautions that must be used by the boat operator in pulling waterskiers and wakeboarders.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 41)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Water Sports Requirement 2: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for life jackets and safety code, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Keeping a Type IV throwable ring buoy or boat seat cushion on board eliminates the need for a strong swimmer to wear a lifejacket while waterskiing.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Type IV throwable cushions and buoys are only meant to be tossed to someone nearby and must never be used in place of a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved Type III ski vest (p. 17).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Throwable Flotation Aid Purpose: Designed to be tossed to a person nearby in the water (p. 17).
- Ring Buoys and Seat Cushions: Type IV throwables include ring buoys and seat cushions with straps (p. 17).
- Never Substitute for Wearable Vests: They should never be used in place of a lifejacket (p. 17).
- Hybrid Buoyancy and Inflation Design: Designed to have internal buoyancy and to inflate for additional flotation (p. 17).

### Req 5: Deepwater Starts and Wake Crossings
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Showing reasonable control while using two skis, one ski, or a wakeboard, do the following: (a) Show how to enter the water from a boat and make a deepwater start without help. (b) Starting from outside the wakes, show you can cross both wakes four times and return to the center of the wake each time, without falling. (c) Show you can fall properly to avoid an obstacle. Also show that you can drop handle and coast to a stop without losing your balance.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 69)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Water Sports Requirement 5: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for deepwater starts and wake crossings, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** During a deepwater start, you should pull the towrope handle into your chest with bent elbows and stand up straight as soon as the boat moves.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You should keep your knees bent and your arms straight during a deepwater start, letting the boat pull you up onto the water before rising and rotating your hips (p. 59).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Front Foot Weight for Stability: To keep the board from wobbling, puts lightly more weight on your front foot than on your back foot as the boat begins Alternate Method for Deep water (p. 59).
- Partially Rotated Wakeboard Start Option: You may prefer to start out with the wake board partially rotated uptoward the front foot (p. 59).
- Begin with the wake board out in front of you and parallel to the water's surface (p. 59).
- Bent Knees and Straight Arms: Keep your knees bent and hold the rope handle with your arms straight (p. 59).

### Req 6: Binding Adjustment and Deepwater Recovery
**Official Requirement 6:** While on shore, show that you know how to properly adjust the bindings of your ski(s) or wakeboard to fit yourself. Then, in deep water, show you can adjust bindings to fit. Recover and put on your ski(s) or wakeboard that has come off during a fall.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Water Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 49)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Shore Show That You Know)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Water Sports Requirement 6: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for binding adjustment and deepwater recovery, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Beginner wakeboard bindings should be mounted right next to each other in the center of the board with both feet pointing straight forward toward the nose.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Beginner wakeboard bindings should be set about shoulder-width apart with the back foot at 0 to 9 degrees and the front foot angled outward at 9 to 27 degrees (p. 17).
