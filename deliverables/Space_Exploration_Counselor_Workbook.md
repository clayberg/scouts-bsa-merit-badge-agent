### Req 1: Space Exploration Core Purposes
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Tell the purpose of space exploration and include the following: (a) Historical reasons (b) Immediate goals in terms of specific knowledge (c) Benefits related to Earth resources, technology, and new products (d) International relations and cooperation
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Space Exploration Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 69, p. 71)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Maintain clear radio communications during tracking operations to prevent field hazards around mobile antennas.
- **Common Myth:** Early space exploration originated solely as a peaceful international scientific endeavor.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** National security concerns and scientific inquiry drove federal investments in orbital rocketry.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Cold War Drivers: Military ballistic missile advancements established foundational boosters for initial crewed orbital flights.
- Solar Radiation Data: Heliophysics satellites measure coronal mass ejections to protect commercial power grids.
- Spin-off Tech Transfer: Medical imaging sensors directly evolved from charged-coupled devices engineered for orbital observatories.
- Global Treaty Frameworks: Outer space accords prohibit national sovereignty claims over planetary bodies and orbits.

### Req 4: Orbital Mechanics Engine Physics
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Discuss and demonstrate each of the following: (a) The law of action-reaction (b) How rocket engines work (c) How satellites stay in orbit (d) How satellite pictures of Earth and pictures of other planets are made and transmitted
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Space Exploration Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 35, p. 39)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear eye protection when demonstrating pressurized air or water rocket action-reaction dynamics.
- **Common Myth:** Satellites orbit Earth because gravitational attraction drops to absolute zero in orbit.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Satellites remain in continuous free fall while their forward velocity matches planetary curvature.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Third Law Propulsion: Rapid expulsion of exhaust mass produces an equal, opposite forward vehicle thrust.
- Expanding gases push against the combustion chamber walls before exiting through nozzles.
- Horizontal Orbital Velocity: Satellites fall toward Earth at the exact rate the surface curves away.
- Digital Sensor Telemetry: Electronic charge detectors convert incoming light wavelengths into binary radio telemetry signals.

### Req 6: Crewed Orbital Vehicle Architecture
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Describe the purpose, operation, and components of ONE of the following: (a) Space shuttle or any other crewed orbital vehicle, whether government-owned (U. S. or foreign) or commercial (b) International Space Station
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Space Exploration Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 76, p. 78)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review pressurized airlock depressurization procedures to understand rapid decompression risks during EVA operations.
- **Common Myth:** Orbital space stations float without experiencing any atmospheric friction in low Earth orbit.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The International Space Station requires regular propulsive reboosts to counteract low Earth orbit drag.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Orbital Atmospheric Drag: Residual gas molecules slowly degrade station altitude, necessitating periodic propulsion thruster reboosts.
- Closed-Loop Life Support: Electrolysis systems split recycled wastewater into breathable oxygen and discarded hydrogen.
- Photovoltaic Power Distribution: Articulated solar arrays track the Sun to charge nickel-hydrogen and lithium-ion batteries.
- Ammonia coolant loops transfer electronics heat away from modules to radiative panels.
