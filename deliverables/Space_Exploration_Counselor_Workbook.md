**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Tell the purpose of space exploration and include the following: (a) Historical reasons (b) Immediate goals in terms of specific knowledge (c) Benefits related to Earth resources, technology, and new products (d) International relations and cooperation
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Space Exploration Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 69, p. 71)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Space Exploration Requirement 1: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying purposes and benefits of space exploration, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Space shuttle flights lasted long enough to complete all necessary long-term microgravity experiments without needing a permanent station.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Space shuttle flights were too few and too brief for extended research, prompting nations to build the International Space Station for continuous orbital study (p. 69).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Long-Term Orbital Research: The United States, Russia, and other countries wanted alarger, longer-lasting facility to do long-term research in space (p. 69).
- The space shuttle had supported some research, but flights had been too few and too short (p. 69).
- A bigger space station also would provide better research support services (such as data transmission) than the shuttle could ever provide (p. 69).
- Purposes of the ISS The million-pound ISS is the largest construction project ever attempted in space (p. 69).

### Req 4: Rocket Physics and Satellite Orbits
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Discuss and demonstrate each of the following: (a) The law of action-reaction (b) How rocket engines work (c) How satellites stay in orbit (d) How satellite pictures of Earth and pictures of other planets are made and transmitted
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Space Exploration Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 35, p. 39)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Space Exploration Requirement 4: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying rocket physics and satellite orbits, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Rockets cannot accelerate in the vacuum of space because there is no surrounding air for the exhaust to push against.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Under Newton's third law of motion, a rocket accelerates forward by expelling high-speed exhaust gases backward, requiring no outside air to push against (p. 35).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Space exploration has been a reality since the late 1950s (p. 35).
- Core Spaceflight Terminology: Space age words such as rocket, satellite, and orbit have become part of nearly everyone's vocabulary (p. 35).
- Newton's Classical Physics Foundations: In the 17th century, a great English mathematician and scientist named Sir Isaac Newton developed the basics of modern physics (p. 35).
- Formulating Gravitational Theory: He formed the theories of gravitation when he was only 23 years old (p. 35).

#### Req 4a: Newton's Action-Reaction Principle
**Official Requirement 4a:** The law of action-reaction.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Space Exploration Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 35, p. 39)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Law of Action-reaction)
- **Common Myth:** Satellites stay in orbit because Earth's gravity stops acting once a spacecraft reaches space.

**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor a robotic space exploration mission and a historic crewed mission.
