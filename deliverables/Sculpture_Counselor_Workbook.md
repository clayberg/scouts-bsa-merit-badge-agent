### Req 1: Sculptural Studio Safety Practices
**Official Requirement 1:** Explain to your counselor the precautions that must be followed for the safe use and operation of a sculptor's tools, equipment, and other materials.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 20)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Precautions That Must Be Followed)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Always carve strokes directed away from hands, torso, and nearby bystanders, keeping both hands behind the sharp cutting edge at all times.
- **Common Myth:** Novice carvers believe dull tools are safer because sharp edges slice flesh more easily.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dull cutting tools require excessive force and slip erratically, creating far higher puncture hazards than razor edges.

**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Model in clay a life-size human head. Explain to your counselor the method and tools you used to make the copy. (c) With your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, visit a museum, art exhibit, art gallery, artists' co-op, or artist's studio. After your visit, share with your counselor what you have learned. Discuss the importance of visual arts and how it strengthens social tolerance and helps stimulate cultural, intellectual, and personal development.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 63)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never pour leftover liquid plaster down residential plumbing drains, because it solidifies underwater and permanently blocks pipes.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Sculpture Req 2 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners think sculpting involves solely carving material away from solid blocks rather than additive modeling.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Sculpture encompasses additive assembly like clay modeling, subtractive reduction like stone carving, and casting via molds.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Armature Core Foundation: Sturdy internal wire supports prevent heavy wet clay figures from sagging downward.
- Anatomical Proportion Ratios: Human facial features align with eye level positioned halfway down the skull.
- Curing plaster heats significantly and expands slightly as crystals lock into matrix.
- Gallery Spatial Analysis: Professional galleries install sculptures with dedicated sightlines and targeted three dimensional illumination.

Research any training needed, expenses, and organizations that promote or support it. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 60)* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** When researching industrial fabrication careers on-site, obey all posted workshop guidelines including steel-toed boots and eye protection.
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Sculpture Req 3 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** Sculpture careers are restricted exclusively to selling bronze statues or gallery display pieces.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Professional sculptors work in automotive design, medical prosthetics, animatronics, videogame character design, and architectural conservation.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Special Effects Fabrication: Entertainment prop builders sculpt detailed biological creatures using digital software and urethane foams.
- Industrial Clay Modeling: Automotive designers shape full scale vehicle prototypes using specialized heated styling wax clays.
- Community Guild Infrastructure: Regional carving clubs provide shared access to expensive foundry furnaces and ventilation hoods.
- Continuing Education Apprenticeships: Aspiring stone carvers often train under master masons before launching independent businesses.
