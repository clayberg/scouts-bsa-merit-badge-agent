### Req 1: Sculptural Studio Safety Practices
**Official Requirement 1:** Explain to your counselor the precautions that must be followed for the safe use and operation of a sculptor's tools, equipment, and other materials.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 20)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Always carve strokes directed away from hands, torso, and nearby bystanders, keeping both hands behind the sharp cutting edge at all times.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does dry sweeping clay or plaster dust pose greater health hazards than damp sponge wiping?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Chisel Sharpness: Dull gouges slip easily across material surfaces and cause severe hand puncture lacerations.
  - Airborne Dust Protection: Mixing dry plaster or clay releases fine silica particles requiring approved particulate respirators.
  - Stock Clamping Rule: Secure wooden blocks firmly in bench vises before applying mallet strike force.
  - Organic Solvent Ventilation: Chemical release agents and resin glues demand continuous cross ventilation during application.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Gear Selection: Don impact rated safety glasses and an approved respirator before handling raw materials.
  - Step 2 - Bench Setup: Clamp all raw workpieces securely using heavy screw clamps on stable tables.
  - Step 3 - Edge Assessment: Hone woodcarving chisels on an oiled whetstone to maintain clean cutting edges.
  - Step 4 - Cleanup Protocol: Wipe residue with damp sponges rather than sweeping hazardous dry mineral dust.
- **Common Myth:** Novice carvers believe dull tools are safer because sharp edges slice flesh more easily.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dull cutting tools require excessive force and slip erratically, creating far higher puncture hazards than razor edges.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Standard adult cranial anatomy places the horizontal eye line precisely midway between the chin and the crown of the skull.

#### Req 2b: Plaster Mold Making and Casting
**Official Requirement 2b:** Make a plaster mold of a fruit or vegetable. In this mold, make a copy of the fruit or vegetable. Explain to your counselor the method and tools you used to make the copy.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 63)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 2b Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For sculpture Requirement 2b (Make a plaster mold of a fruit or vegetable. In this mold, make a copy of the fruit or), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Creating a waste mold of produce requires embedding the subject to its midline in clay and applying plaster mixed to a creamy consistency. Liquid soap or petroleum jelly acts as a necessary parting agent to ensure the positive cast releases cleanly from the negative mold.
- **Common Myth:** Plaster casts will release easily from plaster molds without applying any separator or barrier coat.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Porous plaster surfaces bond permanently unless coated thoroughly with a release agent such as liquid mold soap or petroleum jelly.
