### Req 1: Safe Use of Sculpting Tools
**Official Requirement 1:** Explain to your counselor the precautions that must be followed for the safe use and operation of a sculptor's tools, equipment, and other materials.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 20)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Precautions That Must Be Followed)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Sculpture Requirement 1: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for safe use of sculpting tools, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Because soapstone is categorized as a soft stone, a sculptor can easily shape it with light pressure and basic clay loop tools.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Even though soapstone is relatively soft, carving a form still requires significant physical effort and specialized metal rasps and rifflers with fine cutting points (p. 19).

#### Req 2b: Plaster Mold Making and Casting
**Official Requirement 2b:** Make a plaster mold of a fruit or vegetable. In this mold, make a copy of the fruit or vegetable. Explain to your counselor the method and tools you used to make the copy.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 63)* • **Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 2b Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist (Plaster Mold of A Fruit)
- **Common Myth:** Pouring plaster directly over a fresh fruit or vegetable without a release agent allows easy removal of the cast.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Mold makers must coat the original model with a release agent and build a multi-part mold with keys so the hardened plaster separates cleanly without trapping the object (p. 23).

Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Resources: 12 Careers for Sculptors (website) How To Become an Artist/Sculptor (video) Craft Artist Careers (video) (b) Explore how you could use knowledge and skills from this merit badge to pursue a hobby. Research any training needed, expenses, and organizations that promote or support it. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sculpture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 60)* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Sculpture Requirement 3: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for sculpting careers and creative hobbies, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Sculpture Req 3 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** To become a professional sculptor, you must wait until college to learn how to work with wood, metal, or plastics.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Aspiring sculptors can start right in high school by taking shop and art classes that introduce techniques for working with metal, wood, and plastics (p. 59).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Studio Lessons and Training: If you want to pursue a career as aprofessional sculptor, you will need some studio lessons and hands-on training (p. 59).
- High School Shop Classes: You can start by finding out if your high school offers shop classes that will introduce you to the techniques for working with metal, wood, and plastics (p. 59).
- Artist Apprenticeships and Assisting: Later, you can get hands-on training by working for another artist as an apprentice or assistant (p. 59).
- Advanced Degrees for Sculptors: If you intend to supplement your income in a related field, you might have to earn advanced degrees (p. 59).
