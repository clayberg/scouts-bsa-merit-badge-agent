### Req 1: Woodshop Safety and Health Hazards
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in woodwork activities, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. Explain what precautions you should take to safely use your tools. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries that could occur while woodworking, including splinters, scratches, cuts, severe bleeding, and shock. Tell what precautions must be taken to help prevent loss of eyesight or hearing, and explain why and when it is necessary to use a dust mask. (c) Earn the Totin' Chip recognition.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 36)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Tie back long hair, roll sleeves above elbows, remove all jewelry, and never wear gloves when operating rotating machinery.
- **Common Myth:** Gloves protect hands from revolving power tool blades.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Gloves create dangerous snag hazards around spinning cutters and must never be worn near rotating woodworking machinery.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Eye Protection Protocols: Impact rated safety glasses shield eyes from airborne sawdust and splintering workpieces.
- Hearing Protection Standards: Earmuffs or foam plugs prevent permanent acoustic nerve damage from loud machinery.
- Respiratory Dust Filtration: Particulate respirators block fine wood airborne fibers during heavy sawing and sanding.
- Apparel Entanglement Risks: Loose clothing, jewelry, and long untied hair quickly snag into rotating spindles.

### Req 2: Lumber Harvesting, Curing, and Classification
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe how timber is grown, harvested, and milled. Tell how lumber is cured, seasoned, graded, and sized. (b) Collect and label blocks of six kinds of wood useful in woodworking. Describe the chief qualities of each. Give the best uses of each.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 15)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Inspect recycled or reclaimed timber with a metal detector before machining to avoid striking hidden nails or screws with blades.
- **Common Myth:** A two by four stud purchased at a retail yard measures exactly two inches by four inches.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Nominal lumber dimensions reflect rough green sawn stock before surfacing reduces a two by four to one and one half inches by three and one half inches.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Annular rings reveal seasonal springwood growth cycles and denser late summer cellular bands.
- Kiln drying rapidly bakes out green moisture to stabilize boards against warping.
- Dimensional Lumber Sizing: Surfaced nominal two by four boards measure one and one half inches thick.
- Hardwood Softwood Anatomy: Broadleaf deciduous trees yield hardwoods while cone bearing coniferous evergreen species yield softwoods.

#### Req 2a: Timber Milling and Lumber Grading
**Official Requirement 2a:** Describe how timber is grown, harvested, and milled. Tell how lumber is cured, seasoned, graded, and sized.

- **Common Myth:** Air drying lumber in an outdoor stack removes moisture just as quickly as a heated drying kiln.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Air drying requires roughly one year per inch of board thickness, whereas industrial kilns stabilize lumber in a few weeks.

### Req 3: Hand Tool Maintenance and Sharpening
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Show the proper care, use, and storage of all working tools and equipment that you own or use at home or school. (b) Sharpen correctly the cutting edges of two different tools.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never place fingers across the cutting edge to test sharpness; shave thumbnail surfaces or test cuts on scrap softwood instead.
- **Common Myth:** Sharpening stones should be used dry to avoid making a messy slurry on the workbench.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Lubricating stones with water or honing oil floats away dislodged metal swarf and prevents abrasive pores from clogging.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Bench chisels maintain a primary cutting bevel between twenty five and thirty degrees.
- Smoothing plane soles must stay flat and lightly waxed to reduce pushing friction.
- Waterstone Abrasive Progression: Sharpening begins on coarse grit stones before polishing edges on fine ceramic stones.
- Burr Wire Edge Removal: Stropping on leather charged with chromium oxide paste eliminates delicate sharpening burrs.

#### Req 3a: Tool Care and Bench Storage
**Official Requirement 3a:** Show the proper care, use, and storage of all working tools and equipment that you own or use at home or school.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Care Use and Storage)
- **Common Myth:** Storing hand planes resting flat on their soles on concrete or steel shelves causes no harm.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Resting a hand plane directly on its sole chips the exposed cutting iron against hard bench surfaces, so planes must rest on their sides or on wooden battens.

### Req 4: Fundamental Hand Tool Project Construction
**Official Requirement 4:** Using a saw, plane, hammer, brace, and bit, make something useful of wood. Cut parts from lumber that you have squared and measured from working drawings.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 60, p. 62)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Plane Hammer Brace and Bit)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Clamp workpieces firmly to the bench when boring with a brace and bit to prevent the stock from spinning or shifting under torque.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Boring through from one side blows out splintery wood grain on the back; boring must reverse once the lead screw pokes.

### Req 6: Advanced Joinery and Cabinetry Techniques
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Make working drawings of a project needing beveled or rounded edges and build it. (b) Make working drawings of a project needing curved or incised cuttings and build it. (c) Make working drawings of a project needing miter, dowel, or mortise and tenon joints and build it. (d) Make a cabinet, box, or something else with a door or lid fastened with inset hinges. (e) Help make wooden toys for children OR help carry out a woodworking service project approved by your counselor for a charitable organization.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 62, p. 63)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Keep both hands positioned behind the cutting edge of carving gouges, chisels, and drawknives during shaping operations.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Woodwork Req 6 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Miter joints alone provide strong structural bonds for heavy wooden frames and boxes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Miter joints join weak end-grain surfaces and fail easily unless reinforced with internal splines, biscuits, or dowels.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Block planes and spokeshaves create uniform bevels and rounded profiles along grain edges.
- Curved Scroll Cutting Mastery: Coping saws and band saws cut delicate internal curves without binding blade kerfs.
- Precision Joinery Interlocking: Mortise and tenon or doweled joints resist racking forces through mechanical interlock.
- Recessed butt hinge leaves mount flush with door edges for tight perimeter reveals.
