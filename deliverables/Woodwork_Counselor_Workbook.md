### Req 2: Timber Milling and Wood Identification
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe how timber is grown, harvested, and milled. Tell how lumber is cured, seasoned, graded, and sized. (b) Collect and label blocks of six kinds of wood useful in woodworking. Describe the chief qualities of each. Give the best uses of each.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 15)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Timber Milling and Wood Identification discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** A finished two-by-four board bought at a lumberyard measures a full 2 inches thick by 4 inches wide.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Two-by-four is only the nominal green size before drying and planing, and the actual dressed size of a finished 2x4 is 1.5 inches by 3.5 inches (p. 15).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Bucking Felled Tree Trunks: Then workers called buckers lop off the limbs and slice the trunks into shorter lengths (logs) that are easier to handle (p. 14).
- The logs are trucked, floated, or carried on railroads to asawmill (p. 14).
- Debarking and Sawing Boards: At the mill, logs are debarked (their bark is removed), and saws cut them into boards (lumber) (p. 14).
- Small logs are often cut into boards in a single pass through a set of saws (a gang saw) that makes many cuts at once (p. 14).

### Req 3: Hand Tool Care and Sharpening
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Show the proper care, use, and storage of all working tools and equipment that you own or use at home or school. (b) Sharpen correctly the cutting edges of two different tools.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Woodwork Requirement 3 on hand tool care and sharpening: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** Every time you sharpen a wood chisel or plane iron on a bench stone, you must grind down the entire wide main bevel.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Routine sharpening is done only on the narrow sharpening bevel at the very tip, not the larger ground bevel, which saves time and steel (p. 34).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Dangers of Dull Blades: You are more likely to mar your work and injure yourself with a dull blade than a sharp one because dull blades require extra force and can slip (p. 34).
- Preventing Edge Nicks: Rough handling will nick and dull the cutting edges (p. 34).
- Ground and Sharpening Bevels: Most wood-cutting tools have two wedge shapes on their cutting edge, the main or ground bevel, and the sharpening bevel (p. 34).
- Honing the Secondary Bevel: When you are sharpening an edge, you should be working off the sharpening bevel only, not the larger ground bevel (p. 34).

#### Req 3a: Proper Tool Care and Storage
**Official Requirement 3a:** Show the proper care, use, and storage of all working tools and equipment that you own or use at home or school.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Care Use and Storage)
- **Common Myth:** When using an oils tone to sharpen a chisel, you should start on the finest smooth grit and finish on the roughest grit.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must always progress from a rough stone to medium, fine, and smooth stones in order so each finer grit polishes out the scratches left by the previous stone (p. 34).
