## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (List):** Create your own woodworking project. Begin by making working drawings, list the materials you will need to complete your project, and then build your project. Keep track of the time you spend and the cost of the materials.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Prepare):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Make working drawings of a project needing beveled or rounded edges and build it. (b) Make working drawings of a project needing curved or incised cuttings and build it. (c) Make working drawings of a project needing miter, dowel, or mortise and tenon joints and build it. (d) Make a cabinet, box, or something else with a door or lid fastened with inset hinges. (e) Help make wooden toys for children OR help carry out a woodworking service project approved by your counselor for a charitable organization.

#### Req 3a: Tool Care and Bench Storage
**Official Requirement 3a:** Show the proper care, use, and storage of all working tools and equipment that you own or use at home or school.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For woodwork Requirement 3a (Show the proper care, use, and storage of all working tools and equipment that you own or), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Woodworking tools function best when cutter blades remain retracted, metal components stay protected from rust with camellia oil, and moving gears receive light lubrication. Organizing tools in dedicated wall racks or fitted wooden chests prevents accidental edge dulling caused by contact with other steel tools.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Back Flattening Preparation: Lap the flat blade back against a medium sharpening stone to remove pitting.
  - Step 2 - Honing Bevel Angle: Secure the tool in a honing guide tilted to twenty five degrees.
- **Common Myth:** Storing hand planes resting flat on their soles on concrete or steel shelves causes no harm.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Resting a hand plane directly on its sole chips the exposed cutting iron against hard bench surfaces, so planes must rest on their sides or on wooden battens.

### Req 4: Fundamental Hand Tool Project Construction
**Official Requirement 4:** Using a saw, plane, hammer, brace, and bit, make something useful of wood. Cut parts from lumber that you have squared and measured from working drawings.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 60, p. 62)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Clamp workpieces firmly to the bench when boring with a brace and bit to prevent the stock from spinning or shifting under torque.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must an auger bit be backed out and reversed when its threaded feed screw emerges through the back face?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Check board edges against a try square to establish a flat ninety degree reference.
  - Saw along the waste side of the pencil line to maintain exact component dimensions.
  - Stop boring when the auger lead screw pricks through the reverse side.
  - Strike nail heads squarely with the center of the hammer face to avoid bending.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Face and Edge Truing: Plane one face flat and plane an adjacent reference edge square to it.
  - Step 2 - Component Layout Marking: Scribe length and width cutting lines using a try square and marking gauge.
  - Step 3 - Dimension Cutting and Boring: Hand saw stock to line and bore clean holes with a brace and bit.
  - Step 4 - Fastener Assembly and Alignment: Fasten joints with hammer driven finish nails and glue while checking assembly squareness.
- **Common Myth:** Boring completely through a board from one side with an auger bit produces clean hole edges on both faces.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Boring through from one side blows out splintery wood grain on the back; boring must reverse once the lead screw pokes.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Woodwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 55, p. 56)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 5 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what specific ways do you calculate the board footage of three planks measuring one inch by eight inches by six feet?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Multi-view scale drawings provide exact dimensional guidance for all cut parts and joinery.
  - Bill of Materials Calculation: Itemized cutting lists enumerate piece quantities, finished dimensions, board feet, and unit prices.
  - Dry Fit Assembly Verification: Assembling project joints without glue verifies component alignment and clamp setups before gluing.
  - Time and Expense Tracking: Maintaining a daily workshop log records cumulative labor hours and actual material expenses.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Scaled Drafting Layout: Draw three view orthographic elevations with dimension lines and explicit joinery details.
  - Step 2 - Cut List and Costing: Compile an itemized bill of materials calculating board feet and hardware costs.
  - Step 3 - Dimensioning and Joinery: Machine parts to rough dimensions, true reference faces, and cut matching joints.
  - Step 4 - Gluing Clamping and Logging: Glue subassemblies, clamp square with protective cauls, and log total hours spent.
- **Common Myth:** Wood glue bonds stronger when applied in heavy, dripping layers that squeeze out profusely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Excess glue starves the joint under clamp pressure, contaminates surrounding wood pores, and ruins subsequent stain absorption.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Carpentry Apprenticeship Pathways: Registered union programs combine four years of paid on-the-job training with classroom instruction.
  - Millwork Technical Credentials: Professional trade certifications validate proficiency in architectural blueprint reading and CAD drafting.
  - Modern Automated Manufacturing: Commercial cabinet shops combine hand assembly with automated CNC nested router cutting.
  - Brotherhood of Carpenters union locals negotiate hourly wage scales, pensions, and medical benefits.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Professional Interview: Contact a licensed local cabinetmaker or commercial millwork contractor to request an informational interview.
  - Step 2 - Career Discussion Preparation: Formulate specific questions regarding union apprenticeships, trade certifications, wage scales, and working hours.
  - Step 3 - Shop Site Visitation: Tour a commercial woodworking facility to observe production workflow, machinery, and dust collection systems.
  - Step 4 - Career Summary Reflection: Document key findings about career progression, prevailing industry wages, and technical skill requirements.
- **Common Myth:** Woodworking careers are restricted to hand craftspeople making rustic furniture one piece at a time.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Contemporary woodworking professionals operate advanced multi-axis CNC machines, manage digital CAD models, and oversee complex commercial architectural installations.
