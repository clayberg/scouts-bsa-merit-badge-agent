## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 3 (Describe):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Name four departments of a railroad company. Describe what each department does. (b) Tell about the opportunities in railroading that interest you most and why. (c) Name four rail support industries. Describe the function of each one. (d) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, interview someone employed in the rail industry. Learn what that person does and how this person became interested in railroading. Find out what type of schooling and training is required for this position.
- [ ] **Req 7a (Identify):** Option A, Model Railroading. With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do TWO of the following: (1) Draw a layout of your own model railroad or one that could be built in your home. Design a point-to-point track or loop with different routings. Include one of the following: turnaround or terminal or yard or siding. (2) Build one model railroad car kit or one locomotive kit. (3) Name the scale of four popular model railroad gauges. Identify the scale of four model cars or locomotives. (4) Locate the websites of four model railroad-related manufacturers or magazine publishers. Print information about their products and services, and discuss the information with your counselor. (5) Build one railroad structure (from scratch or using a kit), paint and weather the structure, mount it on your layout or diorama, and make the surrounding area on the diorama scenic. (6) Alone or with others, build a model railroad or modular layout including ballast and scenery. Make electrical connections and operate a train. Describe what you enjoyed most. (7) Participate in a switching contest on a timesaver layout and record your time. (8) Explain the difference between powering and controlling a model railroad by using direct current, and powering and controlling a model railroad using digital command control.
- [ ] **Req 7b (Explain):** Option B, Railfanning. With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do TWO of the following: (1) Visit a railroad museum, historical display, or a prototype railroad-sponsored public event. With permission, photograph, digitally record, or sketch items of interest. Explain what you saw and describe your photos, sketches, or video. (2) Purchase tickets and ride a scenic or historic railroad. Under supervision, photograph the equipment and discuss with your counselor the historic significance of the operation. (3) Locate the websites of four rail historical groups, then find information on the history of the rail preservation operations and purpose of each group. Talk with a member of one of the groups and find out how you might help. (4) Plan a trip by rail between two points. Obtain a schedule and explain when the train should arrive at two intermediate points. Purchase the tickets and make the trip. Explain to your counselor what you saw.

### Req 1: Modern Freight Rail Operations
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do THREE of the following: (a) Name three types of modern freight trains. Explain why unit trains are more efficient than mixed freight trains. (b) Name one Class I or regional railroad. Explain what major cities it serves, the locations of major terminals, service facilities, and crew change points, and the major commodities it carries. (c) Using models or pictures, identify 10 types of railroad freight or passenger cars. Explain the purpose of each type of car. (d) Explain how a modern diesel or electric locomotive develops power. Explain the terms dynamic braking and radial steering trucks.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Railroading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 22)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Diesel-electric locomotives drive their steel wheels directly using mechanical gears connected to the engine crankshaft.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The diesel engine drives an alternator that produces electricity to power axle-mounted electric traction motors.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Unit trains bypass classification yards because every car carries identical bulk cargo.
- Dynamic Braking Mechanism: Traction motors generate opposing magnetic resistance to control train speed on steep downgrades.
- Radial Steering Trucks: Wheel axles steer mechanically into curves to minimize wheel flange friction and wear.
- Double stack container cars drop center gravity and double overall intermodal carrying capacity.

### Req 2: Passenger Rail and Mass Transit
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the purpose and formation of Amtrak. Explain, by the use of a timetable, a plan for making a trip by rail between two cities at least 500 miles apart. List the times of departure and arrival at your destination, the train number and name, and the type of service you want. (b) List and explain the various forms of public/mass transit using rail.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Railroading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 27, p. 46)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Amtrak owns almost all of the railroad tracks and bridges over which its passenger trains operate nationwide.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Outside the Northeast Corridor, Amtrak operates its passenger trains primarily over tracks owned and maintained by host freight railroads.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Amtrak Legislative Origin: The Rail Passenger Service Act relieved private freight carriers of unprofitable passenger operations.
- Timetable Schedule Navigation: Official passenger timetables display scheduled departure, intermediate station arrivals, and route equipment.
- Commuter Rail Corridors: Regional passenger lines connect outlying suburban stations directly to central downtown terminal stations.
- Grade-separated subway and elevated rail systems move high passenger densities along dedicated rights-of-way.

### Req 3: Railroad Industry Careers and Organization
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Name four departments of a railroad company. Describe what each department does. (b) Tell about the opportunities in railroading that interest you most and why. (c) Name four rail support industries. Describe the function of each one. (d) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, interview someone employed in the rail industry. Learn what that person does and how this person became interested in railroading. Find out what type of schooling and training is required for this position.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Railroading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 51, p. 53)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Railroading Req 3 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Railroad employment consists almost entirely of locomotive engineers driving trains and conductors collecting tickets.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Railroads employ thousands of civil engineers, signal maintainers, electricians, software developers, and dispatchers who keep systems operating.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Transportation Department Roles: Dispatchers and train crews manage train movements, yard switching, and mainline schedule compliance.
- Mechanical Department Responsibilities: Shop machinists and electricians service diesel locomotive engines, traction motors, and brake systems.
- Track maintenance gangs inspect ballast, replace treated wooden ties, and align continuous rails.
- Rail Supply Sector: Independent suppliers manufacture specialized signaling electronics, track fasteners, and replacement locomotive components.

### Req 4: Public Rail Crossing Safety
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the purpose of Operation Lifesaver and its mission. (b) List 9 track basic safety tips to remember when you are around a railroad track. (c) List 9 safety considerations that should be followed when walking near a railroad track. (d) Tell your counselor what a driver can do to safely operate near tracks. (e) Explain safety precautions when using a light rail or commuter train.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Railroading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 38, p. 40)* • **Group Organizer:** Activity & Observation Log
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never walk on or between the rails because railroad tracks are private property and walking there is illegal trespassing.
- **Common Myth:** A locomotive engineer can easily steer around obstacles or stop quickly if someone stands on the track.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Trains travel on fixed rails and require tremendous distances to stop, making avoidance maneuvers completely impossible.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Operation Lifesaver delivers education and enforcement partnerships to prevent railway crossing collisions.
- Stopping Distance Physics: A freight train traveling fifty miles per hour requires a full mile to stop.
- Optical Distance Illusion: A train appears to move slower and further away because of its massive size.
- Emergency Notification System: Blue signs at crossings display dispatch telephone numbers and unique crossing inventory numbers.

### Req 5: Railroad Crossing Signs and Signals
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Explain the appearance and meaning of the following rail signs and signals: (a) Passive signs and active signs (b) Devices at the crossing (flashing red lights, with or without bells, flashing red lights and gates, and cantilever flashing lights) (c) Markings on the road (pavement markings and stop bars) (d) Signs before the crossing (advance warning sign, advance warning sign for side streets, no train horn sign, and low ground clearance grade crossing sign) (e) Signs along the railroad property (no trespassing sign) (f) Signs at the crossing (crossbuck sign, emergency notification system sign, multiple track sign, stop sign, and yield sign)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Railroading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 39, p. 42)* • **Group Organizer:** Activity & Observation Log
- **Common Myth:** A crossbuck sign at a rural crossing guarantees that flashing warning lights will activate if a train comes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A crossbuck is a passive sign functioning as a yield sign, requiring drivers to look and listen without automatic warnings.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Passive signs like crossbucks provide regulatory warning without automatic detection of an approaching train.
- Active devices use flashing lights, bells, and gates triggered automatically by approaching rail traffic.
- The blue emergency sign provides an emergency dispatch number and a federal crossing identification.
- Painted white pavement markings with an X and letters RR alert drivers before crossings.
