## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 6 (Discuss):** With your parent or guardian's approval, connect with another Scout or youth your own age who has an identity that's different from yours. (This means a trait, belief, or characteristic different from you.) (a) Share with each other what makes the different aspects of your identity meaningful/special to you (b) Share with each other ONE of the following options: (1) Option 1, A time you felt excluded from a group: What was the situation? How did it make you feel? What did you do? Did anyone stand up for you? What did you learn? Would you do anything differently today? (2) Option 2, This imaginary situation: You're attending a new school and don't know anyone there yet. You notice they dress very differently than you do. At lunchtime, you decide you'll try to sit with a group to get to know other students. People at two tables tell you there is someone sitting at the currently empty seat at their table, so you end up eating by yourself. Discuss: How would that make you feel? What could the students have done? If that happened at your school, what would you do? (c) Discuss with your counselor what you learned from the discussion with the other Scout or youth.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Identify):** Identify and interview an individual in your community, school, and/or Scouting who has had a significant positive impact in promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion. If you feel your community, school, or local Scouting group does not have such an individual, then research a historical figure who meets these criteria, and discuss that person with your counselor. (a) Discover what inspired the individual, learn about the challenges they faced, and share what you feel attributed to their success (b) Discuss with your counselor what you learned and how you can apply it in your life.

### Req 1: Foundational Principles of Civil Society
**Official Requirement 1:** Before beginning work on other requirements for this merit badge, research the following terms and explain to your counselor how you feel they relate to the Scout Oath and Scout Law: identities, diversity, equality, equity, inclusion, discrimination, ethical leadership, and upstander.
**Source:** *Official BSA Citizenship in Society Pamphlet, pp. 4-9* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does treating everyone equally differ from treating everyone equitably during patrol assignments?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Personal identity encompasses family heritage, cultural traditions, personal talents, and individual core values.
  - Equitable Treatment: Equity requires distributing specific resources according to individual needs rather than identical baselines.
  - Intentional Inclusion: Inclusive patrols actively invite every member to participate equally in leadership and decisions.
  - Civil Rights Framework: Discrimination occurs when unfair distinctions deny individuals fair access, opportunities, or basic respect.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Terminology Research: Write standard definitions for all six foundational societal terms in a personal notebook.
  - Step 2 - Law Comparison: Map each investigated term directly to corresponding points of the Scout Law.
  - Step 3 - Patrol Application: Document specific examples of how equity strengthens teamwork during troop campouts or meetings.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Dialogue: Articulate personal perspectives on these concepts clearly during the merit badge discussion.
- **Common Myth:** These definitions represent abstract political slogans rather than practical daily guidelines for patrol conduct.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Scout Oath and Law provide practical moral standards for treating all people with dignity regardless of background.

- **Common Myth:** Positive stereotypes are harmless because they express favorable assumptions about an entire group of people.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Stereotypes dehumanize individuals by erasing their unique attributes, often hardening into institutional discrimination.
