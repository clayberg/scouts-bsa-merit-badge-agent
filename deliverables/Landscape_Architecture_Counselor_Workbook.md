### Req 2: Analyzing Site Function and Plantings
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** After completing requirement 1, discuss the following with your counselor: (a) Tell whether the design had separate spaces, a defined point of entry, a clear path system, and sun and shade variety. (b) Discuss how any structures, the designated seating, eating, or parking areas suited the overall design. (c) Explain how the design reflected consideration for the comfort, shelter, and security of the users. (d) Discuss how the choice of trees, shrubs, and ground covers used in the project contributed to its appeal and function.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Analyzing Site Function and Plantings discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Trees, shrubs, and ground covers are added to a landscape project strictly for visual decoration.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Plantings serve vital functional roles alongside visual appeal by casting shade, blocking wind, stabilizing soil, and framing outdoor rooms (p. 12).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Spatial Organization and Circulation: Tell whether the design had separate spaces, a defined point of entry, a clear path system, and sun and shade variety (p. 12).
- Discuss how any structures, the designated seating, eating, or parking areas suited the overall design (p. 12).
- User Comfort and Security: Explain how the design reflected consideration for the comfort, shelter, and security of the users (p. 12).
- Functional Plant Material Selection: Discuss how the choice of trees, shrubs, and ground covers used in the project contributed to its appeal and function (p. 12).

### Req 4: Site Measurement Drainage and Redesign
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** After obtaining permission from the appropriate authority, look at and study a place of worship, school grounds, or a public building and identify where most people arrive by bus or car. Then do the following: (a) Using a measuring tape, measure and draw the main site entry and its nearby area. Define the scale of your drawing. Be sure to include the driveway and sidewalk or path that leads to the building's main entry. Indicate any sidewalks, structures, trees and plants, lights, drains, utilities, or other site furnishings within the study area. Make two copies of this plan and save the original, then do 4(b) and 4(c) using the copies. (b) On one copy of your site plan, use directional arrows to indicate where the water drains across the site, where ditches occur, and where water stands for a longer period of time. (c) Decide how you can make the place safer and more comfortable for those using it. Redesign the area on another copy of the plan. You may want to include new walks, covered waiting areas, benches, space-defining plantings of trees and shrubs, and drainage structures.

### Req 5: Landscape Architecture Career Pathways
