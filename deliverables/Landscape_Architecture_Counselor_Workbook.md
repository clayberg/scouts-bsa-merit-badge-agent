### Req 2: Landscape Design Evaluation Conference
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** After completing requirement 1, discuss the following with your counselor: (a) Tell whether the design had separate spaces, a defined point of entry, a clear path system, and sun and shade variety. (b) Discuss how any structures, the designated seating, eating, or parking areas suited the overall design. (c) Explain how the design reflected consideration for the comfort, shelter, and security of the users. (d) Discuss how the choice of trees, shrubs, and ground covers used in the project contributed to its appeal and function.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Maintain clear three-foot clearance buffers around emergency exits, fire hydrants, and utility boxes during site evaluations.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners frequently assume outdoor furniture placement is arbitrary rather than strategically organized around sun exposure.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landscape architects position benches, trash receptacles, and shelters along solar angles and natural pedestrian desire lines.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Spatial Definition: Outdoor rooms rely on changes in grade, planting beds, or distinct overhead canopies.
- Circulation Hierarchy: Primary walkways direct heavy pedestrian movement while secondary paths encourage quiet wandering.
- Microclimate Control: Deciduous trees block intense summer sunlight yet allow warming winter rays through.
- User Security: Open sightlines and low illumination fixtures deter crime while supporting welcoming communal park spaces.

### Req 3: Regional Plant Material Identification
**Official Requirement 3:** Identify five shrubs, five trees, and one ground cover, being sure that you select examples of different shapes, sizes, and textures. With the help of your counselor or a local nursery, choose plants that will grow in your area. Bring pictures of the different planting materials or, if possible, examples of their branches, leaves, or flowers to a group such as your troop or class at school. Be prepared to tell how you might use each in the design of a landscape and the maintenance that would follow.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Shrubs Five Trees and One)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear sturdy gloves and long sleeves when handling thorny barberries, holly leaves, or toxic sap-bearing evergreens.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often select nursery plants based purely on retail container blooms rather than mature physical dimensions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landscape architects specify plants based on mature root spread, mature canopy height, and regional climate tolerance.

### Req 4: Public Site Planning and Redesign
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** After obtaining permission from the appropriate authority, look at and study a place of worship, school grounds, or a public building and identify where most people arrive by bus or car. Then do the following: (a) Using a measuring tape, measure and draw the main site entry and its nearby area. Define the scale of your drawing. Be sure to include the driveway and sidewalk or path that leads to the building's main entry. Indicate any sidewalks, structures, trees and plants, lights, drains, utilities, or other site furnishings within the study area. Make two copies of this plan and save the original, then do 4(b) and 4(c) using the copies. (b) On one copy of your site plan, use directional arrows to indicate where the water drains across the site, where ditches occur, and where water stands for a longer period of time. (c) Decide how you can make the place safer and more comfortable for those using it. Redesign the area on another copy of the plan. You may want to include new walks, covered waiting areas, benches, space-defining plantings of trees and shrubs, and drainage structures.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear high-visibility safety vests and look both directions when pulling tape measures near active bus drop-off lanes.
- **Common Myth:** Novices often believe that drawing a site redesign requires artistic rendering rather than accurate measured scales.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Professional site redesigns rely on consistent engineering scales to prove proposed walkways and bus lanes physically fit.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Request administrative permission from property managers before beginning field measurements with survey gear.
- Pedestrian Separation: Physical curbs and planting strips protect walkers from moving cars and unloading buses.
- Drainage Mapping: Direct arrows toward low retention basins to prevent standing water on walkways.
- Universal Accessibility: Incorporate smooth ramps with handrails to ensure wheelchair access across outdoor thresholds.

Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities, and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Organizer (Career & Profession Exploration Card):** Req 5 Career & Professional Pathway Investigation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Adhere to active hardhat and steel-toed boot regulations when shadowing licensed professionals across commercial construction project sites.
- **Common Myth:** Many people assume that a landscape architect is the same trade as a residential lawn maintenance contractor.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landscape architects hold accredited professional degrees and state licenses authorizing them to engineer public grading, drainage, and civic infrastructure.
