### Req 1: Professional Landscape Site Visit
**Official Requirement 1:** Go to a completed landscape project that a landscape architect has designed. Before you visit the site, obtain a plan of the design from the landscape architect if one is available.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does holding a physical site plan alter your awareness of spatial boundaries outdoors?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Plan Acquisition: Contact the designer or municipal archive early to secure blueprint copies before touring.
  - Site Orientation: Align the physical paper blueprint north arrow directly with ambient landscape features.
  - Scale Verification: Match drawn dimensions against real distances between existing walkways, trees, and building faces.
  - Design Realization: Compare mature plant foliage volumes against initial nursery callout symbols on drafting sheets.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Initial Contact: Reach out to the project landscape architect to request approved site plans.
  - Step 2 - Map Orientation: Rotate the drawing at the main entrance until north matches your compass.
  - Step 3 - Field Observation: Walk every pathway shown on the plan while noting built hardscape materials.
  - Step 4 - Layout Comparison: Record any construction deviations or plant substitutions directly upon your field notes.
- **Common Myth:** Novices often believe a landscape architect only selects decorative flowers after the builders finish.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landscape architects engineer site drainage, vehicle circulation, pedestrian connectivity, and structural grading before selecting any plants.

### Req 3: Regional Plant Material Identification
**Official Requirement 3:** Identify five shrubs, five trees, and one ground cover, being sure that you select examples of different shapes, sizes, and textures. With the help of your counselor or a local nursery, choose plants that will grow in your area. Bring pictures of the different planting materials or, if possible, examples of their branches, leaves, or flowers to a group such as your troop or class at school. Be prepared to tell how you might use each in the design of a landscape and the maintenance that would follow.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does planting a tree with aggressive surface roots near a concrete sidewalk cause structural failure?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Hardiness Zones: Check local USDA temperature ratings to confirm perennial winter survival for selections.
  - Growth Habit: Note whether mature tree silhouettes develop weeping, vase, columnar, or rounded branch structures.
  - Foliage Texture: Contrast fine needleleaf evergreens against coarse, broadleaf deciduous shrubs for balanced visual interest.
  - Ground Covers: Low spreading species protect exposed soil banks from wind erosion and runoff degradation.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Nursery Consultation: Visit a local nursery professional to review regionally hardy woody landscape species.
  - Step 2 - Botanical Specimen Selection: Choose five trees, five shrubs, and one spreading ground cover for study.
  - Step 3 - Morphological Analysis: Catalog the form, leaf texture, and mature canopy dimensions for each specimen.
  - Step 4 - Visual Portfolio Assembly: Compile photographs and botanical descriptions into an organized regional plant study notebook.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often select nursery plants based purely on retail container blooms rather than mature physical dimensions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landscape architects specify plants based on mature root spread, mature canopy height, and regional climate tolerance.

### Req 5: Landscape Architecture Career Pathways
**Official Requirement 5:** Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge in landscape architecture. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities, and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Landscape Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Career & Profession Exploration Card):** Req 5 Career & Professional Pathway Investigation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the work of a municipal urban planner differ from that of a private residential landscape architect?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Career Disciplines: Professionals practice across urban design, park system administration, environmental restoration, and residential planning.
  - Accredited Degrees: Licensure candidates typically complete a Landscape Architectural Accreditation Board accredited baccalaureate or masters degree.
  - Licensure Exams: Candidates must pass all four sections of the national Landscape Architect Registration Examination.
  - Continuing Education: Practicing architects complete periodic technical training to maintain professional licenses and state certifications.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Career Investigation: Identify three distinct professions utilizing site planning, grading, and plant ecology expertise.
  - Step 2 - Deep Discipline Selection: Select one specialized vocation such as municipal park planning for comprehensive research.
  - Step 3 - Qualification Discovery: Document required collegiate degrees, state board examination procedures, and initial internship training costs.
  - Step 4 - Professional Dialogue: Interview a practicing professional to discover typical daily responsibilities, software workflows, and career milestones.
- **Common Myth:** Many people assume that a landscape architect is the same trade as a residential lawn maintenance contractor.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landscape architects hold accredited professional degrees and state licenses authorizing them to engineer public grading, drainage, and civic infrastructure.
