(b) Present to your counselor a significant family artifact/heirloom and discuss its history. (c) Make a list of the trash your family throws out during one week. - [ ] **Req 9 (Prepare):** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.
- [ ] **Req 10 (Explain):** Do ONE of the following: (a) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's permission, assist a qualified archaeologist for at least eight hours with a project being worked on. Projects may include surveying, site monitoring, site stabilization, surface excavation, laboratory analysis, use of digital archaeological technology, or public outreach. Describe your involvement in the project, what you learned about archaeology, and the steps of archaeological inquiry. Note: Visiting an archaeological site will require advance planning. An archaeological site during study can be a dangerous place. While there, you will need to closely follow the archaeologist's directions and comply with all the safety procedures. Be aware of the changing conditions at the site. (b) With your counselor's approval, take part in a simulated archaeological project designed by a qualified archaeologist. The project must include the use of a simulated archaeological site including artifacts and features for the site. Using the steps of archaeological inquiry, analyze the artifacts and features and document the spatial relationships of the artifacts and features at the simulated site. Explain how the environment and time can affect the interpretation of an artifact and the overall archaeological site. Tell how you would share the results of your analysis with other researchers and the public. Note: To find out how to make a simulated archaeological site, talk with a professional archaeologist, trained avocational archaeologist, museum school instructor, science teacher, advisor from a local archaeology society, or other qualified instructor.

### Req 3: Archaeological Chronology and Dating Methods
**Official Requirement 3:** Describe at least two ways in which archaeologists determine the age of sites, structures, or artifacts. Explain what absolute dating and relative dating are.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 52, p. 88)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Archaeologists Determine The Age of)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use clean tweezers and avoid touching organic samples with bare fingers to prevent chemical contamination.
- **Common Myth:** Carbon-14 testing can determine the exact construction year of stone projectile points and metal swords.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Radiocarbon dating measures the radioactive decay of carbon in organic materials like wood, bone, and charcoal, not inorganic stone or metal.

![An archaeologist records soil strata layers on a drafting board while a colleague measures trench depths with surveying rods.](/assets/ai_illustrations/archaeology_req_3_nano_hero.png)

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Superposition Principle: Undisturbed sedimentary layers position the oldest cultural deposits underneath younger overlying horizons.
- Seriation Technique: Frequency shifts in artifact styles over time arrange assemblage styles into chronological sequences.
- Radiocarbon Analysis: Decay of radioactive carbon isotopes in organic remains calculates an absolute calendar span.
- Dendrochronological Alignment: Annual growth rings preserved in structural timber provide precise calendar year cutting dates.

### Req 5: Archaeological Legal Frameworks and Preservation
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Learn about the federal laws and international conventions that protect archaeological sites. Find out if your state, county, or local government has regulations that apply to archaeological or historic sites. (b) Identify a national, international, or local organization that helps to protect archaeological sites.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 26, p. 68)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never confront active looters or trespassers in the field; record vehicle license plates and notify law enforcement immediately.
- **Common Myth:** Anyone who finds an arrowhead or pottery shard on federal public land is legally entitled to keep it.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Federal statutes strictly forbid digging, removing, or defacing cultural artifacts on public lands without an official scientific permit.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Antiquities Act Baseline: The 1906 legislation established federal penalties for unpermitted excavation on public lands.
- ARPA Enforcement Powers: The Archaeological Resources Protection Act imposes felony criminal fines against commercial looters.
- UNESCO Treaty Protections: International conventions prohibit the illicit import, export, and transfer of cultural property.
- Nonprofit Preservation Advocacy: Organizations like the Archaeological Conservancy purchase endangered archaeological sites for permanent conservation.

#### Req 5a: Federal and Local Preservation Laws
**Official Requirement 5a:** Learn about the federal laws and international conventions that protect archaeological sites. Find out if your state, county, or local government has regulations that apply to archaeological or historic sites.

- **Common Myth:** State preservation laws are identical to federal statutes and apply identically to private backyard property.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Most state laws govern state and municipal land, but many states have separate strict statutes protecting all unmarked burials everywhere.

### Req 6: Archaeological Stewardship and Site Protection
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain why it is important to protect archaeological sites. (b) Explain what people should do if they think they have found an artifact. (c) Describe the ways in which you can be a protector of the past.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 28)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Mark artifact locations with GPS coordinates or flags rather than picking up items near potentially hazardous terrain.
- **Common Myth:** Picking up an artifact to show an archaeologist at their office is helpful behavior.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Removing an artifact tears it from its stratigraphic context, destroying the critical data that reveals its age and function.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Nonrenewable Heritage Value: Archaeological sites represent fragile finite resources that cannot be replaced once disturbed.
- Spatial Context Significance: The exact physical position of an artifact reveals chronological dates and function.
- Discovery Documentation Steps: Photograph the exposed item with a scale indicator and mark exact coordinates.
- Stewardship Network Participation: Volunteer site stewards monitor remote cultural ruins to detect vandalism and erosion.
