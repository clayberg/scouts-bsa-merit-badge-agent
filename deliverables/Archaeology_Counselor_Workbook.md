## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 7 (Discuss):** Do ONE of the following and discuss your findings with your counselor: (a) Visit a museum to observe how artifacts aid in conveying history. (b) Present to your counselor a significant family artifact/heirloom and discuss its history. (c) Make a list of the trash your family throws out during one week. Discuss with your counselor what archaeologists might learn about you and your family if they found your trash a thousand years from now.
- [ ] **Req 9 (Prepare):** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.
- [ ] **Req 10 (Explain):** Do ONE of the following: (a) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's permission, assist a qualified archaeologist for at least eight hours with a project being worked on. Projects may include surveying, site monitoring, site stabilization, surface excavation, laboratory analysis, use of digital archaeological technology, or public outreach. Describe your involvement in the project, what you learned about archaeology, and the steps of archaeological inquiry. Note: Visiting an archaeological site will require advance planning. An archaeological site during study can be a dangerous place. While there, you will need to closely follow the archaeologist's directions and comply with all the safety procedures. Be aware of the changing conditions at the site. (b) With your counselor's approval, take part in a simulated archaeological project designed by a qualified archaeologist. The project must include the use of a simulated archaeological site including artifacts and features for the site. Using the steps of archaeological inquiry, analyze the artifacts and features and document the spatial relationships of the artifacts and features at the simulated site. Explain how the environment and time can affect the interpretation of an artifact and the overall archaeological site. Tell how you would share the results of your analysis with other researchers and the public. Note: To find out how to make a simulated archaeological site, talk with a professional archaeologist, trained avocational archaeologist, museum school instructor, science teacher, advisor from a local archaeology society, or other qualified instructor.

### Req 1: Archaeological Science and Related Disciplines
**Official Requirement 1:** Tell what archaeology is and explain to your counselor how it differs or relates to other fields of study such as anthropology, geology, paleontology, and history. Explain how archaeology is different than artifact collecting or treasure hunting.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 79)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the loss of provenience ruin an artifact's scientific value to historians?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Material Culture Scope: Systematic recovery of human artifacts reveals daily domestic activities across ancient social classes.
  - Paleontology Distinction: Paleontologists excavate fossilized extinct animal bones without analyzing cultural human tools or hearths.
  - Geological Stratigraphy: Soil layers establish relative chronological sequencing for human artifacts buried within sediment profiles.
  - Looting Damage: Commercial treasure hunting destroys archaeological context and permanently erases vital historical records.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Field Definition: Define material culture boundaries separating human habitation sites from natural fossil deposits.
  - Step 2 - Discipline Comparison: Contrast written historical records with material remnants recovered from subterranean archaeological units.
  - Step 3 - Geological Correlation: Correlate underlying stratigraphic sediment horizons with cultural deposition episodes across the trench.
  - Step 4 - Context Analysis: Contrast scientific contextual mapping against destructive personal artifact collecting without provenience documentation.
- **Common Myth:** Archaeologists spend their careers digging up dinosaur skeletons alongside ancient human stone spearheads.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dinosaurs went extinct millions of years before humans appeared, so archaeology only investigates past human societies and their material culture.

### Req 2: The Comprehensive Archaeological Process
**Official Requirement 2:** Describe the following steps of the archaeological process: site location, development of background research and a research design, site survey and fieldwork, artifact identification and examination, interpretation, preservation, and information sharing.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 48)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why is unrecorded excavation considered identical to the destruction of an archaeological site?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Clear research questions guide field collection methods and prevent unnecessary destructive excavation.
  - Pedestrian Survey Methods: Systematic field walking in transects locates surface artifacts indicating buried cultural features.
  - Provenience Mapping: Total station transit measurements fix the exact three-dimensional coordinates of exposed diagnostic specimens.
  - Curation Responsibilities: Proper washing, stabilization, cataloging, and archival storage protect recovered collections for future study.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Archival Research: Review historical maps, deeds, and aerial photographs to draft an excavation research design.
  - Step 2 - Surface Reconnaissance: Walk parallel transect corridors to document artifact scatter boundaries and landscape features.
  - Step 3 - Stratigraphic Excavation: Remove soil by cultural strata while dry screening matrix through quarter-inch hardware mesh.
  - Step 4 - Catalog Publication: Clean, label, and analyze recovered specimens before authoring the final technical site monograph.
- **Common Myth:** Archaeological excavation involves immediately digging deep holes with shovels to unearth precious relics.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Archaeologists spend months on background research and systematic surface surveys before carefully peeling away soil layer by layer.

### Req 3: Archaeological Chronology and Dating Methods
**Official Requirement 3:** Describe at least two ways in which archaeologists determine the age of sites, structures, or artifacts. Explain what absolute dating and relative dating are.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 52, p. 88)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why cannot a stone arrowhead be dated directly with radiocarbon measurement techniques?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Superposition Principle: Undisturbed sedimentary layers position the oldest cultural deposits underneath younger overlying horizons.
  - Seriation Technique: Frequency shifts in artifact styles over time arrange assemblage styles into chronological sequences.
  - Radiocarbon Analysis: Decay of radioactive carbon isotopes in organic remains calculates an absolute calendar span.
  - Dendrochronological Alignment: Annual growth rings preserved in structural timber provide precise calendar year cutting dates.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Stratum Profiling: Record horizontal stratum interfaces along trench balks to establish relative deposit sequencing.
  - Step 2 - Ceramic Seriation: Group diagnostic pottery rim fragments by stylistic attributes to track technological changes.
  - Step 3 - Organic Sampling: Extract undisturbed charcoal fragments with clean metal trowels into aluminum foil packets.
  - Step 4 - Calibration Comparison: Convert raw radiocarbon laboratory determination ages into calibrated calendar dates using tree-ring curves.
- **Common Myth:** Carbon-14 testing can determine the exact construction year of stone projectile points and metal swords.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Radiocarbon dating measures the radioactive decay of carbon in organic materials like wood, bone, and charcoal, not inorganic stone or metal.

#### Req 6a: Site Conservation and Fragility Rationale
**Official Requirement 6a:** Explain why it is important to protect archaeological sites.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 28)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For archaeology Requirement 6a (Explain why it is important to protect archaeological sites), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Each intact archaeological site contains a unique record of ancient human choices, technologies, and environmental adaptations. When a site is looted or bulldozed, that chapter of human heritage is lost forever because cultural deposits do not regenerate.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Context Preservation: Leave any exposed artifact exactly where encountered without dislodging it from sediment.
  - Step 2 - Surface Documentation: Capture detailed photographs of the artifact alongside a standard pocket scale marker.
- **Common Myth:** Archaeological sites are tough outdoor features that easily withstand casual walking and digging.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Ancient earthen walls, hearths, and ash layers degrade rapidly when walked upon, exposed to rain, or poked with sticks.
