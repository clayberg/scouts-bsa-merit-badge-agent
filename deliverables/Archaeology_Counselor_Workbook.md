Tell how you would share the results of your analysis with other researchers and the public. Note: To find out how to make a simulated archaeological site, talk with a professional archaeologist, trained avocational archaeologist, museum school instructor, science teacher, advisor from a local archaeology society, or other qualified instructor.

### Req 3: Relative and Absolute Dating Methods
**Official Requirement 3:** Describe at least two ways in which archaeologists determine the age of sites, structures, or artifacts. Explain what absolute dating and relative dating are.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Archaeology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 52, p. 88)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Archaeologists Determine The Age of)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Relative and Absolute Dating Methods discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Radiocarbon dating can be used directly on stone spear points and clay pottery shards to find their exact age.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Radiocarbon dating only works on once-living organic materials such as wood charcoal, bone, shell, or plant seeds (p. 52).

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Conduct all counselor discussions, community interviews, and research for Archaeology Requirement 7 on material culture and artifact storytelling under BSA Youth Protection two-deep leadership, copying a parent or guardian on any external correspondence.
- **Common Myth:** Archaeologists only care about gold jewelry, royal tombs, and prized family heirlooms rather than ordinary garbage.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Ancient trash pits, known as middens, are among the richest sources of archaeological data because discarded items reveal daily diet, health, and household habits (p. 48).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Irreversible Artifact Removal: Once it has been moved from the spot where it was found, an artifact can never be returned to exactly the same place (p. 40).
- Excavation destroys a site, so data must be recorded before an artifact and its surroundings are disturbed (p. 40).
- Maps, Photos, and Drawings: The records that archaeologists make include site maps, photographs of features like houses and pits, and drawings of artifacts (p. 40).
- Archival Records for Future Study: When accurate records are kept, archaeologists will be able to study a site even if they were not present during the excavation (p. 40).
