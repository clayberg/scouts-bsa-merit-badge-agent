### Req 1: Diving Physiology and Emergency Care
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while scuba diving, including hypothermia, hyperventilation, squeezes, decompression illness, nitrogen narcosis, motion sickness, fatigue, overexertion, heat reactions, dehydration, injuries by aquatic life, and cuts and scrapes. (b) Identify the conditions that must exist before performing CPR on a person, and explain how to recognize such conditions. Demonstrate the proper technique for performing CPR using a training device approved by your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 25)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never administer oral fluids or allow unassisted walking when evaluating suspected arterial gas embolism.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners believe drinking warm coffee quickly reverses severe underwater hypothermia on dive boats.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Caffeine accelerates fluid loss and vasodilation, so patients require passive warming and warm isotonic fluids.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Pressure Equalization: Divers pinch nostrils and exhale gently to equalize middle ear pressure during descent.
- Decompression Management: Administer pure normobaric oxygen continuously to any diver exhibiting neurological decompression sickness symptoms.
- Thermal Protection: Neoprene wetsuits trap thin water layers that body heat warms against cold thermoclines.
- Gas Exchange: Controlled slow breathing prevents carbon dioxide buildup and reduces hazardous hyperventilation underwater risks.

#### Req 1a: Diving Maladies and Field Treatment
**Official Requirement 1a:** Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while scuba diving, including hypothermia, hyperventilation, squeezes, decompression illness, nitrogen narcosis, motion sickness, fatigue, overexertion, heat reactions, dehydration, injuries by aquatic life, and cuts and scrapes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 25)* • **Organizer (Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix):** Req 1a Clinical Signs, First-Aid Treatment & Prevention Matrix
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Swimmers think ear pain during descent disappears naturally if they kick harder toward the bottom.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Descending with un-equalized middle ears causes tympanic membrane rupture, requiring immediate descent termination.

**Gear & Readiness Checklist:**
- [ ] U.S. Coast Guard-approved Type III Personal Flotation Device (PFD) properly fitted and buckled
- [ ] Waterfront rescue throw bag (50 ft floating polypropylene line), reach pole, and safety whistle
- [ ] BSA Safe Swim Defense / Safety Afloat buddy tags and watercraft inspection checklist
- [ ] Bilge pump or bailer, spare paddle/oar, painter lines, and dry bag with thermal layer

### Req 4: Open Water Diver Certification
**Official Requirement 4:** Earn an Open Water Diver Certification from a scuba organization recognized by the Scouting America scuba policy.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 12)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Earn An Open Water Diver)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Breathe continuously and never hold your breath while breathing compressed gas underwater.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners think holding their breath during ascent helps conserve precious remaining cylinder air volume.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Expanding air in ascending lungs causes fatal alveolar rupture unless breathing normally at all times.
