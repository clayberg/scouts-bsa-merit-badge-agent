### Req 1: Diving First Aid and CPR
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while scuba diving, including hypothermia, hyperventilation, squeezes, decompression illness, nitrogen narcosis, motion sickness, fatigue, overexertion, heat reactions, dehydration, injuries by aquatic life, and cuts and scrapes. (b) Identify the conditions that must exist before performing CPR on a person, and explain how to recognize such conditions. Demonstrate the proper technique for performing CPR using a training device approved by your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 25)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Scuba Diving Requirement 1: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for diving first aid and cpr, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Deliberately hyperventilating before diving is a safe way to stay underwater much longer without needing to breathe.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hyperventilation results from over-breathing due to panic or deliberate effort and causes dizziness, fainting, and a sensation of suffocation rather than safely extending air supply (p. 21).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hyperventilation is the result of over breathing, either deliberately or because of panic (p. 21).
- The likely result of hyperventilation is dizziness and fainting, and victims will feel as if they are being suffocated (p. 21).
- Such a condition is unlikely in diving if the participants are properly prepared for each new skill level (p. 21).
- If a diver shows signs of panic at any time, bring that person back into the boat or onto shore, calm the person, and encourage slow breathing (p. 21).

#### Req 1a: First Aid for Diving Injuries
**Official Requirement 1a:** Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while scuba diving, including hypothermia, hyperventilation, squeezes, decompression illness, nitrogen narcosis, motion sickness, fatigue, overexertion, heat reactions, dehydration, injuries by aquatic life, and cuts and scrapes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 25)* • **Organizer (Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix):** Req 1a Clinical Signs, First-Aid Treatment & Prevention Matrix

#### Req 1b: Conditions for Performing CPR
**Official Requirement 1b:** Identify the conditions that must exist before performing CPR on a person, and explain how to recognize such conditions. Demonstrate the proper technique for performing CPR using a training device approved by your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 25)* • **Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 1b Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist (Conditions That Must Exist Before)
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Scuba Diving Req 1b preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Ear and sinus squeezes happen only during ascent when expanding air pushes outward on the eardrum.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Squeezes occur primarily during descent when increasing water pressure compresses trapped air spaces that a diver fails to equalize early and often (p. 25).

### Req 3: Scuba Diver's Code and Safety
**Official Requirement 3:** Discuss the Scuba Diver's Code with your counselor, and explain the importance of each guideline to a scuba diver's safety.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 50)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss The Scuba Diver's)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Scuba Diving Requirement 3: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for scuba diver's code and safety, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Because compressed scuba air contains mostly oxygen, divers do not need to worry about nitrogen absorption on recreational dives.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Nitrogen makes up 79 percent of the air we breathe, and increasing underwater pressure forces more nitrogen into body tissues the deeper and longer a diver stays down (p. 19).

### Req 5: Exploring Aquatic Ecosystems
**Official Requirement 5:** Explain what an ecosystem is, and describe four aquatic ecosystems a diver might experience.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scuba Diving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 73, p. 75)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Ecosystem Is and Describe Four)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Scuba Diving Requirement 5: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for exploring aquatic ecosystems, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Coral reefs are lifeless mineral rock formations that do not depend on sunlight or microscopic plants.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Coral reefs are living tropical ecosystems powered by sunlight, where symbiotic algae provide essential nutrients and energy that pass from organism to organism up the food web (p. 73).
