### Req 1: Carving Hazards And First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while wood carving, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, or lessen these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries that could occur while wood carving, including minor cuts and scratches and splinters.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wood Carving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 26)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never carve toward your body or toward fingers supporting the wooden workpiece.
- **Common Myth:** A dull knife causes fewer and less severe injuries than a razor sharp blade.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dull blades require excessive cutting force and slip unpredictably off hard wood grain.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Cut Protection: Carving gloves woven with Kevlar or stainless steel mesh protect the holding hand.
- Blood Circle: Maintain a clear arm length boundary in every direction before opening any blade.
- Wound Cleaning: Wash minor carving lacerations thoroughly with mild soap and potable clean running water.
- Splinter Removal: Disinfect tweezers with isopropyl alcohol before gently extracting wood fragments along entry angles.

### Req 2: Totin Chip And Safety Rules
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Earn the Totin' Chip recognition. (b) Discuss with your counselor your understanding of the Safety Checklist for Carving.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wood Carving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 55)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never carve while standing or walking, and never leave open blades resting on benches.
- **Common Myth:** Totin Chip certification is a permanent award that cannot be suspended for careless behavior.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Scout leaders remove corners of a Totin Chip card for safety violations until retesting.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Pocketknife Etiquette: Close folding blades fully before passing or carrying pocketknives across the carving area.
- Sheath Security: Place fixed blade knives directly into rigid protective sheaths when setting them down.
- Stable Seating: Sit squarely on a sturdy chair with elbows supported near knees during cuts.
- Task Lighting: Ensure bright overhead illumination reaches the carving block to track every cut path.

### Req 3: Carving Tools And Sharpening Devices
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor, orally or in writing, the care and use of five types of tools that you may use in a carving project. (b) Tell your counselor how to care for and use several types of sharpening devices, then demonstrate that you know how to use these devices.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wood Carving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Keep sharpening strokes trailing away from fingers and never test sharpness by dragging skin across edges.
- **Common Myth:** Sharpening stones should move in tight circular swirls regardless of the blade profile.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Carving tools require sweeping strokes matching bevel angles to avoid rounding sharp cutting edges.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Gouge Profile: Straight gouges feature curved cutting edges designed for scooping hollows and removing bulk wood.
- Parting tools possess V shaped profiles suited for incising deep clean border lines.
- Whetstone Lubrication: Water or light mineral oil floats away steel swarf during blade edge sharpening.
- Stropping on leather charged with chromium oxide polishes the micro bevel razor sharp.

### Req 4: Fundamental Wood Carving Cuts
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Using a piece of scrap wood or a project on which you are working, show your counselor that you know how to do the following: (a) Paring cut (b) Basic cut and push cut (c) V cut (d) Stop cut or score line
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Wood Carving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 39)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Ensure the supporting thumb wears a leather thumb guard during paring and levering cuts.
- **Common Myth:** Carving wood quickly requires chopping directly downward into the block with raw wrist force.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Wood carving blades must slice across fibers with sliding motion to sever wood cleanly.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Paring Control: Draw the blade gently inward toward a gloved thumb acting as controlled pivot.
- Push Cut: Propel the knife spine forward using the opposite thumb for steady slicing pressure.
- V Cut Technique: Slice two opposing angled incisions that meet precisely along a deep central bottom.
- Stop Cut Barrier: Press the blade perpendicularly into grain to stop subsequent background relief slicing passes.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear dust masks when sanding exotic or domestic wood blanks to avoid respiratory irritation.
