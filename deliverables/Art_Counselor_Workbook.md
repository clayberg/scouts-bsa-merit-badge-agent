### Req 1: Meaning and Forms of Art
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Discuss the following with your counselor: (a) What art is and what some of the different forms of art are (b) The importance of art to humankind (c) What art means to you and how art can make you feel
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 22)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Art Requirement 1: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for meaning and forms of art, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Only paintings and sculptures displayed in museums qualify as real visual art.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Visual art includes both fine arts created purely for beauty and expression and functional arts like architecture, industrial design, pottery, and weaving (p. 8).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- The question of what "art" is has occupied the minds of philosophers and critics ever since humankind began to think and argue (p. 8).
- Traditional Fine Art Media: The term has been applied to many forms of visual media, especially to the fine arts, drawing, painting, sculpture, and print making (p. 8).
- Applied and Functional Arts: But it also includes architecture, industrial design, fashion design, pottery, and weaving (p. 8).
- Expression Versus Practical Function: The difference is that fine arts are concerned only with beauty and expression and the others, though the art may be beautiful, focus first on function (p. 8).

### Req 2: Core Elements of Visual Art
**Official Requirement 2:** Discuss with your counselor the following terms and elements of art: line, value, shape, form, space, color, and texture. Show examples of each element.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 24)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss Elements Line Value)

- **Common Myth:** Shape and form mean the exact same thing when describing the elements of art.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A shape is a flat two-dimensional area defined by an outline, whereas form describes a three-dimensional object having volume and depth (p. 16).

### Req 4: Rendering Subjects Across Four Media
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Render a subject of your choice in FOUR of these ways: (a) Pen and ink (b) Watercolors (c) Pencil (d) Pastels (e) Oil paints (f) Tempera (g) Acrylics (h) Charcoal (i) Computer drawing or painting
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 39, p. 49)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Art Requirement 4: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for rendering subjects across four media, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Requirement 4 allows you to draw four different subjects using four different media.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must render the same single subject of your choice in four different media so you can see how each medium changes the look of that subject (p. 39).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Artists use line and color in different ways to make paintings (p. 39).
- Coloring Over Outlined Forms: In some artwork, the line drawing is the basis for the painting and color is a secondary element, added to describe the outlined forms (p. 39).
- In other works, color is the subject of the painting (p. 39).
- Mixing Pigments and Paint: No matter how you plan to use color, you need to know how to mix pigments and apply the paint (p. 39).

**Official Requirement 6:** With your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, visit a museum, art exhibit, art gallery, artists' co-op, or artist's workshop. Find out about the art displayed or created there. Discuss what you learned with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 58, p. 60)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Museum Art Exhibit Art Gallery)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Art Requirement 6: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for experiencing art in person, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Art Req 6 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Viewing high-resolution photos of paintings online gives you the exact same experience as visiting an art museum.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Books and computer monitors flatten scale, texture, and subtle color layers that you can only appreciate by seeing the original artwork in person (p. 58).

### Req 7: Visual Arts Careers and Education
**Official Requirement 7:** Find out about three career opportunities in art. Pick one and find out the education, training, and experience required for this profession. Discuss this with your counselor, and explain why this profession might interest you.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 61, p. 62)* • **Organizer (Career & Profession Exploration Card):** Req 7 Career & Professional Pathway Investigation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Art Requirement 7: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for visual arts careers and education, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Natural artistic talent alone is enough to start a professional career in the visual arts without formal training.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** While hobbyists need no formal instruction, pursuing a career in the visual arts requires formal lessons, studio training, and a strong portfolio (p. 61).
