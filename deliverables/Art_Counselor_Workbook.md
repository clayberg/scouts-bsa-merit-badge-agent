## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Design something useful. Make a sketch or model of your design. With your counselor's approval, create a promotional piece for the item using a picture or pictures. (b) Tell a story with a picture or pictures or using a 3-D rendering. (c) Design a logo. Share your design with your counselor and explain the significance of your logo. Then, with your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, put your logo on Scout equipment, furniture, ceramics, or fabric.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Prepare):** With your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, visit a museum, art exhibit, art gallery, artists' co-op, or artist's workshop. Find out about the art displayed or created there. Discuss what you learned with your counselor.

### Req 2: Core Elements of Visual Art
**Official Requirement 2:** Discuss with your counselor the following terms and elements of art: line, value, shape, form, space, color, and texture. Show examples of each element.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 24)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does altering the tonal value of an object change the viewer perception of its three-dimensional form?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Linear Structure: Marks moving through space define boundaries and create directional movement across canvases.
  - Tonal Value: Lightness or darkness of surfaces creates the illusion of volume on flat sheets.
  - Dimensional Forms: Three-dimensional structures enclose physical volume while flat two-dimensional shapes enclose distinct geometric areas.
  - Surface Texture: Tactile quality or simulated visual roughness influences how light reflects from rendered objects.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Draw Elements: Sketch distinct examples of line, shape, form, space, and value in a sketchbook.
  - Step 2 - Render Values: Create a nine-step graduated value scale moving smoothly from pure white to solid black.
  - Step 3 - Add Texture: Apply crosshatching and stippling techniques to simulate rough wood and smooth polished metal.
  - Step 4 - Exhibit Samples: Review the completed sample sheet with your counselor to explain each element role.
- **Common Myth:** Novices frequently confuse geometric shapes with three-dimensional forms by using the terms interchangeably.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Shapes are two-dimensional flat boundaries with height and width, whereas forms possess three-dimensional depth and volume.

### Req 3: Principles of Compositional Design
**Official Requirement 3:** Discuss with your counselor the six principles of design: rhythm, balance, proportion, variety, emphasis, and unity.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Art Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 28)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does an artist use variety without destroying the overall unity of a painting?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Repetition of visual elements creates a sense of organized movement across the composition.
  - Equilibrium Balance: Symmetrical or asymmetrical distribution of visual weight stabilizes the entire working pictorial plane.
  - Scale Proportion: Comparative size relationships between components establish realistic perspective and hierarchy within an artwork.
  - Focal Emphasis: High contrast placement draws viewer attention directly toward the primary subject of interest.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Define Principles: Document concise working definitions for balance, rhythm, proportion, variety, emphasis, and compositional unity.
  - Step 2 - Analyze Masterworks: Examine classical museum paintings to identify focal points created through deliberate high-contrast lighting.
  - Step 3 - Sketch Diagrams: Draw thumbnail diagrams illustrating symmetrical balance, radial balance, and asymmetrical dynamic weight distribution.
  - Step 4 - Evaluate Unity: Review how consistent color harmony binds diverse compositional elements into a coherent whole.
- **Common Myth:** Students often think that balance in an art composition requires identical symmetrical mirroring on both halves.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Asymmetrical balance achieves visual stability through the careful counterposition of contrasting colors, textures, and object sizes.
