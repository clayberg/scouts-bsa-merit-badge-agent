### Req 3: Morse Code and Sign Language
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe what Morse code is and the various means by which it can be sent. Spell your first name using Morse code. Send or receive a message of six to 10 words using Morse code. (b) Describe what American Sign Language (ASL) is and how it is used today. Spell your first name using American Sign Language. Send or receive a message of six to 10 words using ASL.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Signs, Signals, and Codes Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Avoid pointing high powered signaling flashlights directly into observers eyes during nighttime optical code practice.
- **Common Myth:** Morse code speed depends on rushing dit sounds rather than maintaining consistent silence spacing between characters.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Standard Morse timing requires three dit lengths between letters and seven dit lengths between words.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Timing Unit Precision: A dah duration equals three dits, with one dit interval between individual elements.
- Transmission Medium Flexibility: Operators send Morse signals using flashing lights, audio buzzers, mechanical clickers, or flags.
- Visual Spatial Grammar: American Sign Language combines manual finger signs with grammatical facial cues and posture.
- Fingerspelling Manual Alphabet: Proper names without designated lexical signs require direct letter spelling using manual handshapes.

### Req 4: Semaphore and Nautical Flag Systems
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Give your counselor a brief explanation about semaphore, why it is used, how it is used, and where it is used. (b) Explain to your counselor the difference between semaphore flags and nautical flags. (c) After 4(a) and 4(b) have been completed, do the following: (1) Spell your first name using semaphore. Send or receive a message of six to 10 words using semaphore. (2) Using illustrations or photographs, identify 10 examples of nautical flags and discuss their importance.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Signs, Signals, and Codes Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 8)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Maintain a safe clear perimeter around the sender to prevent flag sticks from striking nearby participants.
- **Common Myth:** Semaphore flags and maritime international hoist flags function identically and share the same visual designs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Semaphore relies on angular arm positions with two flags, while nautical flags fly from ship halyards.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Clock Face Positioning: Semaphore flag angles align precisely with eight compass directions radiating from the shoulders.
- Rest Position Discipline: Return both flag staffs down across the thighs between words to signal spacing.
- Nautical International Code: Individual maritime hoist flags represent specific phonetic letters and universal shipboard hazard notices.
- High Contrast Fabrics: Semaphore flags use divided red and yellow squares to maximize visibility against ocean horizons.

### Req 5: Tactile Braille Reading and Embossing
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Explain the braille reading technique and how it helps individuals with sight impairment to communicate. Then do the following: (a) Either by sight or by touch, identify the letters of the braille alphabet that spell your name. By sight or touch, decode a braille message at least six words long. (b) Create a message in braille at least six words long, and share this with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Signs, Signals, and Codes Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Handle pointed embossing styluses carefully to prevent puncture wounds to fingertips during manual braille embossing.
- **Common Myth:** Braille is a foreign spoken language with entirely different vocabulary rather than an alphabet tactile code.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Braille is a tactile writing code used to transcribe existing languages including English, Spanish, and mathematics.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Six Dot Cell Layout: Cells consist of two parallel vertical columns containing three embossed dot positions each.
- Readers sweep index finger pads lightly across raised dots from left to right.
- Embossing characters manually with a stylus requires writing from right to left in reverse.
- Grade two braille introduces contractions and word signs to increase reading and printing speed.

### Req 6: Acoustic and Silent Field Signals

Discuss the pros and cons of using sound signals versus other types of signals. (b) Demonstrate to your counselor six different silent Scout signals. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Signs, Signals, and Codes Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 8)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear certified hearing protection when testing industrial horns or high decibel emergency whistles at close range.
- **Common Myth:** Sound signals are always more dependable than visual signals in dense forested outdoor environments.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Wind direction, heavy rain, and terrain ridges can deflect or completely block auditory emergency signals.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Omnidirectional Sound Waves: Acoustic alerts spread in all directions simultaneously, reaching listeners without direct visual sight.
- Environmental Acoustic Masking: High winds, flowing rivers, and ambient traffic noise can drown out distant whistle signals.
- Silent Visual Control: Hand signals enable stealthy troop navigation without alerting game animals or disturbing wilderness tranquility.
- Standard Alert Postures: Raising a clenched fist overhead commands the entire patrol to halt in place immediately.
