### Req 1: Action Sports Environmental Hazard Management
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in action sports (skateboarding or snowboarding) and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for hypothermia, frostbite, dehydration, sunburn, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, blisters, sprains, strains, fractures, head/concussion injuries, and spinal injuries. (c) Show your helmet, wrist guards, knee/elbow pads, boots, and bindings, and explain how to inspect and fit them properly.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Action Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Remove cracked or post-collision helmets from service immediately because internal foam liners crush irreversibly during crashes.
> **Starting Gate (Required Gear & Equipment Inspection):** Required Field Gear / PPE Inspection - Bring required personal gear or PPE for Action Sports Req 1 inspection prior to hands-on station work.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners believe a bicycle helmet provides adequate impact coverage for park skateboarding and snowboarding.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Action sports require ASTM F1492 or ASTM F2040 certified helmets designed for multi-directional and back-of-head impacts.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Impact Attenuation: Multi-impact helmets absorb direct kinetic forces to protect vulnerable skull plates and intracranial tissue.
- Surface Evaluation: Wet wooden ramps and icy hardpack snow eliminate traction, causing severe uncontrolled slides.
- Hypothermia Prevention: Layered synthetic garments manage body moisture while retaining essential core heat during freezes.
- Wrist Protection: Rigid internal splints divert hyperextension torque away from delicate carpal bones upon impact.

### Req 2: Park Etiquette and Athletic Conditioning
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the Skier/Snowboarder Responsibility Code or Skatepark Etiquette and Safety Rules, and why each rule is vital to rider and bystander safety. (b) Demonstrate a warm-up, stretching, and conditioning routine for action sports.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Action Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never sit or stop beneath the lip of a jump or inside a blind skate bowl where approaching athletes cannot spot you.
- **Common Myth:** Skiers and snowboarders stopped in the middle of a trail have the right of way over faster riders behind them.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Responsibility Code requires riders who stop to position themselves where they are clearly visible from above without obstructing trails.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Right Of Way: Downhill riders possess primary clearance, requiring trailing individuals to navigate safely around them.
- Calling your drop line aloud prevents dangerous simultaneous entries into crowded skate transitions.
- Dynamic Warmup: Active leg swings and torso rotations stimulate synovial joint fluid prior to riding.
- Snowboarders must vacate terrain park landing zones immediately to avoid blind aerial collisions.

### Req 4: Fundamental Board Control Techniques
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Choose ONE option (Skateboarding OR Snowboarding) and demonstrate fundamental riding skills: (a) Stance (regular vs. goofy), mounting/dismounting, pushing/gliding on flat terrain, and controlled stopping (foot braking, heel/toe edge stop, or tail scrape). (b) Carving turns (frontside and backside), weight transfer, falling safely, and navigating slopes or skatepark transitions under full control.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Action Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never look down at your board while riding downhill because tracking eye gaze forward stabilizes inner ear equilibrium.
- **Common Myth:** Riders think extending rigid arms directly toward the ground safely breaks a sudden backward fall.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Bracing with stiff locked wrists fractures the scaphoid bone and radial head, requiring tucked shoulder rolls instead.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Stance Determination: The dominant forward foot establishes either regular stance or goofy stance for boarders.
- Edge Engagement: Pressing toe edges or heel edges forces the board into smooth carved arcs.
- Lowering the rear foot flat against asphalt applies friction without destabilizing your center.
- Impact Dissipation: Tucking the chin and rolling sideways redistributes downward fall kinetic energy across shoulders.

### Req 5: Intermediate Maneuvers and Park Stewardship
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Demonstrate three intermediate maneuvers or tricks approved by your counselor in a supervised skatepark or snow terrain park. (b) Explain Leave No Trace and outdoor stewardship principles at mountain resorts and community skateparks, and explore careers or volunteer roles (such as National Ski Patrol, adaptive sports instructor, or equipment engineer) in action sports.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Action Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Pre-ride all park features at slow speed before attempting jumps or grinds, checking for rutted lips or iced landings.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Action Sports Req 5 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Riders believe attempting advanced park tricks beyond current skill levels accelerates balance progression safely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Attempting tricks without mastering prerequisite foundation maneuvers causes uncontrolled high-speed ejections and severe spinal injuries.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Maneuver Progression: Mastering flat-ground pop and edge control must precede launching off elevated park features.
- Insufficient speed off kickers causes dangerous impacts directly onto flat feature knuckles.
- Waste Management: Discarded plastic wrappers and wax shavings damage fragile alpine plants and clog park drains.
- Patrol Support: Volunteer safety patrols manage mountain evacuations and provide first aid across rugged slopes.
