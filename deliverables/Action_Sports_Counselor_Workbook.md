### Req 1: Hazards, First Aid, and Protective Gear
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in action sports (skateboarding or snowboarding) and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for hypothermia, frostbite, dehydration, sunburn, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, blisters, sprains, strains, fractures, head/concussion injuries, and spinal injuries. (c) Show your helmet, wrist guards, knee/elbow pads, boots, and bindings, and explain how to inspect and fit them properly.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Action Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Hazards, First Aid, and Protective Gear discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
> **Starting Gate (Required Gear & Equipment Inspection):** Required Field Gear / PPE Inspection - Bring required personal gear or PPE for Action Sports Req 1 inspection prior to hands-on station work.
- **Common Myth:** A multi-sport bicycle helmet provides identical impact protection to an ASTM-certified skateboarding or snowboarding helmet during backward falls.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Skateboarding and snowboarding helmets are specifically engineered to cover the lower back of the skull and withstand multiple low-impact hits or severe alpine collisions (p. 12).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hazards, First Aid, and Protective Gear: Hazards, first aid, and protective gear, record accurate field observations in your workbook, and demonstrate safe execution during your counselor review (p. 12).
- Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for hypothermia, frostbite, dehydration, sunburn, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, blisters, sprains, strains (p. 12).
- Protective Equipment Inspection: Show your helmet, wrist guards, knee/elbow pads, boots, and bindings, and explain how to inspect and fit them properly (p. 12).

### Req 2: Rider Etiquette and Physical Conditioning
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the Skier/Snowboarder Responsibility Code or Skatepark Etiquette and Safety Rules, and why each rule is vital to rider and bystander safety. (b) Demonstrate a warm-up, stretching, and conditioning routine for action sports.

- **Common Myth:** On a crowded ski slope, the downhill snowboarder must always look uphill and yield the right-of-way to faster riders approaching from behind.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Under the Skier/Snowboarder Responsibility Code, people ahead or downhill of you always have the right-of-way because they cannot see riders approaching from behind (p. 12).

### Skateboarding: Stance, Pushing, Braking & Skatepark Carving
**Official Requirement 4a (Parent Overview):** Option A - Skateboarding. Demonstrate fundamental skateboarding riding skills.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Action Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Action Sports Requirement 4 on fundamental riding stance and control: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** When losing balance during a fall, extending your arms straight out with locked elbows and open palms is the safest way to stop your momentum.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Catching yourself with locked arms and outstretched hands frequently causes wrist and collarbone fractures, so riders should tuck their arms, bend their knees, and roll to disperse impact (p. 12).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Req 4a(1) (1): Skateboarding Stance, Mounting/Dismounting, Pushing & Controlled Braking: Demonstrate regular vs. goofy stance, mounting/dismounting, pushing/gliding on flat terrain, and foot/heel-drag controlled stopping.
