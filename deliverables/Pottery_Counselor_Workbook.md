**Official Requirement 1:** Explain to your counselor the precautions that must be followed for the safe use and operation of a potter's tools, equipment, and other materials.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Pottery Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 39, p. 43)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Precautions That Must Be Followed)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Pottery Requirement 1: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for pottery studio tool and kiln safety, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Sweeping dry clay scraps and glaze powder off the studio floor with a broom is the safest way to keep a pottery workspace clean.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dry sweeping stirs hazardous silica dust and toxic glaze particles into the air, so studio surfaces should be cleaned with wet sponges or mops and proper ventilation (p. 43).

### Req 2: Clay Body Properties and Potters Wheels
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the properties and ingredients of a good clay body for the following: (1) Making sculpture using the hand-building method (2) Throwing on the wheel (b) Tell how three different kinds of potter's wheels work.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Pottery Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Pottery Requirement 2: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for clay body properties and potters wheels, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Pure porcelain is the easiest clay body for beginners to throw on a potter's wheel because it is smooth and fine-grained.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Stoneware is much more plastic than porcelain, making it far easier to shape when throwing on the wheel or hand-building (p. 32).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Stoneware fires to higher temperatures, which leaves the surface very hard and nonporous (p. 32).
- It is usually opaque, but when thinly potted can be somewhat translucent (p. 32).
- Stoneware is more plastic than porcelain, which makes it much easier to use in throwing on the wheel or in hand-building (p. 32).
- Some natural clays are stoneware, but often several clays are combined to make a stoneware clay body (p. 32).

### Req 5: Hand Building Wheel Throwing and Firing
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: Each piece is to be painted, glazed, or otherwise decorated by you. (a) Make a slab pot, a coil pot, and a pinch pot. (b) Make a human or animal figurine or decorative sculpture. (c) Throw a functional form on a potter's wheel. (d) Help an experienced adult to load and fire a kiln OR describe in detail how to load and fire a kiln.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Pottery Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 64)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Pottery Requirement 5: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for hand building wheel throwing and firing, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Letting a clay pot bake in hot summer sunlight for a week vitrifies the clay so it can hold water just like kiln-fired pottery.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Sun-dried clay vessels were never fired to ceramic temperatures and could only store dry grain because liquid water would soak into the raw clay and dissolve it (p. 13).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Archaeologists in the Czech Republic have uncovered human made ceramics that they believe date to at least 24,000 (p. 13).
- Prehistoric Clay Figure Composition: These artifacts are in the form of slabs, balls, and animal- and human-shaped figures, and are made of animal fat, bone, bone ash, and a clay (p. 13).
- The earliest clay vessels were sun-dried, not fired (p. 13).
- Dry Grain Storage Limits: They probably were used to store only grain and other dry foods, because filling them with water would have caused the clay to absorb the liquid (p. 13).

### Req 8: Careers in Studio Pottery and Ceramics
**Official Requirement 8:** Find out about career opportunities in pottery. Pick one and find out the education, training, and experience required for this profession. Discuss this with your counselor, and explain why this profession might interest you.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Pottery Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 90, p. 91)* • **Organizer (Career & Profession Exploration Card):** Req 8 Career & Professional Pathway Investigation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Work in a ventilated studio area for Pottery Requirement 8: check safety data labels on pigments, glazes, clay dust, and solvents used for careers in studio pottery and ceramics, wash hands thoroughly after handling media, and leave kiln operations to qualified adults.
- **Common Myth:** Careers in the ceramics field are limited to self-employed craft potters selling bowls at local art fairs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The ceramics field is extremely diverse, offering careers in chemical, mechanical, and ceramic engineering as well as manufacturing management and industrial design (p. 90).
