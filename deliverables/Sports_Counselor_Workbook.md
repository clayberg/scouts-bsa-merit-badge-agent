## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) With guidance from your counselor, establish a suitable personal training program that you will follow throughout your competition season (or for three months). (b) Create a chart or other tracking system, and document your training, practice, and development during this time. (c) Demonstrate proper technique to play each sport effectively and avoid injury. ### Req 1: Sports Injury Prevention and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely risks you may encounter while participating in sports and what you should do to anticipate, prevent, mitigate, and respond to those risks. (b) Show that you know first aid or understand the treatment for injuries that could occur while participating in sports, including sprains; strains; muscle cramps; contusions; abrasions; blisters; dehydration; heat reactions; fractures; injured teeth; head, neck, and back injuries; and concussions.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 87, p. 89)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Complete a dynamic warm-up, check that the swing or play zone is clear of bystanders before demonstrating sports injury prevention and first aid for Sports Requirement 1, and hydrate regularly according to BSA Sports and Fitness guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** When you feel a warm friction spot on your foot during a game, you should keep running until practice ends before treating it.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** If you feel a hot spot developing, you should stop immediately and cover the tender skin with moleskin or an adhesive bandage before a blister forms (p. 87).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Blisters are usually caused by continual rubbing against the skin (p. 87).
- This friction will cause a hot spot, which signals the beginning of a blister (p. 87).
- Common Foot Blister Hazards: Blisters on the feet are the most common and troublesome type (p. 87).
- Proper Footwear and Socks: So be sure that you wear properly fittings hoes and appropriate socks (p. 87).

### Req 2: Physical Exams and Healthy Athletic Habits
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain the following: (a) The importance of the physical exam (b) The importance of maintaining good health habits for life (such as exercising regularly), and how the use of tobacco products, alcohol, and other harmful substances can negatively affect your health and your performance in sports activities (c) The importance of maintaining a healthy diet
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 16)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Complete a dynamic warm-up, check that the swing or play zone is clear of bystanders before demonstrating physical exams and healthy athletic habits for Sports Requirement 2, and hydrate regularly according to BSA Sports and Fitness guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Occasional smoking or vaping does not affect a young athlete's endurance as long as they run every day.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Tobacco smoke coats the lungs with sticky tars, injures airways and air sacs, and interferes with proper lung growth needed for peak athletic stamina (p. 10).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Avoiding Harmful Substances: To be a strong, healthy performer in sports and in life, avoid tobacco, alcohol, and other harmful substances (p. 10).
- Tobacco smoke interferes with proper lung growth and function (p. 10).
- It coats the lungs with sticky tars and injures the airways and air sacs of the lungs (p. 10).
- Athletes need their lungs working at peak capacity (p. 10).
