### Req 1: Sports Injury Prevention Protocols
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely risks you may encounter while participating in sports and what you should do to anticipate, prevent, mitigate, and respond to those risks. (b) Show that you know first aid or understand the treatment for injuries that could occur while participating in sports, including sprains; strains; muscle cramps; contusions; abrasions; blisters; dehydration; heat reactions; fractures; injured teeth; head, neck, and back injuries; and concussions.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 87, p. 89)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Remove any athlete showing signs of head trauma immediately from play and do not permit return without medical clearance.
- **Common Myth:** A player who collapses without contact is simply experiencing severe exhaustion or heat cramps.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cardiopulmonary resuscitation and automated external defibrillator deployment must begin immediately during sudden cardiac arrest.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Environmental Hazard Recognition: Coaches must monitor wet bulb globe temperature readings during hot outdoor athletic practices.
- Acute Soft Tissue Care: Compression wraps reduce internal bleeding and swelling around strained tendons or torn ligaments.
- Concussion Protocol Adherence: Athletes exhibiting dizziness or confusion after head impact must exit the competition immediately.
- Hydration Schedule Management: Fluid replacement schedules prevent heat cramps before thirst sensations register during strenuous workouts.

### Req 2: Athletic Health Wellness Foundation
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain the following: (a) The importance of the physical exam (b) The importance of maintaining good health habits for life (such as exercising regularly), and how the use of tobacco products, alcohol, and other harmful substances can negatively affect your health and your performance in sports activities (c) The importance of maintaining a healthy diet
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 16)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Athletes diagnosed with heart murmurs or exercise-induced bronchospasm must obtain specialist clearance before engaging in vigorous competition.
- **Common Myth:** An athletic physical exam is unnecessary for teenagers who feel energetic and healthy.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Annual sports physicals identify underlying cardiac anomalies, asthma triggers, and structural vulnerabilities before physical stress.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Preparticipation examinations identify silent cardiac structural abnormalities that create catastrophic risks during competition.
- Substance Abuse Performance Impact: Nicotine constricts peripheral blood vessels, reducing essential oxygen delivery to working muscle fibers.
- Complex carbohydrates supply sustained muscular glycogen stores necessary for extended tournament play endurance.
- Lifelong Habit Formation: Regular aerobic exercise maintains cardiovascular elasticity and prevents chronic adult metabolic disease conditions.

- Strict lifting technique with moderate resistance builds tendon strength without damaging growth plates.
- Amateur athletes compete solely for love of sport without receiving direct financial compensation.
- True sportsmanship honors game rules, respects match officials, and encourages teammates through adversity.

### Req 6: Sports Careers Lifetime Pursuits
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge related to a sport. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career. (b) Identify how you might use skills and knowledge related to a sport to pursue a personal hobby and/or healthy lifestyle. Research the additional training required, expenses, and affiliation with organizations that would help you maximize the enjoyment and benefit you might gain from it. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and share what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sports Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 25, p. 75)* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** All clinical sports medicine practices require strict compliance with pathogen exposure control and patient confidentiality laws.
- **Common Myth:** The only viable careers in athletics are becoming a professional player or head coach.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Sports careers encompass diverse fields including athletic training, biomechanics, physical therapy, and athletic administration.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Sports Medicine Careers: Certified athletic trainers require specialized degrees, clinical internships, and board licensing to practice.
- Biomechanics Analysis Field: Sports biomechanists analyze human kinetic data to enhance equipment design and prevent injuries.
- Recreational Lifetime Participation: Community sports leagues provide sustainable physical activity, social connection, and stress relief opportunities.
- Athletic Administration Path: Sports facility directors manage operational budgets, safety compliance, scheduling, and risk mitigation programs.
