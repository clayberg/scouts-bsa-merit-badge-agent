### Req 1: Chemical Safety and Data Sheets
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Chemistry and Safety. Do the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Obtain safety data sheets (SDS) for sucrose (sugar), isopropyl alcohol (rubbing alcohol), and a waterproofing spray. Compare their properties and potential hazard(s), including handling and disposal guidelines and toxicity information. (b) What types of personal protective equipment (PPE) are recommended for each, and why? How does the recommended PPE change with toxicity and route of exposure? (c) Review the pictograms in Section 2 of each SDS. What do they mean, and how do you see them used in your community? (d) Discuss the safe storage of chemicals. How does the safe storage of chemicals apply to your home, school, community, and the environment?
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chemistry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** 5-Column Hazard Assessment Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Chemistry Requirement 1 on chemical safety and data sheets: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** Common household substances like table sugar do not have official Safety Data Sheets because they are safe to eat.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Every refined chemical substance, including food-grade sucrose, has a standardized Safety Data Sheet that outlines its physical properties, bulk dust hazards, and safe handling procedures (p. 22).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Safe Substance Handling Procedures: An SDS gives both consumers and emergency personnel the correct procedures for using a particular substance (p. 22).
- OSHA Workplace Exposure Monitoring: A government agency called the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) monitors exposure to chemicals in the workplace and SDS reporting (p. 22).
- An SDS allows the hazardous chemical manufacturer to alert the chemical user and emergency personnel about important safety information (p. 22).
- Mandatory Federal Data Standards: Although formats can differ, U. S. law requires an SDS to include certain data (p. 22).

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chemistry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 40, p. 68)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Chemistry Requirement 2 on first aid surfactants and antiseptics: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** Rinsing oily skin with plain water dissolves grease if the water temperature is hot enough.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Oil and water do not mix on their own, so a surfactant like soap is needed to reduce surface tension and lift nonpolar oils off the skin (p. 40).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Closely related to medicines are the chemicals used in everyday foods (p. 40).
- Household Sucrose Purity: An extremely pure chemical available around the house is sucrose, or table sugar (p. 40).
- One area of active research is noncaloric sweeteners like saccharin, aspartame, and sucralose (p. 40).
- Fill a clear empty plastic bottle half full of water (p. 40).

Discuss with your counselor whether a physical or chemical change has occurred, and what happened to molecules in the food during the cooking process. (b) Prepare and use red cabbage indicator to measure the pH of five common household liquids, including water. Discuss your observations with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chemistry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 17)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Chemistry Requirement 3 on culinary reactions and ph indicators: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** Browning food in a skillet is a physical change because only heat is applied to the ingredients.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Maillard browning reaction is a chemical change where heat causes amino acids and reducing sugars to rearrange into hundreds of new flavor and color molecules (p. 17).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- When people hear the word chemicals, they may feel afraid (p. 15).
- Safety of Everyday Substances: They unconsciously may think that all chemicals are poisonous, but not all chemicals are even dangerous (p. 15).
- Household Material Composition: Everything in your house is made from chemicals, including the food you eat and the clothes you wear (p. 15).
- Essential Biological Reactions: To live and breathe, you must continuously carry out many chemical reactions within your body (p. 15).
