### Req 2: Antiseptic Chemistry and Sanitation
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Chemistry and First Aid. After successfully completing requirement 1, do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor why you think soap and alcohol are used to clean cuts and scrapes. Discuss how you could test your hypotheses with your counselor. (b) Coat your hands with a mixture of cooking oil and a nontoxic powder, such as sand or cocoa. Wash your hands with water and observe what happens. Coat your hands again, then wash with soap and water. Discuss the differences with your counselor. (c) Perform an experiment to demonstrate how rubbing alcohol affects microbial growth using baker's yeast. Discuss the results with your counselor. Resource: Watching How Alcohol Kills Virus and Bacteria Under a Microscope (video)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chemistry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 40, p. 68)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Keep rubbing alcohol away from open flames, heat sources, eyes, and broken mucous membranes.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often think alcohol cleans skin by chemically dissolving dirt particles like soap.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Alcohol denatures proteins and kills microbes, but lacks surfactant properties needed to lift physical soils.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Surfactant Mechanics: Hydrophobic tails bind nonpolar oils while hydrophilic heads interact directly with water molecules.
- Membrane Disruption: Alcohols dissolve lipid bilayer envelopes, causing rapid intracellular leakage and cellular lysis.
- Emulsification Process: Mechanical agitation suspends insoluble particulate debris within aqueous micelle spheres for rinsing.
- Protein Denaturation: Alcohol solutions coagulate vital structural enzymes to eliminate active bacterial metabolic pathways permanently.

### Req 3: Culinary Chemical Reaction Analysis
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Chemistry and Cooking. After successfully completing requirement 1, do EACH of the following: (a) Perform a Maillard, or browning, reaction. Discuss with your counselor whether a physical or chemical change has occurred, and what happened to molecules in the food during the cooking process. (b) Prepare and use red cabbage indicator to measure the pH of five common household liquids, including water. Discuss your observations with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chemistry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 17)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use heat resistant gloves when handling hot pans and boil cabbage extracts in ventilated areas.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often view browning toast as a simple physical drying change caused by evaporation.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Maillard reaction is a chemical change that creates hundreds of distinct new flavor molecules.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Maillard Condensation: Reducing sugars react with amino acids to generate complex aromatic melanoidin polymer pigments.
- Chemical Transformation: Heat driven covalent bond restructuring produces entirely new chemical compounds with distinct aromas.
- Anthocyanin Indicators: Red cabbage pigment changes molecular configuration and color across varying hydronium ion concentrations.
- Hydrogen Ion Scale: Logarithmic pH measurements indicate relative acidity or alkalinity in common aqueous culinary solutions.

(d) Under your counselor's supervision and/or the supervision of a knowledgeable adult approved in advance by your counselor, synthesize nylon (may be from a kit).
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chemistry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 40)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Perform flame tests and polymer syntheses only under adult supervision with indirect vent safety goggles.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners frequently believe pouring water on any camp fire is the fastest universal extinguishing method.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water on grease or energized electrical fires causes catastrophic steam explosions and electrical shock hazards.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hydrophobic Repulsion: Nonpolar fluoropolymer or silicone sprays create high water contact angles on woven textiles.
- Fire Triangle: Sustained combustion requires simultaneous fuel presence, oxidizing gas, activation heat, and uninhibited radicals.
- Atomic Emission: Excited electrons emit characteristic visible wavelengths as they transition down to ground states.
- Polymer Synthesis: Diamines react with diacid chlorides at liquid interfaces to form continuous nylon polyamide filaments.

- **Common Myth:** Students often believe all chemists work exclusively in traditional white coat laboratory bench testing environments.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Chemists work in pilot plants, software modeling firms, patent law offices, and environmental field stations.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Academic Research: Laboratory scientists design novel synthetic pathways and characterize reaction mechanisms using advanced instrumentation.
- Industrial Manufacturing: Chemical engineers scale laboratory bench reactions up to massive continuous flow commercial reactors.
- Quality Assurance: Analytical technicians test manufactured product batches against strict purity standards using automated chromatography.
- Career Pathways: Chemistry degrees open diverse opportunities across forensic science, materials engineering, patent law, and medicine.
