## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 3 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Attend an in-person meeting of your city, town, or county council or school board, local court session; OR another state or local governmental meeting approved in advance by your counselor. (b) Choose one of the issues discussed at the meeting where a difference of opinions was expressed, and explain to your counselor why you agree with one opinion more than you do another one.
- [ ] **Req 4 (Demonstrate):** Choose an issue that is important to the citizens of your community; then do the following: (a) Find out which branch of local government is responsible for this issue. (b) With your counselor's and a parent or guardian's approval, interview one person from the branch of government you identified in requirement 4(a). Ask what is being done about this issue and how young people can help. (c) Share what you have learned with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Identify three charitable organizations outside of Scouting that interest you and bring people in your community together to work for the good of your community. (b) Pick ONE of the organizations you chose for requirement 7(a). Using a variety of resources (including newspapers, flyers and other literature, the internet, volunteers, and employees of the organization), find out more about this organization. (c) With your counselor's and your parent or guardian's approval, contact the organization you chose for requirement 7(b), and find out what young people can do to help. While working on this merit badge, volunteer at least eight hours of your time for the organization. After your volunteer experience is over, discuss what you have learned with your counselor.

## 4. Counselor Facilitation Guide, Expected Answer Keys & Verification Rubrics

### Req 1: Foundations of Civic Community Engagement
**Official Requirement 1:** Discuss with your counselor what citizenship in the community means and what it takes to be a good citizen in your community. Discuss the rights, duties, and obligations of citizenship, and explain how you can demonstrate good citizenship in your community, Scouting unit, place of worship, or school.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Citizenship in the Community Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 11)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 1 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does a mandatory civic duty like paying property taxes differ from a voluntary civic obligation?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Civil Liberties: Constitutional protections guarantee freedom of speech, assembly, and equal protection under local laws.
  - Mandatory Civic Duties: Citizens must obey municipal ordinances, pay local property taxes, and serve jury duty.
  - Voluntary Obligations: Responsible community members vote in local elections and support charitable neighborhood initiatives.
  - Youth Civic Practice: Young people demonstrate community citizenship through organized public service and ethical behavior.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Term Definition: Record written definitions for community rights, mandatory duties, and voluntary personal obligations.
  - Step 2 - Local Inventory: List three specific municipal ordinances and two public services affecting your neighborhood.
  - Step 3 - Action Planning: Outline two concrete service activities demonstrating personal investment in neighborhood public welfare.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Discussion: Present your civic analysis and action plans during an individual counseling conference.
- **Common Myth:** A person fulfills community citizenship simply by residing within city boundaries and avoiding criminal behavior.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** True citizenship demands active civic participation, including voting, serving public needs, and defending community members' civil rights.
