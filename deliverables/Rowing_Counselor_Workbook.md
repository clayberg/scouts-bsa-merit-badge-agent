### Req 1: Rowing Hazards and Safety Afloat
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in rowing activities, including weather- and water-related hazards, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for the following injuries or illnesses that can occur while rowing: blisters, hypothermia, heat-related illnesses, dehydration, sunburn, sprains, and strains. (c) Review the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Explain to your counselor how this applies to rowing activities.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Rowing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 14)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Rowing Requirement 1: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for rowing hazards and safety afloat, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Rowers should grip the oar handles as tightly as possible at all times to prevent hand blisters.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Tightly squeezing the oar increases friction, whereas relaxing or adjusting your grip helps prevent hot spots from turning into blisters (p. 13).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hand Blister Risks: In rowing, blisters are most likely to occur on the hands (p. 13).
- Early Blister Warning Signs: Pay attention to any tender or sensitive areas (hot spots) that indicate the start of a blister (p. 13).
- Grip Friction Adjustment: You may be able to adjust or relax your grip to avoid friction on the sensitive area (p. 13).
- Protective Rowing Gloves: If you are prone to blisters, prevent them by wearing rowing gloves (p. 13).

### Req 4: Fundamental Boat Handling and Maneuvers
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Alone or with a passenger, do the following in either a fixed-seat or sliding-seat rowboat: (1) Launch. (2) Row in a straight line for 100 yards. Stop, pivot, and return to the starting point. (3) Backwater in a straight line for 25 yards. Make a turn underway and return to the starting point. (4) Land and moor or rack your craft. (5) Tie the following mooring knots, clove hitch, roundturn with two half-hitches, bowline, Wellman's knot, and mooring hitch. (b) Participate as a rowing team member in a competitive rowing meet. The team may be sponsored by a school, club, or Scout unit. The meet must include competition between two or more teams with different sponsors. Complete at least 10 hours of team practice prior to the meet.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Rowing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 40, p. 67)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist

- **Common Myth:** Dragging a heavy rowboat onto a sandy bank or wooden dock is the safest way to secure it between practices.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dragging a heavy boat onto the bank or dock stresses the frame and damages the hull (p. 40).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Outhauling stresses the craft and will shorten the useful life of the equipment (p. 40).
- Preventing Hull Abrasion Damage: Dragging heavy craft onto the bank or dock will damage the boat's hull (p. 40).

### Req 5: Pier Transfers and Stern Sculling
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) In a fixed-seat rowboat, come alongside a pier and help a passenger into the boat. Pull away from the pier, change positions with your passenger, and demonstrate sculling over the stern or side. Resume your rowing position, return alongside the pier, and help your passenger out of the boat. (b) In a sliding-seat rowboat, come alongside a pier and, with your buddy assisting you, get out onto the pier. Help your buddy into the boat. Reverse roles with your buddy and repeat the procedure.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Rowing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 67, p. 79)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Rowing Requirement 5: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for pier transfers and stern sculling, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** When switching from rowing with a passenger to rowing alone, you should remain on the bow rowing seat so the oars feel the same.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** When rowing alone, you sit on the midship seat to keep the boat properly trimmed and balanced on the water (p. 67).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Midship Seat Trim Balance: The strokes and maneuvers of rowing alone are the same as rowing with a passenger, except that you sit on the midshipseat to maintain trim (p. 67).
- Assisted Mooring Launches: When doing a single-person launch from a mooring where the bow is tied out, it is a good idea to ask another boater for help (p. 67).
- The person assisting will play the role of the passenger, except that instead of getting in when you are ready, he or she will give you a gentle push (p. 67).
- Solo Launch Preparation: If no one is available to help out, begin by loading your gear into the boat, then untying the stern painter (p. 67).

#### Req 5a: Fixed-Seat Passenger Exchange and Sculling
**Official Requirement 5a:** In a fixed-seat rowboat, come alongside a pier and help a passenger into the boat. Pull away from the pier, change positions with your passenger, and demonstrate sculling over the stern or side. Resume your rowing position, return alongside the pier, and help your passenger out of the boat.
