## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 3:** Review the characteristics of life jackets most appropriate for rowing and why one must always be worn while rowing. Then demonstrate how to select and fit a life jacket.
- [ ] **Station Req 4:** Do ONE of the following: (a) Alone or with a passenger, do the following in either a fixed-seat or sliding-seat rowboat: (1) Launch. (2) Row in a straight line for 100 yards. Stop, pivot, and return to the starting point. (3) Backwater in a straight line for 25 yards. Make a turn underway and return to the starting point. (4) Land and moor or rack your craft. (5) Tie the following mooring knots, clove hitch, roundturn with two half-hitches, bowline, Wellman's knot, and mooring hitch. (b) Participate as a rowing team member in a competitive rowing meet. The team may be sponsored by a school, club, or Scout unit. The meet must include competition between two or more teams with different sponsors. Complete at least 10 hours of team practice prior to the meet.
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Do ONE of the following: (a) In a fixed-seat rowboat, come alongside a pier and help a passenger into the boat. Pull away from the pier, change positions with your passenger, and demonstrate sculling over the stern or side. Resume your rowing position, return alongside the pier, and help your passenger out of the boat. (b) In a sliding-seat rowboat, come alongside a pier and, with your buddy assisting you, get out onto the pier. Help your buddy into the boat. Reverse roles with your buddy and repeat the procedure.

### Req 2: Scouting America Swimmer Classification Test
**Official Requirement 2:** Before completing requirements 4, 5, 6, and 7, successfully complete the Scouting America swimmer test. Note: See the Swimming merit badge pamphlet for details about the Scouting America swimmer test.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Rowing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 16)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Conduct all swim tests under the direct visual observation of certified lifeguards equipped with rescue tubes.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does the swim test require elementary backstroke following seventy-five yards of strenuous forward strokes?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Continuous Swim Distance: Complete one hundred yards continuously with seventy-five yards using strong forward strokes.
  - Resting Backstroke Stage: Finish the final twenty-five yards using an easy, restful elementary backstroke.
  - Jump feet first into deep water and submerge before starting the swim.
  - Maintain a stationary floating or resting position for sixty seconds without forward movement.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Feet First Jump: Plunge feet first into water over head height and surface smoothly.
  - Step 2 - Forward Propulsion: Swim seventy-five yards continuously using an effective side, breast, or crawl stroke.
  - Step 3 - Backstroke Recovery: Complete the final twenty-five yards using a relaxed and restful elementary backstroke.
  - Step 4 - Stationary Float: Rest motionlessly on your back for one full minute without paddling feet.
- **Common Myth:** Rowers do not need the swimmer classification test because life jackets keep everyone afloat indefinitely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Strong swimming skills provide the essential physical coordination and panic resistance required to survive unexpected deep-water immersions.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Rowing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 18)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 3 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never wear a life jacket with damaged foam, torn fabric, broken buckles, or missing adjustment straps.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do bulky offshore life jackets hinder rowing mechanics compared to low-profile paddling vests?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Type Three Approval: Choose inherently buoyant vest designs that permit unrestricted arm and shoulder motion.
  - Strap Tensioning Sequence: Tighten lower torso straps first before snugging shoulder adjusters to anchor foam.
  - Pull upward on vest shoulder panels to confirm the neckline stays below ears.
  - Permanent Wear Rule: Fasten the life jacket securely before stepping off docks into any rowboat.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Label Examination: Check the manufacturer label for United States Coast Guard approval and user weight.
  - Step 2 - Vest Loosening: Loosen all adjustment webbing before placing your arms through the armholes properly.
  - Step 3 - Buckle Fastening: Close the front zipper and buckle all torso straps starting from waist level.
  - Step 4 - Snugness Check: Have a buddy pull upward on shoulder straps to test proper torso retention.
- **Common Myth:** Stowing a certified life jacket under the rower seat satisfies all boating safety regulations.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Federal standards and Scouting America policy require life jackets to be worn continuously while underway.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Fixed Seat Rowboat Launching Procedure: Before boarding, the boat should be floating clear of the bottom while the rower steps squarely onto the centerline along the keel rather than onto a gunwale or thwart.   - Step 2 - Straight Rowing: Pull rhythmic power strokes for one hundred yards maintaining a straight compass track.
- **Common Myth:** Stepping onto the nearest thwart or gunwale provides the most stable footing when climbing aboard a floating rowboat.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Rowers must step directly onto the centerline of the hull floor along the keel while keeping their center of gravity low to avoid tipping the craft.
