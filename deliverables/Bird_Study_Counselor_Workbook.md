### Req 2: Bird Topography and Feather Anatomy
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Show that you are familiar with the terms used to describe birds by doing the following: (a) Sketch or trace a perched bird and then label 15 different parts of the bird. (b) Sketch or trace an extended wing and label six types of wing feathers.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Bird Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 18, p. 19)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Bird Topography and Feather Anatomy discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** All feathers on a bird's extended wing are identical in shape and serve the exact same aerodynamic purpose.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** An extended wing has six distinct groups of feathers, including primaries for forward thrust and secondaries for lift, each with a specialized shape and role (p. 18).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- European House Sparrow Releases: In an attempt to control insects that damage crops, people released house sparrows from Europe in many parts of the United States between 1850 and 1900 (p. 18).
- Competition With Native Cavities: Instead of controlling insect pests, these small, drab birds made life difficult for native cavity-nesting species (p. 18).
- Preference for Human Settlements: Like starlings, they do best around humans and are not found in deep forest or wilderness areas (p. 18).
- These birds are native to the western United States and Mexico (p. 18).

### Req 3: Selecting and Focusing Birding Optics
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Demonstrate that you know how to properly use and care for binoculars, a spotting scope, or a monocular. (a) Explain what the specification numbers mean on binoculars, a spotting scope, or a monocular. (b) Show how to adjust the eyepiece and how to focus for proper viewing. (c) Show how to properly care for and clean the lenses. (d) Describe when and where each type of viewing device would be most effective.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Bird Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 45, p. 47)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Selecting and Focusing Birding Optics discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** In 8x42 binoculars, the second number indicates the maximum distance in yards at which you can clearly identify a bird.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** In 8x42 binoculars, the first number is the 8-power magnification and the second number is the 42-millimeter diameter of the front objective lenses (p. 45).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Because each eye differs in its strength, you can adjust one of the ocular lenses (usually the right one) to match (p. 45).
- Hold the binoculars to your eyes, but use one hand to cover the side with the adjustable lens (p. 45).
- Then use the center focus knob to make the view through the other side clear (p. 45).
- Diopter Ring Fine Tuning: Take away your hand, and slowly turn the ring around the adjustable lens until there is no more blurriness and your eyes feel comfortable, with no strain (p. 45).

#### Req 3a: Optic Specification Numbers

### Req 4: Field Guides and Range Maps
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Demonstrate that you know how to use a bird field guide. Show your counselor that you are able to understand a range map by locating in the book and pointing out the wintering range, the breeding range, and/or the year-round range of one species of each of the following types of birds: (a) Seabird (b) Plover (c) Falcon or hawk (d) Warbler or vireo (e) Heron or egret (f) Sparrow
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Bird Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 48)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Bird Study Requirement 4: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying field guides and range maps, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Every adult bird of a given species looks identical to the single bright illustration shown in a pocket guide all year long.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Males, females, and juveniles of the same species often wear different colors, and many birds molt into duller plumage outside the breeding season (p. 10).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Visual Clues for Identification: This is an important clue as you invaluable visual, try to figure out what kind of bird you are watching (p. 10).
- Migration Corridor Range Maps: Some guides also show while in the field. the areas where the bird is found during migration (p. 10).
- Age and Sex Plumages: A good field guide will show the different plum ages of each bird, because the males, females, and immature individuals of many species wear different colors (p. 10).
- Seasonal Plumage Variations: Some species also change their appearance from season to season (p. 10).
