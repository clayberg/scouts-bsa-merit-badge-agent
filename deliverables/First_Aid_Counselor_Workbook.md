## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 15 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Visit an emergency medical station house or training center in person. From the medical first responders that you meet during your visit, learn about how they serve their community and about their careers. Discuss with your counselor what you learned during your tour and interviews. (b) Interview an emergency medical services professional about their work. Learn about how they chose this career and about their duties. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career. (c) Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge in emergency medical services. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities, and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career. (d) Identify how you might use the skills and knowledge in the field of emergency medical services to pursue a personal hobby and/or healthy lifestyle. Research the additional training required, expenses, and affiliation with organizations that would help you maximize the enjoyment and benefit you might gain from it. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and share what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.

## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 1:** Scout brings signed Scoutbook rank verification for Tenderfoot/Second/First Class first aid and walks through the 4-tier triage decision tree.
- [ ] **Station Req 2:** Scout presents their physical personal first-aid kit for inspection and identifies the purpose of every item in the personal and troop kits.
- [ ] **Station Req 3:** Scout explains signs, prevention, and first aid for closed wounds, 1st/2nd/3rd-degree burns, frostbite, blisters, punctures, poisonous plants, and bites/stings.
- [ ] **Station Req 4:** Scout demonstrates nosebleed care, mild wound dressing, direct pressure bandage, and simulated windlass tourniquet placement (without tightening).
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Scout demonstrates 5-and-5 choking rescue positioning, explains asthma inhaler and epinephrine auto-injector use, and describes care for inhalation injuries and altitude sickness.
- [ ] **Station Req 6:** Scout demonstrates placing a breathing unresponsive patient into the recovery position and explains first aid and prevention for all 7 Req 6 conditions (6a–6g).
- [ ] **Station Req 7:** Scout completes at least 2 full cycles of 30:2 CPR and attaches AED training pads accurately on a CPR manikin.
- [ ] **Station Req 8:** Scout applies a padded forearm or lower-leg splint plus sling/swath on a buddy and performs pre- and post-splint CMS checks.
- [ ] **Station Req 9:** Scout explains head/neck/back injury signs and concussion care, demonstrates a triangular head bandage for an open scalp wound, and demonstrates manual in-line spinal stabilization.
- [ ] **Station Req 10:** Scout demonstrates smoke-room extrication drag, 25-yard sprained-ankle transport (solo and two-rescuer), and 25-yard improvised stretcher carry.
- [ ] **Station Req 11:** Scout distinguishes Heat Exhaustion from Heat Stroke across all 4 clinical criteria and explains hypothermia prevention and rewarming.
- [ ] **Station Req 13:** Scout explains signs, prevention, and first aid for eye objects, knocked-out teeth, food poisoning, acute abdominal pain, and stroke (FAST).

### Req 1: Scene Assessment and Emergency Response
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Handling a First Aid Emergency. Do the following: (a) Explain the steps necessary to assess and handle a first aid emergency, including a safety evaluation of the scene. (b) Tell how you would obtain emergency medical assistance from your home and from a remote location on a wilderness camping trip. (c) Demonstrate the precautions you must take to reduce the risk of transmitting an infection between you and the victim while administering first aid, including the safe disposal of used first aid supplies. (d) Demonstrate evaluation of and management of a patient's airway and breathing. (e) Demonstrate a thorough examination of an accident victim. (f) Discuss why shock is an emergency. (g) Define the term triage and describe examples of triage situations that you may encounter.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America First Aid Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 21)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scene Safety & Universal Precautions (Req 1): Size up scene hazards before approaching any victim, wear nonlatex barrier gloves when body fluids may be present, and follow BSA Two-Deep Leadership.
- **Common Myth:** You should immediately move an injured person to a comfortable seat before checking their injuries.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must never move an accident victim unless an immediate hazard threatens their safety or yours, because movement can worsen spinal or internal injuries (p. 19).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hazardous Scene Conditions: How to Handle an Emergency Sometimes a victim's location threatens his or her safety and that of first aiders (p. 19).
- Coordinated Group Lifting: To do this, get the help of others in your group and lift the victim in the same position as found (p. 19).
- Emergency Movement Restrictions: Do not move the victim unless you absolutely have to for safety reasons (p. 19).
- Hidden Disease Risks: It is impossible to know for sure if the victim has any diseases (p. 19).

### Req 13: Miscellaneous Conditions: Eye, Tooth, GI & Stroke (FAST)
**Official Requirement 13 (Parent Overview):** Miscellaneous Conditions. **Source:** *Official Scouting America First Aid Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 63, p. 80)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scene Safety & Universal Precautions (Req 13): Size up scene hazards before approaching any victim, wear nonlatex barrier gloves when body fluids may be present, and follow BSA Two-Deep Leadership.
- **Common Myth:** When a permanent tooth is knocked out, you should scrub the root clean with soap and wrap it in a dry paper towel.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Handle a knocked-out tooth only by the crown, never scrub the root, and keep it moist in milk, saline, or clean water while getting dental care immediately (p. 80).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Critical Medical Escalation: Cold- and Heat-Related Conditions and Injuries Heatstroke Heatstroke, much more serious than heat exhaustion, can lead to death if not treated immediately (p. 63).
- Progressive Symptom Worsening: Left untreated, heat exhaustion can develop into heatstroke (p. 63).
- In heatstroke, the body's cooling system begins to fail and the person's core temperature rises to life-threatening levels (above 105 degrees) (p. 63).
- Exertion and Dehydration: One type of heatstroke develops in young, healthy people from dehydration and over exertion in hot weather, especially in high humidity (p. 63).
