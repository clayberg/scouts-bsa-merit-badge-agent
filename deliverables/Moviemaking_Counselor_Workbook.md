### Req 1: Cinematic Visual Storytelling Fundamentals
**Official Requirement 1:** Discuss and demonstrate the proper elements of a good motion picture. In your discussion, include visual storytelling, rhythm, the 180-axis rule, camera movement, framing and composition of camera shots, and lens selection.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 46)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 1 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does crossing the imaginary line of action confuse an audience about character positions?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Axis Line Preservation: Positioning cameras on one side of action maintains consistent screen direction across cuts.
  - Rule Of Thirds Framing: Aligning subjects along intersecting grid lines creates balanced tension and natural visual interest.
  - Focal Length Selection: Wide lenses expand physical space while telephoto glass compresses distances between background elements.
  - Editing Pace Rhythm: Cutting speed dictates narrative energy by matching shot duration to dramatic emotional tension.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Axis Line Setup: Establish an imaginary line between subjects to lock legal camera shooting angles.
  - Step 2 - Shot Framing Selection: Compose wide establishing views before capturing medium dialogue coverage and tight closeups.
  - Step 3 - Tripod Camera Movement: Execute steady pan and tilt adjustments using smooth mechanical drag resistance settings.
  - Step 4 - Narrative Rhythm Review: Trim transition points to ensure pacing flows without disruptive visual jumps between takes.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often think moving the camera constantly makes a scene feel dynamic and professional.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Stable static shots provide clarity, while camera movement must serve a specific narrative purpose.

#### Req 2c(2): Horizontal Camera Panning Technique
**[Parent Directive]:** *Demonstrate the following motion picture shooting techniques*
**Official Requirement 2c(2):** Panning a camera (3) Framing a shot (4) Selecting an angle (5) Selecting proper lighting (6) Handheld shooting.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 45)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2c
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why should you rehearse a panning motion and hold the camera still at the start and finish before calling cut?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Execute a smooth horizontal pan by rotating your upper torso from the hips while keeping your feet planted in the direction where the pan will end. Always hold a static shot for several seconds before starting the pan and after completing the movement to give editors clean cut points (p. 29).
- **Common Myth:** Rapid panning across a wide scene captures more visual information and keeps viewers energized.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Fast panning creates disorienting motion blur and rolling shutter distortion that prevents viewers from seeing key visual details.

#### Req 2c(3): Cinematic Shot Framing and Composition
**[Parent Directive]:** *Demonstrate the following motion picture shooting techniques*
**Official Requirement 2c(3):** Framing a shot (4) Selecting an angle (5) Selecting proper lighting (6) Handheld shooting.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 45)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2c
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** If a Scout is walking from left to right across your shot, where should you place them in the frame to make the movement look natural?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Cinematic Shot Framing and Composition: Compose shots using the rule of thirds, positioning subjects along grid lines and leaving adequate headroom above the talent. Provide lead room in front of moving or looking subjects so they do not appear trapped against the edge of the frame (p. 32).
- **Common Myth:** Centering every subject directly in the middle of the frame creates the most balanced and natural composition.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Off-center framing guided by the rule of thirds creates visual energy and leaves natural space for eye gaze or physical motion.

#### Req 2c(5): Scene Lighting Selection and Control
**[Parent Directive]:** *Demonstrate the following motion picture shooting techniques*
**Official Requirement 2c(5):** Selecting proper lighting (6) Handheld shooting.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 45)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2c
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What happens to your subject's face when the brightest light source in the room is positioned behind them instead of in front?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Scene Lighting Selection and Control: Establish balanced three-point lighting by positioning a dominant key light to illuminate the subject, a softer fill light to open up harsh shadows, and a backlight to separate the actor from the background. When shooting outdoors, utilize natural sunlight as the key and position reflectors or diffusers to soften harsh facial shadows (p. 39).
- **Common Myth:** Placing a bright light source directly behind a subject produces clear facial details.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Backlighting without adequate front fill turns the subject into a dark silhouette against an overexposed background.

#### Req 2c(6): Handheld Camera Movement and Stabilization
**[Parent Directive]:** *Demonstrate the following motion picture shooting techniques*
**Official Requirement 2c(6):** Handheld shooting.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 45)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2c
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How can you use your own body stance and footwork to act as a physical shock absorber when filming while moving?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Handheld Camera Movement and Stabilization: Tuck your elbows tightly against your ribcage, grasp the camera firmly with both hands, and keep your knees slightly bent to absorb walking shocks like a natural shock absorber. Move smoothly using a heel-to-toe walking cadence while breathing steadily to minimize vertical camera bounce (p. 43).
- **Common Myth:** Electronic image stabilization removes all camera shake regardless of how casually the camera operator walks or holds the device.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Digital stabilization cannot eliminate severe physical vibrations or erratic body shifts without causing warping and motion artifacts.
