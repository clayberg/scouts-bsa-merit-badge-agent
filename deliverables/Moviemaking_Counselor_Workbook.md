### Req 1: Visual Storytelling and Camera Composition
**Official Requirement 1:** Discuss and demonstrate the proper elements of a good motion picture. In your discussion, include visual storytelling, rhythm, the 180-axis rule, camera movement, framing and composition of camera shots, and lens selection.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 46)* • **Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 1 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist (Discuss and Demonstrate The Proper)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Visual Storytelling and Camera Composition discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** The 180-axis rule means the camera operator must rotate the camera in a complete half-circle during every dialogue scene.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The 180-axis rule establishes an imaginary line between two interacting subjects and keeps all camera setups on one side of that line so screen direction stays consistent (p. 36).

### Req 3: Studio Production Sets and Zoom Lenses
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) With your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, visit a film set or television production studio and watch how production work is done. (b) Explain to your counselor the elements of the zoom lens and three important parts.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 25)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Conduct all counselor discussions, community interviews, and research for Moviemaking Requirement 3 on studio production sets and zoom lenses under BSA Youth Protection two-deep leadership, copying a parent or guardian on any external correspondence.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Moviemaking Req 3 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Zooming in with a lens from across the room produces the exact same visual perspective as moving the camera physically closer to the subject.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A zoom lens changes focal length optically and compresses background depth, whereas moving the camera physically closer alters the spatial perspective between foreground and background (p. 25).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- On-Camera Interview Techniques: Heibert offers the following tips about interviewing subjects on camera (p. 23).
- Coordinated Production Crew Teamwork: Good Planning Ensures Success on the Set Good movie making requires working with a team (p. 23).
- Synchronized Set Logistics: Many elements are planned to come together on the same day and at the same time (p. 23).
- Preplanned Shooting Schedules: It is always best to have a preplanned shooting schedule in place to help minimize any surprises (p. 23).

#### Req 3b: Zoom Lens Optical Elements
**Official Requirement 3b:** Explain to your counselor the elements of the zoom lens and three important parts.
