### Req 1: Cinematic Visual Storytelling Fundamentals
**Official Requirement 1:** Discuss and demonstrate the proper elements of a good motion picture. In your discussion, include visual storytelling, rhythm, the 180-axis rule, camera movement, framing and composition of camera shots, and lens selection.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 46)* • **Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 1 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist (Discuss and Demonstrate The Proper)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Route all audio and camera cables along floor perimeters and secure them flat with gaffer tape to eliminate tripping hazards.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often think moving the camera constantly makes a scene feel dynamic and professional.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Stable static shots provide clarity, while camera movement must serve a specific narrative purpose.

### Req 2: Preproduction Planning And Production Execution
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) In a three- or four-paragraph treatment, tell the story you plan to produce, making sure that the treatment conveys a visual picture. (b) Prepare a storyboard for your motion picture. Note: This can be done with rough sketches and stick figures. (c) Demonstrate the following motion picture shooting techniques: (1) Using a tripod (2) Panning a camera (3) Framing a shot (4) Selecting an angle (5) Selecting proper lighting (6) Handheld shooting (d) Using motion picture shooting techniques, plan ONE of the following programs. Start with a treatment and complete the requirement by presenting this program to a pack or your troop, patrol, or class. (1) Film a court of honor and show it to an audience. (2) Create a short feature of your own design, using the techniques you learned. (3) Shoot a vignette that could be used to train a new Scout in a Scouting skill.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 45)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Place twenty-pound sandbags over all lighting stand legs and wear leather gloves when adjusting hot Fresnel barn doors.
- **Common Myth:** A common misunderstanding is that storyboards require master artistic drawing skills to be useful.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Simple stick figures and directional arrows communicate framing and shot transitions clearly to crew members.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Written Story Treatment: Narrative prose outlines character motives and visual story progression without technical script cues.
- Storyboard Sequence Mapping: Graphic panels map camera compositions and planned character movements before set construction begins.
- Three Point Lighting Setup: Balancing key, fill, and backlight sources separates subjects cleanly from background scenery.
- Dedicated Audio Capture: Placing directional microphones close to dialogue sources eliminates muddy room echo artifacts.

### Req 3: Studio Exploration And Optics
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) With your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, visit a film set or television production studio and watch how production work is done. (b) Explain to your counselor the elements of the zoom lens and three important parts.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Moviemaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 25)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow designated studio walkway lines, stay clear of overhead lighting grids, and yield immediately to rolling camera dollies.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Moviemaking Req 3 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** People frequently believe zooming the lens produces the exact visual perspective change as physically tracking closer.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Zooming merely enlarges center pixels optically, whereas moving the camera physically alters spatial perspective and parallax.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Production Set Protocol: Studio environments operate under strict safety guidelines, departmental hierarchy, and structured call sheets.
- Optical Focal Variation: Internal lens elements shift along helicoid tracks to adjust framing without moving stands.
- Iris Aperture Control: Adjustable mechanical blades regulate light exposure levels and govern foreground depth of field.
- Focus Ring Calibration: Rotating the outer barrel aligns optical elements for crisp sharpness on chosen subjects.
