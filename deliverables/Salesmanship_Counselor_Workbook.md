## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Explain):** Explain why it is important for a salesperson to do the following: (a) Research the market to be sure the product or service meets the needs of customers. (b) Learn all about the product to be sold. (c) If possible, visit the location where the product is built and learn how it is constructed. If a service is being sold, learn about the benefits of the service to the customer. (d) Follow up with customers after their purchase to confirm their satisfaction and discuss their concerns about the product.
- [ ] **Req 5 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following and keep a record (cost sheet). Use the sales techniques you have learned, and share your experience with your counselor: (a) Help your unit raise funds through sales of merchandise or of tickets to a Scout event. (b) Sell your services, such as lawn raking or mowing, pet sitting, dog walking, snow shoveling, or car washing, to your neighbors. Follow up after completing the service to determine the customer satisfaction. (c) Earn money through retail selling.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Discuss):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Interview a salesperson and learn the following: (1) What made the person choose sales as a profession? (2) What are the most important things to remember when talking to customers? (3) How is the product sold? (4) Include your own questions. (b) Interview a retail store owner and learn the following: (1) How often is the owner approached by a sales representative? (2) What good traits should a sales representative have? What habits should the sales representative avoid? (3) What does the owner consider when deciding whether to establish an account with a sales representative? (4) Include at least two of your own questions.

### Req 1: Sales Professional Economic Responsibilities
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the responsibilities of a salesperson and how a salesperson serves customers and helps stimulate the economy. (b) Explain the differences between a business-to-business salesperson and a consumer salesperson.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Salesmanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 18, p. 20)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scouts conducting field sales research must travel in buddy pairs and avoid entering unfamiliar private commercial loading docks.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe salespeople manipulate unwilling consumers into buying unwanted merchandise.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Professional salespeople act as consultants who identify authentic customer needs and provide viable solutions.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Economic Exchange Cycle: Professional sales transactions move finished manufactured goods to commercial end users efficiently.
- Customer Problem Resolution: Ethical salespeople diagnose operational challenges and recommend tailored commercial merchandise solutions.
- Market Information Channel: Frontline sellers report customer buying patterns directly back to corporate manufacturing teams.
- Commerce Expansion Factor: Consistent sales revenue provides working capital for corporate payroll and research operations.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Salesmanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 42, p. 43)* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** When researching sales careers online or corresponding with recruiters, safeguard personal identity data and never share financial information.
- **Common Myth:** Sales roles require no formal education or specialized analytical training beyond basic conversational ability.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Modern enterprise sales positions often require university degrees in business, communications, or engineering, plus CRM certification.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Qualification Inventory Drafting: Compiling school coursework, leadership offices, and merit badges highlights core career competencies.
- Educational Foundation Review: Business administration, communications, and psychology courses provide strong foundational preparation for sales.
- Professional Development Seminars: Corporate sales training programs teach advanced negotiation frameworks, CRM software, and compliance.
- Industry Specialization Analysis: Technical sales positions often demand specialized engineering degrees alongside interpersonal presentation capabilities.
