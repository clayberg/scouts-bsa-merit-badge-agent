> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.

### Req 2: Prerequisite Swimming Endurance and Strokes
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Before doing requirements 3 through 15: (a) Earn the Swimming merit badge. (b) Swim continuously for 400 yards using each of the following strokes in a strong manner, in good form with rhythmic breathing, for at least 50 continuous yards: front crawl, sidestroke, breaststroke, and elementary backstroke.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 57, p. 63)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Lifesaving Requirement 2: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for prerequisite swimming endurance and strokes, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Any swimmer who can sprint 100 yards using the front crawl has the complete stroke repertoire needed for water rescues.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A rescuer must master four distinct strokes over a continuous 400-yard swim because approaches and tows require different body positions and kicks (p. 63).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Victim Self-Exit Capability: Conscious victims probably can remove themselves from the water once they have sure footing on the bottom or a firm hold on a ladder (p. 57).
- Choose a safe landing site before entering the water (p. 57).
- Steep Shoreline Exit Assistance: In exceptional cases, such as in a steep-sided quarry, you may both need help from others onshore (p. 57).
- Water Depth Footing Check: When landing a conscious victim, lower your legs to check the water depth if you can do so without causing the victim to lose support (p. 57).

### Req 3: Drowning Recognition and Rescue Order
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Explain the following: (a) Common drowning situations and how to avoid them, such as unfenced residential pools, drop offs, river currents, rip currents, and medical emergencies. (b) How to identify persons in the water who need assistance. (c) The order of methods in water rescue. (d) How rescue techniques vary depending on the setting and the condition of the person needing assistance. (e) Situations for which in-water rescues should not be undertaken.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 59)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Lifesaving Requirement 3: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for drowning recognition and rescue order, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Drowning victims always wave their arms above their heads and shout loudly for help before submerging.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Active drowning victims cannot call out for help or wave because their mouths sink below the surface and their arms press down instinctively to stay afloat (p. 19).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- In-water contact rescues of active victims can be dangerous (p. 19).
- Double drowning incidents can easily be found in news reports documenting failed rescue attempts when a poor swimmer attempted to wrestle a victim (p. 19).
- Value of Flotation Aids: Those events could often have been avoided had a good swimmer responded with a flotation aid (p. 19).
- Mandatory Buoyant Aid Use: If you must enter the water to help such a person, take a buoyant aid (p. 19).

### Req 4: Shore and Deck Reaching Rescues
**Official Requirement 4:** Demonstrate "reaching" rescues using various items such as arm, leg, towels, shirts, paddles, poles.

### Req 16: Rescue Breathing and CPR Procedures
**Official Requirement 16 (Parent Overview):** Demonstrate knowledge of resuscitation procedure: (a) Describe how to recognize the need for rescue breathing and CPR. (b) Demonstrate CPR knowledge and skills, including rescue breathing, on a mannequin under the guidance of a current CPR/AED instructor trained by a nationally certified provider.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 79, p. 83)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Lifesaving Requirement 16: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for rescue breathing and cpr procedures, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Before starting CPR on a drowning victim, you must press on their stomach or perform abdominal thrusts to pump swallowed water out of their lungs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Do not attempt to drain water from the victim's stomach with abdominal thrusts before resuscitation because it delays oxygen delivery and causes vomiting that can block the airway (p. 79).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Immediate Ventilation Priority: Resuscitation and First Aid for Water Rescue It is important to provide ventilations as soon as possible to a person who is not breathing (p. 79).
- Firm Surface for Compressions: Because compress ions may also be needed, and those can only be provided on a firm surface, you should typically remove a person from the water (p. 79).
- In-Water Ventilation Exceptions: However, if you cannot remove the person quickly, it is sometimes effective to provide in-water ventilations (p. 79).
- There are additional first-aid concerns with water rescue other than spinal injuries and respiratory and cardiac arrest from drowning (p. 79).

#### Req 16a: Recognizing Resuscitation Needs
**Official Requirement 16a:** Describe how to recognize the need for rescue breathing and CPR.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 79, p. 83)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 16a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Recognize The Need for Rescue)
- **Common Myth:** Compression-only (hands-only) CPR is ideal for drowning victims because their blood already has plenty of oxygen.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Drowning causes cardiac arrest through oxygen deprivation (hypoxia), so rescue breathing combined with chest compressions is critical for drowning victims (p. 79).

### Req 17: Aquatics First Aid and Illnesses
**Official Requirement 17:** With your counselor, discuss causes, prevention, and treatment of other injuries or illnesses that could occur while swimming or boating, including hypothermia, dehydration, heat-related illnesses, muscle cramps, sunburn, stings, and hyperventilation.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 82, p. 83)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 17 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss Causes Prevention and Treatment)

- **Common Myth:** It is impossible for a swimmer to become dehydrated or suffer heat exhaustion while spending the afternoon in cool lake water.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Swimmers and boaters lose significant fluids through exertion and sun exposure without noticing sweat, making regular hydration and shade breaks essential (p. 82).
