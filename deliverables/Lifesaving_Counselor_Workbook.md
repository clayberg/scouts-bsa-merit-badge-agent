### Req 1: Safe Swim Defense Principles
**Official Requirement 1:** Before doing requirements 3 through 15, review with your counselor the principles of Safe Swim Defense.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 25, p. 27)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never conduct open water swimming activities without a designated adult supervisor present on shore.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does adult leadership remain necessary even when municipal lifeguards supervise the facility?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Qualified Leadership: Adult supervisors must hold current certifications in Safe Swim Defense and basic CPR.
  - Maximum swimming area depth must match the verified ability of the participants.
  - Buddy System Pairing: Swimmers must remain paired and respond immediately to periodic buddy check whistles.
  - Response Team Readiness: Dedicated rescue personnel must remain alert and equipped beside active swimming zones.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Area Inspection: Check water clarity and bottom hazards across the designated swimming boundary line.
  - Step 2 - Ability Classification: Administer standard swim tests before assigning participants to distinct activity areas.
  - Step 3 - Lifeguard Positioning: Post trained rescuers equipped with rescue tubes at elevated surveillance stations.
  - Step 4 - Buddy Assembly: Conduct frequent accountability roll calls to confirm paired swimmer presence and safety.
- **Common Myth:** A certified lifeguard on duty eliminates the need for adult troop supervision.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Designated adult supervisors must hold current training in both Safe Swim Defense and CPR.

### Req 4: Reaching Assist Field Techniques
**Official Requirement 4:** Demonstrate "reaching" rescues using various items such as arm, leg, towels, shirts, paddles, poles.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 45)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 4 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Always maintain a broad, low base of support to avoid being dragged into the water.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must a rescuer lie prone on a dock before extending a reaching device?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Rescuer Stance Anchoring: Rescuers must lie flat or stay low before extending any reaching aid.
  - Rigid Tool Extension: Shepherd crooks and long poles provide sturdy support for struggling swimmers.
  - Improvised Reaching Gear: Towels and clothing create extended grips when rigid poles are unavailable.
  - Targeted Aid Placement: Rescuers must place reaching devices directly into the victim grasp zone.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Body Anchoring: Drop onto knees or lie prone on the solid deck or shoreline.
  - Step 2 - Equipment Securement: Grasp the reaching aid firmly with one hand anchored behind your body.
  - Step 3 - Aid Extension: Push the pole or paddle across the water surface toward the victim.
  - Step 4 - Controlled Retrieval: Pull the grasping subject slowly to the safety edge using smooth motions.
- **Common Myth:** Standing upright provides maximum balance when reaching toward a struggling swimmer from shore.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Anchoring rescuer body weight prevents the victim from pulling the rescuer into deep water.

**Gear & Readiness Checklist:**
- [ ] Patrol and personal first-aid kits (sterile gauze pads, roller bandages, triangular cravats, adhesive tape).
- [ ] Non-latex nitrile exam gloves, CPR breathing barrier mask, and trauma shears.
- [ ] SAM splint or improvised rigid splinting material, elastic wrap, and moleskin for blister care.
- [ ] Emergency action plan card, simulated triage tags, and waterproof notepad.

### Req 17: Aquatic Illness and Injury Management
**Official Requirement 17:** With your counselor, discuss causes, prevention, and treatment of other injuries or illnesses that could occur while swimming or boating, including hypothermia, dehydration, heat-related illnesses, muscle cramps, sunburn, stings, and hyperventilation.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Lifesaving Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 82, p. 83)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 17 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never allow swimmers to practice breath holding contests or hyperventilation before diving.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does hyperventilation cause sudden underwater blackout without warning suffocation sensations?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Hypothermia Rewarming Protocol: Remove wet clothing and insulate core body areas with dry warm blankets.
  - Dehydration Prevention Discipline: Drink water frequently during aquatic outings to prevent debilitating muscle cramps.
  - Heat Exhaustion Recognition: Move pale dizzy victims with clammy skin into cool shaded rest environments.
  - Hyperventilation Blackout Hazard: Avoid repeated deep breathing before underwater swims to prevent sudden blackout.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Hazard Assessment: Identify physical signs of shivering, pale skin, cramping, or severe heat exhaustion.
  - Step 2 - Environment Removal: Remove the casualty from cold water, hot sun, or marine sting hazards.
  - Step 3 - Supportive Care: Provide dry clothing, shade, electrolyte fluids, or marine sting rinses as appropriate.
  - Step 4 - Medical Escalation: Monitor airway stability and transport deteriorating patients to professional emergency facilities.
- **Common Myth:** Drinking hot coffee or rubbing frostbitten skin rapidly warms hypothermic aquatic casualties.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Shivering stops in severe hypothermia, indicating critical failure of autonomic temperature regulation.
