## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 7 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's permission, go to www. Geocaching.com. Type in your city and state to locate public geocaches in your area. Share with your counselor the posted information about three of those geocaches. Then, pick one of the three and find the cache. Note: To fulfill this requirement, you will need to set up a free user account with www. Geocaching.com. Before doing so, ask your parent or guardian for permission and help.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) If a Cache to Eagle® series exists in your council, visit at least three of the locations in the series. Describe the projects that each cache you visit highlights, and explain how the Cache to Eagle® program helps share our Scouting service with the public. (b) Create a Scouting-related Travel Bug® that promotes one of the values of Scouting. Release your Travel Bug into a public geocache and, with your parent or guardian's permission, monitor its progress at www. Geocaching.com for 30 days. Keep a log, and share this with your counselor at the end of the 30-day period. (c) Set up and hide a public geocache, following the guidelines in the Geocaching merit badge pamphlet. Before doing so, share with your counselor a three-month maintenance plan for the geocache where you are personally responsible for those three months. After setting up the geocache, with your parent or guardian's permission, follow the logs online for 30 days and share them with your counselor. You must archive the geocache when you are no longer maintaining it. (d) Explain what Cache In Trash Out (CITO) means, and describe how you have practiced CITO at public geocaches or at a CITO event. Then, either create CITO containers to leave at public caches, or host a CITO event for your unit or for the public.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - A waypoint records precise geographic coordinates marking a specific physical target location.
  - Difficulty ratings measure mental puzzle complexity and container camouflage ingenuity on site.
  - Terrain numbers reflect physical elevation changes, ground vegetation thickness, and accessibility obstacles.
  - Trackable Item Movement: Trackables carry unique serial codes allowing public tracking across sequential container locations.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Waypoint Entry: Enter target latitude and longitude coordinates into the handheld navigation receiver database.
  - Step 2 - Attribute Review: Check listing attributes for warnings regarding poison oak, thorns, or seasonal flooding.
  - Step 3 - Physical Log Signing: Sign the physical paper log sheet using permanent waterproof ink upon discovery.
  - Step 4 - Online Log Update: Log the successful find online with field notes regarding container condition details.
- **Common Myth:** Difficulty and terrain ratings measure the exact same physical hiking challenges.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Difficulty reflects mental hiding cunning while terrain measures physical trail roughness and elevation.

### Req 4: Satellite Navigation Principles and Operations
**Official Requirement 4:** Explain how the Global Positioning System (GPS) works. Then, using Scouting's EDGE, demonstrate to your counselor the use of a GPS unit. Include marking and editing a waypoint, changing field functions, and changing the coordinate system in the unit.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Geocaching Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 13)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 4 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does a receiver require signals from at least four satellites for 3D navigation?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Satellite Constellation Geometry: Twenty-four active satellites orbit Earth transmitting continuous radio timing signals to receivers.
  - Trilateration Positioning Methods: Receivers calculate distance from four distinct satellites to establish accurate three-dimensional fixes.
  - The World Geodetic System 1984 datum provides global standardized coordinate alignment.
  - Waypoint Management Skills: Users save current location coordinates into memory and edit labels for retrieval.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Explain Concept: Explain satellite trilateration and radio signal timing to patrol members using diagrams.
  - Step 2 - Demonstrate Function: Demonstrate marking an active waypoint and editing its displayed name and icon.
  - Step 3 - Guide Practice: Guide the learner through switching coordinate formats to degrees and decimal minutes.
  - Step 4 - Enable Execution: Observe the Scout independently navigating toward a designated test waypoint coordinate target.
- **Common Myth:** Handheld GPS receivers transmit continuous high-power radio signals directly to military satellites.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Consumer GPS units are passive receivers that only detect incoming radio signals from orbit.
