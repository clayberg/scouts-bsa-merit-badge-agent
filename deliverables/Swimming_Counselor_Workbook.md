### Req 2: Swimmer Classification Practical Evaluation
**Official Requirement 2:** Before doing the following requirements, successfully complete the Scouting America swimmer test, found in the Swimming merit badge pamphlet.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 18)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Station a lifeguard with a rescue tube directly alongside test lanes during evaluations.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must a swimmer test evaluate both forward propulsive power and resting strokes?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Continuous Distance: Candidates must complete one hundred yards continuously without touching bottom or resting.
  - Stroke Diversity: Seventy five yards require a strong forward stroke followed by resting backstroke.
  - Swimmers must demonstrate an unassisted resting float immediately upon completing stroke laps.
  - Water Entry: The test begins with a purposeful jump into water over the head.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Feetfirst Entry: Jump feetfirst into water over the head and level off forward smoothly.
  - Step 2 - Stroke Execution: Swim seventy five yards using a strong, rhythmic front crawl or breaststroke.
  - Step 3 - Backstroke Transition: Turn smoothly onto the back and swim twenty five yards using elementary backstroke.
  - Step 4 - Stationary Float: Rest motionlessly on the water surface for several seconds before exiting calmly.
- **Common Myth:** Treading water aggressively satisfies the stationary floating requirement of the swimmer test.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The test requires remaining motionless in a floating position with minimal limb movement.

  - The front crawl utilizes alternate arm turnover, continuous flutter kicking, and rotary breathing to maintain high speed. Swimmers rotate along the longitudinal axis to facilitate high elbow recovery.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Rotary breathing requires turning the face sideways into the bow wave trough.

### Req 4: Continuous Multi-Stroke Distance Endurance
**Official Requirement 4:** Swim continuously for 150 yards in a strong manner using each of the following strokes in any order; front crawl or trudgen (25 yards); back crawl (25 yards); sidestroke (25 yards); breaststroke (25 yards); and elementary backstroke (50 yards).
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 53)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Immediately summon the lifeguard if a swimmer exhibits labored breathing or failing arm turnover.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the strategic order of strokes help a swimmer manage physical fatigue?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Continuous Laps: Swimmers complete six pool lengths continuously without resting or grabbing poolside gutters.
  - Stroke Transitions: Transition smoothly between individual strokes while maintaining steady momentum and rhythmic breathing.
  - Pacing Strategy: Distribute metabolic energy evenly to finish the fifty yard elementary backstroke strongly.
  - Maintain horizontal streamlined body position across every stroke to minimize hydrodynamic drag.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Distance Pacing: Start with twenty five yards of rhythmic front crawl at moderate intensity.
  - Step 2 - Supine Transition: Roll smoothly onto the back and swim twenty five yards back crawl.
  - Step 3 - Lateral Propulsion: Shift into a side lying position for twenty five yards of sidestroke.
  - Step 4 - Restful Finish: Conclude with twenty five yards breaststroke and fifty yards elementary backstroke.
- **Common Myth:** Sprinting the first two lengths guarantees enough momentum to survive the entire distance.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Aggressive sprinting causes early lactic acid buildup, resulting in exhaustion before final laps.
