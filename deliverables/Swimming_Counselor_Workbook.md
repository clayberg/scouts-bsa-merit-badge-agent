### Req 1: Water Safety And Health Management
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Review with your counselor how Scouting America's Safe Swim Defense guidelines anticipate and deal with common drowning situations such as unfenced residential pools, nonswimmers entering deep water, risky behaviors, medical impairment in the water, drop-offs, cold water, murky water, river currents, rip currents, and surf. (b) Discuss the prevention and treatment of health concerns that could occur while swimming, including hypothermia, dehydration, sunburn, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, muscle cramps, hyperventilation, spinal injury, stings and bites, and cuts and scrapes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 25, p. 27)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never allow swimming activities to proceed without dedicated rescue equipment and vigilant adult lookouts.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** A strong swimmer can supervise an open water outing without adult safety training.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Safe Swim Defense requires an adult leader trained in aquatic safety procedures.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Qualified Supervision: Every activity requires an adult supervisor trained in Safe Swim Defense standards.
- Physical Fitness: Swimmers must present current health records before entering open or closed waters.
- Safe Area Boundaries: White ropes and buoys designate nonswimmer, beginner, and swimmer water depth sections.
- Lifeguard Placement: Dedicated spotters and rescue personnel monitor designated zones without personal aquatic distractions.

### Req 3: Aquatic Stroke Mechanics Demonstration
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Correctly perform the following strokes: (a) Demonstrate the front crawl or the trudgen using good form. (b) Demonstrate the back crawl using good form. (c) Demonstrate the sidestroke using good form. (d) Demonstrate the breaststroke using good form. (e) Demonstrate the elementary backstroke using good form.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 52)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Maintain lane clearance and avoid swimming directly behind lead swimmers during multi-lane stroke evaluations.
- **Common Myth:** Thrashing arms and rapid kicking produce superior forward speed across endurance swimming distances.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Efficient stroke mechanics rely on streamlined glides, controlled breathing, and deliberate propulsion.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Front Crawl Rhythm: Alternate arm pulls combine with rhythmic flutter kicking and smooth bilateral head turns.
- Back Crawl Alignment: Maintain horizontal body position with continuous high elbow recovery and rapid flutter kicks.
- Sidestroke Glide: Coordinate scissor kicks with trailing arm reaches during an extended resting glide phase.
- Breaststroke Symmetry: Execute simultaneous arm sweeps and whip kicks followed by a streamlined underwater glide.

### Req 5: Non-Swimming Aquatic Rescue Procedures
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Demonstrate water rescue methods by reaching with your arm or leg, by reaching with a suitable object, and by throwing lines and objects. Explain why swimming rescues should not be attempted when a reaching or throwing rescue is possible, and explain why and how a rescue swimmer should avoid contact with the victim. (b) With a helper and a practice victim, show a line rescue both as tender and as rescuer. The practice victim should be approximately 30 feet from shore in deep water.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 27)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never wrap rescue ropes around wrists or fingers to prevent severe line pinch injuries.
- **Common Myth:** Jumping into the water immediately demonstrates superior heroism during an active drowning emergency.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** In-water contact poses extreme danger, making shoreline reaching and throwing methods top priority.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Reach Foundation: Rescuers anchor their bodies firmly on deck before extending arms or poles.
- Aim throwing devices across victim shoulders so lines drape within immediate reach.
- Line Tender Duty: Rescuers secure coiled rope lines while tenders pull both parties ashore.
- Swimmer Risk: Panicked victims will climb onto swimming rescuers, forcing both individuals below water.

### Req 6: Water Survival And Flotation Positions
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Float faceup in a resting position for at least three minutes with minimal movement. (b) Demonstrate survival floating for at least five minutes. (c) While wearing a properly fitted U. S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket, demonstrate the HELP and huddle positions. Explain their purposes. (d) Explain why swimming or survival floating will hasten the onset of hypothermia in cold water.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 30, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Terminate cold water survival drills immediately if any Scout exhibits violent shivering or slurred speech.
- **Common Myth:** Swimming vigorously warms the body effectively when stranded in frigid open water environments.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Active swimming circulates cold water across skin, tripling core body heat loss rates.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Faceup Resting: Tilt chin back and arch back slightly to keep airway above surface.
- Survival Float: Rest facedown with relaxed limbs, raising head briefly to exhale and inhale.
- Thermal Loss: Unnecessary swimming flushes warm boundary water away from skin in cold conditions.
- Group Huddle: Ring together tightly while wearing lifejackets to conserve core body thermal energy.

**Official Requirement 7 (Parent Overview):** In water over your head, but not to exceed 10 feet, do the following: (a) Use the feetfirst method of surface diving and bring an object up from the bottom. Come to the surface, take a breath, and repeat the sequence twice.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 56, p. 58)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Prohibit repeated breath holding or hyperventilation drills during deep water surface dive training.
- **Common Myth:** Hyperventilating before deep surface dives allows swimmers to remain underwater safely much longer.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hyperventilation dangerously lowers blood carbon dioxide levels, causing sudden underwater shallow water blackout.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Feetfirst Descent: Press water upward with arms while sculling to drive feet down vertically.
- Pike Dive Technique: Bend sharply at hips, driving legs skyward to plunge head downward vertically.
- Tuck Dive Compression: Pull knees into chest, roll forward, and extend legs upward toward surface.
- Underwater Stroking: Pull with breaststroke arm sweeps while maintaining clear vision through deep water.
