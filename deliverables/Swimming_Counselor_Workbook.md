### Req 1: Safe Swim Defense and Health
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Review with your counselor how Scouting America's Safe Swim Defense guidelines anticipate and deal with common drowning situations such as unfenced residential pools, nonswimmers entering deep water, risky behaviors, medical impairment in the water, drop-offs, cold water, murky water, river currents, rip currents, and surf. (b) Discuss the prevention and treatment of health concerns that could occur while swimming, including hypothermia, dehydration, sunburn, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, muscle cramps, hyperventilation, spinal injury, stings and bites, and cuts and scrapes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 25, p. 27)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Swimming Requirement 1: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for safe swim defense and health, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Many Scouts think you only need to worry about dehydration and sunburn when hiking on dry land rather than while swimming.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Swimmers still sweat and lose fluids in the water while water reflections intensify ultraviolet exposure, requiring regular hydration and waterproof sunscreen (p. 27).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Spinal injuries can occur from diving into shallow or obstructed water (p. 25).
- Suspected Spinal Injury Care: Rescuers must be very careful when a spinal injury is suspected (p. 25).
- Spinal injuries can be fatal or can cause paralysis (p. 25).
- Dangers of Improper Handling: If the victim is not handled properly, additional serious injury could result (p. 25).

### Req 3: Five Fundamental Swimming Strokes
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Correctly perform the following strokes: (a) Demonstrate the front crawl or the trudgen using good form. (b) Demonstrate the back crawl using good form. (c) Demonstrate the sidestroke using good form. (d) Demonstrate the breaststroke using good form. (e) Demonstrate the elementary backstroke using good form.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 52)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Swimming Requirement 3: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for five fundamental swimming strokes, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Using a high-energy, bobbing competitive breaststroke will impress your counselor and easily satisfy Requirement 3d.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A vigorous, bobbing competitive breaststroke is unsatisfactory because the requirement calls for a smooth, energy-conserving stroke with a distinct glide (p. 41).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Five Stroke Proficiency Standard: To earn the Swimming merit badge you must demonstrate your swimming strength and stroke proficiency by swimming 150 yards using a combination of five strokes (p. 41).
- If your breaststroke is a vigorous, bobbing stroke as use din competition, it is not satisfactory for this requirement (p. 41).
- Doing the backstroke using a frog kick rather than a whip kick also is unacceptable for this requirement (p. 41).
- Controlled and Strong Swimming: You also are required to swim the specified strokes in a "strong manner." This means no rest stops and no gasping, panting finishes (p. 41).

#### Req 3a: Front Crawl and Trudgen
**Official Requirement 3a:** Demonstrate the front crawl or the trudgen using good form.

- **Common Myth:** The elementary backstroke and the breaststroke use an old-fashioned wide frog kick with the knees spread far apart.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Both the breaststroke and the elementary backstroke require a narrower, circular whip kick rather than an outdated frog kick (p. 41).

### Req 5: Water Rescue Methods and Line Rescues
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Demonstrate water rescue methods by reaching with your arm or leg, by reaching with a suitable object, and by throwing lines and objects. Explain why swimming rescues should not be attempted when a reaching or throwing rescue is possible, and explain why and how a rescue swimmer should avoid contact with the victim. (b) With a helper and a practice victim, show a line rescue both as tender and as rescuer. The practice victim should be approximately 30 feet from shore in deep water.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 27)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Swimming Requirement 5: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for water rescue methods and line rescues, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** When you see a panicked swimmer struggling near the dock, the safest action is to jump in immediately and grab them.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A panicked victim can easily pull an untrained swimmer underwater, so you must always attempt reaching or throwing rescues from shore before entering the water (p. 22).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Spinal injuries can occur from diving into shallow or obstructed water (p. 22).
- Caution During Spinal Rescues: Rescuers must be very careful when a spinal injury is suspected (p. 22).
- Spinal injuries can be fatal or can cause paralysis (p. 22).
- Preventing Secondary Spinal Damage: If the victim is not handled properly, additional serious injury could result (p. 22).

### Req 6: Survival Floating and Cold Water Safety
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Float faceup in a resting position for at least three minutes with minimal movement. (b) Demonstrate survival floating for at least five minutes. (c) While wearing a properly fitted U. S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket, demonstrate the HELP and huddle positions. Explain their purposes. (d) Explain why swimming or survival floating will hasten the onset of hypothermia in cold water.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 30, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Swimming Requirement 6: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for survival floating and cold water safety, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** If you fall into cold water far from shore, swimming or treading water vigorously will warm up your muscles and prevent hypothermia.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Vigorous swimming and treading water flush warm blood to your extremities and flush cold water through your clothing, accelerating heat loss and hypothermia (p. 30).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Coast Guard Approved Life Jackets: A properly fitted U. S. Coast Guard- approved lifejacket should be worn for all activities afloat in small craft (p. 30).
- In addition, it can provide insulation and significantly reduce heat loss in cold water that could lead to hypothermia (p. 30).
- If you are alone in cold water and more than a short distance from safety, your best strategy is to float motionless (p. 30).
- Keep your lifejacket and all clothing on for insulation (p. 30).

### Req 7: Feetfirst and Headfirst Surface Dives
**Official Requirement 7 (Parent Overview):** In water over your head, but not to exceed 10 feet, do the following: (a) Use the feetfirst method of surface diving and bring an object up from the bottom. (b) Do a headfirst surface dive (pike or tuck), and bring the object up again. (c) Do a headfirst surface dive to a depth of at least 5 feet and swim underwater for three strokes. Come to the surface, take a breath, and repeat the sequence twice.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Swimming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 56, p. 58)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Swimming Requirement 7: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for feetfirst and headfirst surface dives, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** When performing a headfirst surface dive, kicking furiously while your legs are still in the air helps push you deeper.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Kicking before your feet are underwater wastes energy and ruins your streamlined alignment; let the weight of your lifted legs drive you down before swimming (p. 56).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Forward Momentum Entry Setup: The easiest way to do the headfirst pike surface dive is while moving forward with a breaststroke (p. 56).
- Arm Scoop and Leg Lift: Then reverse the direction of your arm stroke while lifting your legs out of the water and into the air (p. 56).
- Downward Drive From Leg Weight: In this streamlined position, the weight of your legs above the water will drive you downward (p. 56).
- Smooth Continuous Dive Motion: It all should happen quickly in one smooth motion (p. 56).
