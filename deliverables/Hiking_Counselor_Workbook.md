### Req 1: Trail Hazards and Wilderness First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while hiking, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while hiking, including hypothermia, frostbite, dehydration, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, sunburn, hyperventilation, altitude sickness, sprained ankle, blisters, insect stings, tick bites, and snakebite.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 48, p. 50)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Trail Hazards and Wilderness First Aid discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** You only need to worry about dehydration on hot summer hikes, and feeling thirsty is the earliest reliable sign that your body needs water.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The earliest sign of dehydration is usually dark urine, followed by weariness, headache, body aches, and confusion, and dehydration contributes to both heat reactions and hypothermia (p. 48).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Water is essential for nearly every bodily function (p. 48).
- Causes of Trail Dehydration: People who lose more water than they take in risk becoming dehydrated (p. 48).
- The first sign of dehydration usually is dark urine (p. 48).
- Systemic Symptoms of Fluid Loss: Other signs can include weariness, headache, body aches, and confusion (p. 48).

#### Req 1a: Trail Hazard Prevention and Mitigation
**Official Requirement 1a:** Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while hiking, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards.

### Req 2: Safe Practices and Outdoor Ethics
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain and, where possible, show the points of good hiking practices including proper outdoor ethics, hiking safety in the daytime and at night, courtesy to others, choice of footwear, and proper care of feet and footwear. (b) Read aloud or recite the Leave No Trace Seven Principles, and discuss why each is important while hiking. (c) Read aloud or recite the Outdoor Code, and give examples of how to follow it on a hike.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 29)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Safe Practices and Outdoor Ethics discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Leave No Trace only applies to overnight wilderness backpacking trips and is just a rule against dropping trash on the ground.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Applying Leave No Trace begins during group trip planning by choosing suitable routes, preparing for weather, and deciding how to protect trail surfaces and respect other visitors (p. 28).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Environmental Stewardship on Trails: Caring for the environment is an important responsibility of every hiker (p. 28).
- Leave No Trace is just one set of principles that can help you live up to that responsibility and enjoy the outdoors fully by knowing (p. 28).
- As you and your group plan a hike, ask yourselves how you can follow each of the principles of Leave No Trace (p. 28).
- Leave No Trace and The Outdoor Code are principles that help you respect the environment and be considerate to others who are enjoying the outdoors (p. 28).

#### Req 2a: Footwear Care and Trail Safety
**Official Requirement 2a:** Explain and, where possible, show the points of good hiking practices including proper outdoor ethics, hiking safety in the daytime and at night, courtesy to others, choice of footwear, and proper care of feet and footwear.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 29)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Possible Hiking Outdoor Ethics)
- **Common Myth:** Walking around the outside edge of muddy trail sections is good outdoor ethics because it keeps your boots clean.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Walking through mud puddles or snow on the established path keeps impact concentrated, whereas skirting around puddles widens the trail and causes erosion (p. 28).

### Req 3: Aerobic Conditioning for Long Hikes
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain how hiking is an aerobic activity. Develop a plan for conditioning yourself for 10-mile hikes, and describe how you will increase your fitness for longer hikes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 41)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Hiking Is An Aerobic Activity)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Aerobic Conditioning for Long Hikes discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** If you play short-burst school sports, you can jump straight into a 10-mile or 20-mile hike without a progressive walking and conditioning plan.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Safe conditioning starts with daily walking in place of car rides and short training hikes, gradually increasing mileage and pack weight as aerobic fitness improves (p. 33).

### Req 4: Executing Ten and Twenty Mile Hikes
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Take four 10-mile hikes and one 20-mile hike, each on a different day, and each of continuous miles. Prepare a written hike plan before each hike and share it with your counselor or a designee for approval before starting the hike. Include map routes, a clothing and equipment list, and a list of items for a trail lunch. You may stop for as many short rest periods as needed, as well as one meal, during each hike, but not for an extended period such as overnight. (a) 10-mile hike #1 (b) 10-mile hike #2 (c) 10-mile hike #3 (d) 10-mile hike #4 (e) 20-mile hike
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 43)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 4): Complete all Executing Ten and Twenty Mile Hikes discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** You can combine two 5-mile walks from different days to count as a 10-mile hike, or start a hike before getting your written plan approved by your counselor.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Each of the four 10-mile hikes and the 20-mile hike must be continuous mileage on a separate day, preceded by an approved written hike plan with map routes, bailout points, gear list, and nutrition plan (p. 41).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Go! 20-Mile Hike Twenty miles is a long way, but with the hiking you've done sofar, you will be ready (p. 41).
- Emergency Bailout Route Planning: Mark on a map any places you can cut your trip short if weather, weariness, or injuries surprise you along the way (p. 41).
- Early Morning Trail Departures: Have a good breakfast and get an early start so that you can finish your hike before dark (p. 41).
- Midday Dry Sock Changes: Carry extra socks that you can change into during the day (p. 41).

### Req 5: Written Post Hike Trek Reports
**Official Requirement 5:** After each of the hikes (or during each hike if on one continuous trek) in requirement 4, write a short report on your hike. For each hike, give the date and description (or map) of the route covered, the weather, any interesting things you saw, and any challenges you had and how you overcame them. It may include something you learned about yourself, about the outdoors, or about others you were hiking with. Share this with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 43)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Hikes or During Each Hike)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Hiking Requirement 5: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying written post hike trek reports, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** A post-hike report only needs to state the date and total miles walked, and reporting any difficulties or fatigue will cause your counselor to reject the hike.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A complete post-hike report documents the date, route description or map, weather conditions, interesting sights, and specific challenges encountered along with how you overcame them (p. 41).
