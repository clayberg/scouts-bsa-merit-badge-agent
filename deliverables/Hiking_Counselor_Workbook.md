### Req 1: Trail Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while hiking, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while hiking, including hypothermia, frostbite, dehydration, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, sunburn, hyperventilation, altitude sickness, sprained ankle, blisters, insect stings, tick bites, and snakebite.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 48, p. 50)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Discontinue movement immediately when lightning strikes occur within thirty seconds of thunder, seeking forested depressions rather than isolated trees.
- **Common Myth:** Drinking water only after feeling thirsty prevents dehydration on arduous mountain trails.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Thirst lags behind cellular fluid loss, requiring continuous scheduled fluid consumption throughout hikes.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Lightning Hazard Protocols: Hikers caught in exposed terrain must seek low ground and assume lightning crouch.
- Shivering signals mild hypothermia requiring immediate dry clothing layers and warm caloric liquids.
- Heatstroke Clinical Response: Immediate immersion or cold water dousing lowers dangerous core body temperature during heatstroke.
- Blister Prevention Taping: Moleskin or athletic tape applied over hot spots stops friction before blisters form.

### Req 2: Outdoor Ethics and Foot Care
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain and, where possible, show the points of good hiking practices including proper outdoor ethics, hiking safety in the daytime and at night, courtesy to others, choice of footwear, and proper care of feet and footwear. (b) Read aloud or recite the Leave No Trace Seven Principles, and discuss why each is important while hiking. (c) Read aloud or recite the Outdoor Code, and give examples of how to follow it on a hike.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 29)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** When walking along roads without sidewalks, hike facing oncoming traffic in single file and wear high visibility or reflective gear.
- **Common Myth:** Heavy cotton athletic socks provide the best cushioning and blister protection on backcountry trails.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cotton absorbs moisture and holds it against skin, accelerating friction and causing severe blisters.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Durable Surface Travel: Walking directly through trail center puddles prevents widening pathways and trampling fragile vegetation.
- Footwear Fit Tolerance: Hiking boots need thumb width toe clearance to prevent bruised toenails during descents.
- Hikers along roadways at night must wear reflective clothing and carry active lights.
- Sock Moisture Management: Synthetic or merino wool socks wick foot perspiration to reduce skin maceration risks.

### Req 3: Aerobic Fitness and Conditioning
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain how hiking is an aerobic activity. Develop a plan for conditioning yourself for 10-mile hikes, and describe how you will increase your fitness for longer hikes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 41)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Hiking Is An Aerobic Activity)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never increase weekly mileage or pack load by more than ten percent per week to avoid stress fractures and tendonitis.
- **Common Myth:** Relying solely on school sports without specific hiking practice prepares your body for ten-mile trails.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hiking stresses stabilizer muscles, connective tissues, and endurance systems differently than short sprint athletic sports.

### Req 4: Endurance Trekking and Hike Planning
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Take four 10-mile hikes and one 20-mile hike, each on a different day, and each of continuous miles. Prepare a written hike plan before each hike and share it with your counselor or a designee for approval before starting the hike. Include map routes, a clothing and equipment list, and a list of items for a trail lunch. You may stop for as many short rest periods as needed, as well as one meal, during each hike, but not for an extended period such as overnight. (a) 10-mile hike #1 (b) 10-mile hike #2 (c) 10-mile hike #3 (d) 10-mile hike #4 (e) 20-mile hike
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 43)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Establish a rigid turnaround time before departure, turning back regardless of distance reached if weather or pace compromises safety.
- **Common Myth:** Accumulating two five-mile walks separated by an afternoon car trip satisfies a ten-mile requirement.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The requirement demands continuous trekking on foot during a single organized journey on a distinct day.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Written Flight Plan: Submitting emergency itineraries to designated adult leaders ensures rapid response during backcountry delays.
- Pacing Energy Conservation: Maintaining a steady two mile per hour pace preserves energy across twenty continuous miles.
- Caloric Trail Consumption: Eating small carbohydrate snacks every hour maintains blood sugar levels during long treks.
- Official hikes must consist of continuous trekking miles without vehicular travel or interruptions.

### Req 5: Post Hike Reflection Reports
**Official Requirement 5:** After each of the hikes (or during each hike if on one continuous trek) in requirement 4, write a short report on your hike. For each hike, give the date and description (or map) of the route covered, the weather, any interesting things you saw, and any challenges you had and how you overcame them. It may include something you learned about yourself, about the outdoors, or about others you were hiking with. Share this with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 43)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Hikes or During Each Hike)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Record any near miss incidents or injuries in the report to improve patrol safety protocols on future trips.
- **Common Myth:** Writing a hike report is merely a bureaucratic chore completed days after the event ends.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Prompt post-hike reporting captures fleeting details about gear performance, personal stamina, and environmental conditions accurately.
