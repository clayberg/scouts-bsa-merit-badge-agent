## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 4 (Explain):** Take four 10-mile hikes and one 20-mile hike, each on a different day, and each of continuous miles. Prepare a written hike plan before each hike and share it with your counselor or a designee for approval before starting the hike. Include map routes, a clothing and equipment list, and a list of items for a trail lunch. You may stop for as many short rest periods as needed, as well as one meal, during each hike, but not for an extended period such as overnight. (a) 10-mile hike #1 (b) 10-mile hike #2 (c) 10-mile hike #3 (d) 10-mile hike #4 (e) 20-mile hike

### Req 3: Aerobic Fitness and Conditioning
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain how hiking is an aerobic activity. Develop a plan for conditioning yourself for 10-mile hikes, and describe how you will increase your fitness for longer hikes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 41)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never increase weekly mileage or pack load by more than ten percent per week to avoid stress fractures and tendonitis.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does cellular aerobic respiration differ from anaerobic energy production when climbing steep mountain grades?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Cardiovascular Stamina Demands: Aerobic respiration provides steady oxygen delivery to working leg muscles during continuous hiking miles.
  - Training with gradually increasing pack weight develops core stability and prevents muscle strain injuries.
  - Sustaining heart rates within aerobic training zones maximizes endurance gains without causing premature fatigue.
  - Muscle Recovery Hydration: Post workout stretching combined with electrolyte replenishment restores glycogen and repairs strained muscle fibers.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Baseline Assessment: Measure current resting heart rate and walk three continuous miles to test endurance.
  - Step 2 - Cardiovascular Scheduling: Perform thirty minutes of aerobic activity three times weekly to build lung capacity.
  - Step 3 - Distance Increments: Increase weekly conditioning hike distances by two miles while monitoring personal physical exertion.
  - Step 4 - Pack Weighting: Add five pounds to daypacks during final preparatory walks before ten mile attempts.
- **Common Myth:** Relying solely on school sports without specific hiking practice prepares your body for ten-mile trails.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hiking stresses stabilizer muscles, connective tissues, and endurance systems differently than short sprint athletic sports.

#### Req 4e: Twenty Mile Endurance Trek
**[Parent Directive]:** *Take four 10-mile hikes and one 20-mile hike, each on a different day, and each of continuous miles*
**Official Requirement 4e:** 20-mile hike.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 43)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4e Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For hiking Requirement 4e (20-mile hike), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - The 20-mile continuous hike represents the pinnacle achievement of the merit badge, requiring seven to ten hours of sustained trekking. Flawless pacing, blister mitigation, nutrition, and mental resilience are mandatory for safe completion.
- **Common Myth:** Hiking at a rapid sprint in the morning banks extra time for afternoon exhaustion.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Early sprinting drains glycogen stores rapidly, causing severe late afternoon fatigue and muscle cramps.

### Req 5: Post Hike Reflection Reports
**Official Requirement 5:** After each of the hikes (or during each hike if on one continuous trek) in requirement 4, write a short report on your hike. For each hike, give the date and description (or map) of the route covered, the weather, any interesting things you saw, and any challenges you had and how you overcame them. It may include something you learned about yourself, about the outdoors, or about others you were hiking with. Share this with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Hiking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 43)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Record any near miss incidents or injuries in the report to improve patrol safety protocols on future trips.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does reflecting on unexpected trail obstacles make you a more resilient wilderness leader?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Route Documentation Standards: Accurate hike logs include trail names, total mileage, elevation gain, and elapsed moving time.
  - Environmental Observation Logs: Recording wildlife sightings and changing plant zones deepens environmental awareness during backcountry journeys.
  - Equipment Performance Review: Evaluating how gear performed in wet weather guides equipment choices for future treks.
  - Documenting foot hot spots and physical fatigue levels assists conditioning for future treks.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Data Logging: Record GPS track coordinates, start and finish times, and total elapsed trail mileage.

  - Step 3 - Field Reflections: Write detailed paragraphs covering wildlife encounters, scenic landmarks, and unexpected trail challenges.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Review: Present the completed written hike report to your counselor for constructive feedback.
