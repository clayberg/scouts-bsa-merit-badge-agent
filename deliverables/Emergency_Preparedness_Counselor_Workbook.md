## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 3 (Explain):** Preventing Accidents and Emergencies. Do ONE of the following: (a) Using a home safety checklist included in the Emergency Preparedness merit badge pamphlet or one approved by your counselor, inspect a home (or a similar building near where you live or at a camp) for safety hazards with the help of an adult. Present your completed checklist to and discuss your findings with your counselor. (b) Develop emergency prevention plans for five family activities outside the home, as approved by your counselor. (Examples are taking a picnic to a park, seeing a movie, attending a worship service, an outing at a beach, traveling to visit a relative, or attending a ball game or concert.) Each plan should include an analysis of possible hazards, proposals to prevent, protect from, mitigate, respond to, and recover from emergencies, and the reasons for the actions that you propose.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Discuss):** Emergency Service. Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the duties that a Scout troop should be prepared to do, the training they need, and the safety precautions they should take for the following emergency services: (1) Crowd and traffic control (2) Messenger service during an incident (3) Collection and distribution services (4) Group feeding, shelter, and sanitation (b) Prepare a written plan for mobilizing your troop when needed to do emergency service. If your troop already has a mobilization plan, present the plan to your counselor and tell your part in making the plan work. (c) Using a checklist in the Emergency Preparedness merit badge pamphlet or one approved by your counselor, prepare or inspect a personal emergency service pack for a mobilization call. Explain the needs and uses of the contents to your counselor. (d) Take part in an emergency service project, either a real one or a practice exercise, with a Scouting troop or a community agency or at Scout camp or at a school. Review what you learned and practiced with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 9 (Explain):** First Aid Merit Badge. Earn the First Aid merit badge.
- [ ] **Req 10 (Discuss):** Careers. Do ONE of the following: (a) Interview an emergency services coordinator or a civil servant about their work in disaster management. Learn about how they chose this career and about their duties. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career. (b) Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge in emergency services. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities, and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career. (c) Identify how you might use the skills and knowledge in the field of emergency preparedness to pursue a personal hobby and/or healthy lifestyle. Research the additional training required, expenses, and affiliation with organizations that would help you maximize the enjoyment and benefit you might gain from it. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and share what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Stovetop and Oven Fire Safety: Smother kitchen grease fires by sliding a metal lid over the pan, turning off the heat source, and leaving the pan covered until completely cool. Keep oven doors shut tightly during an oven fire while turning off the power to starve the flames of oxygen (p. 15).

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water causes burning grease to explode into airborne fireballs, while flour can ignite explosively as an aerosolized powder.

#### Req 1b(2): Residential Flammable Liquid Fire Response
**[Parent Directive]:** *Using a chart, spreadsheet, or another method approved by your counselor, demonstrate your understanding of each aspect of emergency preparedness*
**Official Requirement 1b(2):** Home flammable liquid fire.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Emergency Preparedness Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 68)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (5-Column Hazard Assessment Matrix):** Req 1b
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do common extinguishing methods like splashing water fail dangerously when dealing with burning fuels or solvent spills in a garage?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Residential Flammable Liquid Fire Response: Discharge a Class B dry chemical fire extinguisher at the base of burning liquids using the sweep technique, or smother small fuel spills with sodium bicarbonate. Store all household fuels in approved safety containers outside living quarters away from ignition sources (p. 16).
- **Common Myth:** Spraying water from a garden hose is an effective way to put out burning gasoline or paint thinner in a garage.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water floats flammable liquids and spreads the burning pool across floors, requiring a Class B dry chemical agent to break the chemical chain reaction.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Foodborne Illness Prevention and Response: Prevent bacterial proliferation by keeping cold foods below 40 degrees Fahrenheit, cooking meats to recommended internal temperatures, and refrigerating perishable items within two hours. - **Pamphlet Fact:** Reheating kills living bacteria but cannot neutralize heat-stable bacterial toxins already produced in food left at room temperature.

#### Req 1b(6): Desert Vehicle Breakdown Survival Strategies
**[Parent Directive]:** *Using a chart, spreadsheet, or another method approved by your counselor, demonstrate your understanding of each aspect of emergency preparedness*
**Official Requirement 1b(6):** Vehicle stalled in the desert (7) Vehicle trapped in a blizzard (8) Backcountry injury (9) Boating or water accident (10) Toxic chemical spills and releases.

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why is staying close to your stalled vehicle in an arid climate far safer than walking away to look for assistance?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Desert Vehicle Breakdown Survival Strategies: Remain inside or directly beside the disabled vehicle to benefit from shade and provide a recognizable target for search aircraft. Ration physical exertion, raise the hood to signal distress, and drink available water in regular sips rather than dehydrating in an attempt to save it (p. 26).
- **Common Myth:** Stranded drivers in hot desert environments should immediately hike toward town to find help while saving their drinking water for later.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hiking in desert heat rapidly accelerates fatal dehydration and heat stroke, whereas remaining near the vehicle provides shade and maximum aerial visibility.

  - Airway Priority: Clearing blocked breathing passages always precedes treatment of secondary extremity fractures.
  - Hemorrhage Control: Direct pressure and arterial tourniquets stop catastrophic life-threatening blood loss quickly.
  - Shock Management: Raising legs and conserving body heat preserves vital organ perfusion effectively.
  - Triage Classification: Sorting casualties by injury severity ensures optimal allocation of medical resources.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Triage Evaluation: Assess catastrophic scene injuries rapidly to prioritize patients requiring immediate airway interventions.
  - Step 2 - Bleeding Suppression: Apply direct mechanical pressure and tourniquets to arrest arterial bleeding immediately.
  - Step 3 - Shock Prevention: Position patients supine, raise lower extremities, and insulate against hypothermic exposure.
  - Step 4 - Transport Preparation: Stabilize fractures with padded rigid splints before moving casualties toward ambulances.
- **Common Myth:** Basic first aid skills learned years ago remain sharp without ongoing practical recertification.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** First aid psychomotor skills decay rapidly without regular scenario practice and updated training.
