## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 1:** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the following hazards you might encounter while motorboating: flammable fuel; carbon monoxide; propellers; collisions; falls overboard; capsize; or running aground. Explain what you should do to anticipate, prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Explain first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while motorboating, including hypothermia, heat reactions, dehydration, motion sickness, bug bites, and blisters. (c) Discuss the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Tell how it applies to motorboating activities.
- [ ] **Station Req 2:** Do the following: (a) Before doing requirement 5, successfully complete the Scouting America swimmer test. Note: See the Swimming merit badge pamphlet for details about the Scouting America swimmer test. (b) Name the different types of personal flotation devices (PFDs), and explain when each type should be used. Show how to choose and properly fit a PFD.
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** With your counselor or other adults on board, demonstrate proper boat-handling procedures and skills by doing the following: (a) Board and assist others in boarding. Confirm that all passengers on board are wearing properly fitted life jackets. (b) Fuel the boat and complete a safety check. (c) If equipped, attach the engine cut-off switch link and safely start the motor. Get underway from dockside or from a beach launch. (d) Run a course for at least a mile, showing procedures for overtaking and passing slower vessels, yielding right-of-way, passing oncoming traffic, making turns, reversing direction, and using navigation aids. (e) Stop and secure the boat in position on the open water using anchors. Raise and stow the anchor and get underway. (f) Land or dock the boat, disembark, and assist others in doing the same. (g) Moor, dock, or beach the boat and secure all gear. (h) Demonstrate knots used in boating: cleating hitch, bowline, clove hitch, anchor bend, and sheet bend.

### Req 1: Marine Hazard Risk Management
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the following hazards you might encounter while motorboating: flammable fuel; carbon monoxide; propellers; collisions; falls overboard; capsize; or running aground. Explain what you should do to anticipate, prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Explain first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while motorboating, including hypothermia, heat reactions, dehydration, motion sickness, bug bites, and blisters. (c) Discuss the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Tell how it applies to motorboating activities.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Motorboating Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 14)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never run the starter or energize electrical switches before sniffing the engine compartment for gasoline fumes.
- **Common Myth:** Boating hazards only occur during violent ocean storms or high speed operation.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Gasoline vapor settles in low compartments and requires blower operation before engine startup.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Fuel Vapor Risk: Gasoline vapors sink rapidly into lowest bilge compartments creating severe explosion hazards.
- Carbon Monoxide Danger: Slow idling speeds cause odorless exhaust gas accumulations inside enclosed stern areas.
- Propeller Safety Arc: Rotating blades inflict severe trauma before vessel operators can shift into neutral.
- Capsizing Prevention Rule: Evenly distributed gear and seated passengers prevent sudden hull rolls in chop.

### Req 2: Water Competency Life Jackets
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Before doing requirement 5, successfully complete the Scouting America swimmer test. Note: See the Swimming merit badge pamphlet for details about the Scouting America swimmer test. (b) Name the different types of personal flotation devices (PFDs), and explain when each type should be used. Show how to choose and properly fit a PFD.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Motorboating Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 19)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Every scout and adult aboard a motorized craft must wear a properly fitted Coast Guard approved life jacket at all times.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Any comfortable flotation cushion satisfies the life jacket requirement for youth on motorboats.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Type I and II vests provide higher buoyancy designed to turn unconscious swimmers face up.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Swimmer Skill Benchmark: Strong swimming skills provide essential confidence during accidental water entries from moving vessels.
- Offshore Vest Buoyancy: Type one vests provide maximum flotation and turn unconscious swimmers face up.
- Inflatable life jackets require adult maintenance and remain unsuitable for active youth.
- Chest Strap Alignment: Properly adjusted webbing straps keep the flotation foam securely below the chin.

### Req 3: Propulsion Mechanics Operational Safety
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain inboard, outboard, and inboard/outboard motors, and the uses and advantages of each. (b) Explain the safety procedures and precautions involving handling fuel and engine servicing, and equipment storage and placement. (c) Explain how to winterize a boat motor and tell why this procedure is necessary. (d) Explain the safety procedures and precautions involving swimmers and skiers in the water, passenger positions underway, and boat wakes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Motorboating Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 53, p. 70)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Turn off the engine completely before allowing swimmers or water skiers near the stern or swim platform.
- **Common Myth:** Motorboat engine bays naturally ventilate all gasoline vapors without mechanical blowers.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Fuel vapors are heavier than air and collect inside bilge cavities unless evacuated by blowers.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Outboard motors combine engine, transmission, and lower drive units into self contained housings.
- Grounding fuel nozzles against metal fill necks eliminates dangerous static electricity sparks.
- Marine non toxic antifreeze prevents engine block cracks caused by freezing expansion.
- Propeller Hazard Perimeter: Skiers approaching the swim platform require complete motor shutdown rather than neutral.

### Req 4: Navigation Regulations Safety Guidelines
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Show you know safety guidelines for motorboating by doing the following: (a) Review how each item of the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy applies, including checking the weather prior to and during time on the water, confirming all required equipment is present and functional, and following a float plan. (b) Explain the rules or laws that apply to recreational boating in your area or state. Have a permit to operate a motorboat, if required by the laws of your state. Discuss how you would find information regarding the boating laws in different states. (c) Discuss how hazards of weather and heavy water conditions can affect safety and performance in motorboating. (d) Promise that you will follow Scouting America Safety Afloat guidelines. Explain the meaning of each point. (e) Explain and show the correct use of equipment required by both state and federal regulations to be carried aboard a motorboat. (f) Explain federal and state rules for a ventilation system, and tell why these rules are required. (g) Explain the use of lights (sight signals) and sound signals on motorboats. (h) Discuss the common types of anchors used in motorboating and under what conditions each would be preferred. Explain proper anchoring techniques.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Motorboating Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 12)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never anchor a motorboat from the stern because passing wakes can easily swamp low transom cutouts.
- **Common Myth:** Recreational motorboats are exempt from carrying sound signaling devices on inland lakes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Motorboats under power must display red port, green starboard, and white all around navigation lights.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Blower Ventilation Rule: Powered bilge blowers must evacuate enclosed engine compartments before starting the engine.
- Navigation Light Sectors: Red port and green starboard sidelights define vessel orientation during darkness.
- Danforth anchors require five to one scope ratios for dependable bottom holding.
- Weather Barometer Trend: Rapidly falling atmospheric barometric pressures indicate approaching squall lines and chop.
