### Req 1: Boating Hazards and Water First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the following hazards you might encounter while motorboating: flammable fuel; carbon monoxide; propellers; collisions; falls overboard; capsize; or running aground. Explain what you should do to anticipate, prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Explain first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while motorboating, including hypothermia, heat reactions, dehydration, motion sickness, bug bites, and blisters. (c) Discuss the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Tell how it applies to motorboating activities.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Motorboating Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 14)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Motorboating Requirement 1: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for boating hazards and water first aid, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** Carbon monoxide is only a danger inside an enclosed boat cabin because open air immediately disperses engine exhaust.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Odorless carbon monoxide gas can accumulate dangerously around the stern, swim platform, and open cockpit of a motorboat, especially when idling or moving slowly (p. 14).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- The Safety Afloat guidelines were developed to promote boating safety and to set standards for safe Scouting activity afloat (p. 10).
- Consistent Safety Awareness: Be sure to keep these guidelines in mind during all boating activities (p. 10).
- Discipline All participants should know, understand, and respect the rules and procedures for safe activity afloat (p. 10).
- The group should review the applicable rules just before setting (p. 10).

### Req 3: Boat Motors, Maintenance, and Passenger Safety
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain inboard, outboard, and inboard/outboard motors, and the uses and advantages of each. (b) Explain the safety procedures and precautions involving handling fuel and engine servicing, and equipment storage and placement. (c) Explain how to winterize a boat motor and tell why this procedure is necessary. (d) Explain the safety procedures and precautions involving swimmers and skiers in the water, passenger positions underway, and boat wakes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Motorboating Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 53, p. 70)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Motorboating Requirement 3: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for boat motors, maintenance, and passenger safety, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** When a swimmer or water-skier approaches the boat to climb aboard, putting the motor in neutral is enough to keep them safe from the propeller.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A propeller can still spin or be accidentally bumped into gear while idling in neutral, so the operator must shut off the engine completely before anyone boards from the water (p. 70).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Professional Engine Servicing: Care of Boat, Motor, and Equipment The Motor Today's boat motors, inboard and outboard, should be serviced and repaired by professionals (p. 53).
- The owner's manual will provide a maintenance schedule and will identify servicing procedures (p. 53).
- Learn and follow these manufacturer recommendations (p. 53).
- Basic Routine Motor Care: Although much of your motor's servicing will need to be done professionally, you should learn a few basic maintenance practices (p. 53).

**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Show you know safety guidelines for motorboating by doing the following: (a) Review how each item of the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy applies, including checking the weather prior to and during time on the water, confirming all required equipment is present and functional, and following a float plan. (d) Promise that you will follow Scouting America Safety Afloat guidelines. Explain the meaning of each point. (e) Explain and show the correct use of equipment required by both state and federal regulations to be carried aboard a motorboat. (f) Explain federal and state rules for a ventilation system, and tell why these rules are required. (g) Explain the use of lights (sight signals) and sound signals on motorboats. - **Common Myth:** An anchor holds a motorboat in place primarily through its dead weight resting directly beneath the bow.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** An anchor holds best when let out with an anchor line scope of five to seven times the water depth so the horizontal pull drives the flukes into the bottom (p. 12).
