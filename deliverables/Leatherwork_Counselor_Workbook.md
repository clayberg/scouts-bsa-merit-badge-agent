### Req 1: Leatherwork Safety and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while using leatherwork tools and materials, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, or lessen these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while working with leather, including minor cuts and scratches, puncture wounds, ingested poisoning, and reactions from exposure to chemicals such as dyes, cements, and finishes used in leatherworking.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 11)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Leatherwork Requirement 1 on leatherwork safety and first aid: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** A dull utility or swivel knife blade is safer for beginners because it is less likely to slice deeply into skin.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dull blades actually increase injury risk because they slip off tough leather when extra force is applied, so blades should be kept sharp by honing on cardboard rubbed with jeweler's rouge (p. 10).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Sharp Cutting Tool Edges: Many tools used in leather working have sharp cutting edges (p. 10).
- Never put any part of your body in the path of a cutting edge (p. 10).
- A piece of cardboard rubbed with jeweler's rouge should be used for honing the swivel and utility knife blades (p. 10).
- Many dyes, cements, and finishes used in leatherworking contain hazardous chemicals (p. 10).

### Req 2: Origins and Types of Leather
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain the following: (a) Where leather comes from (b) Kinds of hides that are used to make leather (c) Five types of leather (d) Best uses for each type of leather
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 21)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Origins and Types of Leather discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Chrome tanned leather is the best choice when you want to carve and stamp detailed tooling designs into a stiff belt or sheath.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Vegetable tanned leather, cured with tree bark extracts, provides the firm, water-absorbent body needed for tooling and stamping, whereas chrome tanned leather is soft and pliable (p. 15).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Types and Uses of Leather Many kinds of leather are available for leather work (p. 15).
- Tanning Method and Use: For instance, the wayleather has been tanned can determine how it is used (p. 15).
- Vegetable tanned leather (leather that has been tanned with extracts from tree bark) normally is used for items that call for a stiff, firm leather (p. 15).
- Chrome tanned leather (leather that has been tanned with chemicals that contain chromium salts) normally is used for items that call for a soft (p. 15).

### Req 3: Crafting and Finishing Leather Articles
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Make one or more articles of leather that use at least five of the following steps: (a) Pattern layout and transfer (b) Cutting leather (c) Punching holes (d) Carving or stamping surface designs (e) Applying dye or stain and finish to the project (f) Assembly by lacing or stitching (g) Setting snaps and rivets (h) Dressing edges
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 53)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Leatherwork Requirement 3 on crafting and finishing leather articles: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** An unpainted steel carpenter's square is the best straightedge to lay on wet vegetable-tanned leather when cutting or laying out patterns.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Unpainted steel rulers and squares react with wet vegetable-tanned leather and leave dark stains, so aluminum, plastic, or wood straightedges work best (p. 19).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Use these for measuring, pattern and design layout, and for cutting straight lines (p. 19).
- Non-Staining Ruler Materials: Avoid unpainted steel rulers and squares because they will stain leather; ones made of aluminum, plastic, or wood work best (p. 19).
- Use for marking the outlines of cutting patterns and to mark the positions of holes for snaps, rivets, stitching, and lacing (p. 19).
- Any awl with a sharp point, even an ice pick, will work (p. 19).
