### Req 1: Shop Safety and Hazards
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while using leatherwork tools and materials, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, or lessen these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while working with leather, including minor cuts and scratches, puncture wounds, ingested poisoning, and reactions from exposure to chemicals such as dyes, cements, and finishes used in leatherworking.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 11)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never cut toward holding fingers, and always cap rotary cutters between individual bench operations.
- **Common Myth:** Dull blades are safer for beginners because they resist deep tissue penetration.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Stropping a swivel knife on oiled rouge leather realigns rolled cutting edges safely.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Blade Maintenance: Strop cutting edges frequently on leather boards charged with fine jewelers rouge.
- Ventilation Standards: Work with solvent cements outdoors or under dedicated mechanical exhaust hoods.
- Mallet Selection: Strike steel drive punches exclusively using wooden, rawhide, or poly mallets.
- Chemical Handling: Wear nitrile gloves whenever applying alcohol dyes or petroleum based conditioners.

### Req 2: Leather Origin and Types
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain the following: (a) Where leather comes from (b) Kinds of hides that are used to make leather (c) Five types of leather (d) Best uses for each type of leather
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 21)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Avoid breathing dry buffing dust produced during mechanical split suede sanding operations.
- **Common Myth:** Genuine leather labels guarantee high durability and natural full grain fiber density.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Top grain leather contains the outermost epidermis where dense fiber bundles reside.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Rawhide Origins: Animal skins arrive at tanneries as byproduct hides from commercial meat processing.
- Bovine Predominance: Cattle hides constitute the primary raw material source for durable commercial leathercraft.
- Suede Splitting: Mechanical splitters slice fibrous corium layers away from grain surfaces cleanly.
- Tannage Selection: Vegetable tanning utilizes natural tree bark extracts to stabilize wet hides.

### Req 3: Leather Project Fabrication Steps
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Make one or more articles of leather that use at least five of the following steps: (a) Pattern layout and transfer (b) Cutting leather (c) Punching holes (d) Carving or stamping surface designs (e) Applying dye or stain and finish to the project (f) Assembly by lacing or stitching (g) Setting snaps and rivets (h) Dressing edges
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 53)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Secure steel straightedges with hand clamps to prevent slips while slicing heavy leather bends.
- **Common Myth:** Soaking leather submerged in warm water immediately before stamping produces crisp, permanent impressions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Leather casing requires wetting the fibers thoroughly and letting the surface return to natural color.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Moisture Casing: Dampen vegetable tanned leather evenly until surface color lightens before stamping.
- Position drive punch barrels perpendicular to stone slabs before mallet impact.
- Beveling Technique: Tilt stamping tools slightly backward while striking to avoid harsh ridge lines.
- Edge Slicking: Rub wet beveled borders with dense hardwood wheels until glossy friction burns.

Discuss with your counselor the advantages or disadvantages of leather vs. synthetic materials. (d) Visit a leather-related business. This could be a leathercraft supply company, a tannery, a leather goods or shoe factory, or a saddle shop. Report on your visit to your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Leatherwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 88, p. 90)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear splash goggles and chemical aprons when mixing acidic pickling liquors for home tanning.
- **Common Myth:** Commercial mineral spirit solvents clean accumulated trail dirt from dry leather safely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Saddle soap contains mild potassium soaps and oils that clean without desiccating.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Tannery Liming: Alkaline calcium hydroxide baths dissolve hair follicles and swell raw hide fibers.
- Chrome Bathing: Chromium sulfate solutions crosslink collagen proteins within twenty four commercial hours.
- Fatliquor Replenishment: Drum tumbling oils restore lubricating films between collagen fibers after tanning.
- Leather Reconditioning: Warm damp cloths remove gritty trail dust before applying conditioning oils.
