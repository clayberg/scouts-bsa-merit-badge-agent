## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Learn about the commercial tanning process. Report about it to your counselor. (b) Tan the skin of a small animal. Describe the safety precautions you will take and the tanning method that you used. (c) Recondition or show that you can take proper care of your shoes, a baseball glove, a saddle, furniture, or other articles of leather. Discuss with your counselor the advantages or disadvantages of leather vs. synthetic materials. (d) Visit a leather-related business. This could be a leathercraft supply company, a tannery, a leather goods or shoe factory, or a saddle shop. Report on your visit to your counselor.

## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 1:** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while using leatherwork tools and materials, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, or lessen these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while working with leather, including minor cuts and scratches, puncture wounds, ingested poisoning, and reactions from exposure to chemicals such as dyes, cements, and finishes used in leatherworking.

  - Strand Preparation: Skive lace ends to uniform thicknesses before beginning four strand plaits.
  - Tension Uniformity: Pull every cross strand with identical hand pressure throughout the plait.
  - Core Alignment: Center internal support cords squarely between exterior braiding strands during work.
  - Secure braided endings using terminal Turk head knots or metal crimps.

  - Step 1 - Strand Sizing: Cut uniform lace strips to five times the finished cord length.
  - Step 2 - Anchor Setup: Secure strand heads into bench vices using protective smooth wooden jaws.
  - Step 3 - Plait Sequence: Weave outer strands sequentially behind internal groups toward center crossing lines.
  - Step 4 - Terminal Securing: Lock finished braid ends with whipped waxed thread wraps or knots.
- **Common Myth:** Loose plaiting creates stronger flexible cords than tightly dressed round braided leather.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Maintaining constant braid tension ensures plaited round strands lie flat without twisting.
