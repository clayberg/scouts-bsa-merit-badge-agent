### Req 1: Delivering a Personal Introduction
**Official Requirement 1:** Give a three- to five-minute introduction of yourself to an audience such as your troop, class at school, or some other group.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 10)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Three- to Five-minute Introduction of)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Delivering a Personal Introduction discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** A three-to-five-minute self-introduction should simply list every date, school, and achievement in your life like a resume.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** An effective introduction connects with the audience through relatable stories, shared interests, and gentle self-deprecating humor rather than a dry list of facts (p. 7).

### Req 2: Body Language and Visual Aids
**Official Requirement 2:** Prepare a three- to five-minute talk on a topic of your choice that incorporates body language and visual aids.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 31)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Three- to Five-minute Talk)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Body Language and Visual Aids discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Visual aids should contain your entire speech text so the audience can read along while you talk.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Visual aids should be simple, large enough for everyone to see clearly, and used to reinforce key points rather than replace the speaker (p. 31).

### Req 3: Delivering an Impromptu Talk
**Official Requirement 3:** Give an impromptu talk of at least two minutes, either as part of a group discussion or before your counselor. Use a subject selected by your counselor that is interesting to you but that is not known to you in advance and for which you do not have time to prepare.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 43, p. 46)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Public Speaking Patrol Safety Briefing)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Delivering an Impromptu Talk discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** When asked to give an impromptu talk, you must start speaking the exact instant your name is called or the audience will think you froze.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You should not be afraid to pause for a moment to reflect on the topic and mentally organize your thoughts before beginning to speak (p. 43).

### Req 4: Outlining and Conversational Speech Delivery
**Official Requirement 4:** Select a topic of interest to your audience. Collect and organize information about the topic and prepare an outline. Write an eight- to 10-minute speech, practice it, then deliver it in a conversational way.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 33)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Topic of Interest to Your)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 4): Complete all Outlining and Conversational Speech Delivery discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Delivering an eight-to-ten-minute speech in a conversational way means reading a written essay word-for-word from the podium.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Conversational delivery means practicing thoroughly with an outline and note cards so you speak naturally with your audience rather than reading at them (p. 31).

### Req 5: Leading With Parliamentary Procedure
**Official Requirement 5:** Show you know parliamentary procedure by leading a discussion or meeting according to accepted rules of order; or by answering questions on the rules of order.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 45, p. 46)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (You Know Parliamentary Procedure By)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 5): Complete all Leading With Parliamentary Procedure discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** In a formal meeting governed by parliamentary procedure, the chairperson should lead the debate and argue for their favorite motion.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Unlike an informal discussion leader, the chair of a formal meeting remains impartial and only conducts business so every member gets a fair hearing (p. 45).
