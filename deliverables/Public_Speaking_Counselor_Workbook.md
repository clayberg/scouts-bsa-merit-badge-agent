### Req 1: Personal Introduction Speech Delivery
**Official Requirement 1:** Give a three- to five-minute introduction of yourself to an audience such as your troop, class at school, or some other group.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 10)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does sharing a personal vulnerability help establish trust between a speaker and an audience?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Opening Hook: A compelling personal anecdote captures listener attention within the first fifteen seconds.
  - Thematic Focus: Select two memorable experiences that demonstrate personal character rather than reciting resumes.
  - Vocal Pacing: Maintain an even tempo between one hundred twenty and one hundred fifty words.
  - Closing Synthesis: Reiterate the central life lesson and thank the audience for their time.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Hook Selection: Choose an engaging childhood memory illustrating personal growth to introduce your character.
  - Step 2 - Outline Draft: Arrange three chronological milestones into brief bullet points on one index card.
  - Step 3 - Timed Rehearsal: Deliver the introduction before a mirror while monitoring a digital stopwatch display.
  - Step 4 - Podium Delivery: Present the introduction to your patrol with erect posture and forward gaze.
- **Common Myth:** Speakers believe a self-introduction requires reciting a complete chronological list of personal accomplishments and hobbies.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** An effective introduction highlights a single defining narrative or core value that builds genuine audience connection.

### Req 2: Visual Aid Demonstration Talk
**Official Requirement 2:** Prepare a three- to five-minute talk on a topic of your choice that incorporates body language and visual aids.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 31)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** When does a visual aid distract an audience instead of reinforcing the speaker's main points?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Audience Orientation: Keep your torso turned toward the listeners while referencing charts on display.
  - Prop Handling: Hold physical demonstration objects high enough for the back row to observe.
  - Gesture Timing: Match hand movements directly to verbal transitions to emphasize key instructional points.
  - Visual Simplicity: Limit poster illustrations to essential diagrams with high contrast and large lettering.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Prop Selection: Select a functional tool or poster board that clarifies your demonstration topic.
  - Step 2 - Staging Setup: Position visual materials on an easel visible from all patrol seating angles.
  - Step 3 - Gesture Rehearsal: Practice pointing toward diagrams with an open palm while facing forward continuously.
  - Step 4 - Presentation Delivery: Deliver your five-minute topic talk while smoothly revealing and storing display items.
- **Common Myth:** Speakers assume visual aids should contain dense explanatory text so the audience can read along.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Visual aids must rely on bold graphics or minimal text to support the speaker rather than compete for attention.

### Req 3: Impromptu Speech Organization Strategy
**Official Requirement 3:** Give an impromptu talk of at least two minutes, either as part of a group discussion or before your counselor. Use a subject selected by your counselor that is interesting to you but that is not known to you in advance and for which you do not have time to prepare.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 43, p. 46)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What organizational patterns help you group thoughts quickly when given an unexpected speaking topic?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Mental Outlining: Formulate one central premise and two supporting points during thirty preparation seconds.
  - Structural Formula: Use a past, present, and future progression to organize thoughts logically.
  - Controlled Pauses: Replace filler vocalizations like um with silent breaths while formulating next sentences.
  - Strong Conclusion: Summarize the primary argument decisively before thanking listeners and yielding the floor.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Topic Receipt: Listen carefully to the prompt and identify your core viewpoint immediately.
  - Step 2 - Framework Selection: Pick a three-part organizational template such as problem, solution, and final outcome.
  - Step 3 - Opening Delivery: State your main thesis clearly in the opening sentence without apologizing beforehand.
  - Step 4 - Concluding Summary: Deliver two minutes of structured commentary before stating a firm final thought.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners believe impromptu speaking requires speaking instantaneously without taking several seconds to construct an internal roadmap.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Taking five to ten seconds of composed silence to choose an organizational structure yields a coherent, confident speech.

### Req 4: Audience Centered Speech Development
**Official Requirement 4:** Select a topic of interest to your audience. Collect and organize information about the topic and prepare an outline. Write an eight- to 10-minute speech, practice it, then deliver it in a conversational way.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 33)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does audience analysis change the stories and technical data you include in a speech?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Audience Analysis: Tailor topic depth and vocabulary to the age group and background interests.
  - Outline Construction: Group research facts under three main Roman numeral headings with detailed subpoints.
  - Conversational Delivery: Speak from sparse keyword notes instead of reading full written sentences aloud.
  - Vocal Variation: Modulate pitch, pacing, and volume to emphasize critical turning points in narrative.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Research Gathering: Collect verified facts and statistics from multiple credible library or historical sources.
  - Step 2 - Outline Organization: Organize findings into an introduction, three body sections, and a persuasive conclusion.
  - Step 3 - Rehearsal Timing: Practice speaking aloud with a timer to pace content across nine minutes.
  - Step 4 - Final Address: Deliver the ten-minute speech to your group using natural, conversational inflection.
- **Common Myth:** Speakers believe writing a complete manuscript and memorizing every sentence produces the most professional delivery.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Speaking extemporaneously from an outline produces natural vocal inflection and preserves authentic connection with listeners.

### Req 5: Parliamentary Procedure Meeting Leadership
**Official Requirement 5:** Show you know parliamentary procedure by leading a discussion or meeting according to accepted rules of order; or by answering questions on the rules of order.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Speaking Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 45, p. 46)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does requiring a second on every main motion protect the group's time?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Members must stand and obtain recognition from the chair before proposing motions.
  - Motion Precedence: Main motions yield immediately to privileged, subsidiary, and incidental parliamentary actions.
  - A second ensures that at least two individuals wish to discuss business.
  - Chair Impartiality: The presiding officer refrains from debate unless stepping down from the rostrum.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Call Order: Strike the gavel once and announce that the meeting will open immediately.
  - Step 2 - Receive Motion: Recognize a member, hear their motion, and state the question after seconds.
  - Step 3 - Moderate Debate: Alternate speaking turns between proponents and opponents while enforcing strict time limits.
  - Step 4 - Call Vote: Call for voice votes, announce the outcome, and state subsequent business.
- **Common Myth:** Members think anyone can shout out an opinion at any moment during a formal meeting.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Members must be recognized by the presiding officer and speak only to the pending motion.
