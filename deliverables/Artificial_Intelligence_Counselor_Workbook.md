### Req 4: Artificial Intelligence Ethics Governance

Do the following and share what you learned with your counselor: (a) Research ethical concerns and responsible use in AI, including bias, privacy, and AI decision-making. (b) Meet with your counselor, play, and discuss five rounds of the "What Would You Do?" ethical decision-making scenarios. (c) Develop your own ethical guidelines for the use of AI. (d) What is the Turing test?
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Artificial Intelligence Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Algorithms make purely objective evaluations because computer systems operate without subjective human feelings or biases.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Machine learning algorithms reflect statistical historical prejudices present within their initial training datasets unless deliberately mitigated.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Data Imbalance: Skewed training corpora cause predictive algorithms to deliver systematically unequal demographic categorization outcomes.
- Privacy Safeguards: Secure machine learning workflows obfuscate personal record fields to maintain individual statutory anonymity.
- Operational Accountability: Engineers must maintain explainable algorithmic decision logic within automated clinical triage software systems.
- Cognitive Benchmarking: Alan Turing proposed behavioral imitation games to evaluate whether machines exhibit indistinguishable intelligence.

### Req 8: Professional Computational Career Pathways
**Official Requirement 8 (Parent Overview):** Career Exploration. Do ONE of the following: (a) Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge in artificial intelligence or automation. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities, and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career. (b) Interview an artificial intelligence or automation professional. Learn about their day-to-day work, the challenges they face, and their vision for the future of artificial intelligence or automation. Inquire about training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Share what you learned with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Artificial Intelligence Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
- **Common Myth:** Artificial intelligence careers are restricted entirely to academic researchers working in university laboratory facilities.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Machine learning engineers apply statistical mathematics, computer science principles, and distributed cloud computing systems daily.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Engineering Roles: Machine learning engineers optimize neural inference latency across high-throughput distributed server infrastructure clusters.
- Analytical Roles: Data scientists extract actionable statistical insights from petabyte-scale unstructured enterprise corporate database repositories.
- Educational Requirements: Professional computational intelligence careers typically require accredited degrees in computer science or mathematics.
- Industry Dialogue: Professional engineering interviews reveal operational trade-offs encountered while deploying algorithms to production systems.
