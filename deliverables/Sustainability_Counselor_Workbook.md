### Req 1: Foundations of Sustainable Stewardship
**Official Requirement 1:** Describe the meaning of sustainability in your own words. Explain the importance of sustainability to society and how you can contribute to fulfilling the needs of current generations without compromising the needs of future generations.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sustainability Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 93)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does purchasing an imported consumer good affect a farming community overseas?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Brundtland Definition: Meeting current needs without compromising future generational resources defines true sustainability.
  - Triple Bottom Line: Ecological protection, community welfare, and economic viability operate as interdependent pillars.
  - Resource Stewardship: Regenerative harvesting rates prevent renewable natural stocks from facing permanent collapse.
  - Intergenerational Equity: Present consumption habits directly determine environmental quality available to future societies.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Daily Audit: Document all personal consumable resource expenditures across one single calendar day.
  - Step 2 - Category Sorting: Classify recorded resource expenditures into environmental, economic, or social impact categories.
  - Step 3 - Future Assessment: Estimate replenishment times required by nature to regenerate consumed raw resources.
  - Step 4 - Action Commitment: Select two daily habits to replace with sustainable low waste alternatives.
- **Common Myth:** Sustainability refers exclusively to planting trees and recycling household trash.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Sustainability requires balancing environmental health, economic stability, and social justice together.
