### Req 2: Water Resource Management Systems
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Water. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Evaluate your household water usage. If available, review water bills from the past year and evaluate the seasonal changes in water use. Identify three ways to help reduce water consumption. (b) Explain why water is necessary in our lives. Create a diagram to show how your household gets its clean water from a natural source and what happens with the water after you use it. Tell two ways to preserve your community's access to clean water in the future. (c) Different areas of the world are affected by either too much (flooding) or too little (drought) water. Explore whether either or both affect where you live. Identify three water conservation or flood mitigation practices (successful or unsuccessful) that have been tried where you live or in an area of the world that interests you.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Sustainability Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 29, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Avoid touching electrical pump wiring or corroded copper pipes during meter inspections.
- **Common Myth:** Municipal tap water is an infinite resource as long as bills are paid.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Drinking water comes from finite aquifers and reservoirs that require strict conservation.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Aquifer Depletion: Withdrawing groundwater faster than seasonal recharge causes permanent sub-surface storage loss.
- Low-Flow Fixtures: Aerators and pressure valves reduce fixture flow rates without diminishing utility.
- Stormwater Runoff: Impervious concrete surfaces channel urban pollutants directly into pristine natural waterways.
- Graywater Reclamation: Untreated laundry water safely irrigates non-edible landscaping under approved sanitary guidelines.

Identify where four different foods (such as milk, eggs, tuna fish, avocados, or ketchup) come from and how they are processed and transported from the source to you. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Sustainability Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 40, p. 42)* • **Group Organizer:** Activity & Observation Log
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wash hands thoroughly with soap after handling decomposing compost piles or moldy food scraps.
- **Common Myth:** Food waste decomposing in modern landfills turns into beneficial natural garden compost.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Landfill food waste decomposes anaerobically without oxygen, producing methane gas rather than compost.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Trophic Calorie Loss: Feeding crops to livestock loses ninety percent of base food energy.
- Agricultural Water Footprint: Irrigating grain crops consumes thousands of liters per kilogram of beef.
- Soil Microbiome Health: Synthetic pesticide overuse degrades natural fungal and bacterial networks within soil.
- Methane Landfill Emissions: Decomposing food scraps trapped inside oxygen-deprived landfills emit potent greenhouse gas.

Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Create a sketch depicting how you would design a sustainable community and be prepared to explain how the housing, work locations, shops, schools, and transportation systems affect energy, pollution, natural resources, and the economy of the community. > **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear high-visibility reflective vests and stay on sidewalks when conducting neighborhood traffic surveys.
- **Common Myth:** Suburban sprawl with huge single-family lawns is more environmentally friendly than compact cities.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dense urban housing uses far less energy, land, and piping infrastructure per resident.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**

- Active Transportation: Dedicated bicycle lanes and pedestrian walkways encourage healthy, carbon-free personal mobility.
- Planting street trees lowers urban heat island temperatures while filtering airborne particulates.
- Bioswale Filtration: Vegetated roadside swales absorb toxic road runoff before entering subterranean storm sewers.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never touch bare wires, open breaker panels, or insert metal objects into electrical wall outlets.
- **Common Myth:** Turning home electronics off with remote controls stops all electrical consumption completely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Standby power circuits continuously draw electricity to maintain sensors, clocks, and memory settings.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Burning coal produces airborne sulfur dioxide, toxic fly ash, and carbon dioxide.
- Modern rooftop panels convert incoming solar radiation directly into usable direct current.
- Phantom Power Losses: Electronic appliances consume standby electricity continuously even when turned completely off.
- Thermal Building Envelope: Quality attic insulation prevents expensive heat loss through ceilings during winter months.

### Req 6: Material Goods Lifecycle Analysis
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** Stuff. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Create a list of 15 items of your personal "stuff." Classify each item as an essential need (such as soap) or a desirable want (such as a video game). Identify any excess "stuff" you no longer need, working with your family, if possible. Donate, repurpose, or recycle those items you can. (b) List five ways having too much "stuff" affects you, your family, your community, AND the world.
