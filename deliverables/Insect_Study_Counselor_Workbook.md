### Req 1: Field Safety and Bite Prevention
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Safety. Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards associated with exposure to ants and bees and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Discuss the prevention of and treatment for health concerns that could occur while working with ants and bees, including insect bites and anaphylactic shock.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Insect Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 72)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Field Safety and Bite Prevention discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Pinching a honeybee stinger with tweezers is the safest way to pull it out of your skin.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Squeezing a honeybee stinger forces more venom from the attached sac into the wound, so you should scrape it off sideways with a fingernail or card (p. 36).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Whenever you are in the outdoors, make it a habit touse sunscreen with an SPF of at least 15 (p. 36).
- Like all outdoor activities, insect watching has its risks (p. 36).
- Protective Field Clothing: You can start by dressing appropriately, for instance, jeans, boots, gloves, and a long-sleeved shirt for protection (p. 36).
- The bites of mosquitoes, chiggers, and no-see-ums are irritating but not usually dangerous (p. 36).

### Req 2: Insect Anatomy and Major Orders
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Anatomy. Do the following: (a) Tell how insects are different from other animals. (b) Show how insects are different from millipedes and spiders. (c) Point out and name the main parts of an insect. (d) Describe the characteristics that distinguish the major orders of insects.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Insect Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Insect Anatomy and Major Orders discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Spiders, ticks, and millipedes are classified as insects because they are small land arthropods with jointed legs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Adult insects always have three distinct body regions, six legs, and one pair of antennae, whereas spiders have eight legs and two body regions and millipedes have many body segments with two pairs of legs per segment (p. 16).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Antennae as Olfactory Organs: Parts of an Insect How an Insect Smells An insect's antennae function as its nose (p. 16).
- Damsel fly Eyes and Antennae: The damsel fly has immense eyes but small, spike like antennae (p. 16).
- The moth has small eyes but large, fern like feelers (p. 16).
- The damsel fly depends on sight to guide it; the cecropia, flying in darkness, follows its feelers along faint odor trails through the night (p. 16).

### Req 3: Insect Metamorphosis and Life Cycles
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Life Cycles. Do the following: (a) Compare the life histories of a butterfly and a grasshopper. Tell how they are different. (b) Raise an insect through complete metamorphosis from its larval stage to its adult stage (e. g., raise a butterfly or moth from a caterpillar). (Some insects are endangered species and are protected by federal or state law. Every species is found only in its own special type of habitat. Be sure to check natural resources authorities in advance to be sure that you will not be collecting any species that is known to be protected or endangered, or in any habitat where collecting is prohibited. In most cases, all specimens should be returned at the location of capture after the requirement has been met. Check with your counselor for those instances where the return of these specimens would not be appropriate.)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Insect Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 35, p. 60)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Insect Study Requirement 3: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying insect metamorphosis and life cycles, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Grasshoppers go through a caterpillar and pupal cocoon stage before becoming winged adults.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Grasshoppers undergo incomplete metamorphosis with three stages, hatching from eggs into wingless nymphs that molt several times into adults without a pupal stage (p. 60).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- The most accurate observation of an insect in nature comes from watching it undisturbed (p. 35).
- Leaving Specimens Unharmed: When observing insects in their habitat, be careful to leave them unharmed in the place that you found them (p. 35).
- The Seven Principles of Leave No Trace and the Outdoor Code apply to all outdoor activities, including insect study (p. 35).
- Ethical Specimen Collection: Requirements for the Insect Study merit badge call for you to not only observe insects, but in some cases, to collect them (p. 35).

- **Common Myth:** Any group of insects gathering around a porch light or food source is a true social colony.
