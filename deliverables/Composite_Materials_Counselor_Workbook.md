### Req 1: Composite Hazards and Safety Data
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while working with composite materials and what you should do to anticipate, mitigate, prevent, and respond to these hazards. Describe the appropriate safety gear and clothing that should be used when working with composite materials. (b) Explain the precautions that must be taken when handling, storing, and disposing of resins, reinforcements, and other materials used in composites. Include in your discussion the importance of health, safety, and environmental responsibility and awareness. (c) Describe what a safety data sheet (SDS) is and tell why it is used.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Composite Materials Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 44, p. 46)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Composite Hazards and Safety Data discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Leftover mixed resin can be poured directly into a trash can or down a sink as soon as you finish your project.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Leftover catalyzed resin generates exothermic heat as it cures and must be allowed to harden completely in a safe container before disposal according to the Safety Data Sheet (p. 44).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Handling, storing, and disposing of substances used in composite materials can create safety and environmental hazards (p. 44).
- By taking proper precautions, you can reduce these hazards and safely use these materials (p. 44).
- Familiar Workshop Safety Habits: Working on projects for this merit badge is similar to work you have done for other badges involving tools, paints, and coatings or other chemicals (p. 44).
- However, some specific safety requirements apply when you are using composite materials (p. 44).

### Req 3: Reinforcements and Resin Matrix Systems
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Describe how composite materials are made. Then do the following: (a) Discuss three different composite reinforcement materials, their positive and negative characteristics, and their uses. Obtain the SDS for each one and discuss the toxicity, disposal, and safe-handling sections for these materials. (b) Discuss three different resins used in composites, their positive and negative characteristics, and their uses. Obtain the SDS for each one and discuss the toxicity, disposal, and safe-handling sections for these materials. Include thermoset resins and thermoplastic resins in your discussion. (c) For each of the three resins you chose for requirement 3(b), think of a new application that might be worth developing.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Composite Materials Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 35)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Reinforcements and Resin Matrix Systems discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Any cured composite resin can be melted back down into a liquid and reshaped into a new part.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Thermoset resins like polyester, vinyl ester, and epoxy cure through an irreversible chemical crosslinking process and cannot be remelted once hardened (p. 31).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Thermoset and Thermoplastic Categories: Resins used in composites can be either thermoset or thermoplastic (p. 31).
- Irreversible Crosslinking in Thermosets: Thermoset resins are changed from a liquid to a solid in an irreversible process calledcrosslinking (p. 31).
- Thermoplastic resins, on the other hand, can be remelted and recycled; the hardening process is reversible (p. 31).
- Thermoplastics are the typical plastics we are familiar with at home and at school (p. 31).

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Composite Materials Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 60, p. 70)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes

> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Composite Materials Req 5 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** When joining two pieces of fiberglass cloth in a project, you should butt the edges together without overlapping them.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must overlap fiberglass pieces by 1 to 3 inches at a seam so the structural strength of the laminate is not weakened (p. 60).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- In large composites projects, several pieces of fiberglass reinforcement might be necessary to cover the surface (p. 60).
- When you join pieces of fiberglass reinforcement to form a seam, overlap the pieces by 1 to 3 inches (p. 60).
- This ensures that the structural properties of the laminate are not weakened (p. 60).
- Staggering Multi-Layer Overlaps: If you use more than one layer of reinforcement, be sure to stagger or space out the overlaps to avoid thick buildups that also can create weak areas (p. 60).

#### Req 5c: Evaluating Finished Composite Laminates
**Official Requirement 5c:** With your counselor, determine how the finished projects will be evaluated. Using those guidelines, evaluate the completed projects with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Composite Materials Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 60, p. 70)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5c Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Finished Projects Will Be Evaluated)
- **Common Myth:** Adding extra catalyst hardener makes resin stronger when you are in a hurry.
