### Req 2: Equine Anatomy and Physiological Functions
**Official Requirement 2:** Name 15 parts of a horse and discuss the function of each with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 12)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Keep your body oriented facing the horse rear when palpating lower cannon bones and hocks.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the anatomical structure of the frog facilitate blood circulation through the leg?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Withers Ridge Function: Prominent dorsal vertebrae anchor critical saddle trees and back muscle stabilization systems.
  - Hock Joint Purpose: Strong tarsal joints absorb ground impact and power forward motion during cantering.
  - Frog Shock Dissipation: Triangular elastic sole structures pump circulating blood upward while cushioning ground impact.
  - Coronet Band Growth: Vascular tissue rings at the hoof hairline generate protective exterior horn walls.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Head Examination: Locate the muzzle, nostril, poll, and crest by gentle visual assessment along neck.
  - Step 2 - Foreleg Palpation: Trace downward from the shoulder past the elbow, knee, and cannon bone smoothly.
  - Step 3 - Hoof Boundary: Point directly to the coronary band circling above the tough external hoof wall.
  - Step 4 - Hindquarter Review: Identify the flank, stifle, gaskin, and hock while standing beside the hip.
- **Common Myth:** The equine hock bends forward in the same anatomical direction as human knee joints.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The stifle joint corresponds anatomically to the human knee and drives forward hindquarter propulsion.

### Req 3: Equine Breeds and Performance Traits

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Match equine temperament and breed athleticism to rider experience level before mounting strange stock.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do Arabians dominate endurance trail competitions compared to heavier stock breeds?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Quarter Horse Speed: Compact muscular hindquarters provide explosive acceleration across short sprint distances of quarter miles.
  - Arabian Endurance Trait: Large lung capacity and dense bone structure sustain long distance desert journeys.
  - Thoroughbred Sprint Build: Long angled shoulders and deep hearts produce unmatched sustained racing speed across turf.
  - Draft Percheron Power: Heavy bone frames and massive shoulder angles pull substantial agricultural and freight wagons.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Silhouette Comparison: Observe skeletal frame differences between stock breeds, hot bloods, and cold draft types.
  - Step 2 - Muscling Assessment: Examine shoulder thickness and hindquarter volume to evaluate explosive athletic power versus endurance.
  - Step 3 - Bone Density Check: Inspect cannon bone circumference relative to body mass across diverse working breeds.
  - Step 4 - Historical Verification: Correlate observable physical traits with original geographical breeding goals and functional ranch tasks.
- **Common Myth:** All horse breeds possess identical bone density and muscle composition for athletic tasks.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Thoroughbreds excel at intermediate galloping distances due to deep chests and lean skeletal geometry.

### Req 4: Equine Health Problems and Colic
**Official Requirement 4:** Describe the symptoms of colic. Name and describe four other horse health problems.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 48, p. 49)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never walk inside a stall with a violent rolling horse during severe colic attacks.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What behavioral indicators distinguish mild colic distress from life threatening strangulating intestinal blockages?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Abdominal Distress Indicators: Horses experiencing colic repeatedly bite at their flanks and roll on ground.
  - Laminitis Vascular Crisis: Inflammation within sensitive hoof laminae causes extreme pain and characteristic founder stances.
  - Tetanus Bacterial Danger: Clostridium tetani enters puncture wounds, causing severe stiffness, spasms, and third eyelid protrusion.
  - Equine Influenza Contagion: Rapidly spreading viral respiratory infections produce persistent coughing, high fevers, and nasal discharges.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Vital Check: Measure resting heart rate, respiratory cadence, and rectal temperature using veterinary tools.
  - Step 2 - Flank Observation: Listen for normal digestive gut sounds across all four abdominal quadrants carefully.
  - Step 3 - Mucous Inspection: Check capillary refill time by pressing the gum tissue above upper incisors.
  - Step 4 - Veterinary Contact: Report vital metrics, behavioral symptoms, and pain duration to the equine veterinarian.
- **Common Myth:** Colic is a single digestive illness easily treated by feeding extra dry hay.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Colic denotes abdominal pain with diverse causes requiring emergency veterinary diagnosis and treatment.

### Req 5: Conformation, Lameness, and Unsoundness Analysis
**Official Requirement 5:** Explain what conformation is and why it is important. Explain the difference between lameness and unsoundness.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 21)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (2-Column Comparison Table):** Req 5 Technical Comparison Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Do not ride horses exhibiting marked head bobbing or uneven footfall landing patterns.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do upright pastern angles increase concussive bone stress on lower equine joints?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Conformation Athletic Balance: Proper skeletal geometry distributes impact stress evenly across joints during rigorous locomotion.
  - Unsoundness Functional Defect: Structural issues like ringbone or roaring permanently reduce equine athletic performance and usefulness.
  - Blemish Cosmetic Mark: Harmless external scars or capped hocks do not impair practical athletic serviceability.
  - Painful forelimb lameness causes visible head elevation when the affected hoof strikes dirt.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Square Stand: Position the horse square on level ground to inspect leg straightness objectively.
  - Step 2 - Side Profile: Examine shoulder angles, back length, hip slope, and pastern slope alignment carefully.
  - Step 3 - Straight Tracking: Lead the horse at a walk and trot directly toward the evaluator.
  - Step 4 - Joint Palpation: Feel for heat, fluid swelling, or structural enlargement around lower leg tendons.
- **Common Myth:** Every conformational blemish inevitably renders an athletic horse permanently lame and unrideable.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Lameness describes temporary or permanent gait abnormalities caused by pain, while unsoundness encompasses any structural defect impairing serviceability.

### Req 6: Hoof Care and Protective Shoeing
**Official Requirement 6:** Explain the importance of hoof care and why a horse might need to wear shoes.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 39, p. 40)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Stand facing the horse hindquarters and maintain shoulder contact while lifting lower legs safely.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does neglect of regular six week hoof trims distort natural leg bone alignment?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Domestic hooves require balanced trimming every six to eight weeks without fail.
  - Sole Protection Need: Steel shoes prevent excessive wall wear and bruising across rocky mountain paths.
  - Therapeutic Farriery Value: Custom corrective shoes realign pedal bones following severe internal laminitis or fractures.
  - Thrush Infection Prevention: Daily hoof picking removes wet bacteria before sulcus rot deteriorates the frog.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Leg Slide: Run your hand down the back of the cannon bone to signal lifting.
  - Step 2 - Hoof Support: Cradle the lifted hoof securely between knees or in your resting hand.
  - Step 3 - Pick Direction: Point the metal hoof pick forward toward the toe away from your body.
  - Step 4 - Crevice Clearing: Scrape packed manure and small gravel from collateral sulci surrounding the frog.
- **Common Myth:** Wild horses naturally wear shoes to survive outside domestication and human care.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Domestic horses on abrasive rocky trails or hard asphalt wear down hooves faster than horn can grow.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never use a metal or hard plastic curry comb on lower bony legs or face.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does pre ride grooming under the girth line prevent painful saddle sores?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Rubber curry combs loosen caked mud and deep hair dirt using circular motions.
  - Stiff bristle brushes flick loosened debris out of the coat with short strokes.
  - Body Brush Finish: Soft bristles smooth hair coats and distribute natural conditioning skin oils across body.
  - Post Ride Cooldown: Walking hot horses slowly until breathing stabilizes prevents dangerous post exercise muscular tying.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Rubber Curry: Work the rubber curry comb in circular patterns across muscled neck, barrel, hips.
  - Step 2 - Bristle Sweep: Use a stiff dandy brush with firm directional sweeps along hair grain.
  - Step 3 - Soft Finishing: Polish the coat smoothly with a soft face brush around sensitive areas.
  - Step 4 - Sweat Scraping: Sponge sweat marks and walk the hot mount until respiration returns to baseline.
- **Common Myth:** Stabling a sweaty horse immediately after hard work is acceptable if food is available.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cooling down a hot horse with walking and cooling sponges prevents muscular cramping and colic.

### Req 8: Equine Nutrition and Feed Determination
**Official Requirement 8:** Explain how to determine what and how much to feed a horse and why the amount and kind of feed are changed according to the activity level and the breed of horse.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 52, p. 54)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 8 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never feed moldy, dusty, or fermented hay due to severe colic and respiratory risks.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does measuring grain by volume scoops instead of weight lead to dangerous overfeeding?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Forage Foundation Requirement: Horses must consume at least one to two percent body weight in forage daily.
  - Weight Based Rationing: Feeds must be weighed on scales rather than measured using volume coffee cans.
  - Water Hydration Baseline: Mature horses require ten to twelve gallons of fresh clean water each day.
  - Gradual Transition Schedule: Alter grain and hay rations slowly across ten days to prevent cecal acidosis.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Body Scoring: Evaluate rib coverage and crest fat to assign accurate Henneke body condition scores.
  - Step 2 - Workload Calculation: Categorize daily physical exertion as light trail, moderate training, or intense athletic competition.
  - Step 3 - Hay Weighing: Measure daily baled grass hay portions on hanging scales to match calculated weights.
  - Step 4 - Concentrate Measure: Balance supplementary grains carefully and divide portions into several small meals each day.
- **Common Myth:** Horses thrive best on an exclusive diet of sweet grain without grass or hay.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The equine digestive tract requires long stem roughage as the primary fuel source for gut motility.
