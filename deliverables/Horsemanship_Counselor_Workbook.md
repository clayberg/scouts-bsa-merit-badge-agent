### Req 1: Equine Handling and Fire Safety
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe the safety precautions you should take when handling and caring for a horse. (b) Describe the fire safety precautions you should take in a barn and around horses.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 25, p. 54)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never wrap lead ropes or reins around fingers, hands, or wrists under any circumstance.
- **Common Myth:** Horses see directly behind themselves and welcome unexpected approaches from the rear stall entrance.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Handlers must always approach toward the shoulder while speaking calmly to prevent sudden startled flight responses.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Handlers must approach near the shoulder where equine peripheral vision remains clear.
- Footwear Defense Standard: Hard soled riding boots protect delicate human metatarsal bones from crushing hooves.
- Handlers fold lead ropes accordion style without wrapping loops around human hands.
- Aisle Clearance Rule: Stable corridors must remain clear of equipment to prevent trapped equine escapes.

### Req 9: Tack Selection, Saddling, and Mounting
**Official Requirement 9 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Name 10 parts of the saddle and bridle that you will use, and explain how to care for this equipment. (b) Show how to properly saddle and bridle a horse. (c) Demonstrate how to safely mount and dismount a horse.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 31)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Check girth tension again after walking into the arena before mounting your horse.
- **Common Myth:** One universal saddle tree fits every equine back shape without pinching delicate spine bones.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Saddles must clear the withers with two fingers width when fully cinched and mounted.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Saddle gullets must clear dorsal spines by several finger widths under loaded weight.
- Girth Tightness Check: Fasten girths snugly allowing one flat hand between leather and horse barrel skin.
- Throatlatch Spacing Standard: Fasten bridle throatlatches loosely enough to permit four fingers clearance under equine jaws.
- Ensure bit rests quietly in the mouth gap creating one to two gentle lip wrinkles.

### Req 11: Equine Gaits and Maneuver Demonstrations
**Official Requirement 11 (Parent Overview):** On level ground, continuously do the following movements after safely mounting the horse. Do them correctly, at ease, and in harmony with the horse. (a) Walk the horse in a straight line for 60 feet. (b) Walk the horse in a half-circle of not more than 16 feet in radius. (c) Trot or jog the horse in a straight line for 60 feet. (d) Trot or jog the horse in a half-circle of not more than 30 feet in radius. (e) Halt straight. (f) Back up straight four paces. (g) Halt and dismount.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 49, p. 75)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear an ASTM/SEI approved equestrian safety helmet with chin strap securely fastened.
- **Common Myth:** Pulling hard on reins is the only way to steer and stop a horse.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Effective riding aids involve coordinated weight balance, leg pressure, and sensitive rein guidance.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Straight Walk Tracking: Maintain even rein contact and light leg pressure to track straight along path.
- Circle Radius Control: Use inside leg and outside supporting rein to shape sixteen foot walking circles.
- Jog Cadence Rhythms: Establish steady two beat diagonal trot cadences through balanced core posture and hands.
- Straight Halt Balance: Close fingers gently on reins while sinking weight deep into stirrups for halts.
