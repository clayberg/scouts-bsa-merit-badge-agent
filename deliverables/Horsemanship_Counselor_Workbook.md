### Req 1: Ground Handling and Barn Fire Safety
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe the safety precautions you should take when handling and caring for a horse. (b) Describe the fire safety precautions you should take in a barn and around horses.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 25, p. 54)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Ground Handling and Barn Fire Safety discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Walking quietly directly behind a horse is the safest way to pass without disturbing it.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Approaching silently from behind can startle a horse in its blind spot and trigger a dangerous defensive kick, so you should always speak softly and approach near the shoulder (p. 25).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Essential Ground Safety Rules: Before you handle a horse, you should know afew safety rules (p. 25).
- Confident and Careful Demeanor: Because horses can sense if you are afraid, be confident yet careful around them (p. 25).
- Approaching Without Startling Horses: Prevent accidents and injuries by following these rules. Ground Safety -Never startle a horse (p. 25).
- Avoiding Defensive Kicking Risks: You might startle it and cause it to kick, which could result in a serious injury (p. 25).

### Req 9: Tack Care, Saddling, and Mounting
**Official Requirement 9 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Name 10 parts of the saddle and bridle that you will use, and explain how to care for this equipment. (b) Show how to properly saddle and bridle a horse. (c) Demonstrate how to safely mount and dismount a horse.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Horsemanship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 31)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 9): Complete all Tack Care, Saddling, and Mounting discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** When placing a saddle pad and saddle on a horse, you should drop them slightly behind the withers and slide them forward into place.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must always place the pad and saddle slightly high on the withers and slide them backward so the horse's coat hairs lie flat in their natural direction (p. 31).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Bit Placement in Interdental Space: The bit should be placed in the toothless gap between the front and back teeth (p. 11).
- Proper Cheek piece Wrinkle Adjustment: A correctly adjusted bit should cause only one or two wrinkles in the corner of the horse'smouth (p. 11).
- Accounting for Individual Mouth Conformation: However, this might not be appropriate with horses that have an unusually long or short mouth (p. 11).
- Preventing Low Bit Tooth Impact: A bit that suspends too low will bang against the front teeth (p. 11).

**Official Requirement 10:** Explain and demonstrate how to approach and lead a horse safely from a stall, corral, or field and how to tie the horse securely.
