### Req 1: Experiencing Cultural Traditions Firsthand
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do TWO of the following, choosing a different group for each: (a) Go to a festival, celebration, or other event identified with one of the groups. Report on what you see and learn. (b) Go to a place of worship, school, or other institution identified with one of the groups. Report on what you see and learn. (c) Talk with a person from one of the groups about the heritage and traditions of the group. Report on what you learn. (d) Learn a song, dance, poem, or story that is traditional to one group, and teach it to a group of your friends. (e) Go to a library or museum to see a program or exhibit featuring one group's traditions. Report on what you see and learn.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Cultures Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Experiencing Cultural Traditions Firsthand discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** You can complete both parts of Requirement 1 by attending a festival and visiting a museum exhibit focused on the exact same cultural group.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must select two different racial, ethnic, religious, or other cultural groups when completing the two chosen activities for Requirement 1 (p. 31).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Planning Traditional Story Presentations: Going Places. team members, To earn the American Cultures merit badge, Nate plans to learn and teach a traditional story (p. 31).
- Observing Storyteller Expressions: He listens closely and watches the storytellers' expressions and movements (p. 31).
- Their techniques will help him entertain his own audiences (p. 31).
- Historical Origins of Folk tales: During a break, Nate asks the storytellers about the history behind the old stories, when the tales were written or performed in the native country (p. 31).

### Req 2: Cultural Isolation and Diverse Communities
**Official Requirement 2:** Imagine that one of the groups had always lived alone in a city or country to which no other groups ever came. Tell what you think the city or country might be like today. Now tell what you think it might be like if the three groups you chose lived there at the same time.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Cultures Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 46, p. 57)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Imagine Always Lived Alone)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Cultural Isolation and Diverse Communities discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** An isolated society with no outside contact would still develop the exact same foods, tools, and technologies as a multicultural city.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A completely isolated community would have no technology, foods, or customs borrowed from other groups and no awareness that other cultures exist (p. 46).
