## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Prepare):** Visit your county extension office, local university, agricultural college, nursery, farm, botanical garden, or arboretum. Report on what you learned.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Discuss):** Do ONE of the following and record weekly observations. Discuss the results of your project with your counselor. (a) Build a compost bin and maintain it for 90 days. (b) Build a vermicompost bin (worm compost bin) and maintain it for 90 days. (c) Build a hydroponic garden containing three vegetables or herbs, or three ornamental plants. Maintain this garden through harvest or flowering, or for 90 days. (d) Build one water garden, either in a container (at least 12 by 6 inches and 6 inches deep), or in the ground as a small, decorative pond no larger than 6 by 3 feet and 24 inches deep. Maintain the water garden for 90 days. (e) Prepare a honey super for use on a hive or colony. Remove a filled honey super from the hive or colony and prepare the honey for sale. (f) Grow a garden of your own using soil from seed or plantings to harvest or for 90 days, whichever is earlier. This can be an outdoor garden or indoors using appropriate containers, and should include at least three types of plants approved by your counselor.

### Req 4: Seed Germination Viability Testing
**Official Requirement 4:** Test 100 seeds for germination. Determine the percentage of seeds that germinate. Explain why you think some did not germinate.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Gardening Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 72)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What biological factors cause some seeds in a uniform lot to fail to germinate?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Moisture Towel Setup: Space one hundred test seeds evenly across damp paper towel sheets.
  - Incubation Temperature Control: Maintain sixty eight to seventy five degrees Fahrenheit for consistent sprouting.
  - Sprout Viability Standard: Count only seeds displaying clear radicle emergence as successfully germinated.
  - Embryo Dormancy Factor: Hard seed coats sometimes prevent water absorption without prior scarification treatment.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Test Sample Counting: Count exactly one hundred intact seeds from one selected vegetable variety.
  - Step 2 - Moist Chamber Layering: Arrange counted seeds across moist paper towels inside a sealed bag.
  - Step 3 - Daily Temperature Observation: Place container in warm room and inspect daily for emerged radicles.
  - Step 4 - Percentage Rate Calculation: Tally sprouted embryos on day ten to calculate final germination rate.
- **Common Myth:** Every commercial seed within a packet is guaranteed to produce a viable seedling.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Low seed germination percentages often result from old embryonic age or excessive damping off fungal moisture.

### Req 6: Honeybee Crop Pollination Biology
**Official Requirement 6:** Explain to your counselor how and why honeybees are used in pollinating food crops and the problems that face the bee population today. Discuss what the impact to humanity would be if there were no pollinators.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Gardening Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 83)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the anatomical structure of a honeybee facilitate pollen transfer between flowering plants?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Foraging Behavior Mechanism: Worker bees transfer pollen grains between flower stigmas during nectar collection.
  - Pollination Food Impact: Crops like apples, almonds, and squash depend heavily on bee visits.
  - Colony Stress Factors: Varroa destructor mites, habitat loss, and systemic pesticides weaken hive health.
  - Ecological Consequence Alert: Absence of pollinating insects would collapse agricultural diversity and crop yields.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Floral Anatomy Examination: Identify stamens, anthers, pistils, and nectar glands on open crop blooms.
  - Step 2 - Forager Tracking Record: Observe honeybees foraging within vegetable blossoms and note collected pollen baskets.
  - Step 3 - Pollinator Stress Research: Study how parasitic mites, nutritional deficit, and chemical exposures harm colonies.
  - Step 4 - Human Impact Assessment: Chart agricultural crops lost if insect pollination ceased across food systems.
- **Common Myth:** Wind and self pollination can easily replace bee pollination for all commercial fruit crops.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Honeybees carry pollen in specialized corbiculae while pollinating approximately one third of human food crops.
