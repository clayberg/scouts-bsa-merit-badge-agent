### Req 3: Board Setup and Piece Movement
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Demonstrate to your counselor that you know each of the following. Then, using Scouting EDGE to teach someone who does not know how to play chess: (a) The name of each chess piece (b) How to set up a chessboard (c) How each chess piece moves and captures, including: four rules of castling, en passant captures, pawn promotion, check, ways to get out of check, and checkmate (d) The five ways a game can end in a draw
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 64)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Board Setup and Piece Movement discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** When setting up a chessboard, it does not matter which color corner square is on your right hand.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A chessboard must always be oriented with a light square in each player's near-right corner, and each queen starts on a square matching her own color (p. 24).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- White (the player with the white pieces) always moves first, by moving one piece to a different square, following the rules of movement for that piece (p. 24).
- Capturing Opponent Pieces: By moving a piece to a square that contains a piece belonging to the opponent, a player captures (takes) that piece (p. 24).
- Removing Captured Pieces: The opponent's piece is then removed from the board, and it is out of play for the rest of the game (p. 24).
- For requirement 3, you will need to teach someone else how to play chess (p. 24).

### Req 4: Algebraic Notation and Opening Principles
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Demonstrate scorekeeping using the algebraic system of chess notation. (b) Discuss the differences between the opening, the middle game, and the endgame. (c) Explain four opening principles. Demonstrate for your counselor the first five moves of the following openings: Ruy Lopez, French Defense, Queen's Gambit Declined, Sicilian Defense. (d) On a chessboard, demonstrate Scholar's Mate, Fool's Mate, Légal Mate, Fried Liver Attack, and Noah's Ark Trap.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 38)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 4): Complete all Algebraic Notation and Opening Principles discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** In algebraic chess notation, the letter K is used to record knight moves and N is used for king moves.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Algebraic notation uses K for the king and N for the knight, while pawn moves are written using only the destination square without a piece letter (p. 37).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Kings ide and Queens ide Control: Black allows White positional control on the king side, while Black gains space for developing an attack on the queen side (p. 37).
- Aggressive Open Game Play: The openings which begin 1. e4 e5 are called open games an dare known for aggressive attacking play and frequent combinations (p. 37).
- Positional Closed Game Maneuvering: The openings which begin 1. d4 d5 are called closed games and are known for more subtle positional play and less aggressive maneuvering (p. 37).
- Games in which White begins with 1. e4 and Black replies with a move other than 1. . e5 are called semi-open games (p. 37).

#### Req 4a: Algebraic Notation Scorekeeping
**Official Requirement 4a:** Demonstrate scorekeeping using the algebraic system of chess notation.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 38)* • **Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 4a Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist (Scorekeeping Using The Algebraic System)
- **Common Myth:** Bringing your queen out on the first or second move is the strongest opening principle for beginners.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Developing minor pieces (knights and bishops) toward the center first is much safer because an early queen can be chased by opponent pieces with a loss of tempo (p. 37).

### Req 5: Strategy Elements and Tactical Combinations
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain four of the following elements of chess strategy: exploiting weaknesses, force, king safety, pawn structure, space, tempo, and clock management. (b) Explain any five of these chess tactics: clearance sacrifice, decoy, discovered attack, double check, double attack, fork, interposing, overloading, overprotecting, pin, skewer, remove the defender, zwischenzug, and zugzwang. (c) Set up a chessboard as follows and with White to move first, demonstrate how to force checkmate on the Black king: (1) White king on e1, the White rooks on a1 and h1, and the Black king on e5 (2) White king on e1, White queen on d1, Black king on e5 (3) White king on e1, White rook on a1, Black king on e5 (d) With White king on d4, White pawn on e3, and Black king on e6: (1) With White to move, demonstrate how White can force Black to allow their pawn to reach the last rank and be promoted to a queen. (2) With Black to move, demonstrate how Black can force a draw. (e) Set up and solve five direct-mate problems provided by your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 67, p. 70)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 5): Complete all Strategy Elements and Tactical Combinations discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** A pin and a skewer are identical tactics because both involve a long-range piece attacking along a line.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A pin traps a less valuable piece in front of a more valuable piece behind it, whereas a skewer attacks the more valuable piece first and forces it to move, exposing the piece behind it (p. 67).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- A discovered check can threaten all the squares in a triangle next to the king in a single move (p. 67).
- The king may be able to move away, or forcing the king to move to another square could create an opportunity to deliver checkmate (p. 67).
- Implied Castling and En Passant: When no other move seems to work, the composer of the chess problem may have implied a move, like castling or an en passant pawn capture (p. 67).
- Original King and Rook Squares: If the king and rook are on their original squares, assume they have not moved and castling is possible (p. 67).

#### Req 5a: Core Elements of Strategy
**Official Requirement 5a:** Explain four of the following elements of chess strategy: exploiting weaknesses, force, king safety, pawn structure, space, tempo, and clock management.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 67, p. 70)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Elements of Chess Strategy Exploiting)
- **Common Myth:** You can block a double check by placing a piece in front of one of the attacking pieces.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Because a double check attacks the king from two different directions simultaneously, the defending player cannot block or capture both attackers at once and must move the king (p. 67).

#### Req 5b: Five Essential Chess Tactics
**Official Requirement 5b:** Explain any five of these chess tactics: clearance sacrifice, decoy, discovered attack, double check, double attack, fork, interposing, overloading, overprotecting, pin, skewer, remove the defender, zwischenzug, and zugzwang.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 67, p. 70)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5b Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Chess Tactics Clearance Sacrifice Decoy)

- **Common Myth:** If your opponent has no legal moves left and their king is not in check, you win the game by checkmate.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** When the player to move is not in check and has no legal move available, the game ends immediately in a stalemate draw worth half a point to each player (p. 43).
