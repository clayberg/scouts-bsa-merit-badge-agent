### Req 1: Chess History and Heritage
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the history of the game of chess. (b) Research a famous chess player and what accomplishments made him or her famous. Discuss with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 21)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Maintain seated posture and avoid leaning backward on folding chairs during club sessions.
- **Common Myth:** Chess originated in modern Europe during the Renaissance period.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Chaturanga originated in sixth century India before spreading to Persia as chatrang.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Ancient Origin: Indian chaturanga evolved into Persian chatrang prior to spreading across Mediterranean trade routes.
- European Evolution: Medieval players introduced powerful queen movements across Europe around the fifteenth century.
- Paul Morphy demonstrated rapid piece development during open tactical games in Europe.
- Modern Champions: Wilhelm Steinitz established scientific positional principles to become the first official champion.

**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Demonstrate to your counselor that you know each of the following. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 64)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Inspect wooden boards and plastic tournament pieces for sharp splinters or burrs.
- **Common Myth:** Players can castle out of check if the intervening squares are unoccupied.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A player cannot castle if their king is in check or crosses an attacked square.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Orient the board so a white square sits on the right corner.
- Place each queen on the square matching her own color during setup.
- En Passant: Capture passing pawns on the adjacent file immediately on the following move.
- Deliver checkmate by attacking the enemy king without any legal escape path.

### Req 5c: Basic Endgame Checkmate Technique
**Official Requirement 5c (Parent Overview):** Set up a chessboard as follows and with White to move first, demonstrate how to force checkmate on the Black king: (1) White king on e1, the White rooks on a1 and h1, and the Black king on e5 (2) White king on e1, White queen on d1, Black king on e5 (3) White king on e1, White rook on a1, Black king.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Chess Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 67, p. 70)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
- **Common Myth:** A single queen can force checkmate in the corner without friendly king assistance.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Two rooks execute a rolling ladder checkmate without requiring their king assistance.
