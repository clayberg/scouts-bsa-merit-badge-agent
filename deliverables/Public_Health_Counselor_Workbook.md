## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do ONE of the following: (a) Visit a municipal wastewater treatment facility or a solid-waste management operation in your community. (1) Describe how the facility safely treats and disposes of sewage or solid waste. (2) Discuss your visit and what you learned with your counselor. (3) Describe how sewage and solid waste should be disposed of under wilderness camping conditions. (b) Visit a food service facility, such as a restaurant or school cafeteria. (1) Observe food preparation, handling, and storage. Learn how the facility keeps food from becoming contaminated. (2) Find out what conditions allow microorganisms to multiply in food, what can be done to help prevent them from growing and spreading, and how to kill them. (3) Discuss the importance of using a thermometer to check food temperatures. (4) Discuss your visit and what you learned with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do the following: (a) Do ONE of the following: (1) Visit your city, county, state, or federal public health agency. (2) Familiarize yourself with your city, county, state, or a federal health agency's website. (b) After completing either 7(a)(1) or 7(a)(2), do ALL of the following: (1) Compare the four leading causes of mortality (death) in your community for any of the past five years with the four leading causes of disease in your community. Explain how the public health agency you visited is trying to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates of these leading causes of illness and death. (2) Explain the role of your health agency as it relates to the outbreak of diseases. (3) Discuss the kinds of public assistance the agency is able to provide in case of disasters such as floods, storms, tornadoes, earthquakes, and other acts of destruction. Your discussion can include the cleanup necessary after the disaster.

### Req 1: Disease Transmission and Public Health
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain what public health is. (b) Explain how Escherichia coli (E. coli), tetanus, AIDS, encephalitis, salmonellosis, Lyme disease, and coronavirus (COVID-19) are contracted. (c) Choose any four of the following diseases and explain how each one is contracted and possibly prevented: gonorrhea, West Nile virus, botulism, influenza, syphilis, hepatitis, emphysema, meningitis, herpes, or lead poisoning. (d) For all 10 diseases from 1(c), explain the type or form of the disease (viral, bacterial, environmental, toxin), any possible vectors for transmission, ways to help prevent exposure or the spread of infection, and available treatments.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Health Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 18, p. 26)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scene Safety & Universal Precautions (Req 1): Size up scene hazards before approaching any victim, wear nonlatex barrier gloves when body fluids may be present, and follow BSA Two-Deep Leadership.
- **Common Myth:** Tetanus is only contracted by stepping on a rusty nail, because rust itself causes the infection.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Tetanus is caused by bacteria found in soil, dust, and manure that enter the body through any puncture wound, cut, or burn (p. 18).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Complex Disease Categories: Some diseases are easily described; others do not fall into neat categories (p. 18).
- Alternative Transmission Routes: Some diseases that generally are known as sexually transmitted diseases can be spread in other ways as well (p. 18).
- Vectors such as mosquitoes and ticks spread many diseases (p. 18).
- Other diseases are the result of environmental sources, such as secondhand smoke (p. 18).

### Req 2: Immunizations and Vaccine Protection
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the meaning of immunization. (b) Name eight diseases against which a young child should be immunized, two diseases against which everyone should be reimmunized periodically, and one immunization everyone should receive annually. (c) Using the list of diseases and conditions in requirement 1(b), discuss with your counselor those which currently have no immunization available.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Health Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 20, p. 21)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scene Safety & Universal Precautions (Req 2): Size up scene hazards before approaching any victim, wear nonlatex barrier gloves when body fluids may be present, and follow BSA Two-Deep Leadership.
- **Common Myth:** Receiving a single childhood vaccine provides permanent, lifelong immunity against every strain and type of infectious disease.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Although antibodies are long-lasting, the protection from some vaccines decreases over time and requires periodic booster shots, while influenza requires an annual vaccine (p. 20).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Disease Specific Antibody Production: The immune system makes antibodies for each particular disease that it meets (p. 20).
- Unique Vaccines Per Disease: Therefore, a different vaccine is needed for each disease (p. 20).
- Global Disease Vaccine Availability: Immunizations also are available for many other diseases that are uncommon in the United States but common in other parts of the world (p. 20).
- Americans typically receive these immunizations when they travel out of the country or join the armed forces (p. 20).

#### Req 2a: The meaning of immunization
**Official Requirement 2a:** Explain the meaning of immunization.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Health Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 20, p. 21)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Public Health Immunization Principles)
- **Common Myth:** One universal vaccine can stimulate the immune system to protect against multiple unrelated diseases at once.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Because the immune system makes specific antibodies for each particular pathogen it encounters, a distinct vaccine formulation is required for each disease (p. 20).

- **Common Myth:** Municipal wastewater treatment plants simply add chlorine to raw sewage and pump it directly back into rivers.
