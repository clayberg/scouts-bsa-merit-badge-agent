## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do ONE of the following: (a) Visit a municipal wastewater treatment facility or a solid-waste management operation in your community. (1) Describe how the facility safely treats and disposes of sewage or solid waste. (2) Discuss your visit and what you learned with your counselor. (3) Describe how sewage and solid waste should be disposed of under wilderness camping conditions. (b) Visit a food service facility, such as a restaurant or school cafeteria. (1) Observe food preparation, handling, and storage. Learn how the facility keeps food from becoming contaminated. (2) Find out what conditions allow microorganisms to multiply in food, what can be done to help prevent them from growing and spreading, and how to kill them. (3) Discuss the importance of using a thermometer to check food temperatures. (4) Discuss your visit and what you learned with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do the following: (a) Do ONE of the following: (1) Visit your city, county, state, or federal public health agency. (2) Familiarize yourself with your city, county, state, or a federal health agency's website. (b) After completing either 7(a)(1) or 7(a)(2), do ALL of the following: (1) Compare the four leading causes of mortality (death) in your community for any of the past five years with the four leading causes of disease in your community. Explain how the public health agency you visited is trying to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates of these leading causes of illness and death. (2) Explain the role of your health agency as it relates to the outbreak of diseases. (3) Discuss the kinds of public assistance the agency is able to provide in case of disasters such as floods, storms, tornadoes, earthquakes, and other acts of destruction. Your discussion can include the cleanup necessary after the disaster.

#### Req 2a: Biological Mechanisms of Immunization
**[Parent Directive]:** *Do the following*

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Adhere to sterile hypodermic injection protocols and dispose of all used needles in rigid biohazard containers.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For public health Requirement 2a (Explain the meaning of immunization), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Immunization introduces dead or attenuated antigens into the bloodstream to trigger an adaptive antibody response without illness. When real pathogens invade later, memory cells recognize the antigens and destroy them before infection takes hold.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Review Vaccination Records: Examine official state immunization forms to track completed childhood antigen series.
  - Step 2 - Identify Periodic Boosters: Calculate expiration dates for tetanus toxoid boosters requiring administration every ten years.
- **Common Myth:** Vaccines cause the exact active illness that they are engineered to prevent in healthy recipients.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Vaccines utilize killed pathogens, weakened antigens, or messenger RNA fragments incapable of causing active systemic disease.

### Req 3: Safe Drinking Water Sanitation Methods
**Official Requirement 3:** Discuss the importance of safe drinking water in terms of the spread of disease. Then, demonstrate two ways for making water safe to drink that can be used while at camp. In your demonstration, explain how dishes and utensils should be washed, dried, and kept sanitary at home and in camp.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Health Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 53)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 3 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Allow boiled water to cool before handling, and store chemical purification tablets in child-resistant moisture-proof containers.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does chemical chlorine disinfection require much longer contact time in cold snowmelt water?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Vigorous Thermal Disinfection: Rolling boils maintained for one continuous full minute kill vegetative waterborne microbial pathogens.
  - Halogen Chemical Treatment: Chlorine or iodine tablets require specific contact dwell times to destroy waterborne microbes.
  - Submicron Pore Filtration: Ceramic or hollow-fiber membranes physically trap encysted protozoans including Cryptosporidium and Giardia.
  - Three-Pot Sanitation Array: Camp dishwashing demands wash, clear rinse, and warm chemical sanitizing immersion stations.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Clarify Turbid Raw Water: Filter cloudy raw surface water through cloth to eliminate suspended mineral particulates.
  - Step 2 - Apply Thermal Disinfection: Heat clarified water until vigorous bubbling persists for one full continuous minute.
  - Step 3 - Dose Chemical Sanitizers: Add sodium dichloroisocyanurate or iodine tablets following exact manufacturer contact time tables.
  - Step 4 - Wash Camp Utensils: Scrub dishes in hot soapy water, rinse cleanly, and soak in sanitizer.
- **Common Myth:** Crystal clear mountain stream water is naturally pure and completely safe to drink untreated.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Clear wilderness streams frequently harbor microscopic Giardia cysts and bacterial pathogens shed by wild animals.

### Req 4: Vector Identification and Pest Management
**Official Requirement 4:** Explain what a vector is and how insects and rodents can be controlled in your home, in your community, and at camp. Tell why this is important. In your discussion, explain which vectors can be easily controlled by individuals and which ones require long-term, collective action.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Public Health Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 49, p. 50)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear rubber gloves and a particulate respirator when sweeping areas contaminated with rodent droppings to avoid hantavirus.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why can a homeowner control tick breeding in their yard while mosquito control requires municipal aircraft spraying?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Biological Vector Function: Female Anopheles and Culex mosquitoes transmit viral and parasitic pathogens through salivary secretions.
  - Mechanical Vector Transmission: Common houseflies transport enteric bacteria on hairy leg setae directly onto exposed foodstuffs.
  - Larval Habitat Abatement: Emptying residential stagnant water containers halts mosquito development cycles before adult emergence.
  - Rodent Harborage Denial: Sealing exterior foundation cracks prevents mice and rats from nesting near food supplies.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Survey Vector Habitats: Inspect perimeter ditches, clogged gutters, and tire piles for standing stagnant water.
  - Step 2 - Eliminate Breeding Reservoirs: Tip outdoor water receptacles and apply larvicidal Bacillus thuringiensis to persistent ponds.
  - Step 3 - Seal Structural Entryways: Install fine window mesh screens and pack wire wool into wall penetrations.
  - Step 4 - Deploy Food Enclosures: Store campsite rations in airtight rodent-resistant canisters away from canvas sleeping tents.
- **Common Myth:** Insect repellents like DEET kill mosquitoes immediately upon coming into contact with treated skin.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Repellents disrupt the olfactory receptors of insects to deter landing and biting without killing the insect.
