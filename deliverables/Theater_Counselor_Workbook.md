### Req 1: Dramatic Literature and Performance Critique
**Official Requirement 1:** See or read three full-length plays. Write a review of each. Discuss with your counselor the plot or story. If you chose to watch the plays, comment on the acting and the staging. Note: Watching plays on television, video, or as a movie is not permitted.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Theater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 12, p. 17)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does an actor's vocal inflection alter the subtext of a playwright's written dialogue?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Dramatic Text Analysis: Reading full scripts reveals internal character motivations and thematic structure before performance.
  - Live Performance Dynamics: Observing actors in physical space demonstrates vocal projection and spatial audience relationship.
  - Technical Craft Review: Effective reviews critique scenic design, lighting cues, costume choices, and directorial pacing.
  - Comparative Structural Critique: Comparing three different plays highlights contrasting approaches to dramatic conflict and resolution.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Script Selection: Select three distinct full scripts covering diverse theatrical eras and dramatic styles.
  - Step 2 - Performance Observation: Attend live productions while noting staging choices, vocal techniques, and scenic configurations.
  - Step 3 - Critical Composition: Draft structured critiques evaluating plot pacing, character development, and technical production values.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Discussion: Review dramatic themes, directorial interpretations, and staging effectiveness with your counselor.
- **Common Myth:** A play review simply summarizes the plot without analyzing technical elements or performances.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Live theater reviews evaluate dramatic structure, vocal projection, spatial blocking, and technical stagecraft.

### Req 2: One Act Script Composition
**Official Requirement 2:** Write a one-act play that will take at least 10 minutes to perform. The play must have a main character, conflict, and a climax.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Theater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 32)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What urgent need drives your protagonist to take decisive action in the opening scene?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Protagonist Objective Setup: Central characters must pursue clear, urgent objectives that encounter immediate resistance.
  - Conflict Progression Architecture: Rising action intensifies obstacles until the protagonist confronts a decisive dramatic turning point.
  - Stage Direction Specifics: Script formatting must incorporate essential physical movement, emotional beats, and scenic requirements.
  - Performance Timing Calculations: Ten minutes of playing time corresponds to approximately eight to ten script pages.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Scenario Outline: Outline a central protagonist objective, opposing forces, and an impactful climactic moment.
  - Step 2 - Draft Dialogue: Write authentic character exchanges that advance narrative stakes and establish distinct voices.
  - Step 3 - Stage Integration: Insert specific blocking notes, emotional cues, and technical requirements into the draft.
  - Step 4 - Timed Reading: Conduct a timed table reading with fellow actors to verify ten minute duration.
- **Common Myth:** Writing a short one-act play does not require defined conflict or structured character motivation.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A dramatic one-act play requires focused characters, clear stakes, and immediate rising action leading to a climax.
