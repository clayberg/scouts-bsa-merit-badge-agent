## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 6 (Discuss):** Rare, Threatened, or Endangered Species. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Do research on one endangered species found in your state. Learn about its natural habitat, why it is endangered, what is being done to preserve it, and how many individual species are left in the wild. Prepare a 100-word report about the species and include a drawing or photo. Present your report to your patrol or troop. (b) Do research on one species that was endangered or threatened but that has now recovered. Learn about how the species recovered, and what its new status is. Prepare a 100-word report on the species and include a drawing or photo. Present your report to your patrol or troop. (c) With your parent or guardian and counselor's approval, work with a natural resource professional to identify a completed project that has been designed to improve the habitat for a threatened or endangered species in your area. Visit the site and report on what you saw to your patrol or troop.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Discuss):** Pollination. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Investigate pollination and its importance to our environment and ecosystems. Make a list of five pollinators and the plants that attract them in your region. Explain the importance of pollinators and what Scouts can do to support pollinators in their area. (b) Visit an area with flowering plants during pollination season for an hour to observe pollination. Record which pollinators are attracted to which plant. Explain the importance of pollinators and what Scouts can do to support pollinators in their area. (c) Learn about the importance of pollination to agriculture, including the economic costs and benefits. Identify four crop-pollinator pairs. Explain the relationship of pollinators to agriculture.

### Req 2: Ecology and Ecosystem Dynamics
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Ecology. Do the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Choose an area approved by your counselor and observe (sight, sound, and smell) its ecosystem over a two-day period. (b) Make notes about the living, nonliving (e. g., rocks), and formerly living components. Include information about interactions among the components, including the food chain, predators, native species, and invasive species, and identify how human activities have affected the ecosystem.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Environmental Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 68, p. 83)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Survey the selected plot for stinging insects, poison ivy, and unstable terrain before beginning observations.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Environmental Science Req 2 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often believe that nonliving components like rocks and sunlight do not affect living organisms.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Ecosystems depend on continuous energy flow from sunlight through producers to top predators.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Abiotic Influences: Soil mineral chemistry and solar radiation regulate plant growth across terrestrial ecosystems.
- Biotic Interdependence: Decomposers return vital nitrogen compounds to topsoil through digestive metabolic activity.
- Trophic Structure: Primary producers capture solar radiation to support subsequent consumer levels throughout food webs.
- Temporal Changes: Daily temperature swings shift animal activity patterns between diurnal and nocturnal species.

### Req 3: Atmospheric Air Pollution Assessment
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Air Pollution. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Learn what Particulate Matter (PM) is, how PM gets into the air, what the harmful effects of PM are, and what is being done to reduce PM in the air. Then, perform an experiment to test for particulates that contribute to air pollution. (b) Discuss how air pollution and transportation affect each other by giving at least three examples. Then, compare two modes of transportation (e. g., gasoline-powered vs. electric vehicles, gasoline-powered car vs. bicycle, etc.). Resources: Your Car's Exhaust (video) Air Quality and Transportation (video) (c) Learn about the Clean Air Act. Make notes on when it was passed, its environmental goals, what progress has been made and what remains to be done to achieve the law's goals. Describe the impact, benefits, and costs of the law as well as what is required to implement and enforce the law.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Environmental Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 41, p. 45)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Handle petroleum jelly and glass slides with nitrile gloves to avoid skin contact and cuts.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often assume air pollution consists exclusively of visible smoke or noxious odors.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Particulate matter smaller than 2.5 microns can enter the human bloodstream through lung tissue.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Particle Dimensions: Microscopic airborne particles smaller than ten microns penetrate deep into bronchial airways.
- Emission Mechanisms: High-temperature combustion in vehicle engines converts hydrocarbon fuel into hazardous exhaust particles.
- Legislative Framework: National ambient air standards establish legal limits for six criteria air pollutants.
- Transit Efficiencies: Electric propulsion systems eliminate tailpipe emissions at local points of commuter operation.

### Req 4: Hydrological Water Quality Management
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Water Pollution. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Identify where your community sources water, how it is treated, and disposed. Obtain and review a water quality report from your area. (b) Identify a local or regional area that experiences periodic flooding and/or drought. Collect facts on prior event(s) and investigate the environmental impacts of these extreme events. (c) Learn about the Clean Water Act. Make notes on when it was passed, its environmental goals, what progress has been made and what remains to be done to achieve the law's goals. Describe the impact, benefits, and costs of the law as well as what is required to implement and enforce the law.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Environmental Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 45, p. 48)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never consume untreated field water samples and wear protective gloves when collecting stream water.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often think water from mountain streams or municipal taps requires no treatment before consumption.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Drinking water treatment involves coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, and chemical disinfection.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hydrologic Flow: Stormwater runoff transports nonpoint source contaminants directly into unprotected regional watersheds.
- Purification Stages: Chlorine disinfection and rapid sand filtration remove pathogenic bacteria from raw surface waters.
- Riparian Buffers: Vegetated stream banks reduce flood velocity and absorb excess nutrient agricultural runoff.
- Regulatory Protections: The Clean Water Act regulates point source pollutant discharges into navigable surface waters.

### Req 5: Terrestrial Land Pollution Mitigation
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Land Pollution. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) In an area (yard, park, golf course, farm, etc.) approved by your counselor, make a list of the pesticides, herbicides, and fertilizers used and how often they are applied. Identify the benefits of their use and the environmental impact, including effects on non-target species (including humans), what happens if the chemicals infiltrate into the groundwater, and what happens to any runoff of the chemicals. (b) Learn about the erosion process and identify an example of where erosion occurs. Determine where the eroded material ends up and how erosion can be minimized. (c) Learn about a land pollution incident that led to a site being listed on Environmental Protection Agency's Superfund National Priority List. Identify what caused the incident, what the effects were on the environment, what remediation has been done, and the current condition of the site.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Environmental Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 47, p. 52)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Do not touch unknown discarded chemical containers or enter designated hazardous waste cleanup sites.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Environmental Science Req 5 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often think synthetic chemical fertilizers remain permanently in the soil where applied.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Excess nitrogen fertilizers cause aquatic eutrophication, triggering algae blooms and severe hypoxia.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Chemical Migration: Rainwater dissolves unabsorbed lawn chemicals and leaches synthetic compounds toward aquifers.
- Sediment Transport: Rill and sheet erosion detach mineral topsoil particles during heavy precipitation events.
- Remediation Actions: Superfund protocols extract volatile toxic waste chemicals from buried industrial containment drums.
- Erosion Control: Terracing steep slopes and planting cover crops stabilize vulnerable agricultural soil profiles.
