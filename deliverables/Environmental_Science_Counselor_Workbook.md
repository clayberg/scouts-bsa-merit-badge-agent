## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 6 (Discuss):** Rare, Threatened, or Endangered Species. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Do research on one endangered species found in your state. Learn about its natural habitat, why it is endangered, what is being done to preserve it, and how many individual species are left in the wild. Prepare a 100-word report about the species and include a drawing or photo. Present your report to your patrol or troop. (b) Do research on one species that was endangered or threatened but that has now recovered. Learn about how the species recovered, and what its new status is. Prepare a 100-word report on the species and include a drawing or photo. Present your report to your patrol or troop. (c) With your parent or guardian and counselor's approval, work with a natural resource professional to identify a completed project that has been designed to improve the habitat for a threatened or endangered species in your area. Visit the site and report on what you saw to your patrol or troop.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Discuss):** Pollination. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Investigate pollination and its importance to our environment and ecosystems. Make a list of five pollinators and the plants that attract them in your region. Explain the importance of pollinators and what Scouts can do to support pollinators in their area. (b) Visit an area with flowering plants during pollination season for an hour to observe pollination. Record which pollinators are attracted to which plant. Explain the importance of pollinators and what Scouts can do to support pollinators in their area. (c) Learn about the importance of pollination to agriculture, including the economic costs and benefits. Identify four crop-pollinator pairs. Explain the relationship of pollinators to agriculture.

### Req 1: Environmental Science Foundation Principles
**Official Requirement 1:** Describe the meaning of environmental science in your own words. Explain how you think we can use science to understand, conserve, and improve our environment.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Environmental Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 77, p. 91)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does an environmental scientist differ from a political environmental activist in methodology?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Disciplinary Integration: Physical sciences collaborate with biological principles to explain interactions among natural earth systems.
  - Objective Analysis: Scientists collect measurable data to evaluate human impacts on planetary life support systems.
  - Conservation Practice: Ecological knowledge guides practical management decisions that protect fragile habitats from degradation.
  - Ecological Systems: Complex food webs transfer energy across distinct trophic levels within established biomes.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Scope Definition: Record four core scientific disciplines contributing to environmental problem analysis today.
  - Step 2 - System Mapping: Diagram how physical atmospheric factors alter local plant communities in your region.
  - Step 3 - Problem Analysis: Select one regional environmental problem and document three quantitative factors causing it.
  - Step 4 - Solution Drafting: Propose two scientifically verifiable conservation methods to mitigate your selected problem.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often think environmental science is identical to environmental activism or political advocacy.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Environmental science is an interdisciplinary academic field combining biology, chemistry, physics, and geology to solve problems.

#### Req 5a: Agricultural Chemical Application Assessment
**[Parent Directive]:** *Land Pollution. Do ONE of the following and discuss with your counselor*
**Official Requirement 5a:** In an area (yard, park, golf course, farm, etc.) approved by your counselor, make a list of the pesticides, herbicides, and fertilizers used and how often they are applied. Identify the benefits of their use and the environmental impact, including effects on non-target species (including humans), what happens if the chemicals infiltrate into the groundwater, and what happens to any runoff of the chemicals.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Environmental Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 47, p. 52)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 5a Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For environmental science Requirement 5a (In an area (yard, park, golf course, farm, etc.) approved by your counselor, make a list of), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Documenting synthetic lawn chemicals reveals active herbicide ingredients applied to landscaped turf. Overapplication leads to groundwater leaching and unintended mortality of beneficial organisms.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Site Survey: Inventory all synthetic fertilizers, herbicides, and pesticides stored on the property.
  - Step 2 - Application Mapping: Calculate the chemical treatment frequency and active chemical ingredients across application areas.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often assume garden fertilizers do not count as chemical land pollutants.
