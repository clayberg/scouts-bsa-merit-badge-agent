### Req 1: Animation Definition and Historical Origins
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** General Knowledge. Do the following: (a) In your own words, describe to your counselor what animation is. (b) Discuss with your counselor a brief history of animation.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Apply BSA Digital Safety and Cyber Chip guidelines during Animation Requirement 1: protect personal credentials and private data while working on animation definition and historical origins, verify software sources before downloading, and include a parent or counselor on all online communications.
- **Common Myth:** Animation was invented in the twentieth century only after motion picture film cameras and computers were developed.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Attempts to create the illusion of motion date back to prehistoric cave paintings lit by flickering firelight, ancient hand shadows, and nineteenth-century optical devices like the phenakistoscope and zoetrope (p. 21).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Ancient Storytelling and Motion: The history of animation is as old as the history of story telling (p. 21).
- Prehistoric Painted Cave Art: The word animate means "alive." When early humans first painted animals and hunting scenes on cave walls, they lit fires to show off their work (p. 21).
- Flickering Firelight Motion Illusions: The dancing flames cast irregular light in the caves, making the images of prehistoric animals appear to be alive and moving (p. 21).
- Projected Hand Shadow Animals: Also, from the earliest times, children worldwide have used a light source to project hand shadows onto a wall to create the shapes of animals (p. 21).

- **Common Myth:** A full-length animated movie is typically drawn and completed from start to finish by a single solo artist.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Feature animations require hundreds of collaborating specialists across storyboarding, animatics, modeling, rigging, texturing, animation, lighting, and effects, and animation is widely used beyond entertainment in science, education, and design (p. 66).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- From Sketches To Animatics: Scenes are created first with a rough sketch, then a simplified mock-up called an "animatic," and then the final image (p. 66).
- Studying Feature Film Credits: The next time you watch an animated movie, watch the credits at the end (p. 66).
- Large Collaborative Studio Teams: You might be amazed by how many artists are required to generate a full-length feature film (p. 66).
- The different disciplines include animators, modelers, lighters, riggers, texture painters, effects artists (p. 66).
