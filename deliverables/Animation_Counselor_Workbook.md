#### Req 1a: Core Mechanics of Visual Motion
**[Parent Directive]:** *General Knowledge. Do the following*
**Official Requirement 1a:** In your own words, describe to your counselor what animation is.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 23)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For animation Requirement 1a (In your own words, describe to your counselor what animation is), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Animation relies on the brain retaining an image for a fraction of a second after it disappears. This physiological trait allows sequential still pictures to appear as continuous fluid motion.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Motion Definition: Describe the optical illusion of movement created by consecutive sequential drawings.
  - Step 2 - Device Research: Examine early mechanical devices including the zoetrope, thaumatrope, and phenakistoscope.
- **Common Myth:** Novices assume smooth animation requires real physical movement captured continuously on camera.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** All animation relies entirely on static frames filmed individually and played back at sufficient speed.

### Req 2: Core Principles of Believable Animation
**Official Requirement 2:** Principles of Animation. Choose five of the following 12 principles of animation, and discuss how each one makes an animation appear more believable: squash and stretch, anticipation, staging, straight ahead action and pose to pose, follow through and overlapping action, slow in and slow out, arcs, secondary action, timing, exaggeration, solid drawing, and appeal.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 35, p. 37)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does removing anticipation before a fast movement make an action feel jarring or unnatural to viewers?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Squash and Stretch: Deforming a character during impact conveys weight, flexibility, and dynamic physical mass.
  - Anticipation Poses: Preceding a major action with backward preparation cues viewers for upcoming movements.
  - Staging Clarity: Arranging screen elements clearly directs viewer attention toward the primary story action.
  - Follow Through: Residual momentum carries appendages forward after the main body stops moving entirely.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Principle Selection: Select five core principles from the traditional twelve animation guidelines to study.
  - Step 2 - Visual Analysis: Examine professional animated film clips to identify specific implementations of each principle.
  - Step 3 - Sketch Demonstration: Draw simple pencil thumbnails demonstrating physical contrast between rigid and dynamic motion.
  - Step 4 - Believability Discussion: Explain to your counselor how physics and timing establish believable character actions.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often think squash and stretch means altering an object's overall volume when distorting it.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Believable squash and stretch requires maintaining identical total volume regardless of how drastically the shape distorts.

### Req 5: Animation Career Pathways
**Official Requirement 5:** Careers. Learn about three career opportunities in animation. Pick one and find out about the education, training, and experience required for this profession. Discuss your findings with your counselor. Explain why this profession might interest you.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 85, p. 91)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Career & Profession Exploration Card):** Req 5 Career & Professional Pathway Investigation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do employers evaluate a candidate based on a concise two minute demo reel rather than total hours of work?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Storyboard artists translate written scripts into visual cinematic sequences defining shot compositions.
  - Technical directors construct digital skeletal controls and mathematical deformation rigs for character animators.
  - Effects specialists simulate natural physical phenomena including smoke, water fluid dynamics, and explosions.
  - A professional demo reel displays concise, high quality animation samples lasting two minutes.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Career Exploration: Research three distinct animation career paths encompassing creative and technical studio responsibilities.
  - Step 2 - Specialization Deep Dive: Select one specific profession to examine required collegiate education, apprenticeships, and portfolio standards.
  - Step 3 - Experience Mapping: Identify necessary software proficiencies, internship opportunities, and demo reel presentation guidelines for employment.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Presentation: Discuss your findings with your counselor and explain personal motivations for pursuing that career.
- **Common Myth:** Aspiring artists often assume animation studios hire generalists who perform every phase of production.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Major studios primarily recruit specialists who excel at specific pipeline tasks like rigging, lighting, or character performance.
