### Req 1: Distinct American Indian Cultural Areas
**Official Requirement 1:** Identify the different American Indian cultural areas. Explain what makes them each unique.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Indian Culture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 55)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (American Indian Cultural Areas)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Distinct American Indian Cultural Areas discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** All American Indian tribes across North America lived in portable hide tipis and followed nomadic buffalo herds.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Each cultural area adapted its dwellings and lifestyle to the local environment, such as Southwest Pueblo tribes building permanent stone and adobe homes (p. 7).

### Req 2: Tribal Lifeways Governance and Trade
**Official Requirement 2:** Research an American Indian tribe, group, or nation. Tell your counselor about traditional dwellings, way of life, tribal government, religious beliefs, family and clan relationships, language, clothing styles, arts and crafts, food cultivation, foraging and preparation, means of getting around, games, customs in warfare, and where and how they live today.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Indian Culture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 57, p. 71)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (American Indian Tribe Group or)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Tribal Lifeways Governance and Trade discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** American Indian tribes lived in isolated communities and rarely traded goods outside their immediate villages.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Tribes maintained extensive trade networks, such as Plateau groups traveling by dugout canoe along the Columbia and Fraser rivers to exchange coastal and interior goods (p. 57).

### Req 3: Indigenous Languages Place Names and Leaders
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Learn 10 common terms in an American Indian language and their meanings. (b) Identify the meaning of 10 place names of American Indian origin in the United States. (c) Name five well-known American Indian leaders, either from the past or people of today. Give their tribes or nations. Describe what they did or do now that makes them notable.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Indian Culture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 92)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Indigenous Languages Place Names and Leaders discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** American Indian languages and cultural traditions belong only to the past and are no longer spoken or practiced today.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Contemporary American Indians are actively relearning ancestral languages, reviving traditional arts and ceremonies, and preserving their tribal identities in modern society (p. 17).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Indians are relearning lost traditions and languages and reviving their arts and crafts, their songs, dances, and stories (p. 17).
- Reclaiming Tribal Self-Determination: Many have chosen not to disappear into America's "melting pot." Indians are reclaiming control over what happens to them (p. 17).
- Choosing Modern Tribal Futures: They want to decide for themselves how they will live in the modern world (p. 17).
- Balancing Heritage and Modernity: The question is, how can they keep cultural roots alive and protect and preserve tribal identities, yet still be a part of modern society (p. 17).

- **Common Myth:** European colonists survived in North America solely by relying on crops and farming methods brought from Europe.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**

### Req 5: Museum Collections and Contemporary Gatherings
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Visit a museum either in-person or virtually to see American Indian exhibitions and collections. Discuss with your counselor what you observed or learned from two exhibitions and identify 10 artifacts by tribe or nation, their shape, size, and use. (b) Attend a contemporary American Indian gathering. Discuss with your counselor proper etiquette for attending as well as what you learned and observed during your visit.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Indian Culture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 45)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Conduct all counselor discussions, community interviews, and research for American Indian Culture Requirement 5 on museum collections and contemporary gatherings under BSA Youth Protection two-deep leadership, copying a parent or guardian on any external correspondence.
- **Common Myth:** No American Indian language had a written script created by a native speaker during the nineteenth century.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Sequoyah created a written syllabary for the Cherokee language in the early 1800s, enabling widespread literacy among the Cherokee people (p. 37).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Eighteenth-Century Population Decline: By the early 1700s, their populations had dropped about 75 percent (p. 37).
- Resilience and Community Rebuilding: But despite many setbacks, including wars, starvation, and disease, they rebuilt their lives so that by the early 1800s they lived (p. 37).
- Adapting Agricultural Business Practices: They owned plantations and slaves and had learned new farming methods and the white people's ways of doing business (p. 37).
- Sequoyah, a Cherokee, produced a written version of the Cherokee language, the first written Indian language, so that his people (p. 37).
