### Req 1: North American Indigenous Cultural Areas
**Official Requirement 1:** Identify the different American Indian cultural areas. Explain what makes them each unique.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Indian Culture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 55)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How did the natural resources of the Pacific Northwest shape totem carving traditions?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Arctic Adaptation: Marine mammal hunting with skin boats provided essential sustenance for northern coastal communities.
  - Eastern Woodlands: Matrilineal agricultural societies cultivated maize, beans, and squash within permanent fortified palisade villages.
  - Plains Nomads: Portable conical hide tipis supported seasonal bison hunting across expansive inland grasslands.
  - Adobe Pueblos: Multistory sandstone and mud masonry villages supported intensive canal irrigation desert agriculture.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Map Outlines: Trace regional environmental borders across a blank continental North American geographic map.
  - Step 2 - Identify Zones: Label ten standard cultural zones including Arctic, Plateau, Plains, and Southeast.
  - Step 3 - Record Traits: List primary food sources, dominant shelter forms, and transport methods per region.
  - Step 4 - Compare Adaptations: Explain environmental resource differences that created divergent technological solutions between neighboring regions.
- **Common Myth:** Indigenous peoples shared a single monolithic culture and identical lifestyle across North America.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Over five hundred distinct nations inhabited North America with drastically different languages, dwellings, and customs.

### Req 2: Tribal Nation Ethnographic Monograph
**Official Requirement 2:** Research an American Indian tribe, group, or nation. Tell your counselor about traditional dwellings, way of life, tribal government, religious beliefs, family and clan relationships, language, clothing styles, arts and crafts, food cultivation, foraging and preparation, means of getting around, games, customs in warfare, and where and how they live today.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America American Indian Culture Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 57, p. 71)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the clan system of your chosen nation prevent conflict of interest during leadership selection?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Kinship Clans: Matrilineal clan lineages traditionally determine property inheritance, ceremonial duties, and leadership selection.
  - Dwelling Construction: Builders selected local timber, bark, or earth based on structural climate requirements.
  - Subsistence Methods: Agricultural tribes combined domesticated companion crops with seasonal foraging and controlled game hunting.
  - Tribal Governance: Hereditary clan matriarchs appointed civil peace chiefs while councils deliberated by consensus.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Select Nation: Choose one federally recognized tribe and obtain official tribal history documentation.
  - Step 2 - Analyze Systems: Outline social clan structures, dwelling architecture, clothing styles, and subsistence methods.
  - Step 3 - Document Governance: Record historical leadership councils and modern sovereign constitutional government branches.
  - Step 4 - Present Findings: Deliver an organized comprehensive report detailing all cultural categories to the counselor.
- **Common Myth:** Traditional tribal governance disappeared completely after historical relocation onto federal reservations.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Tribes operate modern sovereign constitutional governments with executive, legislative, and judicial court branches today.
