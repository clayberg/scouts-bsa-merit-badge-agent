## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Discuss the importance of having a physical examination each year. Discuss why overall health, immunizations, medications, allergies, and medical history are covered during an examination. Tell your counselor when you last underwent a physical examination. (b) Explain the importance of maintaining good health habits, especially during training, and how the use of tobacco products, alcohol, and other harmful substances can negatively affect your health and your performance in athletic activities. (c) Define a healthy diet and explain the importance of maintaining a healthy diet.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Discuss):** Do TWO of the following and discuss with your counselor: (a) Research and identify two ways you can continue participating in multisport after completing this merit badge. (b) Research an Olympic, Paralympic, or professional triathlete (past or current). Share information on their background in the sport and what inspires you most about this individual. (c) Demonstrate leadership by starting a training group and educating your peers on the importance of physical activity, nutrition, and the disciplines of multisport. (d) Sign up for and participate in a sanctioned multisport event in your area. (e) Volunteer at a local multisport event, running race, biking event, swim meet, or adaptive sporting event.

### Req 1: Multisport Hazard Identification Assessment
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter during multisport activities and what you should do to anticipate, prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while participating in multisport events, including abrasions, blisters, concussions, contusions, dehydration, hypothermia, overheating, sprains, and strains.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Multisport Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 20)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Suspend all water segments immediately whenever lightning occurs within ten miles of the course venue.
- **Common Myth:** Athletes only face dehydration in hot summer weather during the running segment.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Multisport activities require structured prevention plans that address environmental exposure, traffic control, and physical trauma across all disciplines.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hypothermia Prevention: Cold open water immersion demands wetsuits to maintain core body temperature effectively.
- Heat Cramp Management: Oral rehydration salts restore depleted electrolytes during extended endurance exertion in heat.
- Ablative Skin Protection: Petroleum lubricants reduce friction blisters and painful chafe marks around sensitive seams.
- Head Injury Assessment: Concussion protocols mandate immediate removal from training following any sudden bike crash.

- **Common Myth:** Active youth with no existing medical symptoms do not need annual medical examinations.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Annual physical examinations detect silent cardiac anomalies and verify musculoskeletal alignment before intense conditioning begins.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Clinical Assessment Value: Annual physical examinations uncover hidden cardiovascular anomalies and verify overall muscular skeletal readiness.
- Immunization Record Review: Up to date tetanus immunizations guard against infection from unexpected outdoor bicycle crashes.
- Substance Avoidance Protocol: Tobacco smoke restricts bronchial airways and severely reduces cellular oxygen exchange during endurance.
- Endurance Nutrition Balance: Carbohydrate intake replenishes vital glycogen stores and supports rapid muscular recovery after workouts.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Multisport Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 53)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Bicycle helmets must bear CPSC safety certification stickers and remain undamaged by past impacts.
- **Common Myth:** Athletes can complete the three segments of a triathlon in any sequence they prefer.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Standard sprint triathlons follow an exact sequence of swim, bike, and run to maximize open water safety.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Event Order Logic: Swimming precedes cycling to prevent dangerous muscle cramps in deep open waters.
- Distance Classification Rules: Sprint multisport races feature shorter courses designed to test initial multi-sport pacing strategies.
- Discipline Limiter Focus: Concentrated practice on weaker segments produces the greatest overall finishing time reductions.
- Essential Gear Fit: Certified helmets must sit level above eyebrows and buckle snugly under chin.
