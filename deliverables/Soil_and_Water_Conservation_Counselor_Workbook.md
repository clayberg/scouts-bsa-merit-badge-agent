## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 4 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Explain what a watershed is. (b) Outline the smallest watershed that you can find on a contour map. (c) Outline, as far as the map will allow, the next larger watershed that also has the smallest one in it. (d) Explain what a river basin is. Tell why all people living in a river basin should be concerned about land and water use in the basin. (e) Explain what an aquifer is and why it can be important to communities.

## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Do the following: (a) Make a drawing to show the hydrologic cycle. (b) Demonstrate at least two of the following actions of water in relation to soil: percolation, capillary action, precipitation, evaporation, and transpiration. (c) Explain how removal of vegetation will affect the way water runs off a watershed. (d) Tell how uses of forest, range, and farmland affect usable water supply. (e) Explain how industrial use affects water supply.

#### Req 1a: Soil Definition and Formation Mechanics
**Official Requirement 1a:** Tell what soil is. Tell how it is formed.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Soil and Water Conservation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 25)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Soil develops as climate and living organisms weather parent bedrock into fertile loose horizons over centuries. The resulting mixture holds essential water, atmospheric gases, and decaying organic detritus.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Core Extraction: Collect vertical soil core samples using a stainless tube sampler across representative terrain.
  - Step 2 - Ribbon Assessment: Moisten fine aggregate between thumb and forefinger to estimate sand, silt, clay ratios.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts believe dirt and soil are identical substances created through mechanical grinding alone.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Soil represents a complex ecological system combining weathered mineral particles with living biological communities.

#### Req 2a: Mechanics of Soil Particle Detachment
**[Parent Directive]:** *Do the following*
**Official Requirement 2a:** Define soil erosion.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Soil and Water Conservation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 39, p. 41)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For soil and water conservation Requirement 2a (Define soil erosion), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Soil erosion describes the physical process whereby water, wind, ice, or gravity loosens and carries away soil particles from land surfaces. The detachment removes the nutrient-rich surface horizon necessary for vegetation.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Site Identification: Locate an active field incision showing visible root exposure and sediment deposition fans.
  - Step 2 - Erosion Classification: Determine whether detachment occurred through uniform sheet wash, distinct rills, or deep gullies.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts believe erosion begins only when rushing water forms visible rushing surface streams.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Erosion begins the exact instant individual raindrops strike bare earth and shatter dry soil aggregates.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Every dry ridge, suburban rooftop, and city street belongs to a specific drainage watershed.
