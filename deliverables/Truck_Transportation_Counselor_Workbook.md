> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Gasoline and Diesel Truck Engines discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Diesel engines use heavy-duty spark plugs to ignite fuel inside the cylinders just like gasoline engines.

**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Visit a truck terminal and complete items 4(a) through 4(e). After your visit, share what you have learned with your counselor. (a) Find out what kind of maintenance program the company follows to help keep its fleet, drivers, and the roadway safe. (b) Find out how dispatchers maintain communication with drivers on the road. > **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Conduct all counselor discussions, community interviews, and research for Truck Transportation Requirement 4 on truck terminal operations and safety under BSA Youth Protection two-deep leadership, copying a parent or guardian on any external correspondence.
- **Common Myth:** When the federal government deregulated the trucking industry in 1980, it eliminated highway safety and maintenance rules.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Motor Carrier Act of 1980 relaxed economic rules on routes and pricing, while strict federal safety, inspection, and hours-of-service rules remained in force (p. 18).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Fleet Maintenance and Safety: Keeping trucks well-maintained helps ensure not only the driver'ssafety and safety on the road, but also helps keep trucks working so that carriers (p. 18).
- Growth of Federal Oversight: As the trucking industry grew, it became so important to the economy of the United States that the federal government stepped in to set some rules (p. 18).
- Through the Motor Carrier Act of 1935, the government regulated where a trucking company, or carrier, could operate and what it could charge (p. 18).
- An agency known as the Interstate Commerce Commission enforced these rules, which were very helpful at the time they were introduced (p. 18).
