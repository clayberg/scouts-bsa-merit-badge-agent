### Req 1: Local Motor Carrier Network
**Official Requirement 1:** List the major truck lines serving your town.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Truck Transportation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 12)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what specific ways do private carriage fleets differ from common carriers serving regional commercial distribution centers?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Carrier Classification: Common carriers offer scheduled freight service to the general shipping public daily.
  - Terminal Operations: Break-bulk terminals consolidate smaller local shipments into outbound line-haul freight trailers.
  - Motor carrier routing guides identify specific service points across designated industrial zones.
  - Modern commercial fleets utilize satellite tracking to coordinate regional freight delivery schedules.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Directory Search: Review municipal shipping registries to identify motor freight lines operating regional routes.
  - Step 2 - Terminal Survey: Document commercial freight terminals located inside surrounding industrial and logistics distribution parks.
  - Step 3 - Classification Mapping: Classify each identified trucking line as either truckload or less-than-truckload carrier.
  - Step 4 - Service Analysis: Compile direct point destination routes offered by the primary local transport carriers.
- **Common Myth:** Every motor carrier operates identical routes with uniform terminal network capabilities across communities.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Regional less-than-truckload carriers maintain dedicated terminal docks to consolidate daily cross-dock freight.

### Req 3: Gasoline and Diesel Powertrains
**Official Requirement 3:** Describe the difference between the gasoline engine and the diesel engine that power trucks. List the advantages of each.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Truck Transportation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 32)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (2-Column Comparison Table):** Req 3 Technical Comparison Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do heavy long-haul tractor-trailers operate almost exclusively with turbocharged diesel internal combustion engines?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Compression Ignition: Diesel cylinders compress air until intense heat auto-ignites injected fuel mist smoothly.
  - Spark Ignition: Gasoline engines ignite fuel and air mixtures using timed electrical spark plugs.
  - Torque Output: Heavy diesel engines produce tremendous low-speed pulling torque for moving freight.
  - Thermal Efficiency: Diesel combustion cycles extract greater usable energy from every gallon of fuel.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Combustion Review: Compare spark ignition cycles against high-pressure compression auto-ignition operational combustion chamber mechanics.
  - Step 2 - Torque Comparison: Evaluate torque output curves and power delivery bands between both engine varieties.
  - Step 3 - Fuel Analysis: Contrast thermal energy density differences between commercial ultra-low sulfur diesel and gasoline.
  - Step 4 - Application Charting: Construct comparative charts listing equipment acquisition costs versus extended fleet maintenance longevity.
- **Common Myth:** Gasoline and diesel truck engines share identical ignition mechanics and fuel delivery components.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Diesel engines utilize high compression ratios to ignite fuel without electrical spark plug systems.
