#### Req 1a: Tool and Reed Hazard Mitigation
**Official Requirement 1a:** Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while using basketry tools and materials, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Basketry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 9)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Carry shears closed with point down and wipe basin floor leaks immediately.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For basketry Requirement 1a (Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while using basketry), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Working with reed shears, awls, and water basins presents puncture, cutting, and slip hazards. Weavers anticipate risks by organizing tools safely and wiping up water spills promptly.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Setup Area: Position water tubs securely on stable benches beside dry towel staging mats.
  - Step 2 - Inspect Tools: Check cutting shears and packing tools for dull blades or loose joints.
- **Common Myth:** Dull craft shears are safer than sharp shears when trimming thick basket stakes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dull blades slip off round reed fibers, increasing the likelihood of painful hand injuries.

#### Req 2a: Four Primary Basket Architecture Classifications
**Official Requirement 2a:** Show your counselor that you are able to identify the following types of baskets: plaited, coiled, ribbed, and wicker.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Basketry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 12)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For basketry Requirement 2a (Show your counselor that you are able to identify the following types of baskets: plaited,), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Baskets fall into four distinct construction categories based on foundation materials and fabrication mechanics. Plaited baskets use flat splints, coiled use stitched bundles, ribbed use intersecting hoops, and wicker uses rigid stakes with flexible weavers.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Examine Foundation: Identify whether the base uses flat splints, spiral coils, or wooden hoops.
  - Step 2 - Classify Structure: Determine if spokes remain upright or bend across a structural ribbed framework.
- **Common Myth:** Coiled baskets use identical over-and-under weaving actions as wicker and ribbed baskets.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Coiled baskets rely on continuous sewing stitches that bind successive rounds of core material.

#### Req 2b: Core Weaving Pattern Structural Techniques
**Official Requirement 2b:** Describe three different types of weaves to your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Basketry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 12)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2b Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For basketry Requirement 2b (Describe three different types of weaves to your counselor), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Weavers select distinct patterns including simple randing, pairing, and three-rod waling to achieve structural goals. Each weave controls spoke spacing, lateral tension, and decorative surface texture.
- **Common Myth:** Pairing and waling are purely decorative variations with zero added structural strength.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Pairing locks upright spokes in place and prevents outward bowing under heavy loads.

#### Req 3a: Square Base Utility Basket Weaving
**[Parent Directive]:** *Plan and weave the following projects*
**Official Requirement 3a:** Square basket (b) Round basket (c) Campstool seat.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Basketry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 34)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For basketry Requirement 3a (Square basket (b) Round basket (c) Campstool seat), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Square baskets begin with an interlaced grid of flat reed stakes clamped securely during bottom layout. Scoring the stakes allows clean 90-degree bends before side weavers build straight vertical walls.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Soak Materials: Immerse reeds in warm water until fibers bend freely without cracking.
  - Step 2 - Lay Foundation: Weave the bottom grid or insert spokes into slotted wooden bases.
- **Common Myth:** Square basket corners form naturally without pre-bending or scoring the base stakes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Failing to score flat stakes causes ragged breaks and rounded, misshapen corners.

#### Req 3c: Campstool Seat Interlocking Webbing Construction
**[Parent Directive]:** *Plan and weave the following projects*
**Official Requirement 3c:** Campstool seat.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Basketry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 34)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3c Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** For basketry Requirement 3c (Campstool seat), what key BSA pamphlet standard and safety or verification step must you explain or demonstrate to your counselor?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Campstool seating uses wide flat ash or cane splints wrapped continuously around folding wooden rungs. Weaving the cross splints in a herringbone or twill pattern creates a durable, supportive seat.
- **Common Myth:** Splints can be wrapped loose because natural fiber shrinkage tightens the seat later.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Loose initial splint wrapping causes sagging and permanent seat failure under occupant weight.
