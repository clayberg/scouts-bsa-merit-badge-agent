## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 4 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Attend either a Scouting America National Jamboree, OR World Scout Jamboree, OR a national Scouting America high-adventure base. While there, keep a journal documenting your day-to-day experiences. Upon your return, report to your counselor what you did, saw, and learned. You may include photos, brochures, and other documents in your report. (b) Write or visit the National Scouting Museum. Obtain information about this facility. Give a short report on what you think the role of this museum is in the Scouting program. (c) Visit an exhibit of Scouting memorabilia or a local museum with a Scouting history gallery, or (with your parent or guardian's permission and counselor's approval) visit with someone in your council who is recognized as a dedicated Scouting historian or memorabilia collector. Learn what you can about the history of Scouting America. Give a short report to your counselor on what you saw and learned.
- [ ] **Req 5 (Prepare):** Research the history of your unit, council, summer camp, or Order of the Arrow lodge. With the permission of a parent or guardian, your research methods may include an internet or library search, interviews with Scout leaders, or visits to locations where you learn about your topic. Prepare an oral or written report, a presentation, or a video of your research and review it with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Discuss):** Interview at least three people (different from those you interviewed for requirement 5) over the age of 40 who were Scouts. Find out about their Scouting experiences. Ask about the impact that Scouting has had on their lives. Share what you learned with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 9 (Prepare):** Careers. Do ONE of the following: (a) Explore careers related to Scouting. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. With permission of your parent or guardian, your research methods may include an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career. (b) Explore how you could use knowledge and skills from this merit badge to serve as a Scouting volunteer. Research any training needed, expenses, and how you could serve as a unit, district, council, regional, or national volunteer. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.

### Req 1: Lord Baden-Powell and Early Scouting
**Official Requirement 1:** Discuss with your counselor the life and times of Lord Baden-Powell of Gilwell. Explain why he felt a program like Scouting would be good for the young men of his day. Include in your discussion how Scouting was introduced in the United States, and the origins of Boy Scouting and Cub Scouting under Baden-Powell.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scouting Heritage Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 9, p. 13)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss With Your Counselor The)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Lord Baden-Powell and Early Scouting discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Lord Baden-Powell originally wrote Scouting for Boys as a recruitment manual to draft children directly into the British military.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Baden-Powell created the Scouting program to help urban youth build physical fitness, outdoor self-reliance, and strong character during a time when overcrowding and factory labor left many young men unhealthy (p. 9).

### Req 3: Evolution of Scouting Programs
**Official Requirement 3:** Discuss with your counselor how Scouting's programs have developed over time and been adapted to fit different age groups and interests (Cub Scouting, Scouts BSA, Exploring, Venturing).
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scouting Heritage Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 38, p. 40)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss With Your Counselor How)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Evolution of Scouting Programs discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Explorer Scouts started out wearing forest-green uniforms and organizing into posts from the very first day in 1935.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Explorer Scouts initially wore the standard Boy Scout uniform with an 'Explorer Scout, BSA' strip over the right pocket before introducing forest-green uniforms and posts in the 1940s (p. 38).

- Creation of Explorer Scouts: In 1935, the BSA created a program called Explorer Scouts as one option in Senior Scouting (p. 38).
- It offered to older Boy Scouts a land-based alternative to Sea Scouting (p. 38).
