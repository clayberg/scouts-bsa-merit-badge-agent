## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Discuss):** List several reasons why you are important to your family and discuss this with your parent or guardian and with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 3 (Prepare):** Prepare a list of your regular home duties or chores (at least five) and do them for 90 days. Keep a record of how often you do each of them. Discuss with your counselor the effect your chores had on your family.
- [ ] **Req 5 (Prepare):** Plan and carry out a project that involves the participation of your family. After completing the project, discuss the following with your counselor: (a) The objective or goal of the project (b) How individual members of your family participated (c) The results of the project
- [ ] **Req 6 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: Note: Some of the issues surrounding requirement 6 for the family meeting could be considered of a personal nature. Use discretion when reviewing this requirement with the Scout. Discussion of each of these subjects will very likely carry over to more than one family meeting. (a) Discuss with your counselor how to plan and carry out a family meeting. (b) Prepare a meeting agenda that includes the following topics, review it with your parent or guardian, and then carry out one or more family meetings: (1) How living the principles of the Scout Oath and Scout Law contributes to your family life (2) The greatest dangers and addictions facing youth in today's society (examples include mental health challenges, use of tobacco products, alcohol, or drugs and other items such as debts, social media, etc.) (3) Discuss with a parent or guardian how bodily changes can affect the choices you make as you physically and mentally mature. (4) Personal and family finances (5) A crisis situation within your family and whom you can turn to for support during these situations. (6) The effect of technology on your family (7) Good etiquette and manners

### Req 1: Foundations of Family Structure
**Official Requirement 1:** Prepare an outline on what a family is and discuss this with your counselor. Tell why families are important to individuals and to society. Discuss how the actions of one member can affect other members.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Family Life Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 9, p. 10)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the economic stability of individual households influence the resilience of an entire town?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Societal Function: Families transmit cultural heritage, moral principles, and economic support across successive human generations.
  - Mutual Interdependence: Personal decisions regarding chores, spending, and communication immediately influence overall household harmony.
  - Individual Well-Being: Safe domestic spaces provide emotional security essential for personal identity and healthy development.
  - Behavioral Repercussions: Thoughtless words or neglected duties create emotional friction and erode domestic trust.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Topic Brainstorming: Draft an outline defining nuclear, extended, blended, and single-parent domestic living arrangements.
  - Step 2 - Societal Analysis: Write specific examples showing how healthy households stabilize neighborhoods and civil communities.
  - Step 3 - Impact Assessment: Document three distinct personal habits that positively or negatively alter household routines.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Review: Present the completed conceptual outline during a formal merit badge conference session.
- **Common Myth:** Families only consist of parents and children living under one traditional roof.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Families encompass diverse supportive structures including extended relatives, guardians, build placements, and blended households.

- **Common Myth:** Children only contribute to a family when they earn income or drive cars.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Youth provide vital emotional support, household assistance, companionship, and continuity that sustain family morale.

### Req 3: Home Chores and Duty Tracking
**Official Requirement 3:** Prepare a list of your regular home duties or chores (at least five) and do them for 90 days. Keep a record of how often you do each of them. Discuss with your counselor the effect your chores had on your family.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Family Life Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 26, p. 58)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Activity & Observation Log):** Req 3 Quantitative Field Observation & Activity Log
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How did recording your chores daily change your attitude toward helping out around the house?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Routine Maintenance: Daily cleaning tasks maintain sanitary conditions and prevent clutter from causing household accidents.
  - Accountability Tracking: Keeping a written log promotes honesty, disciplined routine, and consistent work completion.
  - Labor Distribution: Sharing domestic tasks equally frees up collective time for relaxation and recreation.
  - Long-Term Habit: Ninety days of deliberate practice transforms assigned chores into automatic lifetime habits.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Chore Selection: Select at least five regular home chores with approval from your guardian.
  - Step 2 - Log Construction: Design a ninety day chart tracking chore names, dates, and completion status.
  - Step 3 - Daily Execution: Perform each assigned household duty diligently and record the frequency without interruption.
  - Step 4 - Reflection Meeting: Review chore consistency and domestic benefits during the final counselor evaluation session.
- **Common Myth:** Completing chores for one week is enough if you write down projections later.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The requirement demands an authentic ninety day log demonstrating real persistence and measurable habit formation.

### Req 4: Independent Home Improvement Project
**Official Requirement 4:** With the approval of your parent or guardian and your counselor, decide on and carry out an individual project that you would do around the home that would benefit your family. After completion, discuss the objective or goal and the results of the project with your family and then your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Family Life Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 26, p. 27)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How did you determine that your chosen project addressed an authentic need rather than personal preference?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Scope Definition: Projects must address a genuine household need such as organization, repair, or preservation.
  - Prior Approval: Formal permission from guardians and counselors ensures appropriate difficulty, utility, and absolute safety.
  - Self-Directed Labor: The Scout must lead the execution rather than relying on adult labor.
  - Outcome Evaluation: Comparing final results with preliminary goals determines whether the effort succeeded completely.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Project Identification: Identify a specific home need like garage shelving reorganization or garden bed restoration.
  - Step 2 - Proposal Approval: Submit a detailed written plan to your parent and counselor before beginning work.
  - Step 3 - Task Execution: Gather needed supplies and complete all physical tasks according to your schedule.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Debrief: Discuss your initial objective, operational hurdles, and final results with your counselor.
- **Common Myth:** A routine weekly chore like taking out the trash qualifies as an individual project.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** An individual project must be a unique, planned endeavor outside regular daily chores that creates lasting value.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Effective guardians balance warm emotional nurture with firm boundaries, accountability, and essential life skill training.
