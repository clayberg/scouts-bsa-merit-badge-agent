### Req 2: Radial Lot Surveying and GPS Comparison
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Set an instrument over one of the corners of a five-sided lot laid out by your counselor and sighting one of the other corners for reference and, using radial measurement methods, record the angle turned and the distance measured to each of the remaining corners, as directed by your counselor. Record the angle and distance to five topographic features (trees, shrubs, rocks, etc.) near your work area. (b) Measure to three of the same points with GPS measurement and compare the distances between the points measured.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Surveying Requirement 2: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying radial lot surveying and gps comparison, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Measuring a five-sided lot with a surveying instrument requires moving the tripod setup to every single corner of the lot.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Using the radial measurement method, you can set the instrument over one corner, sight a reference corner, and measure angles and distances to all remaining corners from that single station (p. 23).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Surveying involves measuring horizontal and vertical distances and angles (p. 19).
- It also involves extensive computation (much of which is done with a computer) and map drawing (much of which is done with a computer-controlled printer) (p. 19).
- Satellite and Receiver Hardware: The Global Positioning System (GPS) uses artificial Earth satellites and portable computers equipped with receivers (p. 19).
- Satellite Signal Location Processing: GPS receivers process signals from the satellites to calculate location (p. 19).

### Req 4: Differential Leveling and Corner Elevations
**Official Requirement 4:** Use one of the corner markers from requirement 2 as a benchmark with an assumed elevation of 100 feet. Using a level and rod, collect and record measurements, and determine the elevation of the other four corner markers.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 57)* • **Organizer (Activity & Observation Log):** Req 4 Quantitative Field Observation & Activity Log (Corner Markers)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Surveying Requirement 4: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying differential leveling and corner elevations, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** A larger foresight rod reading on a corner marker means that corner is at a higher elevation than the benchmark.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Because the level sights along a horizontal plane above the ground, a larger reading on the leveling rod means the ground at that corner is lower in elevation (p. 31).

### Req 5: Property Deeds and Legal Land Records
**Official Requirement 5:** Get a copy of the deed to a piece of property approved by your counselor, from the local courthouse or title agency. Discuss with your counselor the different parts of the deed and their importance.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 61)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Get A Copy of The)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Conduct all counselor discussions, community interviews, and research for Surveying Requirement 5 on property deeds and legal land records under BSA Youth Protection two-deep leadership, copying a parent or guardian on any external correspondence.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Surveying Req 5 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** A property surveyor only needs to measure existing fences in the field without researching county legal documents.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Field measurements are only part of the evidence; surveyors must examine deeds, wills, and easements at the county records office to determine legal ownership and property rights (p. 11).

### Req 7: How Satellite Navigation Transforms Modern Surveying
**Official Requirement 7:** Tell what GPS is; discuss with your counselor the importance of GPS and how it is changing the field of surveying.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 12)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 7 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss Changing Field Surveying)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 7): Complete all How Satellite Navigation Transforms Modern Surveying discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Traditional surveying required clear line-of-sight between every station, and GPS still requires stations to see each other across hills or trees.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** GPS transforms surveying because receivers only need a clear view of the sky to receive satellite signals, eliminating the need for line-of-sight between ground points (p. 19).
