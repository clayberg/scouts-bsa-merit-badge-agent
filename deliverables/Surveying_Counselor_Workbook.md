### Req 1: Survey Field First Aid
**Official Requirement 1:** Show that you know first aid for the types of injuries that could occur while surveying, including cuts, scratches, snake bite, insect stings, tick bites, heat- and cold-related reactions, and dehydration. Explain to your counselor why a surveyor should be able to identify the poisonous plants and animals found in your area.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 16)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (You Know First Aid)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Survey crews must maintain thirty feet of separation when swinging brush axes or clearing sightlines through dense thickets.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe standard office adhesive bandages provide adequate field treatment for survey injuries.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Survey parties require rugged trauma kits containing pressure bandages, splints, and insect bite treatments.

### Req 2: Radial Boundary Traverse Measurements
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Set an instrument over one of the corners of a five-sided lot laid out by your counselor and sighting one of the other corners for reference and, using radial measurement methods, record the angle turned and the distance measured to each of the remaining corners, as directed by your counselor. Record the angle and distance to five topographic features (trees, shrubs, rocks, etc.) near your work area. (b) Measure to three of the same points with GPS measurement and compare the distances between the points measured.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never look through a total station or optical level directly into the sun without approved optical solar filters.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe turning angles without checking the backsight reference yields precise parcel boundaries.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Radial surveying requires resighting the initial reference mark to prove the instrument maintained horizontal orientation.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Instrument Centering Routine: Plumb the optical sight directly over the station tack before leveling footplates.
- Reference Sighting Zero: Align the horizontal circle crosshairs precisely on the reference corner before measurement.
- Radial Point Sighting: Sight target prisms at perimeter corners to obtain direct azimuths and distances.
- Data Logging Integrity: Record every raw clockwise angle and horizontal distance immediately within bound fieldbooks.

### Req 7: Global Positioning System Surveying
**Official Requirement 7:** Tell what GPS is; discuss with your counselor the importance of GPS and how it is changing the field of surveying.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 12)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 7 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Discuss Changing Field Surveying)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never hoist carbon-fiber GNSS antenna range poles near active overhead high-voltage power lines.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often assume survey satellite receivers operate identically to smartphone navigation chips.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Survey GNSS receivers track dual-frequency carrier waves and utilize base station differential corrections for centimeter accuracy.
