### Req 1: Survey Field First Aid
**Official Requirement 1:** Show that you know first aid for the types of injuries that could occur while surveying, including cuts, scratches, snake bite, insect stings, tick bites, heat- and cold-related reactions, and dehydration. Explain to your counselor why a surveyor should be able to identify the poisonous plants and animals found in your area.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 16)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Survey crews must maintain thirty feet of separation when swinging brush axes or clearing sightlines through dense thickets.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must a surveying field party carry far more comprehensive medical supplies than an office team?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Sharp cutting tools require wide clearance circles to prevent accidental crew lacerations.
  - Snakebite Emergency Care: Restrict movement and keep bite locations immobilized below heart level during evacuation.
  - Tick Exposure Mitigation: Tuck pant cuffs inside boots and perform hourly skin checks across brush.
  - Heat Exhaustion Response: Move affected crew members into shaded terrain and administer small water sips.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Kit Assessment: Check field first aid packs for antiseptic wipes, compression wraps, and shears.
  - Step 2 - Laceration Treatment: Irrigate open puncture wounds with potable water before applying sterile pressure dressings.
  - Step 3 - Thermal Care: Loosen tight gear and fan overheated instrument operators resting in shade.
  - Step 4 - Transport Evacuation: Document incident coordinates and move injured personnel to the nearest roadway access.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe standard office adhesive bandages provide adequate field treatment for survey injuries.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Survey parties require rugged trauma kits containing pressure bandages, splints, and insect bite treatments.

### Req 3: Scaled Boundary Plat Drafting
**Official Requirement 3:** From the field notes gathered for requirement 2, and using a protractor and scale, plot the points you measured and draw to scale a map of your survey which includes a north arrow, scale bar, title, date, and a diagram of the lot you measured. Submit a neatly drawn copy.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 50, p. 54)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Store drawing compasses, dividers, and razor trimming blades in protective cases to prevent puncture injuries.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must a professional survey plat include both a written scale ratio and a graphical bar scale?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Position the reference baseline on drafting media before plotting radial boundary angles.
  - Center the protractor vertex exactly over the station point to lay off angles.
  - Engineering Scale Division: Lay off measured radial distances along angle rays using certified engineering ratios.
  - Mandatory Plat Elements: Include a true north arrow, title block, certification date, and bar scale.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Sheet Layout: Fasten drafting paper to the drawing board using low-tack masking tape strips.
  - Step 2 - Station Plotting: Establish the instrument station and draw the initial backsight reference meridian line.
  - Step 3 - Radial Raying: Mark calculated perimeter corners along scaled radial vectors originating from the station.
  - Step 4 - Boundary Inking: Connect corner points with crisp boundary lines and annotate distances and bearings.
- **Common Myth:** Novices often believe architectural scales marked in fractional inches are identical to decimal civil engineering scales.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Survey plats require decimal engineering scales divided into tenths and hundredths of an inch per foot.

### Req 4: Differential Level Elevation Loop
**Official Requirement 4:** Use one of the corner markers from requirement 2 as a benchmark with an assumed elevation of 100 feet. Using a level and rod, collect and record measurements, and determine the elevation of the other four corner markers.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 31, p. 57)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Activity & Observation Log):** Req 4 Quantitative Field Observation & Activity Log
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Watch for overhead electric utility wires before raising metal or fiberglass leveling rods into vertical position.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does the instrument person balance the lengths of backsight and foresight distances during leveling?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Benchmark Elevation Assignment: Assign the starting corner monument an arbitrary datum elevation of one hundred feet.
  - Add the backsight rod value to known benchmark elevation to find instrument height.
  - Subtract foresight rod readings from instrument height to calculate new corner elevations.
  - Close the level circuit back onto the starting benchmark to compute vertical closure error.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Level Placement: Set the optical level midway between the starting benchmark and forward corner.
  - Step 2 - Backsight Addition: Record the vertical rod reading on the benchmark to calculate instrument height.
  - Step 3 - Foresight Subtraction: Sight the rod held over the forward corner to calculate its elevation.
  - Step 4 - Loop Closing: Run leveling turns through all five corners back to the initial benchmark.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe the leveling rod must be tilted toward the instrument to get an accurate reading.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The rod person must hold the rod plumb or wave it slowly through vertical to record the minimum reading.

### Req 5: Property Deed Legal Analysis
**Official Requirement 5:** Get a copy of the deed to a piece of property approved by your counselor, from the local courthouse or title agency. Discuss with your counselor the different parts of the deed and their importance.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 61)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wash hands thoroughly after handling dusty archival record books stored in historic county courthouse basements.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why is the point of beginning considered the most critical element in a metes and bounds deed description?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Courthouse Registry Search: Retrieve official property deed records from the county clerk or land recorder.
  - Locate the grantor transferring real estate and the grantee receiving the land title.
  - Metes Bounds Inspection: Follow the legal boundary narrative tracking bearings, distances, and adjoining land parcels.
  - Encumbrance Covenant Review: Identify recorded utility easements, roadway access reservations, and restrictive covenants within property descriptions.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Document Retrieval: Obtain a certified deed copy from the county recorder or title company.
  - Step 2 - Party Verification: Identify the legal names of the grantor and grantee in the premise clause.
  - Step 3 - Description Tracing: Trace the metes and bounds narrative starting from the designated point of beginning.
  - Step 4 - Encumbrance Review: Highlight recorded easements, road rights of way, and municipal restrictions affecting property use.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe property tax bills provide the legal boundary description necessary for land surveying.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Only a recorded deed contains the legal conveyance language and authoritative metes and bounds property description.

### Req 6: Modern Surveying Technology Evaluation
**Official Requirement 6:** Discuss emerging surveying technology such as drones and laser scanning and the strengths and weaknesses of each.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 12, p. 40)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Crew members must maintain line-of-sight and wear eye protection when operating class three industrial scanning lasers.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Under what terrain conditions does a terrestrial laser scanner outperform an unmanned aerial photogrammetry drone?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Drone Photogrammetry Rapidness: Aerial imagery platforms map large topographies quickly using overlapping detailed digital photographs.
  - Laser Scanner Pointclouds: Terrestrial scanners generate dense spatial coordinate point clouds of complex structures rapidly.
  - Vegetation Penetration Limitations: Aerial optical cameras cannot see ground terrain beneath thick deciduous forest leaf canopies.
  - Modern sensor data requires surveyed ground control points to achieve certified boundary accuracy.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Platform Selection: Evaluate whether the site requires aerial photogrammetry or dense terrestrial laser scanning.
  - Step 2 - Control Surveying: Establish calibrated ground control targets across the project site with total stations.
  - Step 3 - Sensor Mission: Execute automated drone flight passes or set up tripod laser scanning sweeps.
  - Step 4 - Pointcloud Processing: Register point clouds and extract planimetric features into digital drafting software packages.
- **Common Myth:** Novices often believe autonomous drone flights eliminate the need for traditional ground survey targets.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Aerial surveying requires precision ground control points surveyed with GNSS or total stations for georeferencing.

### Req 7: Global Positioning System Surveying
**Official Requirement 7:** Tell what GPS is; discuss with your counselor the importance of GPS and how it is changing the field of surveying.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 12)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 7 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never hoist carbon-fiber GNSS antenna range poles near active overhead high-voltage power lines.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does carrier-phase tracking allow survey GNSS receivers to achieve millimeter precision over code-phase receivers?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Satellite Trilateral Ranging: Ground receivers calculate precise distances to orbiting satellites using synchronized atomic clock signals.
  - Constellation Orbital Geometry: Four or more visible satellites provide necessary mathematical trilateration for spatial positioning.
  - Active kinematic base stations transmit differential corrections to rover units over radio links.
  - Multipath Signal Interference: Reflected satellite radio waves bouncing off buildings degrade receiver coordinate measurement accuracy.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Site Reconnaissance: Select open station locations free from tall tree canopies and steel structures.
  - Step 2 - Base Initialization: Mount the GNSS base receiver over a known control mark and calibrate.
  - Step 3 - Rover Data Collection: Plumb the rover antenna rod over unknown points to acquire fixed positions.
  - Step 4 - Residual Evaluation: Inspect horizontal and vertical precision readouts before storing coordinate data in dataloggers.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often assume survey satellite receivers operate identically to smartphone navigation chips.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Survey GNSS receivers track dual-frequency carrier waves and utilize base station differential corrections for centimeter accuracy.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Surveying Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 61)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Career & Profession Exploration Card):** Req 8 Career & Professional Pathway Investigation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Highway construction survey technicians must wear high-visibility ANSI class three apparel when working near active traffic.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the legal responsibility of a licensed boundary surveyor differ from that of a construction instrument technician?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Licensed Surveyor Responsibilities: Professional land surveyors legally certify property boundaries and prepare recorded subdivision plats.
  - Geodetic Engineering Roles: Geodesists determine precise planetary crustal movements using satellite arrays and gravity measurements.
  - Hydrographic Mapping Opportunities: Hydrographers map underwater lakebeds and coastal shipping channels using sonar acoustic bathymetry.
  - Licensure Examination Steps: Aspiring surveyors pass national fundamentals and principles examinations before earning state licensure.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Career Exploration: Research specialized roles in boundary surveying, construction staking, and geodetic mapping.
  - Step 2 - Academic Preparation: Identify accredited university degree programs in surveying engineering or geomatics science disciplines.
  - Step 3 - Internship Apprenticeship: Gain four years of verified field and office experience under licensed surveyors.
  - Step 4 - Professional Certification: Pass state licensing board examinations to earn legal authority to seal plats.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe anyone who operates a total station can legally sign and seal boundary surveys.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Only state-licensed Professional Land Surveyors have the legal authority to certify property boundary determinations.
