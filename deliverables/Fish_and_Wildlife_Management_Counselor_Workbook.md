## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 6 (List):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Observe and record 25 species of wildlife. Your list may include mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish. Write down when and where each animal was seen. (b) List the wildlife species in your state that are classified as endangered, threatened, exotic, non-native, game species, furbearers, or migratory game birds. Discuss with your counselor management practices in place or being developed for at least three of these species. (c) Start a scrapbook of North American fish and wildlife. Insert markers to divide the book into separate parts for mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and fish. Collect articles on such subjects as life histories, habitat, behavior, and feeding habits on all of the five categories and place them in your notebook accordingly. Articles and pictures may be taken from newspapers or science, nature, and outdoor magazines, or from other sources including the internet (with your parent or guardian's permission). Enter at least five articles on mammals, five on birds, five on reptiles, five on amphibians, and five on fish. Put each animal on a separate sheet in alphabetical order. Include pictures whenever possible.

## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Do ONE of the following: (a) Construct, erect, and check regularly at least two artificial nest boxes (wood duck, bluebird, squirrel, etc.) and keep written records for one nesting season. (c) Develop and implement a fishery improvement project or a backyard wildlife habitat improvement project. Share the results with your counselor. (d) Design and construct a wildlife blind near a game trail, water hole, salt lick, bird feeder, or birdbath and take good photographs or make sketches from the blind of any combination of 10 wild birds, mammals, reptiles, or amphibians.

### Req 1: Fish and Wildlife Conservation Principles
**Official Requirement 1:** Describe the meaning and purposes of fish and wildlife conservation and management.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fish and Wildlife Management Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the principle of sustained yield differ from complete preservation in forested ecosystems?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Conservation Definition: Wise resource use maintains ecological balance while providing sustainable recreational harvest opportunities.
  - Preservation Contrast: Strict nonintervention preserves wilderness areas without permitting active wildlife habitat manipulation.
  - Carrying Capacity: Available seasonal food, cover, and water restrict total sustainable animal numbers.
  - Ecological Balance: Predation and population controls prevent herbivores from destroying vital forage plants.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Resource Definition: Distinguish preservation from sustainable yield management across state wildlife management areas.
  - Step 2 - Goal Identification: Outline recreational harvest, biodiversity protection, and habitat restoration goals for regional populations.
  - Step 3 - Limiting Factors: Graph seasonal food availability, cover quality, and water sources for target game.
  - Step 4 - Synthesis Presentation: Present conservation principles connecting biotic potential with environmental resistance factors to counselors.
- **Common Myth:** Conservation means completely locking away natural resources so humans cannot interact with wildlife.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Conservation represents the wise, sustainable use and active management of renewable natural resources.

### Req 2: State Wildlife Population Threat Assessment
**Official Requirement 2:** List and discuss at least three major problems that continue to threaten your state's fish and wildlife resources.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fish and Wildlife Management Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Which invasive species currently poses the most severe ecological threat to our local waterways?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Habitat Fragmentation: Roads and housing developments divide continuous home ranges into isolated migration fragments.
  - Invasive Competition: Nonnative species outcompete endemic wildlife for nesting cavities and nutritional forage.
  - Water Pollution: Agricultural siltation and industrial effluent degrade spawning beds in shallow riverbeds.
  - Chronic wasting disease spreads through crowded ungulate herds feeding in agricultural clearings.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Threat Identification: Document three specific environmental hazards impacting native fauna across regional state drainage.
  - Step 2 - Habitat Mapping: Chart suburban fragmentation corridors and barrier highways cutting through local game preserves.
  - Step 3 - Water Assessment: Measure agricultural sedimentation rates and chemical runoff in local tributaries using gauges.
  - Step 4 - Mitigation Reporting: Detail management proposals counteracting invasive competition and wildlife epidemic disease clusters.
- **Common Myth:** Regulated hunting serves as the leading cause of wildlife species population decline today.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Habitat loss through agricultural conversion and urban development poses the greatest threat to wildlife.

### Req 3: Public Wildlife Stewardship Action Pathways

  - Sportsperson Funding: Excise taxes on sporting arms directly finance state wildlife restoration programs.
  - Keeping domestic cats indoors preserves hundreds of millions of songbirds annually.
  - Native Landscaping: Planting regional shrubs provides necessary winter shelter and natural forage for songbirds.
  - Poaching Reports: Contacting conservation officers stops illegal harvest of endangered or protected game species.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**

  - Step 2 - Household Assessment: Audit domestic pet containment and chemical lawn treatments around residential property borders.
  - Step 3 - Native Planting: Plant endemic flowering perennials and berry shrubs along fence lines for shelter.
  - Step 4 - Community Engagement: Participate in organized watershed cleanups and record avian sightings on conservation databases.
- **Common Myth:** Public wildlife management receives all of its operational funding from general federal income taxes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hunting and fishing licenses combined with excise taxes on sporting equipment fund state conservation agencies.

### Req 4: State Wildlife Habitat Management Practices
**Official Requirement 4:** List and describe five major fish and wildlife management practices used by managers in your state.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fish and Wildlife Management Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do wildlife managers use clearcuts to benefit species like woodcock and cottontail rabbits?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Prescribed Burning: Controlled fires consume accumulated forest litter and stimulate herbaceous browse seed germination.
  - Fish Stocking: Hatchery fingerlings supplement degraded natural reproduction in urban lakes and reservoirs.
  - Selective Harvesting: Logging creates early successional forest clearings essential for ruffed grouse nesting.
  - Season Regulation: Setting bag limits prevents overharvest of breeding adult game during rutting seasons.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Agency Research: Review state wildlife agency bulletins to catalogue active regional habitat manipulation programs.
  - Step 2 - Practice Selection: Choose five distinct practices including controlled fire, stocking, logging, and hunting seasons.
  - Step 3 - Field Documentation: Photograph physical evidence of timber thinnings, food plots, or fish ladders locally.
  - Step 4 - Technical Evaluation: Explain how each chosen practice alters vegetative succession to support targeted species.
- **Common Myth:** Leaving a forest entirely undisturbed without fire or logging produces optimal habitat for all wildlife.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Active habitat manipulation like prescribed fire and timber thinning creates diverse successional stages vital for wildlife.
