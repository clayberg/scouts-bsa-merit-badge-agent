### Req 1: Flowering Plant Anatomical Structures
**Official Requirement 1:** Make a drawing and identify five or more parts of a flowering plant. Tell what each part does.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 35)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas):** Req 1 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what specific ways do the structural adaptations of xylem and phloem vessels support tall tree growth?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Root Anatomy: Taproots anchor deep in soil while fibrous roots absorb moisture near surface layers.
  - Stem Function: Xylem vessels conduct water upward while phloem tissues transport photosynthesized sugars downward.
  - Leaf Structure: Guard cells regulate stomata apertures to control gas exchange and transpiration rates.
  - Floral Organs: Anthers produce pollen grains that travel down styles to fertilize ovules.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Specimen Selection: Collect a fresh complete dicot blossom showing intact petals, sepals, and pistils.
  - Step 2 - Organ Dissection: Carefully slice the blossom lengthwise using a clean hand lens for observation.
  - Step 3 - Botanical Sketching: Draw five primary organs with clear pencil lines on clean grid paper.
  - Step 4 - Functional Labeling: Annotate each drawn organ with its specific biological function and vascular connection.
- **Common Myth:** Petals are the primary reproductive organs responsible for producing plant seeds.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Pistils contain female reproductive organs including the ovary, style, and stigma, while stamens comprise male anthers and filaments.

### Req 2: Photosynthesis and Human Botanical Dependency
**Official Requirement 2:** Explain photosynthesis and tell why this process is important. Tell at least five ways that humans depend on plants.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 17, p. 21)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How would atmospheric gas concentrations shift if terrestrial plant photosynthesis dropped by half?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Photochemical Reaction: Chlorophyll pigments absorb solar photons within chloroplasts to split water molecules cleanly.
  - Calvin Cycle: Light independent enzymatic reactions fix atmospheric carbon dioxide into glucose molecules continuously.
  - Human Sustenance: Cultivated cereal grains supply dietary carbohydrates essential for human caloric intake globally.
  - Industrial Utility: Plant cellulose fibers provide raw material for textiles, lumber, and pharmaceuticals.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Energy Capture: Diagram photon absorption inside thylakoid membranes where chlorophyll splits absorbed water molecules.
  - Step 2 - Carbon Fixation: Map the conversion of carbon dioxide into triose phosphates during photosynthesis.
  - Step 3 - Dependency Inventory: List five distinct essential commodities that humans harvest directly from living plants.
  - Step 4 - Impact Analysis: Explain how photosynthetic oxygen production stabilizes terrestrial animal respiration across global ecosystems.
- **Common Myth:** Plants produce oxygen solely for human breathing without undergoing cellular respiration themselves.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Plant respiration consumes oxygen day and night, but net daytime photosynthesis yields an abundant surplus of atmospheric oxygen.

### Req 3: Insect Pollination Dynamics and Ecology
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain how honeybees and other pollinating insects are important to plant life.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 43)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What flower adaptations indicate insect pollination rather than abiotic wind pollination?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Foraging worker bees transfer adhesive pollen grains between blossoms on branched body hairs.
  - Crop Yields: Cross pollination by foraging insects significantly increases commercial orchard fruit set percentages.
  - Coevolution Dynamic: Specialized floral nectar guides direct native bumblebees directly toward hidden reproductive organs.
  - Floral Fidelity: Honeybees visit identical blossom species consecutively during single foraging flights across orchards.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Habitat Observation: Observe an active patch of blossoming perennials during warm midday sunlight hours.
  - Step 2 - Insect Identification: Catalog visiting honeybees, solitary native bees, butterflies, and hoverflies within thirty minutes.
  - Step 3 - Interaction Tracking: Record the mechanical contact between insect bodies and sticky floral stigmas systematically.
  - Step 4 - Seed Assessment: Compare the fruit development rates between open pollinated and netting shielded blossoms.
- **Common Myth:** All flowering plants require honeybees to transfer pollen and successfully reproduce.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Wind and self pollination fertilize grasses and grains, while insect pollinators cross pollinate over seventy percent of major commercial fruit, vegetable, and seed crops.

### Req 4: Plant Environment and Soil Fertility Dynamics
**Official Requirement 4:** Explain how water, light, air, temperature, and pests affect plants. Describe the nature and function of soil and explain its importance. Tell about the texture, structure, and composition of fertile soil. Tell how soil may be improved.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 25)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does heavy clay soil texture influence internal root oxygenation during wet seasons?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Environmental Pressures: Extreme temperatures and prolonged drought disrupt cellular turgor pressure within tender shoots.
  - Pest Impacts: Piercing mouthparts of sap feeding aphids transmit debilitating viral plant pathogens quickly.
  - Mineral Horizons: Weathered parent minerals combine with biological humus to form fertile topsoil layers.
  - Organic Amendments: Incorporating cured compost improves water retention capacity and aggregate structure in sandy soils.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Soil Sampling: Collect composite topsoil cores six inches deep from multiple garden plot locations.
  - Step 2 - Texture Analysis: Shake settled soil particles in a water jar to measure sand, silt, clay layers.
  - Step 3 - Structure Examination: Evaluate soil ped aggregation, root penetration paths, and earthworm activity levels visually.
  - Step 4 - Amendment Protocol: Incorporate mature compost into the seedbed to optimize moisture retention and aeration.
- **Common Myth:** Soil is simply pulverized rock that functions purely as an anchor for roots.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Soil structure describes how sand, silt, and clay particles aggregate into granules, whereas soil texture is the fixed percentage of those mineral particles.

### Req 5: Vegetative and Seed Propagation Techniques
**Official Requirement 5:** Tell how to propagate plants by seeds, roots, cuttings, tubers, and grafting. Grow a plant by ONE of these methods.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 29, p. 67)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do commercial apple orchardists utilize scion grafting instead of planting apple seeds?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Cold stratification breaks biochemical seed dormancy to trigger uniform spring embryo germination.
  - Stem Cuttings: Auxin hormone applications stimulate adventitious root initiation along severed herbaceous stem nodes.
  - Splitting perennial root crowns preserves true to type cultivar genetics reliably.
  - Cambium Alignment: Successful grafting requires matching scion and rootstock vascular cambium layers precisely.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Propagule Selection: Select healthy semi hardwood stem tissue showing active vegetative buds without blossoms.
  - Step 2 - Cutting Preparation: Cut stem at an angled node and remove lower foliage cleanly.
  - Step 3 - Media Insertion: Dip the cut base into hormone powder and seat firmly into damp perlite.
  - Step 4 - Moisture Management: Maintain high relative humidity under a clear cloche until adventitious roots establish.
- **Common Myth:** Seeds gathered from hybrid garden fruit will grow into plants identical to the parent cultivar.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Grafting and cuttings produce genetically identical clones of parent stock, whereas sexual seed propagation produces genetically variable offspring.

### Req 6: Native, Cultivated, and Invasive Flora Identification
**Official Requirement 6:** List by common name at least 10 native plants and 10 cultivated plants that grow near your home. List five invasive non-native plants in your area and tell how they may be harmful. Tell how the spread of invasive plants may be avoided or controlled in ways that are not damaging to humans, wildlife, and the environment.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 47, p. 73)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do non native invasive plants spread more rapidly than native species in disturbed forest edges?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Regional native flora sustains specialized local insects, birds, and soil microbial communities.
  - Cultivated Species: Agricultural cultivars require deliberate human intervention including tilling, weeding, and supplemental watering.
  - Invasive Displacement: Aggressive non native species smother native understory seedlings and outcompete indigenous perennials.
  - Mechanical Eradication: Digging taproots before seed set prevents invasive plant patches from spreading further.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Native Survey: Identify and catalog ten wild native plants thriving in undisturbed local woodlots.
  - Step 2 - Cultivar Cataloging: Document ten cultivated ornamental or food crop plants growing in neighborhood gardens.
  - Step 3 - Invasive Assessment: Locate five established invasive exotic species threatening native understory forest diversity.
  - Step 4 - Containment Planning: Formulate a multi season mechanical removal plan targeting taproots before seed dispersal.
- **Common Myth:** Any plant that grows vigorously without human cultivation in a forest is classified as native.

#### Req 8c(6): Field Botany Alternative Investigation Options
**Official Requirement 8c(6):** Do ALL of the requirements in ONE of the following alternatives: (a) Tree Inventory (1) Identify the trees of your neighborhood, a park, a section of your town, or a Scout camp. (2) Collect, press, and label leaves, flowers, or fruits to document your inventory. (3) List the types of trees by scientific name and give common names. Note the number and size (diameter at 4½ feet above ground) of trees observed and determine the largest of each species in your study area. (4) Show two or more people how to use a leaf or twig key to identify at least five species of trees or shrubs. (b) Transect Study (1) Visit two sites, at least one of which is different from the one you visited for Field Botany requirement 1. (2) Use the transect method to study the two different kinds of plant communities. The transects should be at least 500 feet long. (3) At each site, record observations about the soil and other influencing factors AND do the following. Then make a graph or chart to show the results of your studies. (a) Identify each tree within 10 feet of the transect line. (b) Measure the diameter of each tree at 4½ feet above the ground, and map and list each tree. (c) Nested Plot (1) Visit two sites, at least one of which is different from the one you visited for Field Botany requirement 1. (2) Mark off nested plots and inventory two different kinds of plant communities. (3) At each site, record observations about the soil and other influencing factors AND do the following. Then make a graph or chart to show the results of your studies. (a) Identify, measure, and map each tree in a 100-by-100-foot plot. (Measure the diameter of each tree larger than 3 inches in diameter at 4½ feet above the ground.) (b) Identify and count all trees and shrubs in a 10-by-10-foot plot within each of the larger areas. (c) Identify and count all broad-leaved plants (trees, shrubs, vines, and herbaceous plants) and all plants (wildflowers, ferns, grasses, mosses, etc.) of a 4-by-4-foot plot within the 10-by-10-foot plot. Note: If individual plants like ferns, grasses, and mosses cannot be clearly distinguished, make a reasonable estimate of area (square inches of cover) for each species and record that as an estimate within the 4 x 4 subplot. (d) Herbarium Visit (1) Write ahead and arrange to visit an herbarium at a university, park, or botanical garden; OR, visit an herbarium website (with your parent or guardian's permission). (2) Tell how the specimens are arranged and how they are used by researchers. If possible, observe voucher specimens of a plant that is rare in your state. (3) Tell how a voucher specimen is mounted and prepared for permanent storage. Tell how specimens should be handled so that they will not be damaged. (4) Tell about the tools and references used by botanists in an herbarium. (e) Plant Conservation Organization Visit (1) Write ahead and arrange to visit a private conservation organization or government agency that is concerned with protecting rare plants and natural areas. (2) Tell about the activities of the organization in studying and protecting rare plants and natural areas. (3) If possible, visit a nature preserve managed by the organization. Tell about land management practices such as controlled burning, or measures to eradicate invasive (non-native) plants or other threats to the plants that are native to the area.

### Req 9: Outdoor Ethics and Botanical Stewardship
**Official Requirement 9:** Discuss the importance of the Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code as they relate to plant science. Explain how you have followed the Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code while in natural areas during field observation, specimen collection, and identification.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plant Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 80, p. 88)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 9 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does off trail hiking cause long term soil compaction that inhibits native seedling germination?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Concentrating foot travel on durable trail surfaces prevents trampling fragile forest understory seedlings.
  - Specimen Stewardship: Photographing rare native blossoms preserves wild genetic stock rather than picking vulnerable plants.
  - Biosecurity Protocol: Scrubbing hiking boots removes hitchhiking invasive weed seeds before entering pristine nature preserves.
  - Campfire Conservation: Burning only dead and downed wood prevents structural damage to standing living trees.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Surface Selection: Walk exclusively on established gravel or bare soil trails during botanical surveys.
  - Step 2 - Non-Invasive Observation: Document wild specimens using sketch pads and field cameras without removing blossoms.
  - Step 3 - Equipment Sanitation: Clean boot treads and trowels thoroughly to dislodge clinging invasive plant seeds.
  - Step 4 - Stewardship Review: Evaluate campsite and trail areas to ensure zero vegetative disturbance remains visible.
- **Common Myth:** Picking a few wild woodland flowers does not harm natural plant populations.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Harvesting rare wild flowers or peeling birch bark causes permanent damage to wild plants and degrades habitats for future visitors.
