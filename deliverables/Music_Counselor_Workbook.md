## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 3 (Prepare):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Attend a live performance, or listen to three hours of recordings from any two of the following musical styles: blues, jazz, classical, country, bluegrass, ethnic, gospel, musical theater, opera. Describe the sound of the music and the instruments used. Identify the composers or songwriters, the performers, and the titles of the pieces you heard. If it was a live performance, describe the setting and the reaction of the audience. Discuss your thoughts about the music. (b) Interview an adult member of your family about music. Find out what the most popular music was when he or she was your age. Find out what his or her favorite music is now, and listen to three of your relative's favorite tunes with him or her. How do those favorites sound to you? Had you ever heard any of them? Play three of your favorite songs for your relative, and explain why you like these songs. Ask what he or she thinks of your favorite music. (c) Serve for six months as a member of a school band, choir, or other organized musical group, or perform as a soloist in public six times. (d) List five people who are important in the history of American music and explain to your counselor why they continue to be influential. Include at least one composer, one performer, one innovator, and one person born more than 100 years ago.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career. (b) Explore how you could use knowledge and skills from this merit badge to pursue a hobby. Research any training needed, expenses, and organizations that promote or support it. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.

### Req 1: Score Reading and Song Performance
**Official Requirement 1:** Sing or play a simple song or hymn chosen by your counselor, using good technique, phrasing, tone, rhythm, and dynamics. Read all the signs and terms of the score.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Music Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Simple Song or Hymn Chosen)

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Controlled resonance and balanced diaphragmatic support produce superior projection without vocal strain.

### Req 2: Instrument Families and Acoustic Tone
**Official Requirement 2:** Name the five general groups of musical instruments. Create an illustration that shows how tones are generated and how instruments produce sound.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Music Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (General Groups of Musical Instruments)
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe instrument volume depends entirely on how hard someone strikes or blows.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Instrument body resonance and sympathetic vibration dictate acoustic projection far more than brute force.

- Acoustic Oscillation: Vibrating strings, reeds, or air columns create longitudinal compression waves inside resonant chambers.
- Brass Resonance: Buzzing lips against a cup mouthpiece initiate acoustic standing waves along coiled tubing.
- Percussion Impact: Striking membrane heads or wooden bars creates rapid vibrations that displace surrounding air.
- Woodwind Mechanics: Split air streams across tone holes or vibrating cane reeds produce musical pitches.

- **Common Myth:** Listeners frequently think distinct musical genres developed in isolation without mutual artistic influence.

- Ensemble Participation: Rehearsing with choral or instrumental ensembles teaches blend, balance, and collaborative stage discipline.
- Oral Tradition: Families transmit cultural heritage and historical memory across generations through shared musical preferences.
- Pioneering Figures: Visionary composers and performers transform regional idioms into enduring American musical traditions.

### Req 4: Music Instruction Composition and Construction
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Teach three songs to a group of people. Lead them in singing the songs, using proper hand motions. (b) Compose and write the score for a piece of music of 12 measures or more, and play this music on an instrument. (c) Make a traditional instrument and learn to play it.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Music Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
- **Common Myth:** Novice leaders assume conducting merely means waving hands along with the rhythm of the words.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Effective conducting provides advance preparatory beats that show singers the exact pitch, tempo, and breath.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Song Leadership: Confident downbeats and clear preparatory gestures keep vocal groups synchronized in tempo.
- Melodic Composition: Structuring twelve measures requires balanced phrase lengths, rhythmic variety, and harmonic tonal resolution.
- Instrument Fabrication: Selecting resonant materials like dried gourds or hardwoods ensures bright acoustic sound projection.
- Standard meter patterns signal time signature, expressive dynamics, and definitive cutoffs to performers.

### Req 5: Intellectual Property and Music Copyright
**Official Requirement 5:** Define for your counselor intellectual property (IP). Explain how to properly obtain and share recorded music.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Music Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Intellectual Property)
- **Common Myth:** Scouts sometimes think downloading music from unauthorized file-sharing websites is harmless if not resold.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Unauthorized digital distribution directly violates copyright statutes and deprives artists of their legitimate royalties.
