## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do ONE of the following: (a) Visit a managed public or private forest area with the manager or a forester who is familiar with it. Write a brief report describing the type of forest, the management objectives, and the forestry techniques used to achieve the objectives. (b) With a knowledgeable individual, visit a current or past logging operation or wood-using manufacturing plant. Write a brief report describing the following: (1) The species and size of trees harvested or used (2) The origin of the forest or stands of trees utilized (e. g., planted or natural) (3) The forest's successional stage, its future, and, if it is a past logging operation, the regeneration that is occurring, whether planted or natural (4) Where the trees are coming from (land ownership) or where they are going (type of mill or processing plant) (5) The products that are made from the trees (6) How the products are made and used (7) How waste materials from the logging operation or manufacturing plant are or were disposed of or utilized (c) Take part in a forest-fire prevention campaign in cooperation with your local fire warden, state wildfire agency, forester, or counselor. Write a brief report describing the campaign, how it will help prevent wildfires, and your part in it.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Explain):** In your camp, local recreation area (park or equivalent), or neighborhood, do ONE of the following: (a) Inventory the trees within a specific area above (campsite, road, trail, street, etc.) that may be a hazard to structures or people. Note the species and hazardous condition, and suggest a remedy (removal or trimming). Make your list available to the proper authority or agency. (b) Review a hazardous tree report done by a professional for this area and visit those trees and the results of the prescription to prune or remove them.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Prepare):** Visit one or more local foresters and write a brief report about the person (or persons) OR write about a forester's occupation including the education, qualifications, career opportunities, and duties related to forestry.

### Req 1: Forest Plant Field Identification
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Prepare a field notebook, make a collection, and identify 15 species of trees, wild shrubs, or vines in a local forested area. Write a description in which you identify and discuss the following: (a) The characteristics of leaf, twig, cone, or fruiting bodies (b) The habitat in which these trees, shrubs, or vines are found (c) The important ways each tree, shrub, or vine is used by humans or wildlife and whether the species is native or was introduced to the area (If it is not native, explain whether it is considered invasive or potentially invasive.)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 25)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
- **Common Myth:** Tree species can be reliably identified using only leaf shape during the summer growing season.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Botanical identification requires examining multiple diagnostic features including bud scale arrangement, bundle scars, and bark texture.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Leaf Arrangement: Alternate or opposite twig attachments provide immediate diagnostic separation between major tree families.
- True terminal buds feature distinct scales and vascular scars that identify dormant hardwoods.
- Fruiting Structures: Hardwood nut mast and conifer seed cones confirm specific genus and species designations.
- Mature bark furrow depth and color distinguish older specimens lacking reachable lower foliage.

### Req 2: Wood Structure and Growth
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Collect and identify wood samples of 10 species of trees. List several ways the wood of each species can be used. (b) Find and examine three stumps, logs, or core samples that show variations in the growth rate of their ring patterns. In the field notebook you prepared for requirement 1, describe the location or origin of each example (including elevation, aspect, slope, and the position on the slope), and discuss possible reasons for the variations in growth rate. Photograph or sketch each example. (c) Find and examine two types of animal, insect, or disease damage to trees. In the field notebook you prepared for requirement 1, identify the damage, explain how the damage was caused, and describe the effects of the damage on the trees. Photograph or sketch each example.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 36)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
- **Common Myth:** Annual growth rings always maintain uniform width throughout the life of a tree.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Ring width reflects annual environmental factors including precipitation, canopy competition, and insect defoliation events.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Growth Ring Formation: Early springwood cells have large lumens whereas dense latewood cells provide mechanical strength.
- Vascular cambium generates xylem tissue internally while adding thin phloem layers externally.
- Pathological Staining: Internal fungal mycelium creates distinct heartwood discoloration visible in cut log sections.
- Wood Grain Utility: Distinct ring porosity dictates timber selection for furniture, framing, or paper production.

### Req 3: Forest Economic and Environmental Services
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe the contributions forests make to: (1) Our economy in the form of products (2) Our social well-being, including recreation (3) Soil protection and increased fertility (4) Clean water (5) Clean air (carbon cycling, sequestration) (6) Wildlife habitat (7) Fisheries habitat (8) Threatened and endangered species of plants and animals (b) Tell which watershed or other source your community relies on for its water supply.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 45, p. 74)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Forests are static natural reserves that provide recreational hiking trails without measurable economic output.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Forest duff layers and root networks filter surface runoff, recharging aquifers and preventing catastrophic erosion.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Watershed Filtration: Forest litter slows precipitation runoff, facilitating natural percolation into pristine subterranean aquifers.
- Actively growing trees convert atmospheric carbon dioxide into durable cellulosic structural biomass.
- Economic Products: Working woodlands supply sustainable timber, paper pulp, chemical resins, and renewable bioenergy.
- Soil Stabilization: Extensive fibrous root networks anchor steep embankments against torrential stormwater debris flows.

### Req 4: Forest Management and Silviculture
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Describe what forest management means, including the following: (a) Multiple-use management (b) Sustainable forest management (c) Even-aged and uneven-aged management and the silvicultural systems associated with each (d) Intermediate cuttings (e) The role of prescribed burning and related forest-management practices
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 55, p. 58)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Forest management consists solely of planting pine saplings after clearcutting entire timber tracts.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Uneven-aged systems preserve continuous canopy cover and maintain three or more distinct age cohorts across stands.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Multiple-Use Forestry: Timber production coexists alongside wildlife preservation, livestock grazing, and public wilderness recreation.
- Silvicultural Systems: Clearcutting supports shade-intolerant regeneration whereas individual selection favors shade-tolerant understory species.
- Intermediate Cuttings: Stand thinnings allocate water and crown space toward elite high-value timber crop trees.
- Prescribed Burning: Controlled low-intensity surface fires eliminate explosive dead fuels while releasing critical soil minerals.

**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** In your camp, local recreation area (park or equivalent), or neighborhood, do ONE of the following: (a) Inventory the trees within a specific area above (campsite, road, trail, street, etc.) that may be a hazard to structures or people. (b) Review a hazardous tree report done by a professional for this area and visit those trees and the results of the prescription to prune or remove them.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 20, p. 65)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear a hard hat and maintain distance from hung-up limbs or actively failing canopy branches.
- **Common Myth:** Any tree with leaning trunks or minor dead branches must be immediately cut down.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Arborists assess root health, lean angle, structural fungal brackets, and dead limbs before declaring hazards.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Target Assessment: Hazardous tree evaluations require identifying potential targets such as campsites, cabins, or trails.
- Structural Defects: Fungal conks, basal cavities, and severe trunk cracks indicate dangerous internal structural rot.
- Crown Dieback: Extensive dead branches within the upper canopy pose immediate falling limb danger.
- Mitigation Prescriptions: Certified arborists prune dangerous deadwood or cable weak crotches to preserve trees.

### Req 7: Forest Disturbances and Health
**Official Requirement 7 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe the consequences to forests that result from FIVE of the following elements: wildfire, absence of fire, destructive insects, loss of pollinating insect population, tree diseases, air pollution, overgrazing, deer or other wildlife overpopulation, improper harvest, and urbanization. (b) Explain what can be done to reduce the consequences you discussed in 7(a). (c) Describe what you should do if you discover a forest fire and how a professional firefighting crew might control it. Name your state or local wildfire control agency.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 71)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Completely extinguishing all forest fires always produces the healthiest, most stable woodland ecosystem.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Decades of fire suppression create hazardous fuel ladders that feed destructive, soil-sterilizing crown fires.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Fuel Ladder Hazards: Dense brush allows ground fires to climb into crowns, creating catastrophic infernos.
- Invasive Insect Outbreaks: Non-native boring beetles lack local natural predators, devastating vulnerable homogeneous tree stands.
- Pathological Blights: Fungal rusts and vascular wilts spread rapidly through stressed or injured timber.
- Initial Fire Attack: Rapid response crews cut bare mineral firelines to starve oncoming wildfire flames.
