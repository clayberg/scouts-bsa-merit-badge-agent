## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, do ONE of the following: (a) Visit a managed public or private forest area with the manager or a forester who is familiar with it. Write a brief report describing the type of forest, the management objectives, and the forestry techniques used to achieve the objectives. (b) With a knowledgeable individual, visit a current or past logging operation or wood-using manufacturing plant. Write a brief report describing the following: (1) The species and size of trees harvested or used (2) The origin of the forest or stands of trees utilized (e. g., planted or natural) (3) The forest's successional stage, its future, and, if it is a past logging operation, the regeneration that is occurring, whether planted or natural (4) Where the trees are coming from (land ownership) or where they are going (type of mill or processing plant) (5) The products that are made from the trees (6) How the products are made and used (7) How waste materials from the logging operation or manufacturing plant are or were disposed of or utilized (c) Take part in a forest-fire prevention campaign in cooperation with your local fire warden, state wildfire agency, forester, or counselor. Write a brief report describing the campaign, how it will help prevent wildfires, and your part in it.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Explain):** In your camp, local recreation area (park or equivalent), or neighborhood, do ONE of the following: (a) Inventory the trees within a specific area above (campsite, road, trail, street, etc.) that may be a hazard to structures or people. Note the species and hazardous condition, and suggest a remedy (removal or trimming). Make your list available to the proper authority or agency. (b) Review a hazardous tree report done by a professional for this area and visit those trees and the results of the prescription to prune or remove them.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Prepare):** Visit one or more local foresters and write a brief report about the person (or persons) OR write about a forester's occupation including the education, qualifications, career opportunities, and duties related to forestry.

**Official Requirement 2a:** Collect and identify wood samples of 10 species of trees. List several ways the wood of each species can be used.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Wood identification relies on cell porosity, ray width, color, and grain patterns. Commercial utilization separates dense construction lumber from pulpwood, veneer, and specialty furniture woods.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Surface Preparation: Sand cut log ends with fine abrasive paper to expose clean cell boundaries.

**[Parent Directive]:** *Describe the contributions forests make to*
**Official Requirement 3a(4):** Clean water (5) Clean air (carbon cycling, sequestration) (6) Wildlife habitat (7) Fisheries habitat.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Forestry Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 45, p. 74)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3a

- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How has satellite mapping and drone LiDAR changed modern timber inventory methods?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Academic Rigor: Professional forester credentialing requires rigorous collegiate coursework in biology, chemistry, and calculus.
  - Spatial Technology: Modern forest management relies on satellite imagery, LiDAR mapping, and spatial GIS databases.
  - Public Interface: Foresters collaborate with private landowners, timber corporations, conservation groups, and recreationists.
  - Career Diversity: Employment ranges from urban arboriculture to industrial timber procurement and public agency regulation.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Career Research: Review degree programs accredited by the Society of American Foresters online.
  - Step 2 - Professional Interview: Contact a state or consulting forester to discuss daily field responsibilities.
  - Step 3 - Qualification Review: Identify required state licensing exams, continuing education credits, and GIS certifications.
  - Step 4 - Career Profiling: Write a detailed summary outlining forestry career paths and contemporary technological requirements.
- **Common Myth:** A forester spends all working time in wilderness cabins doing manual timber felling.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Professional foresters must earn accredited university degrees in forestry, dendrology, and geographic information systems.
