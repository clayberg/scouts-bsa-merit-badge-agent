## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 7 (Prepare):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Design a stamp, cancellation, or cachet. (b) Visit a post office, stamp club, or stamp show with an experienced collector. Explain what you saw and learned. (c) Write a review of an interesting article from a stamp newspaper, magazine, book, or website (with your parent or guardian's permission). (d) Research and report on a famous stamp-related personality or the history behind a particular stamp. (e) Describe the steps taken to produce a stamp. Include the methods of printing, types of paper, perforation styles, and how they are gummed. (f) Prepare a two- to three-page display involving stamps. Using ingenuity, as well as clippings, drawings, etc., tell a story about the stamps and how they relate to history, geography, or a favorite topic of yours.

### Req 1: Postal History and World Heritage
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss how you can better understand people, places, institutions, history, and geography as a result of collecting stamps. (b) Briefly describe some aspects of the history, growth, and development of the United States postal system. Tell how it is different from postal systems in other countries.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Stamp Collecting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 12, p. 13)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wash and dry hands thoroughly before handling fragile historical covers to prevent transfer of acidic body oils.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe postage stamps serve solely as prepaid shipping receipts without educational value.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Every stamp design represents deliberate government commemoration of significant cultural figures, architecture, or science.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Historical Chronology: Postage stamp portraits commemorate pivotal civil leaders, military conflicts, and revolutionary political movements.
- Geographical Boundaries: Territorial names printed on vintage stamps reflect shifting colonial borders and national sovereignty.
- Domestic Rate Systems: The Postal Act of 1845 established cheap uniform rates across all states.
- Universal Postal Union: International agreements standardized cross-border mail dispatch fees between sovereign countries without surcharge.

### Req 3: Philatelic Classifications and Stamp Forms
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Show at least ONE example of each of the following: (a) Perforated and imperforate stamps (b) Mint and used stamps (c) Sheet, booklet, and coil stamps (d) Numbers on plate block, booklet, coil, or marginal markings (e) Overprint and surcharge (f) Metered mail (g) Definitive, commemorative, semipostal, and airmail stamps (h) Cancellation and postmark (i) First day cover (j) Postal stationery (aerogramme, stamped envelope, and postal card)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Stamp Collecting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use blunt spade-tip metal tongs to transfer stamps without creasing edges or tearing perforations.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners frequently assume scissors were originally used to create jagged perforation teeth on purpose.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Perforating machines punch rows of circular holes through paper web to facilitate easy tearing.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Perforation Status: Perforated stamps feature punched holes while imperforate varieties possess straight cut edges.
- Condition Category: Mint stamps retain original uncancelled gum whereas used stamps bear cancellation ink.
- Marginal Identification: Plate block numbers printed on pane selvage identify the specific printing cylinder.
- Overprints add text for special service while surcharges alter the face denomination.

### Req 4: Catalog Valuation and Stamp Condition
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Demonstrate the use of ONE standard catalog for several different stamp issues. Explain why catalog value can vary from the corresponding purchase price. (b) Explain the meaning of the term condition as used to describe a stamp. Show examples that illustrate the different factors that affect a stamp's value.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Stamp Collecting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 49)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Handle fragile unmounted stamps over padded felt surfaces to prevent accidental folding or creasing.
- **Common Myth:** Novices believe catalog numbers represent immutable cash prices dealers must pay to sellers.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Catalogs provide baseline retail guides, while real transaction prices fluctuate according to condition and demand.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Published catalog prices represent national dealer retail estimates rather than guaranteed buy offers.
- Margin Centering: Superb stamps feature perfectly balanced margins surrounding the engraved design on all sides.
- Structural Defects: Paper thins, creases, pinholes, and missing perforations severely discount a specimen's commercial value.
- Never hinged mint gum commands premium valuations over previously hinged stamp backs.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Stamp Collecting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 55, p. 56)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use specialized non-toxic watermark fluid only in well-ventilated spaces away from open flames.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners think stamp tongs are interchangeable with common sharp household cosmetic tweezers.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cosmetic tweezers possess sharp serrated jaws that pinch, puncture, and permanently crease stamp paper fibers.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Metal spade-tip tongs prevent the transfer of corrosive skin grease to stamp paper.
- A perforation gauge counts the number of perforation teeth within two centimeters.
- Ten-power optical loupes reveal microprinting, engraved plate flaws, and flat plate print dots.
- Specialized watermark fluid makes hidden paper watermarks visible inside a black ceramic tray.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use a rotary paper trimmer with a finger guard when cutting plastic mounts to length.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often store albums flat in damp basements or hot unventilated attic spaces.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Stacking albums flat causes intense pressure that fuses stamp gum permanently to adjacent album pages.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Album Selection: Loose-leaf albums with acid-free quadrille pages allow flexible custom arrangement and continuous expansion.
- Mount Application: Archival split-back polyester mounts protect mint stamp gum from moisture and physical abrasion.
- Vertical Shelving: Always store stamp albums upright on bookshelves to prevent excessive weight from fusing gum.
- Climate Safeguards: Maintain steady room temperature and low relative humidity to prevent destructive fungal foxing.
