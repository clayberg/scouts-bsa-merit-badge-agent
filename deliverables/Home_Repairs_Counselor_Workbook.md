### Req 1: Home Repair Safety Protocols
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while working on home repairs and what you should do to anticipate, mitigate, prevent, and respond to these hazards. Describe the appropriate safety gear and clothing that should be used when working on home repairs. (b) Discuss general precautions related to home repairs. Name at least 10 safe practices that every home repairer should exercise.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Home Repairs Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 17)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear ANSI approved Z87.1 safety glasses and closed toe leather work boots on all repair sites.
- **Common Myth:** Ordinary prescription eyeglasses provide sufficient impact protection while operating rotary power tools.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Safety glasses with side shields prevent permanent eye damage from flying debris and chemical splashes.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Eye Protection Standard: ANSI approved impact goggles prevent corneal punctures from flying wood or metal chips.
- Respiratory Hazard Filter: Proper particulate respirators block toxic silica particles and mold spores during demolition.
- Ladder Fall Protection: Maintaining three points of contact on rung ladders drastically reduces tipping risks.
- Chemical Vapor Ventilation: Active mechanical exhaust fans prevent volatile organic fumes from concentrating in rooms.

- Window Draft Sealing: Foam compression tape stops cold drafts around poorly fitted wooden sash perimeters.

- Mortar Joint Tuckpointing: Polymer modified asphalt patch stabilizes fractured driveway slabs against freezing water infiltration.

### Req 3: Furniture Shop Structure Repairs
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Under the supervision of your parent, guardian, or counselor, do THREE of the following: (a) Install or build equipment for storing tools. (b) Build a workbench. (c) Repair a piece of furniture. (d) Paint or varnish a piece of furniture, a door, or trim on a house. (e) Repair a sagging door or gate. (f) Repair a loose step or railing. (g) Repair a fence.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Home Repairs Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 74, p. 77)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear hearing protection and eye shields when cutting structural lumber with circular saws.
- **Common Myth:** Gluing end grain wood joints with white PVA glue produces unbreakable load bearing bonds.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cross bracing and diagonal gussets distribute load stresses to eliminate frame racking in workbenches.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Heavy wooden tool cabinets require wall studs engagement with hardened lag screws.
- Workbench Joint Rigidity: Bolted half lap joints prevent heavy wooden workbenches from swaying under lateral forces.
- Furniture Tenon Clamping: Polyvinyl acetate woodworking adhesive requires four hours under firm clamp pressure.
- Sanding parallel with the wood grain prevents unsightly scratches across stained timber surfaces.

(b) Replace an electrical cord or repair a plug or lamp socket. (c) Install a single-pole light switch. (d) Replace an electrical wall outlet.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Home Repairs Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 26)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Lock out breaker panels and verify dead circuits using reliable voltage testers before touching conductors.
- **Common Myth:** Switching off a single wall light switch guarantees that outlet boxes are fully de-energized.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Non-contact voltage testers must be verified against known live receptacles before testing suspect wires.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Circuit breaker handles must flip fully off before reset into on positions.
- Polarized Cord Attachment: Ribbed neutral conductor wires attach to silver colored terminal screws on plugs.
- Terminal Loop Direction: Clockwise copper wire loops stay clamped firmly under tightening outlet brass screws.
- Bare copper equipment grounding conductors must connect securely to green grounding screws.

### Req 5: Domestic Plumbing Fixture Maintenance
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Under the supervision of your parent, guardian, or counselor, locate and know how to turn off the main water source, including well pumps, and do TWO of the following: (a) Clear a clogged drain or trap. (b) Repair a leaky water faucet. (c) Repair a flush toilet. (d) Repair a leaky hose or connector. (e) Clean or replace a sprinkler head.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Home Repairs Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Depressurize supply lines by opening downstream faucets before unthreading valve stems or supply fittings.
- **Common Myth:** Chemical drain cleaners dissolve mechanical clogs safely without damaging domestic copper or PVC pipes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Greasing plumbing slip joint rubber washers prevents friction tears during compression nut tightening.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Main Shutoff Location: Quarter turn ball valves shut off pressurized municipal water supplies immediately.
- Drain Trap Disassembly: Removing sink P-traps requires placing buckets beneath slip joints to catch wastewater.
- Faucet Stem Rebuilding: Worn neoprene seat washers cause continuous water dripping from compression faucet spouts.
- Adjusting flapper chain slack prevents ghost flushing caused by leaking flush valves.
