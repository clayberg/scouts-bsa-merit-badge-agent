**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while working on home repairs and what you should do to anticipate, mitigate, prevent, and respond to these hazards. Describe the appropriate safety gear and clothing that should be used when working on home repairs. (b) Discuss general precautions related to home repairs. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Home Repairs Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 17)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Home Repair Safety and Hazard Prevention discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Carrying a small toolbox or paint can in one hand while climbing a ladder is safe as long as you hold the rail with your other hand.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must always face the ladder and use both hands to climb without carrying anything in your hands (p. 10).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- When climbing, face the ladder and use both hands, don'tcarry anything in your hands (p. 10).
- Preventing Ladder Tipping Hazards: Don't overreach when you are on a ladder, as it may tip and fall (p. 10).
- Respiratory Protection for Toxins: Use a special respirator when working with toxic substances (p. 10).
- Flammable Chemical Container Storage: Keep the containers tightly closed and away from heat or flames (p. 10).

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Rougher surfaces require a longer roller nap so the fibers can reach and work paint into irregular crevices like concrete block and stucco (p. 45).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Wall Works, Caulking, and Weather-Stripping PAINT TRAY ROLLERS Using rollers gets a paint job done quickly (p. 45).

- Matching Nap to Texture: Generally, the rougher the surface you are painting, the longer the nap you should use (p. 45).
- Painting Stucco and Concrete: The longer nap makes it possible to work paint into irregular surfaces such as concrete block and highly textured walls like stucco (p. 45).

### Req 4: Basic Household Electrical System Repairs
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Under the supervision of your parent, guardian, or counselor, do TWO of the following: (a) Locate a main electrical switch box and know how to replace a fuse or reset a circuit breaker. (b) Replace an electrical cord or repair a plug or lamp socket. (c) Install a single-pole light switch. (d) Replace an electrical wall outlet.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Home Repairs Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 26)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Home Repairs Requirement 4 on basic household electrical system repairs: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** When a circuit breaker trips to the center position, you can restore power by pushing the toggle directly back to the on position.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** To reset a tripped circuit breaker that has moved to the center position, you must first flip the toggle all the way to full off and then back to on (p. 24).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Toggles in a circuit breaker flip to "off" or to a center position for both short circuits and circuit overloads (p. 24).
- To reset the circuit and restore power, flip the switch to full "off" and then back to "on." Circuit breaker fuses have a reset button or switch (p. 24).
- Push-Button Fuse Reactivation: When blown, they can be reactivated with the button or switch (p. 24).
- Full Off-to-On Reset Sequence: Push the switch to the "off" position, then back to the "on" position (p. 24).
