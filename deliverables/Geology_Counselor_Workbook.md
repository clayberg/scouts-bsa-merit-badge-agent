### Req 1: Fundamentals of Earth Science
**Official Requirement 1:** Define geology. Discuss how geologists learn about rock formations. In geology, explain why the study of the present is important to understanding the past.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Geology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 79, p. 81)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what specific ways do current mud cracks in dry riverbeds help us interpret ancient shale layers?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Discipline Definition: Planetary geologists investigate crustal materials, mineral compositions, and deep tectonic dynamics.
  - Rock Outcrop Analysis: Exposed strata formations reveal sedimentary layers through fossil identification and strike measurements.
  - Uniformitarianism Principle: Present day physical erosion and volcanic eruptions explain ancient stratigraphic sedimentary sequences.
  - Core Sample Drilling: Deep cylindrical rock cores provide pristine evidence of buried subsurface structural folds.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Field Notebook Setup: Record local outcrop coordinates, weather conditions, and precise bedding strike measurements.
  - Step 2 - Outcrop Visual Observation: Sketch visible horizontal rock strata while marking color changes and erosional disconformities.
  - Step 3 - Rock Texture Comparison: Examine grain boundary sorting with a ten power hand lens across specimens.
  - Step 4 - Modern Process Correlation: Compare river ripple marks on mud bars with ancient lithified sandstone silt.
- **Common Myth:** Earth rocks formed through catastrophic, unrepeatable events unlike modern surface processes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** James Hutton demonstrated that ongoing slow processes like sedimentation and weathering shape rocks over deep time.

### Req 2: Commercial Earth Mineral Resources
**Official Requirement 2:** Pick three resources that can be extracted or mined from Earth for commercial use. Discuss with your counselor how each product is discovered and processed.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Geology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 43)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what specific ways do seismic waves reveal the presence of porous underground sandstone traps containing hydrocarbons?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Geophysical Exploration: Aeromagnetic surveys and gravity anomaly measurements pinpoint deep hidden metallic ore bodies.
  - Surface Mining Extraction: Open pit quarries remove overburden rock layers to expose massive copper porphyry.
  - Chemical froth baths separate pulverized copper sulfide minerals from crushed gangue rock.
  - Petroleum Hydrocarbon Refining: Fractional distillation towers heat crude oil to isolate gasoline, diesel, and bitumen.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Resource Target Selection: Research three distinct economic commodities such as copper ore, petroleum, and aggregate.
  - Step 2 - Geological Prospecting Mapping: Chart magnetic anomalies and geochemical stream sediment assays indicating concentrated subsurface deposits.
  - Step 3 - Extraction Method Evaluation: Outline surface quarry excavation steps versus underground room and pillar shaft operations.
  - Step 4 - Industrial Processing Flowchart: Diagram smelting furnaces or fractional distillation columns separating target minerals from impurities.
- **Common Myth:** Valuable minerals and metals occur naturally as pure solid blocks ready for immediate manufacturing.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Most commercial ores contain low percentages of desired metals bound chemically inside host rock requiring intense beneficiation.

#### Req 4a(5): Field Indicators of Stream Flow Direction
**Official Requirement 4a(5):** Visit a nearby stream. Find clues that show the direction of water flow, even if the water is missing. Record your observations in a notebook, and sketch those clues you observe. Discuss your observations with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Geology Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 70, p. 86)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4a
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** If you stand in an empty desert wash with no water in sight, what physical clues tell you which way the flash flood ran?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Field Indicators of Stream Flow Direction: Geologists establish past flow direction in dry arroyos by examining imbricated pebbles that tilt upstream, asymmetric current ripples, and debris caught on the upstream sides of boulders. Documenting these directional sedimentary structures in a field notebook preserves physical records of peak flood energy (p. 31).
- **Common Myth:** A dry streambed leaves no physical evidence of which direction water flowed without active current.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Pebbles shingled against one another lean upstream, sediment piles on the downstream sides of obstacles, and current ripples record flow direction indefinitely.
