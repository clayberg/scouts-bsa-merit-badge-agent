### Req 1: Whitewater Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in whitewater activities, including branches and trees in water along a shore and stretching across the stream, rocks, hydraulics over ledges or low-head dams, strong wind, low water or air temperature, and thunder and lightning storms. Explain what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review with your counselor the prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for the following injuries or illnesses that could occur while participating in whitewater activities: cold-water shock; hypothermia; head, neck, and back injuries; heat-related illnesses; sunburn; dehydration; blisters; bruises; cuts; sprains and strains; shoulder dislocation; and submersion injuries. (c) Discuss with your counselor the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy and the American Whitewater safety guidelines, including the use of helmet and life jackets.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Whitewater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Whitewater Requirement 1: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for whitewater hazards and first aid, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Reaching high overhead during a brace gives you the safest and strongest leverage to prevent a capsize.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Reaching overhead during a high brace places extreme stress on the shoulder joint and is a primary cause of shoulder dislocations in whitewater paddling (p. 21).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Rapid Heatstroke Cooling: Cool the victim immediately through immersion with cold packs in the groin area and armpits (p. 21).
- Conscious Victim Rehydration: Increase the body's fluid level by having a conscious victim sip water (p. 21).
- Sunburn is an inflammation of the skin caused by too much exposure to the sun (p. 21).
- Shoulder dislocations are one of the most serious injuries associated with whitewater activities (p. 21).

### Req 2: River Features and Boat Control
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Demonstrate understanding of the following river features by drawing lines to show the flow of water: upstream V, downstream V, riffle, eddy with an eddy line, ledge, river bend, shallows, current at different depths, drop, horizon line, and hydraulic. (b) Describe how waves form including standing waves and wave trains. (c) Explain how to tilt or edge the boat without leaning your body (1) When entering and exiting an eddy. (2) When ferrying in downstream and upstream directions. (d) Explain when, why, and how you should scout a river while ashore, while on the river, and when you should portage your boat.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Whitewater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 30, p. 61)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Whitewater Requirement 2: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for river features and boat control, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** When crossing an eddy line, you should tilt the boat by throwing your upper torso and head over the gunwale.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Proper boat edging is done with your hips and knees while keeping your upper body centered vertically over the hull for stability (p. 30).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Lateral Ferrying Across Current: Upstream (forward) ferries and downstream (back) ferries move you sideways across the current (p. 30).
- In an upstream ferry, you face upstream and paddle forward with your bow at an angle to the current (p. 30).
- Bow Direction Movement: You move across the current in the direction your bowis pointing (p. 30).
- To perform a downstream ferry, you face downstream and back paddle with your stern pointed upstream at an angle to the current (p. 30).

#### Req 2a: Mapping River Current Features
**Official Requirement 2a:** Demonstrate understanding of the following river features by drawing lines to show the flow of water: upstream V, downstream V, riffle, eddy with an eddy line, ledge, river bend, shallows, current at different depths, drop, horizon line, and hydraulic.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Whitewater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 30, p. 61)* • **Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 2a Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist (Understanding of The Following River)
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** An upstream V pointing toward you indicates the deepest, safest chute between two rocks.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** An upstream V points directly at a rock or obstruction near the surface that you should steer around, whereas a downstream V marks the open chute (p. 30).

### Req 3: Prerequisite Canoe and Kayak Skills
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Before doing requirements 4 through 12, earn the Canoeing merit badge if you will be using a canoe to earn this merit badge. If you will be using a kayak, earn the Kayaking merit badge. Then do ONE of the following: (a) If you will be using a canoe to earn this merit badge, demonstrate strokes and maneuvers from the Canoeing merit badge to the satisfaction of your counselor. (b) If you will be using a kayak to earn this merit badge, demonstrate strokes and maneuvers from the Kayaking merit badge to the satisfaction of your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Whitewater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 56, p. 62)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Whitewater Requirement 3: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for prerequisite canoe and kayak skills, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** A Scout who has earned the Canoeing merit badge is automatically qualified to complete the Whitewater merit badge in a solo kayak without further prerequisites.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** You must earn the specific prerequisite merit badge matching the craft you use for Whitewater, meaning kayakers must first earn Kayaking and canoeists must earn Canoeing (p. 56).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Canoeists and kayakers use backstrokes to back paddle, that is, to slow or reverse the forward motion of the boat (p. 56).
- Stopping in River Eddies: As you paddle downstream, you will want to stop in an eddy to rest or to scout a rapid (p. 56).
- The maneuver to enter an eddy while heading downstream is called an eddy turn (p. 56).
- Exiting via Peel: Later you will learn how to exit an eddy using a peel (p. 56).

### Tandem Canoeist: Bow & Stern Strokes, Braces & Righting Pry
**Official Requirement 4a (Parent Overview):** Option A, Tandem Canoeist. Do ALL of the following strokes and techniques on calm water: (1) Demonstrate the following strokes in the bow: cross forward, bow draw, cross bow draw, bow pry, and sculling draw. (2) Demonstrate the following strokes in the stern: stern draw, stern pry, sculling draw, and forward with stern pry. (3) Demonstrate a high brace, low brace, and righting pry.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Whitewater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 50, p. 60)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Whitewater Requirement 4: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for calm-water whitewater stroke mastery, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** In a tandem canoe, only the stern paddler is responsible for steering the boat into a turn.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Effective tandem maneuvers require synchronized strokes, such as the bow paddler applying a cross-bow draw while the stern paddler sweeps or powers forward (p. 50).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Req 4a(1) (1): Demonstrate the following strokes in the bow: cross forward, bow draw, cross bow draw, bow pry, and sculling draw.
- Req 4a(2) (2): Demonstrate the following strokes in the stern: stern draw, stern pry, sculling draw, and forward with stern pry.
- Req 4a(3) (3): Demonstrate a high brace, low brace, and righting pry.
- In the peel out, note how both paddlers use theirleft knees to lean into and tilt the boat into the turn.

If a tandem canoe is used, the skills must be demonstrated from both the bow and stern positions. (a) Launch and land. (b) Paddle forward in a straight line at least 10 boat lengths. (c) Backpaddle in a straight line at least five boat lengths. (d) Ferry upstream from both sides of the river. (e) Ferry downstream from both sides of the river. (f) Eddy turn from both sides of an eddy. (g) Peel out from both sides of an eddy.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Whitewater Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 46, p. 61)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Operate under BSA Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat for Whitewater Requirement 10: confirm swimmer classification, wear a properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket when on the water for moving-water maneuvers and ferries, and maintain buddy checks throughout the session.
- **Common Myth:** To ferry across a very fast current, you should turn your boat at a 90-degree angle perpendicular to the river flow.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Turning broadside to a strong current pushes the boat rapidly downstream and risks flipping on the upstream edge, whereas a slight angle to the current lets the water glide the hull sideways (p. 46).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Sideways Current Crossing: Upstream (forward) ferries and downstream (back) ferries move you sideways across the current (p. 46).
- In an upstream ferry, you face upstream and paddle forward with your bow at an angle to the current (p. 46).
- Tracking Toward Bow Angle: You move across the current in the direction your bowis pointing (p. 46).
- To perform a downstream ferry, you face downstream and back paddle with your stern pointed upstream at an angle to the current (p. 46).

- **Common Myth:** If you capsize and wash into a shallow, rocky rapid, you should stand up immediately to walk back to shore.
