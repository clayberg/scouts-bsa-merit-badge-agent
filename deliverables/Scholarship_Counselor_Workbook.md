## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Prepare):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Make a list of educational places located where you live (other than schools). Visit one, and report on how you used the place for self-education. (b) With your counselor's and your parent or guardian's approval, interview two professionals (other than teachers or other professionals at your school) with established careers. Find out where they were educated, what training they received, and how their education and training have helped prepare them for the career they have chosen. Find out how they continue to educate themselves. Discuss what you find out with your counselor. (c) Using a daily planner, show your counselor how you keep track of assignments and activities, and discuss how you manage your time. (d) Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the different methods of research available to you for school assignments, such as the library, books and periodicals, and the internet.

**Official Requirement 1a:** Show that you have had an average grade of B or higher (80% or higher) for one term or semester.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scholarship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 21)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Scouts document an overall B average or eighty percent benchmark across all registered subjects during a full school term. Counselors review cumulative score sheets rather than isolated test papers.

  - Step 1 - Record Retrieval: Request an official printed report card or sealed grade transcript from school.

- **Common Myth:** Advanced placement or honors weightings are mandatory to establish an acceptable B grade threshold.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Standard unweighted grade point averages of 3.0 or numeric averages of eighty percent fully satisfy criteria.

### Req 3: School Citizenship Official Certification
**Official Requirement 3:** Get a note from the principal of your school (or another school official named by the principal) that states that during the past year your behavior, leadership, and service have been satisfactory. Note: If you are home-schooled or your school environment does not include a principal, you may obtain a note from a counterpart such as your parent or guardian.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Scholarship Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 49, p. 51)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why is satisfactory personal behavior considered just as vital as high academic test scores?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Administrator Endorsement: School principals certify that student character, leadership, and conduct meet institutional standards.
  - Alternative Educator Authority: Designated guidance counselors or homeschool educators provide valid behavioral certification notes when needed.
  - Service Evaluation Scope: School citizenship encompasses classroom courtesy, helpful peer support, and active campus service.
  - Document Submission Process: Signed administrative letters must be delivered directly to the merit badge counselor.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Official Contact: Schedule an appointment with your principal, vice principal, or headmaster office.
  - Step 2 - Requirement Explanation: Explain the Scholarship merit badge character, service, and leadership verification criteria clearly.
  - Step 3 - Statement Collection: Obtain a signed letter or official statement confirming satisfactory past year standing.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Submission: Present the original signed statement to your counselor during badge review session.
- **Common Myth:** Only the head principal of a public school has authority to sign the behavioral endorsement letter.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Vice principals, designated school deans, or homeschool program directors possess recognized authority to provide documentation.
