### Req 3: Plumber Hand Tool Mastery
**Official Requirement 3:** Show how to use five important plumber's tools.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plumbing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 24)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 3 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never pull a pipe wrench toward your face; push firmly with an open palm or pull away.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must you remove the internal burr from a copper pipe after using a wheel cutter?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Align jaw opening towards the direction of pull so serrated teeth grip firmly.
  - Rotate the cutter wheel smoothly while tightening the adjustment knob every two revolutions.
  - Swiveling spring loaded jaws reach deep behind sink basins to loosen faucet mounting nuts.
  - Tongue Groove Pliers: Offset slip jaws adjust across multiple parallel widths to grasp irregular drainage slip nuts.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Wrench Jaw Alignment: Position the straight pipe wrench so the hook jaw faces the direction of turn.
  - Step 2 - Copper Tube Cutting: Clamp the tubing cutter wheel lightly onto the scored copper cut mark line.
  - Step 3 - Reaming Burr Removal: Extend the cutter reamer blade inside the pipe bore to scrape away burrs.
  - Step 4 - Basin Nut Removal: Engage spring loaded basin wrench jaws onto the undersink mounting nut and turn.
- **Common Myth:** A pipe wrench and an adjustable wrench can be used interchangeably on chrome-plated brass nuts.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Serrated pipe wrench teeth will gouge and crush delicate chrome finish, which requires smooth-jaw wrenches.

### Req 4: Plumbing Component Terminology Recognition
**Official Requirement 4:** Identify and explain the following terms: washer, retaining nut, plunger (rubber force cup), solder, flux, elbow, tee, nipple, coupling, plug, union, trap, drainpipe, and water meter.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plumbing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 19)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Avoid touching chemical flux paste with bare fingers because zinc chloride causes severe skin and eye irritation.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why is a sanitary tee curved differently than a pressurized water supply tee fitting?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Short lengths of male threaded pipe connect closely positioned valves and female fittings.
  - Directional Elbow Fitting: Ninety or forty-five degree elbows alter flow direction without crimping or kinking pipes.
  - Sanitary Waste Trap: Curved piping retains a standing water plug that halts poisonous sewer gas infiltration.
  - Solder Flux Cleaning: Chemical paste prevents oxidation and draws molten filler metal deep into heated copper fittings.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Component Function: Separate fittings into supply, drainage, sealing, directional, and flow measurement groups.
  - Step 2 - Fitting Thread Identification: Inspect internal female and external male threads across nipples, couplings, and plugs.
  - Step 3 - Trap Seal Inspection: Measure the vertical distance between the dip and crown weir on a P trap.
  - Step 4 - Meter Dial Reading: Read the municipal water meter register dials to record consumption in gallons or feet.
- **Common Myth:** A pipe union and a standard coupling perform identical permanent connections between two pipe ends.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A union allows quick disconnection without cutting pipes, whereas a coupling creates a fixed connection.

### Req 5: Common Piping Material Applications
**Official Requirement 5:** Name the kinds of pipe that are used most often in a plumbing system. Explain why these pipes are used.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Plumbing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 31)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never test plastic pipe systems like PVC or CPVC using compressed air due to explosive shattering hazards.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why has flexible PEX largely replaced rigid copper in modern residential domestic water supply lines?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Rigid copper provides exceptional heat tolerance, biostatic properties, and durable high pressure containment.
  - Flexible PEX Tubing: Cross-linked polyethylene resists scaling, expands during freezes, and installs with minimal joint fittings.
  - Drainage PVC Pipe: Polyvinyl chloride offers smooth interior surfaces and chemical resistance for gravity drain waste stacks.
  - Added chlorine enables CPVC to handle pressurized hot water that standard PVC cannot tolerate.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Material Property Comparison: Evaluate pipe temperature limits, working pressure ratings, and flexibility for the specific installation.
  - Step 2 - Copper Type Selection: Select Type M, L, or K copper tubing based on wall thickness requirements.
  - Step 3 - Plastic Pipe Designation: Verify schedule forty ratings on PVC drainage pipe before assembling gravity sewer lines.
  - Step 4 - Thermal Expansion Allowance: Install expansion loops along long runs of CPVC or PEX to accommodate heat.
- **Common Myth:** White PVC pipe can safely convey both pressurized hot and cold household drinking water.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Standard PVC softens and fails under hot water; chlorinated CPVC or PEX is required.
