### Req 1: Livestock Breeds and Origins
**Official Requirement 1:** Name two breeds of livestock in each of the following classifications: horses, dairy cattle, beef cattle, sheep, hogs, poultry, and goats. Tell their principal uses and merits. Tell where the breeds originated.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animal Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 15)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Maintain safe positioning outside the flight zone and kick arc when evaluating large farm animals.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the native environment of a breed influence its heat tolerance and forage efficiency?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Draft Horse Merits: Percheron horses provide heavy pulling strength developed originally in the French Perche region.
  - Holstein cattle produce high milk volume and trace their lineage to northern Netherlands.
  - Angus cattle yield heavily marbled beef carcasses adapted to diverse American pasture environments.
  - Rambouillet sheep produce dense fine fleece suited for arid western range rangeland conditions.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Identify Species: Categorize livestock classes across equine, bovine, caprine, porcine, and avian agricultural groups.
  - Step 2 - Research Origins: Map the geographic history and development conditions of two selected livestock breeds.
  - Step 3 - Contrast Merit: Compare market yield, hardiness, and feed efficiency between distinct selected livestock breeds.
  - Step 4 - Record Utility: Document primary commercial outputs including dairy, meat, wool, work, or egg production.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe all cattle breeds provide identical milk and meat yields across farm environments.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Breed genetics dictate specialized performance differences between lean beef cattle and high-volume dairy cows.

### Req 2: Livestock Pathogens and Biosecurity
**Official Requirement 2:** List two diseases that afflict the animals in each of the classifications in requirement 1. Describe the symptoms of each disease and explain how each is contracted and how it could be prevented.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animal Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 60)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear disposable nitrile gloves and sanitizable rubber boots when examining livestock displaying infectious symptoms.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why must newly purchased animals undergo a thirty-day quarantine period before joining herds?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Equine Colic Symptoms: Abdominal pain causes horses to paw ground, sweat excessively, and roll repeatedly.
  - Bovine Respiratory Disease: Viral shipping fever causes fever, nasal discharge, and rapid labored breathing in cattle.
  - Pigs contract circovirus through direct exposure to contaminated feces and infected bodily fluids.
  - Poultry producers manage coccidiosis using ionophore feed additives and clean dry brooding bedding.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Recognize Symptoms: Observe herd members for lethargy, abnormal gait, nasal discharge, and appetite loss.
  - Step 2 - Identify Vector: Determine whether pathogens spread via airborne droplets, vectors, parasites, or oral ingestion.
  - Step 3 - Establish Biosecurity: Implement boot wash stations, vehicle disinfection, and quarantine pens for incoming stock.
  - Step 4 - Schedule Vaccines: Administer preventative vaccines following veterinary guidance to establish durable herd pathogen immunity.
- **Common Myth:** Novices frequently assume farm animal diseases spread exclusively through dirty water and soiled barns.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Many devastating livestock diseases spread rapidly through aerosol transmission, bloodsucking insects, or subclinical carriers.

### Req 3: Comparative Digestive System Anatomy
**Official Requirement 3:** Draw models of the digestive tracts of ruminants, horses, pigs, and poultry. Explain how the differences in structure and function among these types of digestive tracts affect the nutritional management of these species.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animal Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 44, p. 46)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Introduce dietary grain transitions gradually across two weeks to prevent fatal ruminal acidosis and bloat.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why can a sheep survive on rough pasture grass while a pig requires concentrated grain feed?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Ruminant Fermentation Vats: Cattle rumen microbes ferment structural plant cellulose into usable volatile fatty acids.
  - Equine Hindgut Digestion: Horses digest coarse structural hay within a large microbial cecum and colon.
  - Swine simple stomachs secrete hydrochloric acid and enzymes to break down cereal proteins.
  - Avian Gizzard Grinding: Birds utilize swallowed insoluble grit within the muscular gizzard to grind seeds.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Illustrate Chambers: Sketch the four stomach compartments of ruminants including rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum.
  - Step 2 - Trace Hindgut: Draw the enlarged cecum and colon responsible for equine microbial cellulose fermentation.
  - Step 3 - Map Avian: Detail the poultry crop, proventriculus, muscular gizzard, paired ceca, and common cloaca.
  - Step 4 - Formulate Rations: Match concentrated grains and fibrous forages directly to anatomical digestive capabilities.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts often assume cows possess four separate stomachs rather than one four-compartment stomach system.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Ruminants have a single complex stomach partitioned into four anatomically distinct chambers with specialized functions.

### Req 4: Livestock Management and Husbandry
**Official Requirement 4:** Select one type of animal: beef cow, dairy cow, horse, sheep, goat, or hog, or a poultry flock, and tell how you would properly manage it. Include in your discussion nutritional (feeding) concerns, housing, disease prevention, waste control/removal, breeding programs, and biosecurity as appropriate.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Animal Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 44, p. 76)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Ensure adequate air exchange in manure storage pits to prevent asphyxiation from toxic hydrogen sulfide gas.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** In what specific ways do ambient humidity and poor barn ventilation accelerate pneumonia outbreaks among confined calves?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Nutritional Balance Priorities: Balanced livestock rations must provide precise ratios of protein, energy, minerals, and vitamins.
  - Barn Ventilation Standards: Continuous airflow removes hazardous ammonia gases, moisture, and airborne dust from animal quarters.
  - Disease Prevention Routines: Scheduled herd health protocols combine annual vaccinations, parasite control, and regular body condition scoring.
  - Covered containment facilities prevent agricultural manure runoff from contaminating regional water tables and streams.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Calculate Rations: Determine daily dry matter intake and crude protein needs based on animal weight.
  - Step 2 - Design Housing: Provide adequate square footage, clean bedding, and draft-free ventilation for the selected species.
  - Step 3 - Implement Healthcare: Administer scheduled parasite dewormers and core vaccines following licensed large-animal veterinary protocols.
  - Step 4 - Manage Waste: Collect and compost barn manure to generate organic fertilizer without environmental water contamination.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe farm animal housing functions primarily to provide heated indoor comfort during winter.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Livestock housing primarily protects animals from moisture, direct wind, and heat stress while maintaining high ventilation.

#### Req 5b: Purebred and Crossbreeding Systems
**Official Requirement 5b:** Explain the terms pure breeding and cross breeding.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Pure breeding mates animals within one breed to maintain genetic uniformity, breed standards, and predictable ancestral pedigrees. Crossbreeding combines two or more distinct breeds to capitalize on hybrid vigor and combine complementary traits.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** Structured crossbreeding systems produce hybrid vigor that boosts offspring growth rates, vitality, and lifetime reproductive efficiency.
