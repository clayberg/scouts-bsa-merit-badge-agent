### Req 1: Farm Shop Safety and Hazard Symbols
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Discuss with your counselor the safety equipment, tools, and clothing used while checking or repairing farm equipment. Use this equipment, tools, and/or clothing (when needed or called for) in meeting the requirements for this merit badge. (b) Draw a plan showing a well-equipped farm shop. Point out the shop's mandatory safety devices and features. (c) Find all the universal warning and safety symbols on a piece of equipment and explain what they mean. (d) Describe what a safety data sheet (SDS) is and tell why it is used. Obtain the SDS for any engine coolant, oil, grease, fuel, hydraulic or transmission fluid, or other flammable or hazardous materials you use in meeting the requirements for this merit badge.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Farm Mechanics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 38, p. 56)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Farm Mechanics Requirement 1 on farm shop safety and hazard symbols: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** A Scout might think that reading the brief caution statement on an oil or coolant bottle provides all the emergency and toxicological information found on a Safety Data Sheet.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A Safety Data Sheet (SDS) is a standardized 16-section manufacturer document that supplements container labels with detailed chemical properties, safe handling practices, and emergency first-aid procedures (p. 38).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- A safety data sheet, or SDS, gives details about how to safely handle, use, and store a potentially hazardous material or product (p. 38).
- Manufacturer Label Supplements: The information on a data sheet is provided by the manufacturer and is meant to supplement the information listed on a product's label (p. 38).
- SDSs have 16 sections that provide general information about the chemical, identification, hazards, composition, safe handling practices, and emergency (p. 38).
- Additionally, other technical and scientific information such as physical and chemical properties, exposure control information, and toxicological (p. 38).

### Req 2: Farm Equipment Power and Transmission
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain how power is produced or transferred in a: (a) Diesel engine (b) Hydraulic system (c) Transmission or any other power system
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Farm Mechanics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 50, p. 56)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Farm Mechanics Requirement 2 on farm equipment power and transmission: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** A Scout might assume that a diesel tractor engine uses spark plugs just like a gasoline lawn mower and that hydraulic cylinders generate their own energy without the engine.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Diesel engines compress air until it is hot enough to ignite injected fuel without spark plugs, while transmissions and hydraulic systems transfer that engine power through gears, shafts, and pressurized fluid (p. 50).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Most tractors use combined transmission-hydraulic fluid to lubricate the power train and operate the hydraulic system (p. 50).
- Recommended Operator Manual Fluids: In any tractor, use the type of fluid or fluids recommended in the operator's manual (p. 50).
- Check fluid levels daily and maintain their proper levels (p. 50).
- Scheduled Fluid and Filter Changes: Change fluids and filters at the recommended intervals (p. 50).

#### Req 2a: Diesel Engine Power Production
**Official Requirement 2a:** Diesel engine.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Farm Mechanics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 50, p. 56)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Diesel Engine)
- **Common Myth:** Diesel engines mix fuel and air in a carburetor and rely on an electrical spark plug to ignite the mixture.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A diesel engine draws in only air on the intake stroke, compresses it to high pressure and temperature, and then injects fuel directly into the hot air for ignition (p. 50).

### Req 3: Hand Tool Maintenance and Storage
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Replace the handle of any tool found on the farm. (b) Organize a tool rack or a storage system for nails, bolts, nuts, and washers. (c) Using a hand file, properly dress the mushroomed head of a chisel or punch. (d) Using a hand file, correctly dress a screwdriver tip.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Farm Mechanics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 25)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Farm Mechanics Requirement 3 on hand tool maintenance and storage: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** A Scout might think that a mushroomed metal lip on the top of a cold chisel or punch makes the tool easier to hit with a hammer and is safe to ignore.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** When repetitive hammering causes the struck end of a chisel or punch to mushroom outward, you must dress the head with a hand file to restore a clean bevel and prevent metal splinters from breaking off (p. 24).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Choose a chisel only large enough for the task so that you use the full cutting edge (p. 24).
- Preventing Flying Metal Chips: Using only a corner of a large chisel could break the corner, damage the cutting edge, and send a snapped piece of metal flying (p. 24).
- Choose the smallest cold chisel that suits the task, but not so small that you risk breaking the tool (p. 24).
- Wear eye protection (p. 24).

### Req 4: Engine Maintenance and Seasonal Storage
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) On an engine-powered machine: Grease all fittings, change the oil and oil filter, clean the air filter, clean the radiator fins, and replace the fuel filters. (b) For any engine-powered machine, create a preoperational checklist; include checking the engine coolant, engine oil, hydraulic and/or transmission fluid, and battery voltage (using a voltmeter). Using your checklist, conduct a preoperational check of that machinery or equipment. (c) Prepare any farm machine for winter storage.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Farm Mechanics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 56, p. 58)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Farm Mechanics Requirement 4 on engine maintenance and seasonal storage: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** A Scout might think that farm machinery only needs oil changes and filter checks once a year after the harvest season ends.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Routine engine servicing includes greasing all fittings, changing oil and fuel filters, cleaning air filters and radiator fins, and following a daily preoperational checklist or seasonal winter storage protocol (p. 56).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Shared Transmission and Hydraulic Fluid: Most tractors use combined transmission-hydraulic fluid to lubricate the power train and operate the hydraulic system (p. 56).
- Following Operator Manual Specs: In any tractor, use the type of fluid or fluids recommended in the operator's manual (p. 56).
- Check fluid levels daily and maintain their proper levels (p. 56).
- Routine Filter Replacement Intervals: Change fluids and filters at the recommended intervals (p. 56).
