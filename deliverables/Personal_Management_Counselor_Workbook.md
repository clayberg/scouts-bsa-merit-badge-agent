## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Prepare a budget reflecting your expected income (allowance, gifts, wages), expenses, and savings for a period of 13 consecutive weeks. (b) Compare expected income with expected expenses. (1) If expenses exceed budget income, determine steps to balance your budget. (2) If income exceeds budget expenses, state how you would use the excess money (new goal, savings). (c) Track and record your actual income, expenses, and savings for 13 consecutive weeks (the same 13-week period for which you budgeted). (You may use the forms provided in the Personal Management merit badge pamphlet, devise your own, or use a computer-generated version.) When complete, present the records showing the results to your counselor. (d) Compare your budget with your actual income and expenses to understand when your budget worked and when it did not work. With your counselor, discuss what you might do differently the next time.
- [ ] **Req 9 (Prepare):** Prepare a written project plan demonstrating the steps below, including the desired outcome. This is a project on paper, not a real-life project. Examples could include planning a camping trip, developing a community service project or a school or religious event, or creating an annual patrol plan with additional activities not already included in the troop annual plan. Discuss your completed project plan with your counselor. (a) Define the project. What is your goal? (b) Develop a timeline for your project that shows the steps you must take from beginning to completion. (c) Describe your project. (d) Develop a list of resources. Identify how these resources will help you achieve your goal. (e) Develop a budget for your project.

### Req 1: Major Family Expense Planning
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Choose an item that your family might want to purchase that is considered a major expense. (b) Write a plan that tells how your family would save money for the purchase identified in requirement 1(a). (1) Discuss the plan with your counselor. (2) Discuss the plan with your family. (3) Discuss how other family needs must be considered in this plan. (c) Develop a written shopping strategy for the purchase identified in requirement 1(a). (1) Determine the quality of the item or service (using consumer publications or rating systems). (2) Comparison shop for the item. Find out where you can buy the item for the best price. (Provide prices from at least two different price sources.) Call around; study ads. Look for a sale or discount coupon. Consider alternatives. Can you buy the item used? Should you wait for a sale?
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Personal Management Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 16)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Guard family financial records and online banking credentials against identity theft during purchase research.
- **Common Myth:** A major family expense only involves checking the retail sticker price at checkout.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Major family purchases require analyzing financing alternatives, warranty terms, and long term operating expenses before buying.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Major Expense Assessment: Family purchases require evaluating initial retail pricing against ongoing operational maintenance costs.
- Dedicated Savings Plan: Establishing scheduled bank deposits ensures accumulated capital covers big ticket household expenses.
- Consumer Comparison Research: Objective product testing reports protect households from substandard materials and poor warranties.
- Family Financial Discussion: Collaborative budget meetings help align household priorities before committing substantial financial resources.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Personal Management Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 23)* • **Group Organizer:** Ledger 13Wk
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Store physical cash receipts and transaction logs securely to protect personal spending data from loss.
- **Common Myth:** A financial budget is a permanent rigid document that never changes once drafted.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Budgets require regular adjustments as unexpected expenditures occur during the active tracking period.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Weekly Budget Construction: Tracking projected earnings against scheduled commitments prevents overdraft fees and deficit spending.
- Balanced Cash Flow: Surpluses should fund emergency savings accounts while deficits require immediate spending reductions.
- Daily Expense Logging: Recording every cash and digital transaction guarantees accurate thirteen week financial reconciliation.
- Periodic Variance Analysis: Comparing budgeted targets against actual receipts reveals hidden personal consumption patterns.

### Req 3: Consumer Psychology And Spending Behavior
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Discuss with your counselor FIVE of the following concepts: (a) The emotions you feel when you receive money. (b) Your understanding of how the amount of money you have with you affects your spending habits. (c) Your thoughts when you buy something new and your thoughts about the same item three months later. Explain the concept of buyer's remorse. (d) How hunger affects you when shopping for food items (snacks, groceries). (e) Your experience of an item you have purchased after seeing or hearing advertisements for it. Did the item work as well as advertised? (f) Your understanding of what happens when you put money into a savings account. (g) Charitable giving. Explain its purpose and your thoughts about it. (h) What you can do to better manage your money.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Personal Management Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 16)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Avoid carrying large sums of physical cash in public environments to prevent theft or robbery.
- **Common Myth:** Purchasing decisions are always driven by logical calculations rather than emotional impulses.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Emotional states directly influence impulsive spending choices unless disciplined cooling off periods are practiced.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Immediate emotional highs from earning money can stimulate unplanned impulse spending habits.
- Carrying excess physical cash increases the likelihood of unnecessary retail impulse buys.
- Post Purchase Evaluation: Buyer remorse occurs when the initial excitement of acquiring novel goods fades.
- Commercial Advertising Influence: Marketing campaigns exploit psychological desires rather than presenting objective product utility facts.
