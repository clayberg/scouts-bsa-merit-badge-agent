### Req 1: Foundations of Psychological Science
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Define psychology in your own words and explain its impact on daily life. (b) Identify two famous psychologists and describe their contributions to the field.
**Source:** *Psychology Test Lab Resource Guide, pp. 4-9* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Psychology consists entirely of common sense wisdom or casual intuitive advice about human personalities.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Psychology relies on controlled empirical research, cognitive testing, and behavioral observation to analyze mental processes.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Scientific Scope: Experimental research analyzes overt human actions alongside neural systems and internal cognition.
- Behavioral Observation: Empirical methodologies collect systematic quantitative data rather than subjective personal impressions.
- Historical Foundations: Wilhelm Wundt established modern experimental research using structured laboratory reaction experiments.
- Applied Principles: Developmental models explain how adolescents acquire emotional regulation during group challenges.

### Req 3: Youth Mental Health Dynamics
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) In your own words, define mental health. (b) Discuss with your counselor the concepts of protective factors, risk factors, and resilience for the mental health of youth close to your age in your community. Share ideas about how your community could strengthen the resilience of young people.
**Source:** *Psychology Test Lab Resource Guide, pp. 16-21* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Mental health represents the complete absence of sadness, anger, or situational worry.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Mental health is a dynamic state of emotional balance and social adaptation.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Health Continuum: Psychological wellness fluctuates continuously in response to environmental demands and coping.
- Protective Factors: Supportive peer networks and adult mentorship buffer youth against extreme distress.
- Risk Identification: Chronic isolation and academic overload raise vulnerability to clinical psychological conditions.
- Resilience Cultivation: Deliberate coping mechanisms transform acute setbacks into opportunities for psychological growth.

### Req 5: Empathy and Psychological Resilience
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) In your own words, define empathy and give examples of how you might show empathy to others. (b) Discuss the importance of resilience and how Scouting experiences develop resilience.
**Source:** *Psychology Test Lab Resource Guide, pp. 28-33* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
- **Common Myth:** Empathy requires feeling pity or immediately solving another individual's personal emotional distress.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Empathy combines cognitive perspective-taking with emotional resonance, whereas resilience is learned through coping.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Perspective Taking: Cognitive empathy involves understanding another person's viewpoint without adopting their emotional state.
- Empathetic Action: Active validation and physical presence provide comfort during difficult personal crises.
- Scouting Hardship: Challenging outdoor expeditions build distress tolerance through manageable real-world physical adversity.
- Adaptive Recovery: Reflecting on failed attempts transforms acute disappointment into durable psychological grit.
