### Req 1: Field Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Hazards and First Aid. Do the following: (a) Show or explain first aid for, and prevention of, injuries and conditions that searchers and subjects could develop during an SAR situation, including: (1) Dehydration (2) Heatstroke (3) Hypothermia (4) Shock (5) Blisters (6) Eye injuries (7) Ankle and knee sprains (8) Bug bites of chiggers, ticks, mosquitoes, and biting gnats (9) Bee stings (10) Bites of spiders (11) Sting of a scorpion (12) Bite of a wild mammal (13) Bite of a venomous snake (b) Explain how the Scout Basic Essentials address hazards outdoors and help lost Scouts stay safe before they are rescued. (c) Discuss how the safety gear carried by SAR team members in their field packs addresses SAR hazards.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Search and Rescue Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 43, p. 47)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scene Safety & Universal Precautions (Req 1): Size up scene hazards before approaching any victim, wear nonlatex barrier gloves when body fluids may be present, and follow BSA Two-Deep Leadership.
- **Common Myth:** Rescuers should wait until an injured subject shows obvious pale, clammy skin or fainting before beginning treatment for shock.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Because untreated shock can quickly lead to organ failure and death, you must treat every accident victim for shock even if no symptoms appear yet (p. 43).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- The circulatory system of a person who is injured or under greats tress might not provide enough blood and oxygen to the tissues of the body (p. 43).
- Left untreated, it can be deadly (as organs can begin to fail) (p. 43).
- Treat every accident victim for shock even if no symptoms appear (p. 43).
- Elevating Legs for Circulation: If you do not suspect back, neck, or head injuries, or fractures in the hip or leg, raise the feet about 12 inches to move blood from the legs to the vital (p. 43).

### Req 3: Topographic Maps and Coordinate Systems
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Maps. Using a map, a compass and a GPS device or app approved by your counselor, do the following: (a) Point out and explain the 5 D's (Date, Description, Details, Direction or Declination, Distance) of the map. (b) Choose a location on the map and record the altitude, latitude, longitude, and US National Grid coordinates. Describe how these coordinate systems differ. (c) Orient the map and take a bearing to another map location. Estimate the distance between, and describe the terrain between, the two locations. (d) Show a hypothetical place last seen and point out an area on your map that could be used for containment using natural or human-made boundaries.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Search and Rescue Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 59, p. 63)* • **Group Organizer:** Activity & Observation Log
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Scene Safety & Universal Precautions (Req 3): Size up scene hazards before approaching any victim, wear nonlatex barrier gloves when body fluids may be present, and follow BSA Two-Deep Leadership.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Search and Rescue Req 3 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Search and rescue ground teams prefer latitude and longitude over UTM coordinates because degrees and minutes are easier to measure in meters.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** SAR teams frequently use the Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) and US National Grid systems because distances are easy to calculate and accurate to 1 meter (p. 59).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- To find a location on a USGS topographic map, you will need to know whether it is a 7.5-minute map (also known as a 1, 24,000 scale or quad map) (p. 59).
- Estimating Map Target Locations: Once you have this information, you can start to estimate the location you want to find (p. 59).
- Distances are easy to calculate using UTM because it is accurate to 1 meter (p. 59).
- Reading Latitude Longitude Margins: By starting with a UTM coordinate or an estimated location on a map, you can estimate your location using lat/lon coordinates by reading the side (p. 59).
