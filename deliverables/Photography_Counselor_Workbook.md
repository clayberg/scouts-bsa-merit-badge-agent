### Req 1: Photography Safety and Risk Management
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Safety. Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while working with photography and what you should do to anticipate, mitigate, prevent, and respond to these hazards. Explain how you would prepare for exposure to environmental situations such as weather, sun, and water. (b) View the Personal Safety Awareness "Digital Safety" video (with your parent or guardian's permission).
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Photography Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 63)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never look through an optical viewfinder while walking along roadways, ledges, or trails.
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Photography Req 1 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** Looking through an electronic viewfinder keeps a photographer aware of surrounding physical hazards.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Photographers must secure camera neck straps around their necks and maintain situational awareness when moving.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Equipment Security: Secure camera straps firmly around the neck before navigating uneven outdoor terrain.
- Anchor heavy tripod legs securely to prevent tripping hazards for passing pedestrians.
- Weather Protection: Encase electronic camera bodies in sealed plastic bags during unexpected severe rainstorms.
- Solar Safety: Never point telephoto lenses directly toward midday sun without certified solar filters.

### Req 2: Elements of Photographic Image Quality
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain how the following elements and terms can affect the quality of a picture: (a) Light, natural light (ambient/existing), low light (such as at night), and artificial light (such as from a flash) (b) Exposure, aperture (f-stops), shutter speed, ISO (c) Depth of field (d) Composition, rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, depth (e) Angle of view (f) Stop action and blur motion (g) Decisive moment (action or expression captured by the photographer)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Photography Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Avoid firing high-powered electronic strobes directly into people or animals at close range.
- **Common Myth:** Modern digital cameras automatically correct poor lighting without affecting image detail or noise.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Aperture, shutter speed, and sensor sensitivity balance together to establish correct image exposure.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Natural Lighting: Low angle morning sunlight casts long shadows that reveal intricate physical surface textures.
- Aperture Control: Wider lens openings decrease depth of field to isolate subjects from background elements.
- Fast shutter speeds freeze rapid movement while slow speeds introduce intentional motion blur.
- Positioning visual centers of interest along grid intersections creates balanced, compelling compositions.

- Light Comparison: Diffused window daylight produces soft tones unlike direct electronic flash highlight glares.
- Depth Contrast: Switching from wide to narrow apertures demonstrates background isolation versus environmental context.
- Framing Variation: Natural architectural doorways frame central focal subjects more dynamically than unbordered compositions.

Select a photo you have taken, then do ONE of the following, and share what you have done with your counselor: (a) Crop your photograph. (b) Adjust the exposure or make a color correction. (c) Show another way you could improve your picture for impact.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Photography Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 43, p. 45)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Take scheduled screen breaks every twenty minutes to prevent eye fatigue while editing.
- **Common Myth:** Digital editing exists solely to salvage severely out-of-focus or ruined photographic exposures.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Post-processing software allows tonal balance adjustments and geometric cropping without altering original raw data.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Edge Cropping: Trimming extraneous border elements directs viewer gaze straight toward central narrative action.
- Histogram Balance: Adjust tonal sliders to prevent blown highlight peaks or crushed shadow blacks.
- Color Accuracy: Neutralize unnatural yellow or blue tints using manual white balance eyedropper tools.
- Local Adjustments: Selective dodging brightens key focal points without overexposing broader background sky areas.
