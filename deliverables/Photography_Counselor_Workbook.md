**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Safety. Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while working with photography and what you should do to anticipate, mitigate, prevent, and respond to these hazards. Explain how you would prepare for exposure to environmental situations such as weather, sun, and water. (b) View the Personal Safety Awareness "Digital Safety" video (with your parent or guardian's permission).
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Photography Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 63)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Field Hazards and Digital Safety discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Photography Req 1 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** Looking through a camera viewfinder in the field keeps you aware of everything around your feet and immediate path.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Focusing intently through a viewfinder narrows your situational awareness, so you must check your footing for drop-offs, traffic, and tripping hazards before stepping to frame a shot (p. 63).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- The angle of view is the amount of a scene that a lens can take (p. 33).
- Some point and-shoot cameras have a fixed focal length lens, giving you only one angle of view (p. 33).
- Standard Human Eye Perspective: This angle of view is much like what human eyes see in normal vision, roughly equivalent to a 50mmlens (p. 33).
- Variable Focal Length Zooms: Other cameras have zoom lenses, which give a varied focal length and varied angle of view (p. 33).

### Req 2: Exposure, Light, and Visual Composition
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Explain how the following elements and terms can affect the quality of a picture: (a) Light, natural light (ambient/existing), low light (such as at night), and artificial light (such as from a flash) (b) Exposure, aperture (f-stops), shutter speed, ISO (c) Depth of field (d) Composition, rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, depth (e) Angle of view (f) Stop action and blur motion (g) Decisive moment (action or expression captured by the photographer)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Photography Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Exposure, Light, and Visual Composition discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** A larger f-stop number such as f/16 opens the lens wider and lets in more light than f/2.8.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Because f-stops represent fractions of the focal length, a smaller number like f/2.8 indicates a wider lens opening and shallower depth of field than f/16 (p. 22).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- ISO is simply an acronym for the International Standards Organization (p. 22).
- Sensor Light Sensitivity Standards: This organization sets the standard by which camera sensors and their sensitivity to light are measured (p. 22).
- By changing the ISO setting, you control the relative sensitivity of the electronics in your camera to light (p. 22).
- With every doubling of the number, the camera's digital sensor becomes twice as sensitive to the light available for the picture (p. 22).

#### Req 2a: Natural and Artificial Light
**Official Requirement 2a:** Light, natural light (ambient/existing), low light (such as at night), and artificial light (such as from a flash)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Photography Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 22, p. 33)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Natural Light Ambient/existing Low)
- **Common Myth:** Raising the ISO to its highest number always improves image sharpness in bright daylight.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Doubling the ISO makes the digital sensor twice as sensitive to light for dim conditions, but very high ISO settings introduce grainy digital noise into the picture (p. 22).
