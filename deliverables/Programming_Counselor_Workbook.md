### Req 1: Digital Safety and Injury Prevention
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Safety. Do the following: (a) View the Personal Safety Awareness "Digital Safety" video (with your parent or guardian's permission). (b) Discuss first aid and prevention for the types of injuries that could occur during programming activities, including repetitive stress injuries and eyestrain.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Programming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 88, p. 89)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Apply BSA Digital Safety and Cyber Chip guidelines during Programming Requirement 1: protect personal credentials and private data while working on digital safety and injury prevention, verify software sources before downloading, and include a parent or counselor on all online communications.
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Programming Req 1 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** Working at a computer desk cannot cause physical injuries because typing does not require heavy lifting.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Programming can cause repetitive stress injuries when repeated motion places continuous strain on tendons and muscles around a joint without time to recover (p. 88).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Unexpected Programming Injuries: You might not think that writing programs could cause injuries (p. 88).
- Athletic Prevention Techniques: But in fact, injuries from programming have much in common with sports injuries and can be prevented using some of the same techniques that athletes use (p. 88).
- The main programming-related injuries are repetitive stress injuries, or RSIs (p. 88).
- Joint and Tendon Stress: Just as in athletic activities, an RSI occurs when stress is placed on a joint, pulling on the tendons and muscles around the joint (p. 88).

### Req 4: Software Licensing and Intellectual Property
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Intellectual Property. Do the following: (a) Explain the four types of intellectual property used to protect computer programs. (b) Describe the difference between licensing and owning software. (c) Describe the differences between freeware, open source, and commercial software, and why it is important to respect the terms of use of each.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Programming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 84, p. 85)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Apply BSA Digital Safety and Cyber Chip guidelines during Programming Requirement 4: protect personal credentials and private data while working on software licensing and intellectual property, verify software sources before downloading, and include a parent or counselor on all online communications.
- **Common Myth:** When you buy a video game or software package on a disc, you legally own the underlying program code and can sell copies to friends.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Software developers sell a license granting permission to use their coded instructions under specific terms, much like renting a bicycle rather than buying it outright (p. 84).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- The value of software is in its electronically coded instructions, not in the plastic disc or other tangible media upon which the instructions are stored (p. 84).
- Beyond Physical Storage Media: Software developers do not sell DVDs or other storage media (p. 84).
- What they actually sell is permission touse the programs they have created (p. 84).
- Licensing is more like renting a bike than buying one (p. 84).

(b) In the second language and environment, write or modify a program, debug and demonstrate, and explain as above. (c) In the third language and environment, write or modify a program, debug and demonstrate, and explain as above.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Programming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 14, p. 18)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Apply BSA Digital Safety and Cyber Chip guidelines during Programming Requirement 5: protect personal credentials and private data while working on writing and debugging multi-language programs, verify software sources before downloading, and include a parent or counselor on all online communications.
- **Common Myth:** A computer processor directly understands English words like print, if, and while without any translation step.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A processor natively executes binary numeric codes, so a programming language must translate human-written instructions into machine-readable form (p. 14).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Processor Task Instruction Sets: A program is a set of instructions that tells a processor how to do aparticular task (p. 14).
- Translating Human Code Instructions: A programming language is the tool that converts the programmer'sinstructions into a form the processor can understand (p. 14).
- The roots of modern programming stretch back to before the first computers (p. 14).
- Most programming languages have their origins in early efforts to automate machines like calculating machines (p. 14).
