### Req 4: Software Intellectual Property Law
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Intellectual Property. Do the following: (a) Explain the four types of intellectual property used to protect computer programs. (b) Describe the difference between licensing and owning software. (c) Describe the differences between freeware, open source, and commercial software, and why it is important to respect the terms of use of each.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Programming Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 84, p. 85)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Download source code and precompiled binaries exclusively from signed, official repositories to prevent malicious payload execution.
- **Common Myth:** Purchasing a software license gives the buyer total legal ownership of the underlying codebase.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Copyright automatically covers original written code upon creation, while patents protect novel functional processes.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Copyright protects original source text against unauthorized direct duplication or redistribution.
- Patent Functional Scope: Patents safeguard novel algorithmic processes rather than specific written line implementations.
- License Permission Boundaries: Software licenses grant limited operational rights without transferring intellectual property ownership.
- Open Source Obligations: Permissive licenses require preserving copyright notices while copyleft rules mandate source distribution.
