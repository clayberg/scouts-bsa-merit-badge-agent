## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Demonstrate):** Do the following with your counselor: (a) Discuss the importance of having a physical examination each year. Discuss why overall health, immunizations, medications, allergies, and medical history are covered during an examination. Tell your counselor when you last underwent a physical examination. (b) Explain how you maintain good health habits, especially during training, and how the use of tobacco products, alcohol, and other harmful substances can negatively affect your health and your performance in athletic activities. (c) Describe the elements of a nutritious, balanced diet, and explain what you do to maintain a healthy diet.
- [ ] **Req 3 (Explain):** Select an athletic activity that interests you, then do the following: (a) With guidance from your counselor, establish a personal training program suited to the activity you have chosen. Follow this training program for three months. (b) Use a chart or other tracking method to monitor your progress during this time. (c) Explain to your counselor the equipment necessary to participate in this activity and the appropriate clothing for the activity and the time of year. (d) At the end of three months, review your records from requirement 3(b), and discuss with your counselor what progress you have made during training. Tell how your development has affected you mentally and physically.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Prepare plans for conducting a sports meet or field day that includes 10 activities, at least five of which must come from the groups mentioned in requirement 5. Outline the duties of each official needed and list the equipment the meet will require. (b) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval, serve as an official or volunteer at a sports meet to observe officials in action. Tell your counselor about your responsibilities at the meet and discuss what you learned.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Explore three careers related to athletics. Research one career area by interviewing an expert in the field, visiting a site, or using other resources. Learn about training, education, expenses, job outlook, salary, and advancement. Discuss your findings and career interest with your counselor. (b) Explore how an area of athletics could contribute to a hobby or healthy lifestyle. Research education, costs, and organizations related to this activity. Discuss your findings and goals with your counselor.

### Req 1: Sports Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following with your counselor: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter during athletics activities, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for injuries or illnesses that could occur while participating in athletics events, including sprains, strains, contusions, abrasions, blisters, dehydration, heat reactions, and concussions.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Athletics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 57, p. 59)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Immediately suspend outdoor practices and seek grounded indoor shelter when thunder sounds or lightning appears within ten miles.
- **Common Myth:** Painful muscle pulls should be vigorously stretched immediately after the injury occurs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Athletic injuries require immediate assessment of soft tissue swelling followed by standardized RICE protocols or heat illness interventions.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Environmental Hazards: Extreme heat humidity and severe lightning storms present severe risks to outdoor athletes.
- Tendon Protection: Acute sprains demand immediate rest ice compression and elevation to control internal swelling.
- Fluid Balance: Dehydration degrades aerobic performance and increases the risk of dangerous heat exhaustion.
- Blister Prevention: Clean synthetic socks reduce friction on damp skin to stop painful hot spots.

### Req 2: Physical Conditioning and Wellness
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following with your counselor: (a) Discuss the importance of having a physical examination each year. Discuss why overall health, immunizations, medications, allergies, and medical history are covered during an examination. Tell your counselor when you last underwent a physical examination. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Athletics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 11)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Athletes who experience unexplained chest pain, dizziness, or shortness of breath must obtain physician clearance before resuming practice.
- **Common Myth:** Healthy youth athletes without obvious symptoms do not need annual preventive physical examinations.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Yearly examinations establish a physical baseline and uncover hidden cardiovascular conditions that athletic stress aggravates.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Physician Evaluation: Annual clinical examinations identify undetected cardiac murmurs and joint instability before vigorous training.
- Immunization Records: Current tetanus boosters protect athletes from dangerous bacterial infections caused by open turf wounds.
- Substance Dangers: Nicotine and alcohol degrade oxygen transport and slow muscle recovery after intense workouts.
- Dietary Balance: Complex carbohydrates and lean proteins provide sustained glycogen storage for demanding sports activities.

### Req 3: Personal Athletic Training Plan
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Select an athletic activity that interests you, then do the following: (a) With guidance from your counselor, establish a personal training program suited to the activity you have chosen. Follow this training program for three months. (b) Use a chart or other tracking method to monitor your progress during this time. (c) Explain to your counselor the equipment necessary to participate in this activity and the appropriate clothing for the activity and the time of year. (d) At the end of three months, review your records from requirement 3(b), and discuss with your counselor what progress you have made during training. Tell how your development has affected you mentally and physically.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Athletics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 35)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Inspect athletic footwear for worn soles and replace shoes showing collapsed midsoles to protect joints from impact trauma.
- **Common Myth:** Training at maximum intensity every single day produces the fastest performance gains.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Gradual progression in training volume prevents overtraining syndrome and overuse tendon injuries over twelve weeks.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Program Design: Structured conditioning schedules balance aerobic volume anaerobic intervals and mandatory muscular rest days.
- Progress Documentation: Tracking daily workout repetitions running times and perceived exertion highlights measurable physical improvements.
- Footwear Selection: Activity specific athletic shoes support the foot arch and absorb damaging ground impact.
- Quarterly Assessment: Comparing initial baseline metrics with final performance data reveals specific areas of physiological adaptation.

### Req 5: Athletic Events and Performance
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Complete the activities in FOUR of the following options and show improvement over a three-month period: (a) Option A, Sprinting. Do ALL of the following: (1) 100-meter dash (2) 200-meter dash (b) Option B, Long-Distance Running. Do ALL of the following: (1) 3k run (2) 5k run (c) Option C, Long Jump OR High Jump. Do ALL of the following: (1) Running long jump OR running high jump (best of three tries) (2) Standing long jump OR standing high jump (best of three tries) (d) Option D, Swimming. Do ALL of the following: (1) 100-meter swim (2) 200-meter swim (e) Option E, Pull-Ups AND Push-Ups. Do ALL of the following: (1) Pull-ups in two minutes (2) Push-ups in two minutes (f) Option F, Baseball Throw. Do ALL of the following: (1) Baseball throw for accuracy, 10 throws at a target (distance to be determined by age): ages 11 to 12, 20 feet; ages 13 to 15, 30 feet; ages 16 to 17, 40 feet (2) Baseball throw for distance, five throws (total distance) (g) Option G, Basketball Shooting. Do ALL of the following: (1) Basketball shot for accuracy, 10 free-throw shots (2) Basketball throw for skill and agility, the following shots: (a) Left-side layup (b) Right-side layup (c) Left side of hoop, along the key line (d) Right side of hoop, along the key line (e) Where key line and free-throw line meet, left side (f) Where key line and free-throw line meet, left side (g) Top of the key (h) Another spot along the three-point line (h) Option H, Football Kick OR Soccer Kick. Do ALL of the following: (1) Goals from the 10-yard line, eight kicks (2) Football kick or soccer kick for distance, five kicks (total distance) (i) Option I, Weight Training. Do ALL of the following: (1) Chest/bench press, two sets of 15 repetitions each (2) Leg curls, two sets of 15 repetitions each
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Athletics Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 24, p. 32)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Ensure throwing zones and swimming lanes are completely clear of bystanders before initiating throws, jumps, or sprint trials.
- **Common Myth:** Athletes can only build athletic fitness by specializing exclusively in a single competitive movement.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Tracking performance across multiple athletic events reveals balanced improvements in cardiovascular stamina, speed, and muscular power.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Sprint Mechanics: Explosive arm drive and high knee lift maximize forward acceleration during the hundred meters.
- Steady aerobic pacing prevents early glycogen depletion during the three kilometer cross country run.
- Jumping Technique: Powerful penultimate strides convert horizontal sprinting velocity into maximum vertical lift off the board.
- Core Stability: Proper spinal alignment during pushups and pullups maximizes muscular efficiency and prevents back strain.
