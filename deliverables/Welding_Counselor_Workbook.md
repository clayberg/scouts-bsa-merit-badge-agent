### Req 3: Welding Metallurgy and Principles
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain the terms welding, electrode, slag, and oxidation. Describe the welding process, how heat is generated, what kind of filler metal is added (if any), and what protects the molten metal from the atmosphere.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 64)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear approved safety goggles underneath your helmet when chipping brittle glassy slag off welds.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does molten steel absorb nitrogen and oxygen so rapidly from unprotected surrounding air?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Coalescence Bond Definition: Welding fuses metallic parts together through atomic bonding across liquid or solid interfaces.
  - The electrode conducts electrical current while frequently depositing necessary filler metal into joints.
  - Slag Impurity Trapping: Solidified flux floats upward to shield hot beads and collect surface metal oxides.
  - Atmospheric Oxidation Prevention: Gaseous argon envelopes molten pools to prevent air from forming brittle weld porosity.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Surface Rust Removal: Grind mill scale and surface rust off parent plate edges using abrasive wheels.
  - Step 2 - Electric Arc Initiation: Strike the electrode tip against base steel to create an electric arc column.
  - Step 3 - Molten Puddle Manipulation: Guide the molten puddle forward while feeding filler wire into the leading edge.
  - Step 4 - Solidified Slag Chipping: Chip glassy slag off the cooled bead using a pointed chipping hammer.
- **Common Myth:** Soldering, brazing, and arc welding join metals through the exact same physical mechanism.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Welding melts the actual base metal while soldering and brazing only melt lower-temperature filler alloy.

### Req 4: Mechanical and Thermal Cutting
**Official Requirement 4:** Name the different mechanical and thermal cutting methods. Choose one method and describe how to use the process. Discuss one advantage and one limitation of this process.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 39, p. 43)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never exceed fifteen pounds per square inch line pressure on acetylene fuel gas lines.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does plasma arc cutting work effectively on aluminum while oxyfuel cutting fails completely?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Cold mechanical saws produce clean edges without heat affected zones or hardened edge margins.
  - Oxyfuel torches direct high pressure oxygen streams to burn preheated steel in exothermic reactions.
  - Constricted plasma arcs sever both ferrous and nonferrous conductors including stainless steel and aluminum.
  - Thermal cutting torches consume plate width through a kerf gap requiring measurement compensation.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Gas Pressure Regulation: Adjust oxygen and acetylene regulators to manufacturer recommended pressures on dual gauges.
  - Step 2 - Neutral Flame Ignition: Light the tip with a friction striker and adjust valves to neutral flame.
  - Step 3 - Edge Preheating Technique: Hold the flame cones slightly above plate edges until steel reaches cherry red.
  - Step 4 - Cutting Lever Depress: Depress the high-pressure oxygen cutting lever smoothly to blow out molten kerf metal.
- **Common Myth:** Oxyfuel torches can cut any metallic material including aluminum, copper, and stainless steel.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Oxyfuel cutting depends on iron oxidation combustion and fails on nonferrous metals lacking high iron content.
