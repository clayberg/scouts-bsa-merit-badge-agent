### Req 1: Welding Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while welding, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, or lessen these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for, and the prevention of, injuries or illnesses that could occur while welding, including electrical shock, eye injuries, burns, fume inhalation, dizziness, skin irritation, and exposure to hazardous chemicals, including filler metals and welding gases.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 22)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Welding Requirement 1 on welding hazards and first aid: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** Once you turn off your welding machine or torch, the risk of a workshop fire is immediately over.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hot slag and stray sparks can smolder unnoticed in cracks, so you must continue inspecting the work area for fire for at least 30 minutes after welding (p. 21).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Continue inspecting for fire for 30 minutes after welding (p. 21).
- Superficial burns are mild burns that affect only the outer layer of skin (p. 21).
- Cooling Minor Burns: Treat them by holding the burn under cold water or applying cool, wet compresses until the pain eases (p. 21).
- Partial-thickness burns are more serious than superficial burns and affect the outer layer of skin and part of the layer of skin (p. 21).

### Req 3: Core Welding Terms and Principles
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain the terms welding, electrode, slag, and oxidation. Describe the welding process, how heat is generated, what kind of filler metal is added (if any), and what protects the molten metal from the atmosphere.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 64)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Welding Electrode Slag and Oxidation)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Welding Requirement 3 on core welding terms and principles: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** Slag is just waste dirt from the metal that serves no purpose and should be chipped away while the weld puddle is still glowing red.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Slag forms from the melting flux coating to shield the molten weld metal from atmospheric oxidation and slows cooling, so it should only be chipped off after the weld solidifies (p. 64).

### Req 6: Practical Bead and Joint Welding
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** After successfully completing requirements 1 through 5, use the equipment you prepared for the welding process in 5(b) to do the following: (a) Using a metal scribe or soapstone, sketch your initial onto a metal plate, and weld a bead on the plate following the pattern of your initial. (b) Cover a small plate (approximately 3" x 3" x 1/4") with weld beads side by side. (c) Tack two plates together in a square groove butt joint. (d) Weld the two plates together from 6(c) on both sides. (e) Tack two plates together in a T joint, have your counselor inspect it, then weld a T joint with fillet weld on both sides. (f) Tack two plates together in a lap joint, have your counselor inspect it, then weld a lap joint with fillet weld on both sides.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 71, p. 72)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Review task hazards with your counselor before starting Welding Requirement 6 on practical bead and joint welding: inspect tools and work surfaces before use, keep bystanders clear of active demonstrations, and follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting supervision standards.
- **Common Myth:** You can skip tack welding and clamp two steel plates loosely on the table before running a full seam weld.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Intense welding heat causes metal plates to expand and warp out of alignment, so you must tack weld joints at the ends and have your counselor inspect the fit-up before running final beads (p. 72).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Torch Valve Shutdown Sequence: Welding for the Novice When you are ready to close down the equipment, turn off the torch oxygen valve; then, turn off the fuel valve (p. 71).
- Draining Oxygen Line Pressure: Close the cylinder valves, then open the torch oxygen valve to allow the oxygen to drain, and close the torch oxygen valve (p. 71).
- Release the adjusting screw on the oxygen regulator (p. 71).
- Open the torch-fuel valve to drain fuel gas, and then close (p. 71).
