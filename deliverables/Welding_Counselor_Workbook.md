### Req 1: Welding Hazards and First Aid
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the hazards you are most likely to encounter while welding, and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, or lessen these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for, and the prevention of, injuries or illnesses that could occur while welding, including electrical shock, eye injuries, burns, fume inhalation, dizziness, skin irritation, and exposure to hazardous chemicals, including filler metals and welding gases.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 22)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Keep a fully charged ten-pound ABC dry chemical fire extinguisher within twenty feet of hot work.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe that standard sunglasses provide adequate optical protection against welding arc light.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Electric arcs emit intense ultraviolet and infrared radiation requiring shade ten through twelve optical filter lenses.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Arc flash creates ultraviolet radiation that causes painful corneal burns without immediate noticeable symptoms.
- Electric Shock Hazards: Damp work gloves drastically decrease electrical resistance between the welder and live electrode holders.
- Metal Fume Control: Inhaling vaporized zinc oxides from galvanized steel causes severe systemic metal fume fever.
- Shielding gas displacement in unventilated confined areas creates an immediate oxygen deficiency hazard.

### Req 2: Welding Safety and Protective Equipment
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) With your counselor, discuss general safety precautions and safety data sheets (SDS) related to welding. Explain the importance of the SDS. (b) Describe the appropriate safety gear and clothing that must be worn when welding. Then, present yourself properly dressed for welding, in protective equipment, clothing, and footwear. (c) Explain and demonstrate the proper care and storage of welding equipment, tools, and protective clothing and footwear.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 29)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never wear cuffed trousers or open top pockets that trap molten metal sparks.
- **Common Myth:** Synthetic fleece or nylon shirts are acceptable under leather welding jackets.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Synthetic fibers easily melt under stray heat and fuse to human skin during accidents.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Safety Data Sheets document hazardous decomposition products generated during thermal decomposition of fluxes.
- Natural Fiber Selection: Heavy weight cotton denim resists sparks while synthetic polyester melts into severe burns.
- High Top Footwear: Leather work boots with eight-inch uppers prevent falling sparks from entering shoe collars.
- Electrode Storing Practice: Low hydrogen stick electrodes require heated holding ovens to prevent moisture contamination.

### Req 3: Welding Metallurgy and Principles
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain the terms welding, electrode, slag, and oxidation. Describe the welding process, how heat is generated, what kind of filler metal is added (if any), and what protects the molten metal from the atmosphere.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 64)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Welding Electrode Slag and Oxidation)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Wear approved safety goggles underneath your helmet when chipping brittle glassy slag off welds.
- **Common Myth:** Soldering, brazing, and arc welding join metals through the exact same physical mechanism.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Welding melts the actual base metal while soldering and brazing only melt lower-temperature filler alloy.

### Req 5: Welding Processes and Equipment Setup
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Select two welding processes, and make a list of the different components of the equipment required for each process. **Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 69)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Stand to the side rather than facing gauge faces when opening high-pressure cylinder valves.
- **Common Myth:** The work clamp serves as an electrical ground rather than completing the active live welding circuit.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The work clamp carries full welding current back to the power source to sustain an arc.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- GMAW Continuous Feeding: Gas Metal Arc Welding continuously feeds spool wire to yield high production speeds.
- SMAW Outdoor Capability: Shielded Metal Arc Welding functions reliably outdoors in wind where gas diffusers fail.
- Attaching work clamps to bright unpainted steel completes low-resistance return current loops.
- Flowmeter Regulation Purpose: Shielding gas flowmeters meter cubic feet per hour to prevent turbulent air intake.

### Req 6: Joint Assembly and Weld Execution
**Official Requirement 6 (Parent Overview):** After successfully completing requirements 1 through 5, use the equipment you prepared for the welding process in 5(b) to do the following: (a) Using a metal scribe or soapstone, sketch your initial onto a metal plate, and weld a bead on the plate following the pattern of your initial. (b) Cover a small plate (approximately 3" x 3" x 1/4") with weld beads side by side. (c) Tack two plates together in a square groove butt joint. (d) Weld the two plates together from 6(c) on both sides. (e) Tack two plates together in a T joint, have your counselor inspect it, then weld a T joint with fillet weld on both sides. (f) Tack two plates together in a lap joint, have your counselor inspect it, then weld a lap joint with fillet weld on both sides.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 71, p. 72)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use steel tongs or gripping pliers when handling hot freshly welded coupon assemblies.
- **Common Myth:** Faster torch travel speed always creates a cleaner, stronger weld bead with fewer surface flaws.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Excessive travel speed produces inadequate penetration, narrow underfilled beads, and severe edge undercutting.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Marking plates with soapstone creates high visibility guidelines that withstand intense preheating temperatures.
- Stringer Bead Overlap: Overlapping adjacent beads by fifty percent forms smooth protective padding across plate faces.
- Placing small tack welds at opposing edges maintains alignment during subsequent weld shrinkage.
- Balanced fillet welds have equal leg lengths with flat or slightly convex faces.

### Req 7: Welding Careers and Professional Organizations
**Official Requirement 7 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Find out about three career opportunities in the welding industry. Pick one and find out the education, training, and experience required for this profession. Discuss this with your counselor, and explain why the profession might interest you. (b) Discuss the role of the American Welding Society in the welding profession.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Welding Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 36, p. 82)* • **Group Organizer:** Career & Profession Exploration Card
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Professional welders must maintain current OSHA ten-hour certifications before working on commercial job sites.
- **Common Myth:** Welding is strictly manual shop labor with zero opportunities for advanced technical education.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Welding engineering degrees combine metallurgy, automated robotics, computer-aided design, and destructive testing science.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Certified Welding Inspectors evaluate weld quality according to structural fabrication codes and standards.
- Pipeline welders travel to remote sites to weld high-pressure cross-country petroleum transmission lines.
- AWS Code Development: The American Welding Society publishes D1.1 structural welding standards governing building construction.
- Welder Certification Rigor: Standardized AWS performance qualification tests verify a welder's technical capability in specific positions.
