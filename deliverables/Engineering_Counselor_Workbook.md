## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 2 (Explain):** Select an engineering achievement that has had a major impact on society. Using resources such as the internet (with your parent or guardian's permission), books, and magazines, find out about the engineers who made this engineering feat possible, the special obstacles they had to overcome, and how this achievement has influenced the world today. Tell your counselor what you learned.
- [ ] **Req 4 (Prepare):** Visit with an engineer (who may be your counselor, parent or guardian) and do the following: (a) Discuss the work this engineer does and the tools the engineer uses. (b) Discuss with the engineer a current project and the engineer's particular role in it. (c) Find out how the engineer's work is done and how results are achieved. (d) Ask to see the reports that the engineer writes concerning the project. (e) Discuss with your counselor what you learned about engineering from this visit.
- [ ] **Req 9 (Prepare):** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.

## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 6:** Do TWO of the following: (a) Transforming Motion. Using common materials or a construction set, make a simple model that will demonstrate motion. Explain how the model uses basic mechanical elements like levers and inclined planes to demonstrate motion. Describe an example where this mechanism is used in a real product. (b) Using Electricity. Make a list of 10 electrical appliances in your home. Find out approximately how much electricity each uses in one month. Learn how to find out the amount and cost of electricity used in your home during periods of light and heavy use. List five ways to conserve electricity. (c) Understanding Electronics. Using an electronic device such as a smartphone or tablet computer, find out how sound, video, text or images travel from one location to another. Explain how the device was designed for ease of use, function, and durability. (d) Using Materials. Do experiments to show the differences in strength and heat conductivity in wood, metal, and plastic. Discuss with your counselor what you have learned. (e) Converting Energy. Do an experiment to show how mechanical, heat, chemical, solar, and/or electrical energy may be converted from one or more types of energy to another. Explain your results. Describe to your counselor what energy is and how energy is converted and used in your surroundings. (f) Moving People. Find out the different ways people in your community get to work. Make a study of traffic flow (number of vehicles and relative speed) in both heavy and light traffic periods. Discuss with your counselor what might be improved to make it easier for people in your community to get where they need to go. (g) Building an Engineering Project. Enter a project in a science or engineering fair or similar competition. (This requirement may be met by participation on an engineering competition project team.) Discuss with your counselor what your project demonstrates, the kinds of questions visitors to the fair asked you, and how well you were able to answer their questions.

### Req 1: Manufactured Product Engineering Investigation
**Official Requirement 1:** Select a manufactured item in your home (such as a toy or an appliance) and, under adult supervision and with the approval of your counselor, investigate how and why it works as it does. Find out what sort of engineering activities were needed to create it. Discuss with your counselor what you learned and how you got the information.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Engineering Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 19, p. 43)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why did the manufacturer choose molded plastic over stamped sheet metal for this housing?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Disassembly Protocol: Unplug electrical appliances completely before loosening any external housing screws or fasteners.
  - Component Mapping: Trace kinetic energy pathways through gears, drive belts, levers, and rotating shafts.
  - Material Selection: Injection molded thermoplastics provide structural support while reducing total consumer assembly weight.
  - Manufacturing Tolerances: Precision mating surfaces require defined clearances to prevent premature operational friction wear.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Device Selection: Choose a broken or obsolete household appliance with adult counselor approval.
  - Step 2 - Safe Teardown: Remove external fasteners systematically while cataloging component positions on labeled paper.
  - Step 3 - Mechanism Analysis: Trace power transfer mechanisms from the electrical input to mechanical output.
  - Step 4 - Engineering Synthesis: Document how manufacturing methods shaped each part for strength and assembly.
- **Common Myth:** Consumer devices are engineered by a single person using one discipline.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Commercial products require multidisciplinary teams including mechanical, electrical, materials, and manufacturing engineers.

### Req 3: Core Engineering Disciplines Comparison
**Official Requirement 3:** Explain the work of six types of engineers. Pick two of the six types and explain how their work is related to engineering.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Mechanical engineers design physical machines, thermal management systems, and dynamic moving linkages.
  - Civil engineers construct bridges, municipal water treatment networks, foundations, and transportation corridors.
  - Electrical engineers develop power distribution grids, microprocessors, sensor arrays, and electromagnetic communications.
  - Chemical engineers convert raw feedstocks into refined fuels, polymers, and synthetic compounds.

  - Step 2 - Role Definition: Summarize daily job duties, typical workspaces, and end products for each discipline.

  - Step 4 - Technical Synthesis: Explain how both chosen fields apply physics, mathematics, and iterative prototype testing.
- **Common Myth:** Engineers in different fields use completely unrelated scientific rules to solve problems.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** All branches rely on fundamental conservation laws, calculus, material science, and the engineering method.

### Req 5: Systems Engineering Design Process
**Official Requirement 5:** Use the systems engineering approach to design an original piece of patrol equipment, a toy, or a useful device for the home, office, or garage.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Engineering Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 11, p. 46)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does setting strict operational requirements at the beginning prevent costly design mistakes later?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Problem Definition: Define operational constraints, target users, functional requirements, and performance objectives before sketching.
  - Trade Studies: Compare competing prototype concepts against cost, weight, durability, and ease of manufacturing.
  - Subsystem Integration: Ensure mechanical linkages, electrical power feeds, and user control interfaces fit together.
  - Subject physical prototypes to realistic stress testing to expose unexpected mechanical failure modes.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Requirements Definition: Establish specific user needs, dimensional constraints, and operational goals for the device.
  - Step 2 - Conceptual Ideation: Generate multiple design sketches evaluating different mechanical linkages and structural material choices.
  - Step 3 - Prototype Construction: Build a working model utilizing readily available shop materials and hand tools.
  - Step 4 - Performance Validation: Test the prototype under field conditions and record required design modifications systematically.
- **Common Myth:** Engineering design is a linear sequence where the initial drawing is constructed directly without changes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Systems engineering relies on cyclical iteration where testing reveals flaws that force design revisions.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Licensure Path: Candidates must earn accredited degrees, pass two examinations, and complete four practice years.
  - Legal Authority: The PE seal certifies engineering drawings meet building codes and protect public safety.
  - Public Infrastructure: Licensure is mandatory when approving civil bridges, water systems, and commercial building structures.
  - Ethical Accountability: Licensed engineers bear direct legal liability for structural failures resulting from negligence.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Academic Preparation: Graduate from an ABET accredited engineering university program with a bachelor degree.
  - Step 2 - Fundamentals Examination: Pass the comprehensive Fundamentals of Engineering exam to earn Engineer Intern certification.
  - Step 3 - Progressive Experience: Complete four years of verified professional engineering practice under licensed practitioner supervision.
  - Step 4 - Principles Examination: Pass the discipline specific Principles and Practice of Engineering exam for licensure.
- **Common Myth:** Any person who graduates with an engineering degree can legally stamp public building plans.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Only a registered Professional Engineer with a state license can stamp and seal municipal structural drawings.
