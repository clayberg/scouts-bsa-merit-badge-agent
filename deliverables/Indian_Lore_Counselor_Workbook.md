### Req 3: Indigenous Languages, Toponyms, and Leaders
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Learn 10 common terms in an American Indian language and their meanings. (b) Identify the meaning of 10 place names of American Indian origin in the United States. (c) Name five well-known American Indian leaders, either from the past or people of today. Give their tribes or nations. Describe what they did or do now that makes them notable.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Indian Lore Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 91, p. 92)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Ensure moderate audio volume when utilizing headphones for language pronunciation files to protect hearing.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often think indigenous American words across all tribes belong to one parent language family.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** North America contained dozens of distinct language families with structures as diverse as Indo-European and Sino-Tibetan.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Cherokee Syllabary Phonetics: Sequoyah developed eighty-five written symbols representing every distinct syllable in Cherokee speech.
- Algonquian Toponymic Roots: Mississippi derives from the Ojibwe words misi-ziibi, signifying great and flowing river.
- Tribal Statesmanship Traditions: Wilma Mankiller served as the first female Principal Chief of the Cherokee Nation.
- Wampanoag Language Reclamation: Modern linguists revive dormant native dialects using archival deed records and missionary bibles.

### Req 4: Indigenous Contributions and Traditional Games
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Describe how life might have been different for the European settlers if there had been no Native Americans to meet them when they came to this continent. (b) Describe eight things adopted by others from American Indians. (c) Learn a game played by a group or tribe. Teach and lead it with a Scout group.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Indian Lore Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 9, p. 12)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Clear the game field of tripping hazards, sharp debris, and holes before starting physical play.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often think early colonists brought all vital agricultural techniques with them from Europe.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** European colonists survived primarily by adopting native domestic crops like maize and local companion planting.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Three Sisters Agriculture: Interplanting corn, climbing beans, and squash provided natural soil fertility and balanced nutrition.
- Indigenous Navigation Trails: Birchbark canoes and portage paths formed the foundation for inland continental commerce.
- Pharmacopeia Botanical Knowledge: Indigenous healers extracted willow bark salicin to treat fevers centuries before synthetic aspirin.
- Traditional Field Games: Native athletic contests developed physical stamina, tribal cohesion, and vital hunting reflexes.

Discuss with your counselor what you observed or learned from two exhibitions and identify 10 artifacts by tribe or nation, their shape, size, and use. (b) Attend a contemporary American Indian gathering. Discuss with your counselor proper etiquette for attending as well as what you learned and observed during your visit.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Indian Lore Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 92)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Obtain explicit permission from performers before taking photographs or recording audio at public cultural gatherings.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often mistake the elaborate dance garments worn at powwows for Halloween costumes.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Dancers wear handmade regalia containing sacred family heirlooms, beadwork, and honors that command deep respect.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Professional museum curation records the specific tribal origin, age, and cultural purpose of artifacts.
- NAGPRA Legal Compliance: Federal statutes mandate repatriating sacred objects and human remains to affiliated lineal tribal nations.
- Powwow Arena Etiquette: Visitors must stand respectfully and remove hats during the Grand Entry ceremonial flag procession.
- Dancers wear sacred personal regalia rather than costumes, requiring express permission before taking photographs.
