### Req 1: Major North American Cultural Areas
**Official Requirement 1:** Identify the different American Indian cultural areas. Explain what makes them each unique.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Indian Lore Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 91, p. 92)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How did local rainfall and timber availability dictate whether a tribe built plank houses or tipis?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Plains Nomadic Ecology: Bison herds provided portable skin lodge covers, winter clothing, tools, and staple dried meat.
  - Giant red cedar logs yielded ocean whaling canoes and split plank clan houses.
  - Southwest Masonry Architecture: Arid stone pueblos utilized multistory mud adobe bricks and irrigation ditch networks.
  - Eastern Woodland Subsistence: Deciduous forests supported matrilineal longhouse communities utilizing agriculture, fishing, and deer hunting.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Continental Map Partitioning: Trace the boundaries of the ten primary indigenous cultural areas on paper.
  - Step 2 - Ecological Factor: Record dominant natural vegetation, local climate patterns, and primary regional game species.
  - Step 3 - Architecture Comparison: Match specific traditional dwelling types with regional climate and available construction materials.
  - Step 4 - Subsistence Model Analysis: Document regional food procurement methods including farming, foraging, coastal fishing, and hunting.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe all indigenous North American nations shared identical customs, languages, and dwellings.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Over ten distinct cultural areas supported hundreds of sovereign nations with diverse languages and technologies.

### Req 2: Tribal Ethnography and Cultural Systems
**Official Requirement 2:** Research an American Indian tribe, group, or nation. Tell your counselor about traditional dwellings, way of life, tribal government, religious beliefs, family and clan relationships, language, clothing styles, arts and crafts, food cultivation, foraging and preparation, means of getting around, games, customs in warfare, and where and how they live today.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Indian Lore Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 71, p. 92)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How did clan membership determine mutual obligations and property inheritance within traditional villages?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Matrilineal clan mothers appointed male civil chiefs and held veto authority.
  - Pueblo Dryland Agriculture: Terraced check dams directed rainfall runoff to nourish heritage blue flint corn.
  - Navajo Loom Weaving: Upright wooden looms produced tightly spun wool textiles featuring symbolic geometric patterns.
  - Lakota Government Councils: Decisional consensus among heads of warrior societies governed seasonal camp band movements.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Tribal Nation Selection: Select one federally recognized tribe and access official tribal government publications.
  - Step 2 - Social Structure Investigation: Chart kinship lineages, clan totems, leadership council structures, and civic roles.
  - Step 3 - Material Culture Analysis: Document traditional architecture, textile construction, food preservation, and specialized utilitarian tools.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Presentation Delivery: Explain the collected research findings through an organized oral briefing session.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often assume tribal chiefs held absolute dictatorial control over all communal decisions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Most tribal governments operated through consensus councils, clan checks, and distributed leadership structures.
