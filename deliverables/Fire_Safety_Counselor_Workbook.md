### Req 1: Fire Dynamics and Chemistry
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Science of and Hazards of Fire. Do the following: (a) Explain the concept of fire. (b) Name the parts of the fire tetrahedron and the products of combustion. (c) Describe the life cycle of a fire. (d) Explain the three methods of heat transfer.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fire Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 9)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Thermal decomposition creates toxic, invisible gases such as carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide before visible flames emerge.
- **Common Myth:** Fire is a single physical element rather than a rapid exothermic oxidation reaction.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Combustion requires fuel, heat, oxygen, and an uninhibited chemical chain reaction to maintain self-sustaining flaming fire.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Rapid oxidation releases heat and light energy through sustained chemical molecular conversion.
- Tetrahedron Components: Self-sustaining combustion demands fuel, oxygen, sufficient heat, and an uninterrupted chain reaction.
- Combustion Products: Incomplete burning produces toxic carbon monoxide gas along with airborne particulate smoke.
- Thermal Transfer: Convection circulates superheated buoyant gases vertically toward enclosed interior ceiling surfaces.

### Req 2: Portable Fire Extinguishing Systems
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Fire Extinguishers. Do the following: (a) Explain the different classes of fires. (b) Identify the different classes of fire extinguishers and which should be used for each class of fire. (c) Demonstrate how to use a fire extinguisher and a fire blanket.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fire Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 9)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Discharging water onto energized electrical gear or deep cooking grease causes immediate electrocution or explosive fireball spread.
- **Common Myth:** Pressurized water extinguishers can extinguish any standard indoor fire safely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Water conducts electric current back to the operator when applied to energized electrical equipment fires.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Fuel properties determine specific fire classifications designated by lettered and geometric symbols.
- Numerical extinguisher ratings specify relative extinguishing capability under standardized laboratory test conditions.
- Discharge Sweep: Directing extinguishing stream across the base breaks up the active flame seat.
- Draping a fire blanket across cooking oil cuts off incoming atmospheric oxygen.

### Req 3: Burn Injury Mitigation and Care
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Fire-related Injuries. Do the following: (a) Explain and demonstrate the technique of stop, drop, roll, cool, and call. (b) Describe how clothing fires occur, how to prevent them, and how clothing can prevent or contribute to burn injuries. (c) Identify the most frequent causes of burn injuries and how to prevent them. (d) Review the prevention of, symptoms and signs of, and treatment of airway burns and carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fire Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 9)* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never remove charred clothing melted onto burn wounds, as tearing loose fabric strips away underlying dermis.
- **Common Myth:** Rolling quickly across dry grass spreads clothing flames instead of smothering them.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Synthetic fabrics like polyester melt into liquid plastic that fuses directly to human skin tissue during fires.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Suppression Technique: Dropping immediately to the ground eliminates the upward chimney effect on clothing.
- Smothering Motion: Rolling back and forth smothers active flames by depriving fabric of air.
- Cooling Protocol: Cool clean water stabilizes skin temperature and stops deeper tissue thermal destruction.
- Inhalation Hazards: Toxic smoke particles sear delicate bronchial linings and cause severe airway swelling.

### Req 4: Fire Origin and Cause Determination
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Origins of Fires. Do the following: (a) Explain the four classifications of fire origin (natural, accidental, incendiary, or undetermined) and give an example of each. (b) Describe how a fire classified as incendiary might lead to criminal prosecution of a person charged with arson.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fire Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 9)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Structural stability collapses unpredictably in burned buildings where floor joists and roof trusses have experienced fire degradation.
- **Common Myth:** Every house fire of unknown cause is automatically classified by fire marshals as accidental.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Accelerant pour patterns and multiple isolated ignition points indicate an intentional incendiary fire.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Burn Pattern Analysis: V-shaped charring patterns along interior walls point downward toward initial origin points.
- Accelerant Detection: Trained hydrocarbon sniffer dogs detect minute traces of petroleum fuels beneath charred floorboards.
- Cause Elimination: Certified investigators systematically eliminate electrical faults and heating equipment before declaring arson.
- Forensic Preservation: Securing physical fire scenes preserves fragile ash residues and chemical accelerant samples.

### Req 5: Residential Fire Prevention and Preparedness
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Fire Safety at Home. Do the following: (a) Describe how to prevent the frequent causes of home fires, including unattended cooking, electricity, smoking, clothes dryers, and rechargeable batteries. (b) Describe how to prevent frequent causes of seasonal and holiday-related home fires, including fires related to home heating, holiday trees, candles, and fireworks. (c) Demonstrate how to safely light and extinguish a candle. Discuss with your counselor, then with your family, how to safely use candles. (d) Using the Home Fire Safety Checklist in the Fire Safety merit badge pamphlet or one approved by your counselor, and with the help of an adult, inspect a home (or a similar building near where you live or at a camp) for fire safety hazards. Present your completed checklist to your counselor and discuss your findings. (e) Determine if, and what types of, smoke and CO alarms are required in homes in your community. (f) Demonstrate maintenance of smoke and CO alarms to your counselor and review plans to replace expired alarms. (g) Develop a home fire-escape plan and a fire-drill schedule with your family, draw a floor plan of your home with exits marked and a map showing your family meeting point, and conduct a home fire drill. (h) Explain what to do and what not to do if you smell natural gas and if you smell smoke. (i) Explain how you would report a fire to have the fire department respond.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fire Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 7, p. 9)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never attempt to extinguish a flaming grease pan with water; slide a metal lid over the skillet to smother flames.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Fire Safety Req 5 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Unplugged kitchen appliances pose no fire danger even if power cords remain pinched behind counters.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Lithium-ion batteries undergoing thermal runaway generate internal oxygen and burn without relying on outside air.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Kitchen Vigilance: Unattended frying pans ignite rapidly when cooking grease reaches autoignition temperature thresholds.
- Alarm Placement: Working ionization and photoelectric smoke alarms provide critical seconds for residential evacuation.
- Clean dryer lint screens before every load to prevent heating element ignition.
- Battery Inspection: Damaged lithium rechargeable cells swell and vent violently inside indoor storage areas.
