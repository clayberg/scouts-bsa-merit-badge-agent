### Req 1: Science and Hazards of Fire
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Science of and Hazards of Fire. Do the following: (a) Explain the concept of fire. (b) Name the parts of the fire tetrahedron and the products of combustion. (c) Describe the life cycle of a fire. (d) Explain the three methods of heat transfer.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fire Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 9)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Science and Hazards of Fire discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Flames are the primary cause of injuries and fatalities during an indoor structure fire.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Toxic smoke, superheated gases, and oxygen depletion cause far more structure fire deaths than direct contact with flames (p. 9).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Campfire Fuel Preparation: Collect, prepare, and set up tinder, kindling, and fuel for a campfire following the Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code (p. 6).

### Req 3: Preventing and Treating Burn Injuries
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Fire-related Injuries. Do the following: (a) Explain and demonstrate the technique of stop, drop, roll, cool, and call. (b) Describe how clothing fires occur, how to prevent them, and how clothing can prevent or contribute to burn injuries. > **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Preventing and Treating Burn Injuries discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Applying ice or butter to a fresh thermal burn stops tissue damage faster than cool running water.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Ice causes vasoconstriction and frostbite-like tissue damage while butter traps heat, whereas cool running water safely draws heat out of burned skin (p. 18).
