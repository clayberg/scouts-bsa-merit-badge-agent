### Req 3: Natural Habitat Mammal Field Observations
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Spend three hours in two different kinds of natural habitats or at different elevations for a total of six hours. List the different mammal species and how many of each you identified by sight or sign. Tell why all mammals do not live in the same kind of habitat. (b) Spend three hours on five different days in at least a 4-acre area (about the size of 3 football fields) for a total of 15 hours. List the mammal species you identified by sight or sign. (c) From study and reading, write a simple life history of one nongame mammal that lives in your area. Tell how this mammal lived before its habitat was affected in any way by humans. Tell how it reproduces, what it eats, and its natural habitat. Describe its dependency upon plants and other animals (including humans), and how they depend upon it. Describe how humans have benefited from the mammal you have chosen and whether the mammal has benefited from association with humankind.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Mammal Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 27, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Carry adequate water, dress for terrain hazards, and avoid approaching wild animals closely.
- **Common Myth:** Wildlife field studies produce meaningful population counts only when animals are directly seen.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Systematic track and scat counts provide reliable indirect census indices without disturbing shy animals.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Habitat Contrast: Comparative field surveys reveal how microclimates and plant cover dictate mammal distribution.
- Sign Identification: Mammal tracks, droppings, and burrows confirm presence even when target animals remain unseen.
- Mountain elevation changes alter forest canopy composition and related small mammal populations.
- Observation Logs: Detailed field notes preserve dates, weather readings, habitat types, and accurate specimen tallies.
