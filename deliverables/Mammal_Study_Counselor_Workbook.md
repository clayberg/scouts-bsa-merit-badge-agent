### Req 1: Defining Characteristics of Class Mammalia
**Official Requirement 1:** Explain the following terms: animal, invertebrate, vertebrate, and mammal. Name three characteristics that distinguish mammals from all other animals.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Mammal Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 16)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Animal Invertebrate Vertebrate and Mammal)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Defining Characteristics of Class Mammalia discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Whales and dolphins are classified as fish rather than mammals because they live entirely in the ocean and appear to have smooth, hairless skin.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** All mammals have some true hair on their bodies at some stage of life, and even aquatic whales and dolphins possess sensory bristles around their snouts alongside mammary glands that produce milk for their young (p. 13).

### Req 3: Field Observation and Life Histories
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Spend three hours in two different kinds of natural habitats or at different elevations for a total of six hours. List the different mammal species and how many of each you identified by sight or sign. Tell why all mammals do not live in the same kind of habitat. (b) Spend three hours on five different days in at least a 4-acre area (about the size of 3 football fields) for a total of 15 hours. List the mammal species you identified by sight or sign. (c) From study and reading, write a simple life history of one nongame mammal that lives in your area. Tell how this mammal lived before its habitat was affected in any way by humans. Tell how it reproduces, what it eats, and its natural habitat. Describe its dependency upon plants and other animals (including humans), and how they depend upon it. Describe how humans have benefited from the mammal you have chosen and whether the mammal has benefited from association with humankind.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Mammal Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 27, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Field Observation and Life Histories discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** If you do not see live mammals walking around during a midday hike, there are no mammals living in that habitat.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Most wild mammals are secretive, nocturnal, or crepuscular, so field observers identify far more species by reading tracks, scat, gnaw marks, and antler rubbings than by direct daytime sightings (p. 27).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Long-Term Shared Wildlife Trails: Animals may share a single pathway through the woods, across a meadow, or up a hillside, and use that trail for many years (p. 27).
- Tracks, Scat, and Hair Clues: Look for evidence of animal tracks, droppings, and hair on any well-used pathway through the brush (p. 27).
- Choosing Off-Trail Observation Spots: Find a secluded spot a little bit off the trail to observe the mammals that pass (p. 27).
- Keeping Distance From Active Trails: Be careful not to get too close to the trail so as not to spook the animals that use (p. 27).

### Req 5: Habitat Carrying Capacity Projects
**Official Requirement 5:** Working with your counselor, select and carry out one project that will influence the numbers of one or more mammals.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Mammal Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 51, p. 60)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Working Project Will Influence)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Mammal Study Requirement 5: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying habitat carrying capacity projects, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Scattering bags of human food on the ground is the best way to permanently increase a wild mammal population in a neighborhood woodlot.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Long-term mammal population numbers are governed by the habitat's carrying capacity, meaning sustainable changes require modifying food, water, protective cover, or sanitation rather than dumping loose piles of food (p. 51).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Four Essential Habitat Needs: All mammals need food, water, shelter, and livings pace (p. 51).
- Understanding Habitat Carrying Capacity: And so, if you want to We have seen that the number of mammals a given area of land or water will support is called its carrying capacity (p. 51).
