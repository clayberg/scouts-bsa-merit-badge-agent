### Req 1: Workshop Safety Rules and Equipment
**Official Requirement 1:** Read the safety rules for metalwork. Discuss how to be safe while working with metal. Discuss with your counselor the additional safety rules that apply to the metalwork option you choose for requirement 5.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Metalwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 19)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Tie back long hair, roll up loose sleeves, and remove rings and necklaces before operating rotating machinery.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do natural fibers like cotton protect against hot embers better than polyester athletic clothing?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Eye Protection: Always wear safety glasses with side shields around cutting and grinding tools.
  - Natural Fibers: Wear untreated cotton denim or leather garments to resist ignition from hot slag.
  - Hearing Conservation: Insert foam earplugs or ear defenders when hammering sheet metal against hard anvils.
  - Respiratory Protection: Don a fitted respirator when buffing alloys or heating metals producing toxic fumes.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Gear Selection: Put on flame resistant cotton work clothing and approved impact resistant safety glasses.
  - Step 2 - Footwear Check: Lace heavy leather work boots tightly to guard feet against falling heavy billets.
  - Step 3 - Perimeter Clearance: Remove combustible debris and flammable solvents within thirty feet of the forge area.
  - Step 4 - Ventilation Setup: Turn on exhaust hoods or open shop doors to extract harmful airborne particulates.
- **Common Myth:** Synthetic athletic shirts provide adequate comfort and protection inside a hot metalworking shop.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Synthetic fabrics melt rapidly upon contact with flying sparks, bonding molten plastic directly to skin.

#### Req 2c: Mechanical Work Hardening Techniques
**[Parent Directive]:** *Explain the following terms*

  - Striking or bending cold metal distorts its crystalline lattice, generating internal dislocations that restrict atomic slippage. This physical deformation increases yield strength and surface hardness while progressively diminishing ductility.

- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Bending sheet brass or copper introduces mechanical strain along the fold line. Peening this ridge with a ball-peen hammer packs crystal lattice dislocations, raising resistance against further movement.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Angle Bending: Clamp sheet metal in smooth vise jaws and bend cleanly to forty-five degrees.

- **Common Myth:** Light surface hammering on a sheet bend does not alter metal stiffness underneath.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Peening redistributes internal mechanical stresses and multiplies dislocations throughout the entire cross section.

#### Req 5a(1): Basic Sheet Metalworking Hand Tools
**Official Requirement 5a(1):** Name and describe the use of the basic sheet metalworking tools.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Metalwork Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 37, p. 49)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5a
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why would a metalworker need three distinct pairs of snips to cut out a simple curved template?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Basic Sheet Metalworking Hand Tools: Sheet metal fabrication relies on layout tools such as scratch awls and combination squares, along with cutting snips, forming stakes, and setting hammers. Aviation snips come in color-coded varieties designed specifically for straight lines or tight curves in light-gauge stock (p. 26).
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Option Selection: Choose between sheet metal fabrication, silversmithing art, foundry casting, or traditional blacksmithing.
