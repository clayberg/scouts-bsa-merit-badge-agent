## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** Watch the national evening news for five days in a row or read the main stories in a national media organization (e. g., a newspaper or news website) for five days in a row. Discuss the national issues that you learned about with your counselor. Choose one issue and explain how it affects you, your family, and community.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Prepare):** Do TWO of the following: (a) Visit a place that is listed as a National Historic Landmark or that is on the National Register of Historic Places. Tell your counselor what you learned about the landmark or site and what you found interesting about it. (b) Tour your state capitol building or the U. S. Capitol. Tell your counselor what you learned about the capitol, its function, and the history. (c) Tour a federal facility. Explain to your counselor what you saw and what you learned about its function in the local community and how it serves this nation. (d) Choose a United States national monument that interests you. Using books, brochures, the internet (with your parent or guardian's permission), and other resources, find out more about the monument. Tell your counselor what you learned, and explain why the monument is important to this country's citizens.

### Req 3: Three Branches of Federal Government
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** List the three branches of the United States government. Explain: (a) The function of each branch of government (b) Why it is important to divide powers among different branches (c) How each branch checks and balances the others (d) How citizens can be involved in each branch of government
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Citizenship in the Nation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 27, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Civic & Constitutional Study Organizer
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** When visiting courthouse facilities or legislative galleries, observe security screening checkpoints and silence all mobile devices.
- **Common Myth:** A common misunderstanding is that the president holds supreme command over the other two branches.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The three branches are constitutionally coequal, with distinct duties and robust interbranch checks.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Tripartite Structure Division: The Constitution delegates distinct lawmaking, enforcement, and adjudicative duties across three coequal branches.
- Congress possesses exclusive authority to levy taxes, declare war, and draft federal statutes.
- Executive Branch Administration: The president commands armed forces, directs foreign diplomacy, and manages federal regulatory departments.
- Judicial Review Function: Federal courts interpret constitutional intent and invalidate statutory laws conflicting with foundational doctrine.

### Req 4: Foundational Documents and National Motto
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Discuss the importance of: (a) Declaration of Independence (b) Bill of Rights (the first 10 Amendments to the Constitution) and the 14th Amendment (c) E Pluribus Unum, the traditional United States motto
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Citizenship in the Nation Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 15, p. 22)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Handle delicate historical books and library archive materials with clean dry hands in designated study areas.
- **Common Myth:** A common misunderstanding is that the Declaration of Independence is an enforceable part of statutory law.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Declaration expresses foundational political philosophy, whereas the Constitution and its amendments provide binding law.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Declaration Philosophical Foundation: The 1776 document declared that governments derive legitimate power solely from governed consent.
- Bill of Rights: The first ten constitutional amendments restrict federal authority regarding speech, religion, and trials.
- Fourteenth Amendment Guarantees: Equal protection and due process clauses extend constitutional safeguards against state level discrimination.
- Motto Symbolism Meaning: E Pluribus Unum celebrates unity formed by diverse states and cultures into one nation.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** The Declaration justified colonial separation, while the subsequent Constitution constructed our formal governmental framework.
