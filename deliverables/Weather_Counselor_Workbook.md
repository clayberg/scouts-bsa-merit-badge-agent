## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 10 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Make one of the following instruments: wind vane, anemometer, rain gauge, or hygrometer. Keep a daily weather log for one week using information from this instrument as well as from other sources such as local radio and television stations, NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards, and internet sources (with your parent or guardian's permission). Record the following information at the same time every day: wind direction and speed, temperature, precipitation, and types of clouds. Be sure to make a note of any morning dew or frost. In the log, also list the weather forecasts from radio or television at the same time each day and show how the weather really turned out. (b) Visit a National Weather Service office or talk with a local radio or television weathercaster, private meteorologist, local agricultural extension service officer, or university meteorology instructor. Find out what type of weather is most dangerous or damaging to your community. Determine how severe weather and flood warnings reach the homes in your community.
- [ ] **Req 12 (Prepare):** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.

### Req 1: Atmospheric Science and Societal Impacts
**Official Requirement 1:** Define meteorology. Explain what weather is and what climate is. Discuss how the weather affects farmers, sailors, aviators, and the outdoor construction industry. Tell why weather forecasts are important to each of these groups.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 72, p. 74)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Halt all outdoor training activities immediately when forecasted severe storms threaten personnel safety.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does a three day forecast differ fundamentally from a regional climate model?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Meteorology examines physical atmospheric dynamics across short and medium temporal horizons.
  - Climate Baseline Metrics: Climatology calculates statistical standard normals using thirty consecutive years of recorded data.
  - Agricultural Weather Impacts: Freezing surface temperatures destroy blooming orchard crops during critical spring germination periods.
  - Aviation Flight Planning: Low cloud ceilings and shifting wind shear dictate safe aircraft navigation profiles.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Science Definition: Define atmospheric science using standard physical principles governing planetary air mass movements.
  - Step 2 - Comparative Analysis: Contrast hourly local weather observations against thirty year regional climate average datasets.
  - Step 3 - Sector Evaluation: Document specific operational hazards weather shifts impose upon farmers mariners and builders.
  - Step 4 - Forecast Utility: Outline how accurate barometric warnings protect aviation flight paths and construction crews.
- **Common Myth:** Weather and climate mean the exact same atmospheric condition over short durations.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Weather describes daily atmospheric fluctuations whereas climate represents thirty year statistical averages of conditions.

### Req 4: Pressure Dynamics and Frontal Cross Sections
**Official Requirement 4:** Explain the difference between high and low pressure systems in the atmosphere. Tell which is related to good and to poor weather. Draw cross sections of a cold front and a warm front, showing the location and movements of the cold and warm air, the frontal slope, the location and types of clouds associated with each type of front, and the location of precipitation.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 29, p. 30)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Secure camp structures and tie down rain tarps when sudden pressure drops indicate squall lines.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does descending air in a high pressure ridge inhibit condensation and cloud growth?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Anticyclone Air Characteristics: High pressure centers feature descending cool dry air yielding stable clear atmospheric conditions.
  - Cyclone Core Dynamics: Low pressure cores force ascending moist air creating cloud cover and precipitation.
  - Dense advancing cold air wedges steeply under warm air creating violent squalls.
  - Ascending warm air glides gradually over retreating cold air forming stratiform clouds.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Pressure System Comparison: Compare atmospheric density variations between sinking anticyclones and rising cyclonic low pressure centers.
  - Step 2 - Weather Association: Correlate fair conditions with rising barometers and foul conditions with falling barometric gradients.
  - Step 3 - Cold Front Cross Section: Illustrate advancing steep wedge margins driving moist warm air into towering cumulonimbus clouds.
  - Step 4 - Warm Front Cross Section: Sketch gradual warm air ascents producing progressive cirrus altostratus and nimbostratus cloud decks.
- **Common Myth:** Air masses mix together smoothly and immediately whenever high and low pressure systems meet.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cold air is denser than warm air, forcing warm air upward rapidly along steep fronts.

### Req 5: Wind Rain Lightning and Hail Formation
**Official Requirement 5:** Tell what causes wind, why it rains, and how lightning and hail are formed.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Seek hard enclosed vehicular or structural shelter immediately when hail indicates severe thunderstorm updrafts.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the Coriolis effect alter the path of wind across different hemispheres?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Horizontal air movement originates from atmospheric pressure differentials driving flow toward low pressure.
  - Precipitation Condensation Triggers: Air parcel cooling below its dew point condenses moisture onto microscopic airborne aerosols.
  - Charge Separation Dynamics: Collisions between graupel and supercooled droplets generate massive electrostatic charges within updrafts.
  - Hail Formation Cycling: Severe convective updrafts suspend frozen water embryos while accumulating successive concentric ice layers.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Wind Genesis: Trace horizontal atmospheric motion directly from differential solar heating to regional pressure gradients.
  - Step 2 - Rain Mechanics: Map adiabatic cooling condensation around microscopic aerosols and subsequent water droplet coalescence.
  - Step 3 - Lightning Genesis: Explain electrostatic charge buildup caused by graupel collisions inside vigorous convective storm updrafts.
  - Step 4 - Hail Growth Cycle: Diagram how severe updraft currents suspend ice pellets through repetitive freezing accretion cycles.
- **Common Myth:** Lightning strikes from cloud to ground exclusively without traveling upward or through cloud channels.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hail forms when powerful thunderstorm updrafts repeatedly cycle ice pellets above freezing levels.

### Req 6: Atmospheric Cloud Genera and Forecasting
**Official Requirement 6:** Identify and describe clouds in the low, middle, and upper levels of the atmosphere. Relate these to specific types of weather.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 23)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Evacuate exposed ridgelines immediately upon sighting anvils or rapidly growing vertical cumulonimbus towers.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do high level cirrus clouds consist of ice crystals instead of liquid droplets?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Low Cloud Classifications: Stratus and stratocumulus clouds form below six thousand feet producing overcast drizzly conditions.
  - Altocumulus and altostratus occupy middle altitudes between six thousand and twenty thousand feet.
  - Cirrus clouds consist entirely of delicate ice crystals drifting above twenty thousand feet.
  - Convective Storm Towers: Towering cumulonimbus clouds generate severe turbulence torrential precipitation lightning and damaging hail shafts.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Low Cloud Survey: Catalog stratus stratocumulus and nimbostratus structures observing their low horizontal uniform cloud bases.
  - Step 2 - Mid Level Assessment: Identify altostratus sheets and altocumulus ripples within mid tropospheric atmospheric pressure layers.
  - Step 3 - High Cloud Inspection: Examine fibrous cirrus cirrostratus and cirrocumulus formations drifting within subfreezing high altitudes.
  - Step 4 - Weather Correlation: Correlate observed cloud genera against imminent frontal shifts precipitation probabilities and atmospheric stability.
- **Common Myth:** All high altitude clouds produce active surface rain and heavy localized snowstorms.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Cumulonimbus clouds possess intense vertical development reaching into the stratosphere despite having low bases.

  - Step 4 - Ground Flow Explanation: Trace rain runoff percolation through soil matrices back into streams rivers and oceans.
- **Common Myth:** Evaporation from oceans is the only process that returns moisture to the atmosphere.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Transpiration from plant leaves releases vast quantities of water vapor into the atmosphere.

- **Common Myth:** Human land development and industrial emissions affect only indoor air quality without altering climates.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Urban concrete and asphalt retain heat, creating localized microclimates known as urban heat islands.

### Req 9: Earth Axial Tilt and Global Climates
**Official Requirement 9:** Describe how the tilt of Earth's axis helps determine the climate of a region near the equator, near the poles, and across the area in between.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 66, p. 68)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 9 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Apply high SPF sunscreen and protective sunglasses during polar travel because snow reflects ultraviolet radiation.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why do equatorial regions experience minimal seasonal temperature variation compared to temperate zones?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Axial Tilt Constant: Earth rotational axis maintains a steady twenty three point five degree orbital tilt.
  - Equatorial latitudes receive nearly perpendicular solar rays year round producing consistently warm climates.
  - Polar regions receive shallow oblique solar rays distributing minimal solar thermal energy broadly.
  - Seasonal Insolation Shifts: Mid latitude regions experience contrasting seasons due to shifting solar angles and daylight lengths.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Tilt Modeling: Model Earth twenty three point five degree axial tilt relative to the ecliptic plane.
  - Step 2 - Insolation Geometry: Demonstrate how perpendicular rays concentrate thermal energy while oblique rays diffuse solar warmth.
  - Step 3 - Equatorial Analysis: Detail year round high solar angles that produce continuous warm equatorial climate bands.
  - Step 4 - Polar Comparison: Explain how extreme low sun angles create cold polar climates despite continuous daylight.
- **Common Myth:** Earth experiences hot summer weather because the planet orbits physically closer to the Sun.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Earth tilted axis maintains constant spatial orientation while orbiting, altering hemispheric solar incidence angles.

  - Step 2 - Lightning Protocol: Teach audiences to seek enclosed shelter when the flash to bang interval nears thirty.
  - Step 3 - Flood Evacuation: Explain how rapidly rising mountain waters require immediate vertical evacuation to higher ground.
  - Step 4 - Tornado Tactics: Instruct audiences to abandon vehicles and seek interior basements or low ditches outdoors.
- **Common Myth:** Rubber shoe soles protect an outdoor hiker from being struck by lethal cloud to ground lightning.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The thirty thirty rule mandates seeking shelter when thunder occurs within thirty seconds of flashes.
