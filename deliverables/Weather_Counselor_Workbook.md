## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 10 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Make one of the following instruments: wind vane, anemometer, rain gauge, or hygrometer. Keep a daily weather log for one week using information from this instrument as well as from other sources such as local radio and television stations, NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards, and internet sources (with your parent or guardian's permission). Record the following information at the same time every day: wind direction and speed, temperature, precipitation, and types of clouds. Be sure to make a note of any morning dew or frost. In the log, also list the weather forecasts from radio or television at the same time each day and show how the weather really turned out. (b) Visit a National Weather Service office or talk with a local radio or television weathercaster, private meteorologist, local agricultural extension service officer, or university meteorology instructor. Find out what type of weather is most dangerous or damaging to your community. Determine how severe weather and flood warnings reach the homes in your community.
- [ ] **Req 12 (Prepare):** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.

**Official Requirement 1:** Define meteorology. Explain what weather is and what climate is. Discuss how the weather affects farmers, sailors, aviators, and the outdoor construction industry. Tell why weather forecasts are important to each of these groups.

### Req 5: Wind, Rain, Lightning, and Hail
**Official Requirement 5:** Tell what causes wind, why it rains, and how lightning and hail are formed.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 34, p. 37)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Wind, Rain, Lightning & Hail Formation)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow BSA Guide to Safe Scouting field protocols for Weather Requirement 5: travel with a buddy, carry the Scout Basic Essentials and water while studying wind, rain, lightning, and hail, and respect wildlife distance and Leave No Trace guidelines.
- **Common Myth:** Hail only forms during freezing winter storms when rain freezes on its way down to the ground.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Hailstones form inside tall cumulonimbus thunderstorms when powerful updrafts repeatedly carry water droplets above the freezing level to accumulate layers of ice (p. 37).

### Req 6: Cloud Classifications and Weather Patterns
**Official Requirement 6:** Identify and describe clouds in the low, middle, and upper levels of the atmosphere. Relate these to specific types of weather.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 8, p. 23)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 6 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Low, Middle & Upper Cloud Classification)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 6): Complete all Cloud Classifications and Weather Patterns discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** All white, fluffy clouds in the sky are made of water vapor gas floating in the air.

### Req 9: Earth's Axial Tilt and Climate Zones
**Official Requirement 9:** Describe how the tilt of Earth's axis helps determine the climate of a region near the equator, near the poles, and across the area in between.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Weather Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 66, p. 68)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 9 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Earth's Axial Tilt & Regional Climate Zones)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 9): Complete all Earth's Axial Tilt and Climate Zones discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Summer happens when Earth's orbit brings the entire planet closer to the sun, and winter happens when Earth moves farther away.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Seasons and latitude climates are caused by Earth's 23.5-degree axial tilt, which changes the angle and duration of sunlight striking each hemisphere (p. 66).

**Official Requirement 11:** Give a talk of at least five minutes to a group (such as your unit or a Cub Scout pack) explaining the outdoor safety rules in the event of lightning, flash floods, and tornadoes. Before your talk, share your outline with your counselor for approval.

- **Common Myth:** Standing underneath a tall, isolated tree in a meadow is the best way to stay dry and safe during a lightning storm.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Isolated trees provide a tall path of least resistance for lightning, making them prime targets you must avoid during thunderstorms (p. 37).
