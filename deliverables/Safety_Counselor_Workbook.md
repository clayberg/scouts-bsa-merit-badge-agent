### Req 1: Understanding Safety and Hazard Management
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Safety and Hazards. Do the following: (a) Explain what safety is and what it means to be safe. (b) Explain what hazards are and how people manage hazards to reduce risks of harm and to improve safety.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 15)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 1): Complete all Understanding Safety and Hazard Management discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Accidents are completely random events of bad luck that cannot be anticipated or prevented.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Practicing good safety habits, recognizing hazards early, and using proper protective tools help prevent most accidents before they happen (p. 10).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Realizing Personal Accident Risk: It is not hard to imagine yourself becoming part of the accident statistics (p. 10).
- Everyday News of Accidents: Just read the daily newspaper or watch the news on TV (p. 10).
- Daily Reports of Injury: Not a day goes by without a story about some victim who was hurt or killed in an accident (p. 10).
- However, learning good safety habits may help prevent an accident (p. 10).

### Req 5: Safeguarding Youth in Scouting Activities
**Official Requirement 5 (Parent Overview):** Safeguarding Youth. Do the following: (a) Discuss Scouting America's Safeguarding Youth guidelines that adults and Scouts must follow so that everyone is safe at Scouting activities. (b) Discuss with your counselor what situations and behaviors would prompt you to Recognize, Resist, and Report a possible abuse situation. (c) Discuss how you, other Scouts, leaders, and anyone can report situations suspicious for abuse through the Scouts First Helpline and other means.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 28, p. 29)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 5): Complete all Safeguarding Youth in Scouting Activities discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** You can easily tell if someone is a child molester because they are always strangers who act frightening.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Anyone could be a molester, including someone a youth knows and trusts, which is why recognizing risky situations and boundary violations is essential (p. 28).
