### Req 1: Fundamental Hazard Assessment Principles
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Safety and Hazards. Do the following: (a) Explain what safety is and what it means to be safe. (b) Explain what hazards are and how people manage hazards to reduce risks of harm and to improve safety.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 10, p. 15)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Stop any activity immediately whenever an uncontrolled hazard or unsafe condition appears in the operating area.
- **Common Myth:** Safety means eliminating all possible danger from every daily task.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Safety is the continuous process of identifying, controlling, and mitigating manageable risks to prevent harm.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Risk Reduction: Systematic controls eliminate potential dangers before operational activities begin in earnest.
- Hazard Definition: Unsafe physical conditions or reckless actions directly create severe injury risks.
- Control Hierarchy: Physical hazard elimination provides significantly higher protection than behavioral personal warnings.
- Situational Readiness: Continuous environmental observation prevents unexpected workplace incidents during troop project work.

### Req 2: Residential Hazard Mitigation Systems
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Family and Home Safety. Do the following: (a) Explain common causes of and prevention of home injuries. (b) Discuss the dangers involved with the use of fireworks. (c) Explain common causes of and prevention of home fires. (d) Using the Home Safety Checklist in the Safety merit badge pamphlet, or one approved by your counselor, inspect your home or a similar building for hazards with an adult. Review your checklist and findings with your counselor. (e) Develop a fire-escape plan for your home or for a similar building. Include in your drawings exit routes from each room and a meeting place outside. Conduct a fire-escape drill. Review your plan and your drill with your counselor. (f) Develop a family emergency action plan for evacuation because of weather or other natural disasters that occur in your area. Using a checklist in the Safety merit badge pamphlet or one approved by your counselor, assemble or inspect an emergency supplies kit. Explain to your counselor how a family would use the plan and the kit supplies in an emergency.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 51, p. 52)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never re-enter a burning structure under any circumstances once you have evacuated to the designated assembly point.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Safety Req 2 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Common Myth:** Residential fires allow ample time to gather personal valuables before exiting.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Flashover and toxic gas accumulation can incapacitate occupants in less than two minutes.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Fall Prevention: Secure stairway handrails and remove floor clutter to prevent falls.
- Firework Hazards: Consumer pyrotechnics cause catastrophic hand burns and permanent eyesight destruction.
- Test battery operated smoke detectors monthly to ensure reliable operational capability.
- Evacuation Routes: Identify two distinct emergency exit passageways from every residential bedroom.

- Perimeter Lighting: Mount motion sensor floodlights to illuminate vulnerable residential yard access ways.
- Theft Deterrence: Keep personal electronics and expensive gear concealed while walking in public.

Explain each of the following to your counselor: (a) How a person would safely exit from public buildings, a hotel, and a stadium in an emergency (b) How and why Scouts would take shelter at camp (c) How should Scouts respond to an active shooter (d) The meaning of this saying: "If You See Something, Say Something®"
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Safety Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 63, p. 65)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** In an active shooter emergency, keep your hands visible, fingers spread, and empty when first encountering law enforcement officers.
- **Common Myth:** Crowds will always evacuate public venues through the main front entrance in an orderly fashion.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Main entrances become deadly bottlenecks during panics, making secondary side exits and service stairwells safer.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Locate multiple emergency exit doors immediately upon entering any public auditorium.
- Seek sturdy interior masonry rooms when severe thunderstorms threaten camp sites.
- Active Threats: Run away immediately from gunshots if an unobstructed escape route exists.
- Suspicious Activity: Report unattended luggage or unusual behaviors to facility security personnel promptly.

### Req 5: Youth Protection Operational Guidelines

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** In an emergency involving imminent danger of child abuse, contact local police authorities immediately before notifying council personnel.
- **Common Myth:** Youth protection rules apply only to adult volunteers and not to older youth leaders.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Youth protection barriers and anti-bullying policies govern interactions among all participants, regardless of age or rank.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Two-Deep Leadership: Maintain two registered adult leaders on all official troop scouting trips.
- Prohibit one-on-one contact between adults and youth in all settings.
- Identify boundary violations immediately, resist unwanted interactions, and report to authorities.
- Call the twenty-four-hour helpline to report any suspected youth mistreatment.
