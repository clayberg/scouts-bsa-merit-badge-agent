## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 8:** Do ONE of the following: (a) Take custody of one or more reptiles or amphibians in a manner approved by your counselor. Maintain one or more reptiles or amphibians for at least a month. Record food accepted, eating methods, changes in coloration, shedding of skins, and general habits; or keep the eggs of a reptile from the time of laying until hatching; or keep the eggs of an amphibian from the time of laying until their transformation into tadpoles (frogs) or larvae (salamanders). Whichever you choose, keep records of and report to your counselor how you cared for your animal, eggs, or larvae, including lighting, habitat, temperature and humidity maintenance, and any veterinary care requirements. Unless you are the long-term owner, at the conclusion of this study, turn the animal(s) over to another responsible party approved by your counselor. (b) Choose a reptile or amphibian that you can observe or build at a local zoo, aquarium, nature center, local rescue, or other such exhibit (such as your classroom or school). Study the specimen weekly for a period of three months. At each visit, sketch the specimen in its captive habitat and note any changes in its coloration, shedding of skins, and general habits and behavior. Discuss with your counselor how the animal you observed was cared for to include its housing and habitat, how the lighting, temperature, and humidity were maintained, and any veterinary care requirements. Find out, either from information you locate on your own or by talking to the caretaker, what this species eats and what are its native habitat and home range, preferred climate, average life expectancy, and natural predators. Also, identify any human-caused threats to its population and any laws that protect the species and its habitat. After the observation period, share what you have learned with your counselor.

### Req 1: Native Herpetological Species Identification
**Official Requirement 1:** Describe the identifying characteristics of six species of reptiles and four species of amphibians found in the United States. For any four of these, make sketches from your own observations or take photographs. Show markings, color patterns, or other characteristics that are important in the identification of each of the four species. Discuss the habits and habitats of all 10 species.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Reptile and Amphibian Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 8)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never handle unidentified snakes or reach under deadfall logs where venomous pit vipers conceal themselves during daylight.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What physical microstructures distinguish the glandular skin of a true frog from the keratinized epidermis of a lizard?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Reptiles possess dry keratinized scales that prevent desiccation in arid terrestrial environments.
  - Cutaneous Gas Exchange: Amphibians absorb moisture and oxygen directly through permeable glandular outer skin layers.
  - Plastron Suture Patterns: Turtle shell identification relies on counting scutes across the carapace and plastron.
  - Costal Groove Counts: Salamander identification requires tallying lateral trunk grooves between the forelimbs and hindlimbs.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Specimen Survey: Locate native species along wetland margins or upland rocky slopes during active periods.
  - Step 2 - Diagnostic Examination: Record focal morphological characteristics including dorsal blotches, toe pads, and cranial scale shapes.
  - Step 3 - Photographic Capture: Photograph the dorsal pattern, lateral profile, and ventral surfaces alongside a field scale.
  - Step 4 - Observation Sketching: Draft detailed structural sketches showing scute arrangements, tympanum diameters, and distinct limb proportions.
- **Common Myth:** Salamanders are classified as lizards because both share an elongated body shape and four legs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Reptile scales contain keratin while amphibian skin lacks scales and absorbs water directly through cutaneous pores.

### Req 2: United States Herpetofauna Distribution
**Official Requirement 2:** Discuss with your counselor the approximate number of species and general geographic distribution of reptiles and amphibians in the United States. Prepare a list of the most common species found in your local area or state.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Reptile and Amphibian Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 8)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Check local game regulations and obtain required observer permits before collecting herpetological distribution data on state wildlife lands.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does the southeastern United States support significantly more salamander species than the arid southwest?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Southeastern Salamander Density: The Appalachian mountain range supports the highest geographic diversity of plethodontid salamanders globally.
  - Arid Southwestern desert ecosystems support diverse desert tortoises, horned lizards, and specialized rattlesnakes.
  - Ecto thermic Thermal Drivers: Regional ambient temperatures and annual moisture gradients govern native herpetofauna distribution limits.
  - State wildlife agencies publish verified county distribution records based on curated museum vouchers.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Regional Checklist Review: Obtain current county species distribution lists from the state wildlife conservation department.
  - Step 2 - Habitat Mapping: Chart local drainage basins, vernal pools, and rocky outcrops that provide microhabitat resources.
  - Step 3 - Distribution Analysis: Compare regional species richness totals across temperate deciduous forests and adjacent open prairies.
  - Step 4 - Local Inventory Compilation: Assemble a verified table listing prevalent reptiles and amphibians within thirty miles.
- **Common Myth:** Reptiles and amphibians maintain uniform species diversity across all fifty states regardless of regional climate.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Over three hundred native reptile species and two hundred thirty amphibian species inhabit distinct regional life zones across North America.

### Req 4: Herpetological Ecology and Conservation
**Official Requirement 4:** Explain how reptiles and amphibians are an important component of the natural environment. List four species that are officially protected by the federal government or by the state you live in, and tell why each is protected. List three species of reptiles and three species of amphibians found in your local area that are not protected. Discuss the food habits of all 10 species.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Reptile and Amphibian Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 8)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Disinfect field wading boots with a ten percent bleach solution between ponds to prevent spreading chytrid fungal spores.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the biphasic life cycle of amphibians make them vulnerable to both aquatic and terrestrial disruptions?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Mid Trophic Linkages: Herpetofauna regulate invertebrate prey densities while providing biomass for larger predatory birds and mammals.
  - Permeable Bioindicator Integrity: Thin amphibian skin absorbs dissolved environmental toxins, providing early warnings of wetland contamination.
  - The Endangered Species Act legally protects declining taxa like the flatwoods salamander from extinction.
  - Exotic Invasive Disruptions: Introduced Burmese pythons consume native marsh mammals and severely disrupt delicate Everglades food webs.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Trophic Role Evaluation: Chart the position of native herpetofauna within local wetland and upland food webs.
  - Step 2 - Protected Species: Identify four federally or state protected herpetofaunal species from official state conservation listings.
  - Step 3 - Threat Factor Analysis: Document primary mortality drivers including habitat loss, road mortality, and emerging chytridiomycosis infections.
  - Step 4 - Invasive Species Assessment: Detail ecological damage caused by three invasive reptiles or amphibians established within American habitats.
- **Common Myth:** Removing predatory snakes and bullfrogs from an ecosystem produces no measurable effect on rodent or insect numbers.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Amphibians serve as primary biological indicators because environmental pollutants absorb rapidly through their moist, permeable skin.

### Req 5: Herpetological Reproductive Biology Comparisons
**Official Requirement 5:** Compare how reptiles reproduce to how amphibians reproduce.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Reptile and Amphibian Study Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 6, p. 7)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (2-Column Comparison Table):** Req 5 Technical Comparison Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never disturb active sea turtle nests or vernal pool egg masses during seasonal nocturnal breeding migrations.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does the evolution of the allantois and chorion enable reptile embryos to develop away from water?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Leathery reptilian eggshells contain fluid membranes that support embryonic development entirely on dry land.
  - Male frogs grasp females in amplexus to fertilize extruded egg strings directly in water.
  - Amphibian larvae absorb aquatic oxygen through gills before transforming into lung breathing adult forms.
  - Squamate Viviparity Adaptations: High elevation garter snakes retain embryos internally to protect developing young from freezing temperatures.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Embryonic Membrane Comparison: Compare the protective layers of shelled reptile eggs against gelatinous amphibian egg masses.
  - Step 2 - Fertilization Mechanism: Contrast external fertilization during anuran amplexus with internal squamate and chelonian copulation.
  - Step 3 - Larval Stage Tracking: Document amphibian metamorphosis stages from gilled aquatic tadpole to lung breathing adult terrestrial form.
  - Step 4 - Reproductive Strategy: Record environmental parameters including water temperature and substrate moisture essential for incubation success.
- **Common Myth:** All reptiles and amphibians must return directly to open water pools to deposit their eggs.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Reptiles practice internal fertilization and produce cleidoic eggs, whereas most frogs and toads release sperm over eggs during amplexus.
