### Req 2: Automated Identification and Biometric Systems
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Explain the difference between the automated fingerprint identification systems (AFIS) now used by law enforcement agencies and the biometric fingerprint systems used to control access to computers and places like buildings and airports. (b) Discuss how our society uses identification systems based on tokens, passwords, and biometrics.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fingerprinting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 35, p. 36)* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 2): Complete all Automated Identification and Biometric Systems discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** A biometric door scanner stores a full photograph of your fingerprint and searches a massive police database every time you unlock a building.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Biometric access systems convert specific fingerprint characteristics into a compact digital template to quickly verify an authorized user's identity on the spot, whereas law enforcement AFIS searches millions of full fingerprint records to identify unknown prints (p. 35).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Methods of Personal Identification: Biometrics in our Society People can identify themselves for access and for privileges in several general ways (p. 35).
- Token-based identification systems include driver's licenses and passports (p. 35).
- Knowledge-based identification systems include a computer password, apersonal identification number, and security questions for password (p. 35).
- Because biometric identifiers are unique to individuals, biometric identification systems are more reliable than token- or knowledge-based systems (p. 35).

### Req 3: Friction Ridges and Identification Principles
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Name the surfaces of the body where friction or papillary ridges are found. (b) Name the two basic principles supporting the science of fingerprints and give a brief explanation of each principle. (c) Explain what it takes to positively identify a person using fingerprints.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fingerprinting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 32, p. 33)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 3): Complete all Friction Ridges and Identification Principles discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** United States law requires a strict minimum of 12 matching ridge points before a fingerprint examiner can declare a positive identification.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** No minimum area or minimum number of features or points is required to make a positive identification, provided the friction-ridge details match with no unexplainable dissimilarities (p. 32).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Matching Friction-Ridge Details: For an identification to be made, the friction-ridge details from two impressions must match without any unexplainabledissimilarities (p. 32).
- Even one unexplainable difference means the fingerprints are not from the same finger (p. 32).
- No minimum amount of area or minimum number of features or points is required to make an identification (p. 32).
- Often, an area as small as the tip of a pencil eraser may be identified, if the friction-ridge detail that is present is clearly represented (p. 32).

### Req 4: Recording Rolled and Plain Prints
**Official Requirement 4 (Parent Overview):** Take a clear set of prints using ONE of the following methods: (a) Make both rolled and plain impressions. Make these on an 8-by-8-inch fingerprint identification card, available from your local police department or your counselor. (b) Using clear adhesive tape, a pencil, and plain paper, record your own fingerprints or those of another person.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fingerprinting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 18)* • **Group Organizer:** Activity & Observation Log
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 4): Complete all Recording Rolled and Plain Prints discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** Pressing a heavily inked finger straight down with firm pressure produces the clearest rolled impression on an 8-by-8-inch fingerprint card.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Excessive ink or heavy downward pressure clogs the valleys between friction ridges and smears the print, whereas a thin, even layer of ink rolled smoothly from nail edge to nail edge captures clear ridge details (p. 16).

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Identify and Label Patterns: When you have learned the three basic types of fingerprint patterns (seethe next chapter, "Fingerprint Pattern Types"), identify and label (p. 16).
- Smudge-Resistant Print Handling: You can handle fresh work immediately with little danger of smudging (p. 16).
- Cleaning Supplies and Print Cards: Denatured alcohol works well as a cleansing fluid. You can get paper fingerprint cards from your local police department (p. 16).
- Be sure to ask for the personal identification type (p. 16).

#### Req 4a: Rolled and Plain Card Impressions
**Official Requirement 4a:** Make both rolled and plain impressions. Make these on an 8-by-8-inch fingerprint identification card, available from your local police department or your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fingerprinting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 18)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4a Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Rolled and Plain Impressions)
- **Common Myth:** When rolling a fingerprint on a card, you should roll the finger back and forth across the paper twice to make sure the ink transfers completely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A rolled impression must be rolled in a single smooth motion from one side of the fingernail to the other and lifted straight up so the ridges do not double-print or smudge (p. 16).

### Req 5: Fingerprint Pattern Types and Subcategories
**Official Requirement 5:** Show your counselor you can identify the three basic types of fingerprint patterns and their subcategories. Using your own hand, identify the types of patterns you see.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Fingerprinting Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 18, p. 23)* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (You Can Identify The Three)
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** BSA Youth Protection & Buddy System (Req 5): Complete all Fingerprint Pattern Types and Subcategories discussions and demonstrations with Two-Deep Leadership and your patrol buddy.
- **Common Myth:** A radial loop always slopes toward the left side of a fingerprint card regardless of whether the print came from the left or right hand.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A radial loop opens and slopes toward the thumb (the radius bone side of the forearm), so its direction on a card reverses between the left hand and the right hand (p. 18).
