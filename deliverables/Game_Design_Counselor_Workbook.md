## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 8 (Explain):** Do ONE of the following: (a) With your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval, visit with a professional in the game development industry and ask them about their job and how it fits into the overall development process. (b) Meet with a professional in game development education and discuss the skills they emphasize in the classroom.

## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 6:** Do the following: Note: You must have your counselor's approval of your concept before you begin creating the prototype. (a) Prototype your game from requirement 5. If applicable, demonstrate to your counselor that you have addressed player safety through the rules and equipment. Record your work in your game design notebook. (b) Test your prototype with as many other people as you need to meet the player format. Compare the play experience to your descriptions from requirement 5(b). Correct unclear rules, holes in the rules, dead ends, and obvious rule exploits. Change at least one rule, mechanic, or objective from your first version of the game, and describe why you are making the change. Play the game again. Record in your game design notebook whether or not your change had the expected effect. (c) Repeat 6(b) at least two more times and record the results in your game design notebook.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** True difficulty encompasses cognitive load, spatial reasoning, resource scarcity, and time pressure imposed on players.

### Req 3: Intellectual Property Protection Systems
**Official Requirement 3:** Define the term intellectual property. Describe the types of intellectual property associated with the game design industry. Describe how intellectual property is protected and why protection is necessary. Define and give an example of a licensed property.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Game Design Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 48, p. 63)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does copyright law refuse to protect general game mechanics like jumping or rolling dice?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Copyright protects original artistic assets, music, code, and dialogue, but not mechanics.
  - Trademark Function: Trademarks protect studio brands, franchise logos, game titles, and commercial merchandising identities.
  - Patent Utility: Patents protect novel mechanical innovations, physical hardware controllers, and specialized technical systems.
  - Licensing contracts permit developers to build games using external proprietary story worlds.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Legal Definition: Define intellectual property as proprietary creations of the mind recognized by law.
  - Step 2 - Category Classification: Differentiate copyright, trademark, trade secret, and patent protections within game development.
  - Step 3 - Protection Analysis: Detail why developers require legal protections to secure financial returns on investments.
  - Step 4 - Licensing Review: Examine an existing licensed franchise game to explain owner permissions and obligations.
- **Common Myth:** Game developers can copyright abstract rule mechanics like roll-and-move or worker-placement.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Patent law protects unique apparatuses, but copyright protects only the specific text, art, and audiovisual presentation.
