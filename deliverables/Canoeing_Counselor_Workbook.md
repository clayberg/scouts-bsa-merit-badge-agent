## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 4:** Do the following: (a) Name and point out the parts of a paddle. (b) Demonstrate how to correctly size a paddle for a paddler in a sitting position and a kneeling position.
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Do the following: (a) Review with your counselor the characteristics of life jackets most appropriate for canoeing and tell why a life jacket must always be worn while paddling. (b) Demonstrate how to select and properly fit the correct size life jacket. (c) Review the importance of safety and rescue equipment such as a sound signal device, extra paddle, sponge, bailer, bilge pump, rescue sling, ropes, and throw bag.
- [ ] **Station Req 6:** Review with your counselor the general care and maintenance of canoes, paddles, and other canoeing equipment.
- [ ] **Station Req 7:** With a companion, use a properly equipped canoe to demonstrate the following: (a) Safely carry and launch the canoe from a dock or shore (both, if possible). (b) Safely land the canoe on a dock or shore (both, if possible) and return it to its proper storage location. (c) Demonstrate kneeling and sitting positions in a canoe and explain the proper use for each position. (d) Change places while afloat in the canoe.
- [ ] **Station Req 8:** With a companion, use a properly equipped canoe to demonstrate the following: (a) Safely exit the canoe in deep water without losing contact with the canoe. Reenter the canoe with the assistance of the other paddler without capsizing the boat. (b) Safely perform a controlled capsize of the canoe and demonstrate how staying with a capsized canoe will support both paddlers. (c) Swim, tow, or push a swamped canoe 50 feet to shallow water. In the shallow water, empty the swamped canoe and reenter it. (d) In deep water, rescue a swamped canoe and its paddlers. After checking that those in the water do not need immediate assistance, empty the swamped canoe and help the paddlers safely reenter their boat without capsizing. (e) Perform a canoe rescue of a conscious swimmer. (f) Using a rescue (throw) bag from shore, lay the line within 3 feet of a conscious swimmer 30 feet away. Show how to repack the bag when done.
- [ ] **Station Req 9:** With a companion, using a properly equipped canoe to demonstrate the following tandem maneuvers while paddling in unison on opposite sides and without changing sides. Each paddler must demonstrate these maneuvers in both the bow and stern and on both sides of the canoe while maintaining trim and balance of the canoe: (a) Pivot (spin) the canoe 180° (half circle) to the right from a stationary position, stop, and return to the starting position by pivoting to the left using draw and pushaway strokes or using forward and reverse sweeps staying within 2 boat lengths of the starting position. (b) Move a canoe sideways (abeam) in one direction for 10 feet and then return to the starting position using the draw and pushaway strokes. (c) While in forward motion, stop the canoe within two boat lengths using the backstroke. (d) While in the stern position and without assistance from the bow paddler, paddle in a straight line 15-20 boat lengths using an appropriate steering stroke, e. g., the J-stroke or thumb-down rudder stroke with or without a stern pry. (e) Move the canoe in a straight line 15-20 boat lengths with the bow paddler using as necessary the forward stroke, draw stroke or forward sweep and the stern paddler using an appropriate steering stroke, e. g., the J-stroke or thumb-down rudder stroke with or without a stern pry. (f) While maintaining forward motion, turn the canoe 90° in an arc to the right in 5-10 boat lengths with the bow paddler using as necessary either the draw stroke or forward sweep and the stern paddler using only the forward stroke. Repeat the maneuver turning the canoe 90° to the left. (g) Move the canoe backwards in a straight line 3-4 boat lengths using as necessary the back stroke, reverse sweep or draw stroke in the bow or the stern. (h) Complete a figure of 8 course around markers 3-4 boat lengths apart using appropriate strokes including the draw stroke, and the forward and reverse sweeps.

### Req 1: Paddling Hazard Response Protocols
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Review with your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in canoeing activities and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Review prevention, symptoms, and first-aid treatment for the following injuries or illnesses that could occur while canoeing: blisters, cold-water shock and hypothermia, dehydration, heat-related illnesses, sunburn, sprains, and strains. (c) Discuss the Scouting America Safety Afloat policy. Tell how it applies to canoeing activities.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Canoeing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 16, p. 17)* • **Group Organizer:** Symptoms, First Aid & Prevention Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never canoe on rivers where high flow rates or submerged trees create impassable strainers.
> **Starting Gate (Prerequisite Merit Badge / Certification):** BSA Swimmer Classification Gate - Scout must complete the official BSA Swimmer test (100 yards + 1-minute float) before on-water skill stations.
- **Common Myth:** Paddlers can rely on swimming ability instead of wearing personal flotation devices on calm flatwater.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Safety Afloat mandates adult supervision, physical fitness, lifejacket wear, and skilled leadership on all trips.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Weather Assessment: Check local forecasts for wind velocity and approaching squalls before launching any craft.
- Fallen trees and river sweepers trap paddlers against fast current through suction.
- Immersion in water below seventy degrees triggers involuntary gasping and rapid hypothermia.
- Lightning Precautions: Exit the water immediately and seek low ground during sudden electrical storms.

### Req 3: Canoe Anatomy Performance Characteristics
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Name and point out the major parts of a canoe. (b) Describe how the length and shape of a canoe affect its performance.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Canoeing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 27, p. 28)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Do not step directly on canoe thwarts or gunwales during loading or balance maneuvers.
- **Common Myth:** A wider canoe always performs better than a narrow hull under windy paddling conditions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Canoes with rocker pivot easily while straight keellines track straighter across windy open water.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Hull Terminology: The bow points forward while the stern sits at the rear section.
- Gunwale Structure: Gunwales run along top edges to provide rigidity and support wooden thwarts.
- A full external keel aids tracking but impedes quick sideways river turns.
- Rocker Profile: Upward curvature at hull ends improves maneuverability but sacrifices straight tracking speed.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never use a cracked paddle shaft that could splinter suddenly under heavy forward stroke loads.

- **Pamphlet Fact:** A kneeling paddler requires a shorter shaft length than a paddler seated on benches.

- Kneeling Sizing: Measure shaft length from water level up to the nose when kneeling.

### Req 7: Canoe Boarding Trim Maneuvers
**Official Requirement 7 (Parent Overview):** With a companion, use a properly equipped canoe to demonstrate the following: (a) Safely carry and launch the canoe from a dock or shore (both, if possible). (b) Safely land the canoe on a dock or shore (both, if possible) and return it to its proper storage location. > **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Always keep your weight centered directly over the keel line when boarding or changing positions.
- **Common Myth:** Paddlers should jump into the canoe together while pushing off from the shoreline.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Keeping your center of gravity low along the hull centerline prevents capsizing during entry.

- Shoreline Launching: Place the canoe parallel to shore in knee deep water before boarding.
- Kneeling Stability: Kneel with knees spread wide against bilge chins for rough water balance.
- Centerline Movement: Move along the middle floor while crouched and holding gunwales for balance.

**Official Requirement 8 (Parent Overview):** With a companion, use a properly equipped canoe to demonstrate the following: (a) Safely exit the canoe in deep water without losing contact with the canoe. > **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never wrap a rescue throw line around your wrists, arms, or fingers under tension.
- **Common Myth:** Paddlers must immediately climb onto top of an overturned swamped canoe hull for safety.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A swamped modern canoe maintains positive flotation and supports paddlers holding onto the gunwales.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Stay With Craft: Never abandon a capsized canoe because it provides positive flotation and visibility.
- Invert the rescued canoe across the gunwales of the upright rescue boat.
- Controlled Deep Reentry: Enter over the side gunwale while your partner counterbalances the opposite side.
- Aim the rescue throw bag past the victim shoulder and pull steadily.

### Req 9: Tandem Canoe Stroke Maneuvers
**Official Requirement 9 (Parent Overview):** With a companion, using a properly equipped canoe to demonstrate the following tandem maneuvers while paddling in unison on opposite sides and without changing sides. Each paddler must demonstrate these maneuvers in both the bow and stern and on both sides of the canoe while maintaining trim and balance of the canoe: (a) Pivot (spin) the canoe 180° (half circle) to the right from a stationary position, stop, and return to the starting position by pivoting to the left using draw and pushaway strokes or using forward and reverse sweeps staying within 2 boat lengths of the starting position. (b) Move a canoe sideways (abeam) in one direction for 10 feet and then return to the starting position using the draw and pushaway strokes. (c) While in forward motion, stop the canoe within two boat lengths using the backstroke. (d) While in the stern position and without assistance from the bow paddler, paddle in a straight line 15-20 boat lengths using an appropriate steering stroke, e. g., the J-stroke or thumb-down rudder stroke with or without a stern pry. (e) Move the canoe in a straight line 15-20 boat lengths with the bow paddler using as necessary the forward stroke, draw stroke or forward sweep and the stern paddler using an appropriate steering stroke, e. g., the J-stroke or thumb-down rudder stroke with or without a stern pry. (f) While maintaining forward motion, turn the canoe 90° in an arc to the right in 5-10 boat lengths with the bow paddler using as necessary either the draw stroke or forward sweep and the stern paddler using only the forward stroke. Repeat the maneuver turning the canoe 90° to the left. (g) Move the canoe backwards in a straight line 3-4 boat lengths using as necessary the back stroke, reverse sweep or draw stroke in the bow or the stern. (h) Complete a figure of 8 course around markers 3-4 boat lengths apart using appropriate strokes including the draw stroke, and the forward and reverse sweeps.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Canoeing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 51, p. 71)* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never pry your paddle against gunwales violently enough to risk snapping the shaft.
- **Common Myth:** Tandem paddlers should switch sides frequently whenever the canoe begins to turn off course.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** The stern paddler steers using J-strokes and pushes while the bow paddler provides power.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Tandem partners paddle on opposing sides to balance lateral propulsion forces effectively.
- Draw Mechanics: Reach outward with blade vertical and pull water directly toward the canoe.
- Pushaway Execution: Rotate blade parallel to gunwale and push water firmly away from hull.
- J-Stroke Mastery: Hook the paddle thumb downward at stroke end to counteract natural turning.
