## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 1 (Describe):** Do ONE of the following: (a) For one day, keep a log in which you describe your communication activities. Keep track of the time and different ways you spend communicating, such as talking person-to-person, listening to teachers, listening to the radio or podcasts, watching television, using social media, reading books and other print media, and using any electronic communication device. Discuss with your counselor what your log reveals about the importance of communication in your life. Think of ways to improve your communication skills. (b) For three days, keep a journal of your listening experiences. Identify one example of each of the following, and discuss with your counselor when you have listened to each: obtain information, be persuaded, appreciate or enjoy something, and understand someone's feelings. (c) In a small-group setting, meet with other Scouts or with friends. Have them share personal stories about significant events in their lives that affected them in some way. Take note of how each Scout participates in the group discussion and how effectively each Scout communicates their story. Report what you have learned to your counselor about the differences you observed in effective communication. (d) List as many ways as you can think of to communicate with others (face-to-face, by telephone, letter, email, text messages, social media, and so on). For each type of communication, discuss with your counselor an instance when that method might not be appropriate or effective.
- [ ] **Req 4 (Discuss):** Interview someone you know fairly well, like, or respect because of his or her position, talent, career, or life experiences. Listen actively to learn as much as you can about the person. Then prepare and deliver to your counselor an introduction of the person as though this person were to be a guest speaker, and include reasons why the audience would want to hear this person speak. Show how you would call to invite this person to speak.
- [ ] **Req 5 (Prepare):** Attend a public meeting (city council, school board, debate) approved by your counselor where several points of view are given on a single issue. Practice active listening skills and take careful notes of each point of view. Prepare an objective report that includes all points of view that were expressed, and share this with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 7 (Prepare):** Do ONE of the following: (a) Write to the editor of a magazine or your local newspaper to express your opinion or share information on any subject you choose. Send your message by fax, email, or regular mail. (b) Create a webpage or blog of special interest to you (for instance, your troop or crew, a hobby, or a sport). Include at least three articles or entries and one photograph or illustration, and one link to some other webpage or blog that would be helpful to someone who visits the webpage or blog you have created. Note: It is not necessary to post your webpage or blog to the internet, but if you decide to do so, you must first share it with your parent or guardian and counselor and get their permission. (c) Use desktop publishing to produce a newsletter, brochure, flyer, or other printed material for your troop or crew, class at school, or other group. Include at least one article and one photograph or illustration.

### Req 3: Five Minute Speech Delivery
**Official Requirement 3:** Write a five-minute speech. Give it at a meeting of a group.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Communication Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 33, p. 35)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What verbal hooks proved most effective at capturing immediate listener attention?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Speech Topic Narrowing: Limit the central thesis to one specific concept manageable within five minutes.
  - Introduction Hook Design: Open with a compelling question or narrative that immediately engages the audience.
  - Speech Transition Phrasing: Connect supporting body paragraphs using clear verbal signposts that guide listeners smoothly.
  - Vocal Projection Control: Project your voice from the diaphragm to reach listeners in the back.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Topic Outline Formulation: Draft a three part outline containing an introduction, body, and conclusion.
  - Step 2 - Script Manuscript Writing: Write the complete speech draft ensuring logical flow between all supporting points.
  - Step 3 - Timed Speaking Rehearsal: Practice speaking the text aloud with a stopwatch to confirm five minutes.
  - Step 4 - Audience Presentation Delivery: Deliver the finished speech before a group using natural eye contact sweeps.
- **Common Myth:** Memorizing every word verbatim prevents nervous delivery mistakes during formal public speeches.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Extemporaneous delivery from key phrase note cards allows natural audience eye contact adaptation.

### Req 5: Public Meeting Objective Report
**Official Requirement 5:** Attend a public meeting (city council, school board, debate) approved by your counselor where several points of view are given on a single issue. Practice active listening skills and take careful notes of each point of view. Prepare an objective report that includes all points of view that were expressed, and share this with your counselor.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Communication Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 30, p. 35)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 5 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Follow Youth Protection guidelines when attending public meetings.
> **Starting Gate (Counselor Pre-Approval Required):** Counselor Pre-Approval Required - Review your Communication Req 5 preparation with your Merit Badge Counselor before moving to subsequent tasks.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** What techniques did effective public speakers use to persuade the governing board?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Study the public meeting agenda beforehand to understand upcoming debate action items.
  - Multi Perspective Note Taking: Document arguments presented by competing speakers without recording personal subjective opinions.
  - Objective Summary Composition: Write a balanced report stating both supporting and opposing civic policy arguments.
  - Note how meeting chairs enforce parliamentary procedure and public speaking time limits.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Meeting Approval Confirmation: Secure counselor approval before attending a local municipal council or school board.
  - Step 2 - Civic Debate Attendance: Attend the scheduled session while observing speaker decorum and official parliamentary rules.
  - Step 3 - Perspective Argument Recording: Take systematic notes detailing evidence presented by speakers representing contrasting viewpoints.
  - Step 4 - Balanced Report Submission: Write an impartial written summary outlining opposing positions for the counselor review.
- **Common Myth:** An objective report should declare a winning side based on personal Scout opinions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Objective reporting accurately outlines all presented arguments without inserting personal editorial commentary.
