### Req 1: Global Citizenship and World Community
**Official Requirement 1:** Explain what citizenship in the world means to you and what you think it takes to be a good world citizen.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Citizenship in the World Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 13, p. 16)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 1 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How can a person demonstrate patriotism toward the United States while honoring duties as a global citizen?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Universal Human Rights: World citizens respect fundamental individual liberties across diverse national and cultural borders.
  - Global Interdependence: Nations depend on international trade networks to satisfy domestic consumer and industrial needs.
  - Civic Engagement: Responsible world citizens participate actively in community projects addressing global environmental challenges.
  - Cultural Empathy: Thoughtful dialogue dismantles national prejudices and builds mutual trust among disparate populations.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Personal Journaling: Write your personal philosophy regarding global obligations to promote international peace.
  - Step 2 - Character Assessment: Identify three moral traits that define an effective and ethical world leader.
  - Step 3 - Community Connection: Map local agricultural goods exported abroad to demonstrate economic global interdependence.
  - Step 4 - Counselor Presentation: Share personal reflections with your counselor during a scheduled patrol roundtable.
- **Common Myth:** World citizenship requires allegiance to a single world government that replaces individual nation states.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** World citizenship describes a personal ethical awareness of global interconnectedness while honoring national loyalty.

### Req 2: United States Citizenship Obligations
**Official Requirement 2:** Explain how one becomes a citizen in the United States, and explain the rights, duties, and obligations of U. S. citizenship. Discuss the similarities and differences between the rights, duties, and obligations of U. S. citizens and the citizens of two other countries.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Citizenship in the World Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 12, p. 13)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (2-Column Comparison Table):** Req 2 Technical Comparison Matrix
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does the United States require jury service as a mandatory civic duty rather than a voluntary option?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Naturalization Process: Foreign nationals complete lawful residency periods, pass civics evaluations, and swear loyalty oaths.
  - Jus Soli Doctrine: The Fourteenth Amendment guarantees birthright citizenship to persons born on United States territory.
  - Civic Duties: Citizens must obey statutory laws, pay levied taxes, and serve on judicial juries.
  - Constitutional Protections: The Bill of Rights guarantees freedom of speech, assembly, and fair legal proceedings.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Pathway Examination: Compare naturalization requirements with birthright citizenship doctrines established by constitutional law.
  - Step 2 - Civics Testing: Review official naturalization exam questions covering American history and democratic institutions.
  - Step 3 - Rights Analysis: Contrast civil liberties in constitutional republics against individual rights in authoritarian regimes.
  - Step 4 - Chart Creation: Construct a comparison table outlining civic duties in democratic and non-democratic countries.
- **Common Myth:** Every country in the world grants automatic birthright citizenship to infants born within its physical borders.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Most nations follow jus sanguinis citizenship principles based strictly on parental heritage rather than location.
