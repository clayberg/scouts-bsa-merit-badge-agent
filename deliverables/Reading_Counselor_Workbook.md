## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 1 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Take a tour of a library. Discuss with your counselor how the library is organized and what resources and/or services are offered in the library. (b) Learn how to search for material using a library's card catalog or computerized catalog by author, title, and subject. (c) In a library, search the card catalog or computerized catalog for six books of four different types, such as poetry, fiction, nonfiction, and biographies. (d) With the assistance of your counselor or the librarian, see if you can locate on the shelves the six books you selected. (e) Explain what a library card is, why it is needed, and how to get one.
- [ ] **Req 4 (Explain):** Read a nonfiction book or magazine that teaches you how to do something like cooking, wood-building projects, video game design, science experiments, knot-tying, etc. With your counselor's and parent or guardian's permission, complete a project from the book. Share your experience with your counselor. Reading a merit badge pamphlet will not count toward completing this requirement.
- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** Read about the world around you from any two sources: books, magazines, newspapers, the internet (with your parent or guardian's permission), field manuals, etc. Topics may include Scouting, sports, environmental problems, politics, social issues, current events, nature, religion, etc. Discuss what you have learned with your counselor.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Explain):** With your counselor's and parent or guardian's permission, choose ONE of the following activities and devote at least four hours of service to that activity. Discuss your participation with your counselor. (a) Read to a sick, blind, or homebound person in a hospital or in an extended-care facility. (b) Perform volunteer work at your school library or a public library. (c) Read stories to younger children, in a group or individually. (d) Organize a book swap in your troop, school, or place of worship. (e) Organize a book drive to collect books. Donate them to an organization in need.

### Req 1: Library Systems and Catalog Navigation
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Take a tour of a library. Discuss with your counselor how the library is organized and what resources and/or services are offered in the library. (b) Learn how to search for material using a library's card catalog or computerized catalog by author, title, and subject. (c) In a library, search the card catalog or computerized catalog for six books of four different types, such as poetry, fiction, nonfiction, and biographies. (d) With the assistance of your counselor or the librarian, see if you can locate on the shelves the six books you selected. (e) Explain what a library card is, why it is needed, and how to get one.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Reading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 21, p. 34)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use step stools with locking caster wheels when reaching for volumes located on high shelves.
- **Common Myth:** Beginners often believe librarians memorize the location of every physical book in the building.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Librarians rely on systematic alphanumeric classification schemes like Dewey Decimal or Library of Congress call numbers.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Dewey Decimal System: Ten numerical classes organize all human knowledge across nonfiction book shelves.
- Catalog Search Fields: Online public access catalogs index resources by author name, title, and subject.
- Call Number Labels: Spine stickers display classification numbers to guide physical retrieval on stack shelves.
- Borrower Card Privileges: Registration establishes patron accounts granting access to physical books and digital databases.

### Req 3: Multi Genre Reading and Response
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Read four different types of books, such as poetry, fiction, nonfiction, or biographies. Do one of the following for each book you have read: (a) Write a review of the book. Include what you liked and/or didn't like about the book. Include whether you would recommend this book, and if so, who might enjoy reading it. (b) Watch a movie based on the book. What was the same between the book and movie? What was different? Which did you enjoy more? Discuss this with your counselor. (c) Give a book talk to your class, troop, or patrol.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Reading Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 20, p. 36)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Take ten-minute visual breaks every hour to prevent eye fatigue during long reading sessions.
- **Common Myth:** Scouts sometimes think a book review is merely a detailed chronological plot summary.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A book review analyzes thematic meaning, literary craftsmanship, and provides a reasoned reader recommendation.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Four Genre Requirement: Readers explore varied literary forms like poetry, fiction, biography, and informational texts.
- Constructive Review Drafting: Critical written evaluations identify plot strengths, character arcs, and target audience recommendations.
- Cinematic Adaptation Comparison: Film adaptations frequently alter narrative subplots and internal monologues for visual pacing.
- Dynamic book talks convey central themes to engage peers without spoiling endings.
