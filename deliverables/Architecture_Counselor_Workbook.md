## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 1 (Demonstrate):** Do the following: (a) Tour your community and list the different types of buildings you see. Try to identify buildings that can be associated with a specific period of history or style of architecture. Make a sketch of the building you most admire. (b) Select a historical architectural achievement that has had a major impact on society. Using resources such as the internet (with your parent or guardian's permission), books, and magazines, find out how this achievement has influenced the world today. Tell your counselor what you learned.
- [ ] **Req 3 (Explain):** Do ONE of the following: (a) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's permission and approval, arrange to meet with an architect. Ask to see the scale model of a building and the drawings that a builder would use to construct this building. Discuss why the different building materials were selected. Look at the details in the drawings and the model to see how the materials and components are attached to each other during construction. (b) With your parent or guardian's and counselor's permission and approval, arrange to meet with an architect at a construction site. Ask the architect to bring drawings that the builder uses to construct the building. While at the site, discuss why the different building materials being used were selected. Discuss how the different building materials and components are attached to each other during construction. Note: To visit a construction site will require advance planning. You will need permission from a parent or guardian, counselor, the architect, and the construction site manager. A construction site is a very dangerous place. While there, you will need to closely follow the site manager's directions and comply with all the safety procedures, including wearing a hard hat, protective eyewear, and proper footwear. Be aware of the changing conditions at the site, and stay with the architect or site manager. (c) Interview someone who might be your client (such as a prospective homeowner or business owner) if you were an architect. Find out what your client's requirements would be for designing a new home or business building. Write a short program including a list of requirements for the project, the functions of the building and site, how the functions relate to one another, and the goals of the project.

### Req 1: Community Architectural Style Survey
**Official Requirement 1 (Parent Overview):** Do the following: (a) Tour your community and list the different types of buildings you see. Try to identify buildings that can be associated with a specific period of history or style of architecture. Make a sketch of the building you most admire. (b) Select a historical architectural achievement that has had a major impact on society. Using resources such as the internet (with your parent or guardian's permission), books, and magazines, find out how this achievement has influenced the world today. Tell your counselor what you learned.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Field Sketch & Diagram Canvas
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Architecture Req 1 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** Architectural style is merely decorative exterior trim applied over identical wooden frames.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Style reflects structural engineering traditions, regional climate needs, and indigenous building material availability.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Historic Style Identification: Exterior moldings and window pediments reveal specific construction eras in regional neighborhoods.
- Structural Load Distribution: Heavy stone arches redirect gravity forces downward through stout supporting masonry piers.
- Field Sketching Method: Proportional grid guidelines help draft accurate elevations before recording fine decorative details.
- Aqueduct Civil Infrastructure: Durable Roman water channels transformed continental urban settlement density and public health.

### Req 2: Sustainable Design and Conservation
**Official Requirement 2 (Parent Overview):** In following the Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code, Scouts pledge to "Travel and camp on durable surfaces" and to "Leave what you find" and to "Be conservation-minded." Discuss the following with your counselor: (a) The term sustainable architecture. Identify three features typical of green buildings. (b) The difference between renewable building materials and recycled building materials, and how each can be used in construction. (c) The relationship of architecture with its surrounding environment and the community. (d) How entire buildings can be reused rather than torn down when they no longer serve their original purpose.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** 2-Column Comparison Table
- **Common Myth:** Green architecture depends exclusively on expensive rooftop photovoltaic panels and computer sensors.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Passive solar design, natural cross-ventilation, and high thermal mass reduce carbon footprints without mechanical systems.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Passive Solar Orientation: Placing large glazed openings along southern walls maximizes natural winter daytime heating.
- Renewable Material Sourcing: Rapidly harvested bamboo flooring reduces logging pressures on mature old-growth hardwood forests.
- Environmental Topography Integration: Bermed earth construction tempers indoor temperatures by using ground thermal mass effectively.
- Adaptive Building Preservation: Converting obsolete industrial brick warehouses into modern residential apartments saves embodied energy.

**Source:** *Official Scouting America Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Group Organizer:** Skill Demonstration Checklist
> **Starting Gate (Parent / Guardian Permission Required):** Parent/Guardian Consent & Review - Review and complete Architecture Req 3 with your parent or guardian's approval in compliance with BSA Youth Protection.
- **Common Myth:** Architects produce finished building blueprints in a single draft without extensive client revisions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Design progresses iteratively through schematic design, design development, and detailed construction documentation phases.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Contract Document Packages: Working drawings detail dimensions, wall cross-sections, and finish schedules for trade contractors.
- Physical Scale Prototyping: Three-dimensional massing models allow clients to evaluate volume, shadow casting, and sightlines.
- Jobsite Field Coordination: Architects conduct site visits to confirm that construction aligns with stamped specifications.
- Client Program Development: Architectural programming identifies spatial square footage, occupant headcount, and accessibility requirements.

### Req 4: Measured Room Floor Plan
**Official Requirement 4:** Measure a room such as one where you live or where your troop meets. Make an accurately scaled drawing of the room's floor plan showing walls, doors, closets, windows, and any built-in furniture or cabinets. Neatly label your drawing with the following: your name, the date, what room you drew, and the scale of the drawing. (Drawing scale: ¼ inch = 1 foot)
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Architecture Merit Badge Pamphlet* • **Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 4 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix (Room Such As One Where)
- **Common Myth:** Floor plans show a view looking straight down from the ceiling rafters.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** A floor plan is a horizontal section cut roughly four feet above the floor plane.
