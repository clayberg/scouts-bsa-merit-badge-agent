## 2. Pre-Class Prerequisite Checklist ('Bring Proof to Class')

- [ ] **Req 3 (Explain):** Modern Particle Physics. Do the following: (a) Explain how a particle accelerator works. (b) Do ONE of the following: (1) Visit an accelerator, research lab, or university and discuss with a scientist how they study the properties of the nucleus or nucleons. (2) List three particle accelerators and describe several experiments conducted and their associated basic science and practical applications.
- [ ] **Req 5 (Explain):** Radiation Safety. Do ONE of the following: (a) Using a radiation survey meter and a radioactive source, show how the counts per minute change as the source gets closer to or farther from the radiation detector. Adults must handle radioactive materials. Place three different materials between the source and the detector, then explain any differences in the measurements per minute. Explain how time, distance, and shielding can reduce an individual's radiation dose. (b) Describe how radon is detected in homes. Discuss the steps taken for the long-term and short-term test methods, tell how to interpret the results, and explain when each type of test should be used. Explain the health concerns related to radon gas and tell what steps can be taken to reduce radon in buildings. (c) Visit a place where X-rays are used. Draw a floor plan of this room. Show where the unit, the unit operator, and the patient would be when the X-ray unit is operated. Explain the precautions taken and the importance of those precautions for the safety of the operator.
- [ ] **Req 6 (Discuss):** Nuclear Energy. Do ONE of the following; then discuss with your counselor how nuclear energy is used to produce electricity: (a) Make a drawing showing how nuclear fission happens. Observe a mousetrap reactor (setup by an adult) and use it to explain how a chain reaction could be started. Explain how a chain reaction could be stopped or controlled in a nuclear reactor. Explain what is meant by a "critical mass." (b) Visit a local nuclear power plant or nuclear reactor either in person or online (with your parent or guardian's permission). Learn how a reactor works and how the plant generates electricity. Find out what percentage of electricity in the United States and in your state is generated by nuclear power plants and by other methods. Make a graph of the information you find.
- [ ] **Req 8 (Prepare):** Careers. Explore careers related to nuclear science. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include, with your parent or guardian's permission, an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Always maintain maximum separation distance from active sources and wear nitrile gloves to prevent skin contamination.

> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Use blunt craft wire and safety glasses when constructing structural orbital models of atomic nuclei.
- **Common Myth:** Every isotope of an element possesses an unstable radioactive nucleus that decays spontaneously.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Most elements contain stable nonradioactive isotopes alongside unstable radioisotopes that undergo radioactive decay.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Subatomic Composition: Protons and neutrons reside inside the nucleus while electrons orbit within discrete energy shells.
- Nuclear Decay Modes: Radioactive nuclei achieve stability by emitting alpha particles, beta electrons, or energetic gamma photons.
- Quark Composition: Protons consist of two up quarks and one down quark held by gluons.
- Isotope Identification: Isotopes share identical atomic numbers of protons but differ in total nuclear neutron count.

### Req 3: Particle Accelerators And Physics
**Official Requirement 3 (Parent Overview):** Modern Particle Physics. Do the following: (a) Explain how a particle accelerator works. (b) Do ONE of the following: (1) Visit an accelerator, research lab, or university and discuss with a scientist how they study the properties of the nucleus or nucleons. (2) List three particle accelerators and describe several experiments conducted and their associated basic science and practical applications.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Nuclear Science Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 23, p. 24)* • **Group Organizer:** Concept & Examples Notes
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Stay behind radiation interlocks and shielding doors during facility operations when accelerators generate active beam radiation.
- **Common Myth:** Particle accelerators generate dangerous nuclear explosions during high speed subatomic particle collisions.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Accelerators manipulate minute quantities of subatomic particles within vacuum pipes, producing microscopic localized particle reactions.

**Official Pamphlet Summary:**
- Beam Acceleration: Radiofrequency cavities supply oscillating electric fields that transfer kinetic momentum to charged particle bunches.
- Magnetic Steering: Powerful superconducting dipole magnets curve particle paths inside circular synchrotron vacuum beamlines.
- Collision Physics: High energy impacts convert kinetic energy into mass, generating novel short lived subatomic hadrons.
- Detector Systems: Multilayer sensor arrays track particle momentum, electrical charge sign, and total absorbed energy.
