## 3. Hands-On Skill Station Verification Rubric

- [ ] **Station Req 1:** Do the following: (a) Explain to your counselor the most likely hazards you may encounter while participating in climbing and rappelling activities and what you should do to anticipate, help prevent, mitigate, and respond to these hazards. (b) Show that you know first aid for and how to prevent injuries or illnesses that could occur during climbing activities, including heat and cold reactions, dehydration, stopped breathing, sprains, abrasions, fractures, rope burns, blisters, snakebite, concussions, and insect bites or stings. (c) Identify the conditions that must exist before performing CPR on a person.
- [ ] **Station Req 3:** Present yourself properly dressed for belaying, climbing, and rappelling. Explain why you are wearing each piece of gear and discuss qualities of easy movement, entanglement-free, and good weather protection and comfort.
- [ ] **Station Req 4:** Location. Do the following: (a) Explain how the difficulty of climbs is classified, and apply classifications to the rock faces or walls where you will demonstrate your climbing skills. (b) Explain the following: top-rope climbing, lead climbing, and bouldering. (c) Evaluate the safety of a particular climbing area. Consider weather, visibility, the condition of the climbing surface, and any other environmental hazards. (d) Determine how to summon aid to the climbing area in case of an emergency. Discuss what medical training is needed for your group when climbing and rappelling in remote areas.
- [ ] **Station Req 5:** Verbal signals. Explain the importance of using verbal signals during every climb and rappel, and while bouldering. With the help of the counselor or another Scout, demonstrate the verbal signals used by each of the following: (a) Climbers (b) Rappellers (c) Belayers (d) Boulderers and their spotters
- [ ] **Station Req 6:** Rope. Do the following: (a) Describe the kinds of rope acceptable for use in climbing and rappelling. (b) Show how to examine a rope for signs of wear or damage. (c) Discuss ways to prevent a rope from being damaged. (d) Explain when and how a rope should be retired. (e) Properly coil a rope.
- [ ] **Station Req 7:** Knots. Demonstrate the ability to tie each of the following knots. Give at least one example of how each knot is used in belaying, climbing, or rappelling. (a) Figure eight on a bight (b) Figure eight follow-through (c) Water knot (d) Double fisherman's knot (grapevine knot) (e) Prusik hitch applied to rope
- [ ] **Station Req 8:** Harness, Helmet and Gloves. Explain the purpose of each of the following and demonstrate how to put them on correctly. (a) Commercially made climbing harness (b) Climbing helmet (c) Climbing or rappelling gloves
- [ ] **Station Req 10:** Belaying. Do the following: (a) Explain the importance of belaying climbers and rappellers and when it is necessary. (b) Belay three different climbers on three different routes. (c) Serve as a backup belayer on three different climbs. (d) Belay three different rappellers on three different routes. (e) Serve as a backup belayer on three different rappels.
- [ ] **Station Req 11:** Climbing. Do the following: (a) Show the correct way to directly tie a belay rope to your harness. (b) Climb at least three different routes on a rock face or climbing wall, demonstrating good technique and using verbal signals with a belayer.
- [ ] **Station Req 12:** Rappelling. Do the following: (a) Show the correct way to directly tie a belay rope to your harness. (b) Using a carabiner and a rappel device, attach a rappel rope to your harness. (c) Rappel down three different rock faces or three rappel routes on a climbing wall. Use verbal signals to communicate with a belayer, and demonstrate good rappelling technique.
- [ ] **Station Req 13:** Demonstrate ways to store rope, hardware, and other gear used for climbing, rappelling, and belaying.

### Req 2: Climbing Environmental Stewardship Principles
**Official Requirement 2:** Learn the Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code and explain what they mean.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Climbing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 9, p. 92)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 2 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never establish climbing descents through sensitive wildlife habitats or areas marked with seasonal closures.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** How does brushing chalk off handholds preserve natural rock surfaces for future climbers?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Durable Surfaces: Travel on established trails to preserve sensitive vegetation growing around cliff bases.
  - Waste Removal: Pack out all athletic tape, food scraps, and solid personal sanitary waste.
  - Leave natural rocks, plants, and historical artifacts undisturbed for other crag visitors.
  - Respect Wildlife: Avoid disturbing nesting cliff birds during designated springtime nesting restriction periods.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Trail Travel: Walk exclusively along marked trails when approaching the designated rock cliff base.
  - Step 2 - Staging Placement: Position backpacks and gear on durable flat rocks rather than fragile vegetation.
  - Step 3 - Chalk Cleanup: Brush excess chalk residue from holds before packing equipment at day end.
  - Step 4 - Waste Sweep: Collect every stray wrapper, tape scrap, and gear strand before departing.
- **Common Myth:** Leaving colored climbing chalk on natural stone surfaces causes no ecological or visual impact.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Modern chalk formulas must brush off cleanly, and climbers must pack out all tape and human waste.

### Req 3: Climber Apparel Safety Requirements
**Official Requirement 3:** Present yourself properly dressed for belaying, climbing, and rappelling. Explain why you are wearing each piece of gear and discuss qualities of easy movement, entanglement-free, and good weather protection and comfort.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Climbing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 49, p. 90)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Concept & Examples Notes):** Req 3 Structured Technical Study & Explanation Matrix
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Remove rings and tuck in drawstrings before handling ropes to prevent severe traumatic entanglement.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does a loose shirt hem pose a major hazard during rappelling operations?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Snug Fit: Wear form-fitting athletic layers that cannot catch inside active belay friction hardware.
  - Tie long hair securely back against the head to avoid moving gear.
  - Jewelry Removal: Remove all rings, necklaces, and wristwatches before touching climbing ropes or hardware.
  - Weather Shells: Pack breathable, water-resistant outer layers to withstand sudden temperature drops at crags.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Jewelry Removal: Take off all rings, necklaces, bracelets, and watches before putting on gear.
  - Step 2 - Hair Securing: Fasten long hair tightly behind the neck using a clean elastic band.
  - Step 3 - Garment Tucking: Tuck loose shirts and waist drawstrings completely inside the climbing harness waistbelt.
  - Step 4 - Footwear Lacing: Tie climbing shoes securely to prevent foot slippage on small rock edges.
- **Common Myth:** Loose athletic sweatpants are optimal because baggy fabrics never catch inside rappelling carabiners.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Climbing apparel must combine full mobility with tight cuffs to prevent jamming in belay devices.

### Req 13: Climbing Equipment Maintenance Storage
**Official Requirement 13:** Demonstrate ways to store rope, hardware, and other gear used for climbing, rappelling, and belaying.
**Source:** *Official Scouting America Climbing Merit Badge Pamphlet (p. 42, p. 85)* • **Scout Workbook Organizer (Skill Demonstration Checklist):** Req 13 Hands-On Skill Station & EDGE Verification Checklist
> **Guide to Safe Scouting Warning:** Never store nylon life-safety gear in garages near car batteries, fertilizers, or petroleum solvents.
- **Socratic Prompt (Ask Scouts First):** Why does sulfuric acid vapor destroy nylon webbing without leaving obvious visual signs?
- **Official Pamphlet Summary & Expected Answer Key:**
  - Store ropes in well-ventilated dry areas away from damp concrete ground surfaces.
  - Chemical Isolation: Keep nylon climbing gear away from automotive battery acid and bleach fumes.
  - Hardware Protection: Hang metal carabiners and belay devices to prevent corrosion and grit accumulation.
  - Sunlight Shielding: Shield synthetic harnesses and slings from ultraviolet radiation to stop fiber degradation.
- **Step-by-Step Facilitation & Station Procedure:**
  - Step 1 - Gear Cleaning: Wipe dirt and dried grit from hardware using a clean dry cloth.
  - Step 2 - Rope Airing: Air dry damp ropes completely in indirect shade before packing into bags.
  - Step 3 - Equipment Bagging: Place coiled ropes and harnesses inside breathable canvas gear storage bags.
  - Step 4 - Shelf Placement: Store gear bags on elevated shelves away from direct sun and chemicals.
- **Common Myth:** Climbing ropes and harnesses can be stored long-term in hot vehicle trunks safely.
- **Pamphlet Fact:** Store climbing gear in cool, dark, dry locations completely separated from acids and exhaust fumes.
